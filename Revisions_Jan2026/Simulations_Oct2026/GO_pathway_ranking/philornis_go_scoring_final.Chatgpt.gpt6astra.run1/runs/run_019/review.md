## Mechanisms and evidence

**Blood loss and compensation.** Early larvae feed within nostrils and other tissues; later larvae usually occupy nest material and feed externally on nestlings, principally at night. Tissue injury and substantial blood loss cause anemia and high mortality [1]. Small ground finches with greater parasite burdens had lower hemoglobin and more immature erythrocytes, providing direct evidence of a compensatory erythropoietic response [2]. This supports high relevance for erythrocyte production and strong predictions for iron handling, oxygen delivery, hypoxia responses, circulatory compensation, and hemostasis. Avian thrombocytes provide the relevant functional counterpart when considering platelet-labeled hemostatic terms.

**Tissue damage and repair.** Larval feeding damages skin and nasal tissues. Naris and beak abnormalities can persist into adulthood; a study of adult small ground finches did not find corresponding differences in body size or condition [3]. Responses to wounding are directly relevant. Wound repair, epithelial barriers, extracellular matrix repair, angiogenesis, and inflammatory regulation are strong mechanistic predictions, rather than evidence that each particular pathway has been demonstrated to confer resistance.

**Immune resistance.** Parasite-binding antibodies increased experimentally in adult female medium ground finches exposed to larvae. Higher antibody levels were associated with a tendency toward fewer parasites, but the response did not rescue fledging success. Males and young nestlings did not show the same detectable response [4]. A later finch–mockingbird comparison found no significant antibody response in either host species [5]. Thus adaptive immunity is documented but context dependent; B-cell mechanisms are core components. Innate recognition, complement, inflammatory mediators, leukocyte recruitment, and damage clearance are reasonable predictions, without claiming they have each been directly established for this fly. Secondary bacterial defense is plausible after injury; virus-specific processes have little specific support here.

**Nutritional tolerance.** In the comparative experiment, parasitized mockingbird nestlings increased begging, associated with greater parental provisioning and maintained reproductive success despite parasite exposure. Finches lacked the comparable response [5]. Mockingbird tolerance subsequently varied among years and was weaker under low rainfall, consistent with food availability limiting compensatory provisioning [6]. These results motivate energy and nutrient homeostasis, fuel mobilization, growth maintenance, and starvation responses. Specific intracellular metabolic pathways remain predictions or supporting processes.

**Behavioral resistance and avoidance.** Preening, agitation, scratching, and larval removal have been observed, but responses and effectiveness vary with species and nestling age [4,7]. Galapagos flycatchers were filmed removing larvae and capturing adult flies; flies preferentially visited when parents were absent [7]. Finch use of insecticide-treated nesting cotton demonstrates an assisted behavioral control opportunity [8]. Finches also apply Psidium galapageium leaves to feathers; extracts repel adult flies and inhibit larvae experimentally, while protection and fitness benefits in wild birds remain unproven [9]. Sensation, motor control, learning, and circadian behavior can therefore support defenses, but generic synaptic or neural-development terms do not by themselves identify an antiparasite mechanism.

## Application of the scale

Scores distinguish directly documented responses (5), core components or strong predictions (4), supporting/downstream processes (3), weak indirect connections (2), and no specific connection (1). Regulation in either direction can be relevant to balanced defense or tolerance; a score is not a prediction that increased activity is beneficial. General housekeeping, reproduction, embryonic patterning, and unrelated pathogen-specific processes were not promoted merely because they are necessary for life. No target score distribution was imposed.

# A priori physiological relevance to Philornis downsi

## Evidence and mechanisms

This assessment uses published ecological, behavioral, and physiological evidence plus general biological knowledge. No genomic, transcriptomic, gene-expression, or epigenetic study results were used. Records of those kinds encountered in searches were excluded. Scores concern potential relevance, not demonstrated selection, expression, benefit, or the direction of a response.

- **Blood feeding and tissue injury.** Early larvae occupy the nares and sometimes other cavities, damaging tissue; later larvae largely inhabit nest material and feed externally, mainly at night. Adults are nonparasitic. Larval feeding causes blood loss, anemia, reduced growth, and mortality; nasal injury can persist. These effects support blood replacement, iron handling, oxygen delivery, hemostasis, epithelial integrity, and tissue repair as candidate responses [1–3]. Increased immature erythrocytes associated with parasitism provide direct evidence for compensatory erythropoiesis [2]. Hypoxia responses, coagulation, angiogenesis, and specific repair pathways are strong physiological predictions, not claims that every pathway has been measured in this system.
- **Immune resistance.** Experimental infestation increased fly-binding antibodies in adult female medium ground finches, with higher maternal antibody levels tending to accompany fewer parasites. Males and nestlings did not show the same induced response, and maternal immunity did not rescue nestling survival [4]. Thus adaptive immunity is directly relevant, while B-cell activation, antigen handling, and immune signaling are components. Skin inflammation, itching, and ingestion of defensive host molecules by larvae are proposed mechanisms in that literature, rather than proven universal defenses [4]. Innate inflammatory pathways merit strong predictions; bacterial defenses have a less direct wound-associated link. Virus-specific processes have no equivalent direct connection.
- **Tolerance and energy compensation.** Mockingbirds can tolerate burdens that harm finches. Increased nestling begging and parental provisioning were associated with compensation; later work showed that tolerance varies with environmental conditions and food provisioning [5,6]. Energy and nutrient regulation, metabolic support, and maintenance of growth are therefore relevant, but generic metabolism is less specific than the documented compensatory behavior. Finches do not consistently increase provisioning [5].
- **Behavioral resistance and avoidance.** Observations document preening, eating larvae, and nestlings climbing on nest mates [7]. Sensation, movement, and learning could support such responses, but this does not make every neuronal or synaptic-development term specifically associated with the parasite. Guava-leaf anointing has been observed, and leaf extracts repel adult flies in assays; its protective effectiveness in wild nests remains a hypothesis [8]. Nest incorporation of supplied insecticide-treated cotton demonstrates a management-assisted behavioral route [9], not evidence for endogenous insecticide production.
- **Limits on stress extrapolation.** An experiment found no parasite-treatment effect on baseline or acute stress-induced corticosterone in breeding female finches [10]. General stress and steroid pathways therefore were not treated as established fly-specific responses.

## Scoring interpretation

Each supplied term was judged independently. Directly documented response categories received 5; core components or strong predictions received 4; supporting processes received 3; weak or nonspecific connections received 2; unrelated or highly remote processes received 1. Positive and negative regulation can both be relevant: the score does not predict the favorable direction. Broad transcription, translation, chromosome, and organelle functions were not upgraded merely because all defenses require them. Developmental processes were distinguished from repair of mature tissue. Platelet terms were interpreted in relation to the homologous avian thrombocyte/hemostatic mechanism.

The input labels GO:0160240 with its identifier alone. Its official name, RNA polymerase II transcription initiation surveillance, was verified in AmiGO and assessed without changing the input identifier [11].

# Independent a priori review: Philornis downsi host responses

## Scope and scoring approach

Scores use published natural-history, experimental parasitology, physiological, and behavioral evidence plus general biological knowledge. No genomic, transcriptomic, gene-expression, or epigenomic results from Galápagos birds were used. Only this run's supplied input file was read on the machine. Every term was considered individually, without targeting a score distribution. Scores indicate potential relevance if a term were subsequently found under selection; they do not establish adaptation, direction of an effect, or successful protection.

The supplied scale was applied conservatively: 5 for a term directly encompassing a documented response; 4 for a core component or strong mechanistic prediction; 3 for plausible support or a downstream process; 2 for a weak or indirect connection; 1 for no specific connection. Positive and negative regulation can both be relevant because limitation of damage and inappropriate inflammation can matter alongside activation of defense. Generic housekeeping was not treated as specifically relevant merely because every defense requires functioning cells.

## Mechanisms and evidence

- **Blood loss, tissue injury, and impaired growth.** Early larvae occupy the nares and other tissues; later larvae commonly shelter in nest material and feed externally, especially at night. Tissue destruction, wounds, anemia, reduced hemoglobin, poor growth, and nestling mortality are central effects. These support strong predictions for blood-cell replacement and iron handling, hemostasis, hypoxia responses, epithelial protection, extracellular-matrix repair, and restoration of vascular integrity. The latter molecular mechanisms are predictions, not claims that each has been demonstrated in these birds. Sources: Fessl, Sinclair & Kleindorfer (2006), *The life-cycle of Philornis downsi ... and its impacts on nestling survival*, https://doi.org/10.1017/S0031182006001089 ; Dudaniec, Kleindorfer & Fessl (2006), *Effects ... on haemoglobin level and nestling survival*, https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1442-9993.2006.01553.x ; Koop et al. (2011), experimental fitness consequences, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3092749/ .

- **Immune resistance is possible but incompletely effective.** Experimental fly removal demonstrated higher fly-binding antibody levels in parasitized adult female medium ground finches. The association between antibody level and lower parasite abundance was only marginal, and the response did not rescue nesting success. Males and very young nestlings did not show the same induced antibody response. Antibody production and its B-cell and T-cell support therefore have a strong basis; inflammatory recruitment, complement, phagocytosis, and immune regulation are plausible associated mechanisms rather than demonstrated molecular outcomes. The study discusses inflammation and itching as potential links between antibodies and defense against biting insects. Source: Koop et al. (2013), *Experimental demonstration of a parasite-induced immune response in wild birds*, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3930052/ .

- **Tolerance depends on nutrition and compensation.** Parasitized mockingbird nestlings increased begging, with greater parental provisioning, whereas medium ground finches did not show comparable compensation. Mockingbird tolerance subsequently proved poorer in low-rainfall years, consistent with resource limitation. Neither species showed a significant induced antibody response in the comparative experiment, illustrating context dependence. Nutrient acquisition and energy homeostasis have strong relevance; glucose, lipid and amino-acid metabolism, mitochondrial function, growth, and thermoregulation are plausible supporting processes. Sources: Knutie et al. (2016), *Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate introduced parasites that affect Darwin's finches*, https://doi.org/10.1890/15-0119.1 ; McNew et al. (2019), *Annual environmental variation influences host tolerance to parasites*, https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2019.0049 .

- **Behavioral resistance and avoidance.** Reported responses include preening, scratching, agitation, changing position, and reduced maternal brooding with more standing. Galápagos flycatcher nestlings have been observed dislodging larvae through flapping and preening, but behavioral protection is neither uniform nor necessarily sufficient. Sensation, pain detection, motor control, learning, and activity timing are plausible contributors; this does not make every neural-development term directly implicated. Sources: Koop et al. (2013), above; *Behavior of the Avian Parasite Philornis downsi ... in and Near Host Nests in the Galapagos Islands* (2022), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8813692/ .

- **Chemical protection through behavior.** Finches apply Psidium galapageium leaves to feathers; experiments found repellent effects on adult flies and inhibition of larval growth. A fitness benefit of the natural behavior remained unproven. Birds can also incorporate experimentally supplied insecticide-treated cotton into nests. These support behavioral flexibility, but do not demonstrate endogenous insecticide production or host detoxification as a natural fly response. Sources: Cimadom et al. (2016), https://doi.org/10.1038/srep34559 ; Knutie et al. (2014), *Darwin's finches combat introduced nest parasites with fumigated cotton*, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24801182/ .

## Important limits

A stress-endocrine mechanism should not be presumed: an experimental study found no parasite-treatment effect on adult female corticosterone responses, condition, or hematocrit. General stress and metabolic responses remain plausible in nestlings, but this is not evidence of an established maternal endocrine defense. Source: Knutie et al. (2013), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23892015/ .

Wounds make secondary antibacterial defenses plausible; they do not establish that P. downsi is an intracellular pathogen or that virus-specific pathways defend against it. Similarly, blood loss motivates hematopoietic and circulatory predictions without proving every such pathway is induced. Avian thrombocytes differ from mammalian platelets, so platelet-named terms received a weaker score than general coagulation. Embryonic patterning, germ-cell processes, and unrelated specialized development generally lack a specific connection. Broad transcriptional machinery was not elevated by reference to any expression study.

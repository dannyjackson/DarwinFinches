# Literature basis and scoring approach

The ratings concern potential host responses to *Philornis downsi*, not evidence that any particular GO process is under selection. I used published ecological, physiological, behavioral, and immunological evidence plus general biological knowledge. No genomic, transcriptomic, or gene-expression results were used. A retrieved study containing gene-expression analyses was excluded in full.

## Mechanisms and evidence

- **Blood loss and physiological tolerance.** First and early second instars invade nestling nares and other tissues; older larvae shelter in nest material and feed externally on blood, often at night. Tissue destruction and anemia contribute to nestling mortality [1, 7]. In small ground finches, greater parasite intensity correlated with lower hemoglobin and more immature erythrocytes [2]. Thus erythrocyte production directly matches an observed host response. Hematopoietic precursors, iron handling, oxygen delivery, and responses to hypoxia are strong physiological predictions; particular molecular implementations are not demonstrated by these studies.
- **Injury, barriers, and repair.** Larvae cause skin lesions and damage to nasal tissues; experimental parasite reduction improves reproductive success and can improve nestling size [1, 3]. Hemostasis, epithelial restoration, extracellular matrix repair, vascular repair, and inflammatory control therefore have strong a priori relevance. These are predictions from documented injury, not claims that the cited studies measured every repair pathway. Antibacterial defenses are plausible secondary protection at breached barriers, but bacterial infection is not assumed to be the principal mechanism of fly damage.
- **Immune resistance.** Experimental manipulation showed increased fly-binding antibodies in adult female medium ground finches. The association between higher antibodies and fewer parasites was suggestive, not conclusive; the maternal response did not save the parasitized broods. Males and young nestlings lacked the corresponding detectable response in that experiment [4]. This directly supports adaptive/humoral immune responses and strongly supports their cellular machinery. Innate inflammation, complement, antigen handling, and cytokine regulation are defensible predictions, without asserting experimentally proven efficacy against this fly. Specific antiviral mechanisms receive weaker scores than general antiparasite immunity.
- **Energetic and behavioral tolerance.** Galápagos mockingbirds maintained reproductive performance despite parasitism. Parasitized nestlings begged more, with greater begging associated with increased parental provisioning, consistent with energetic compensation. Medium ground finches did not show the same compensation in that study; neither species showed a significant induced antibody response there [5]. Food acquisition, energy homeostasis, nutrient use, and maintenance of growth are therefore relevant, but the evidence does not establish every metabolic or hormonal pathway.
- **Behavioral resistance and avoidance.** Observations include preening, scratching, agitation, occasional larval removal, nest sanitation, and altered maternal brooding or standing behavior; effectiveness and occurrence depend on age and species [4, 7]. Nest building can also be harnessed for resistance: birds incorporate supplied insecticide-treated cotton, reducing infestation [6]. This is an assisted intervention, not evidence that birds naturally synthesize insecticides. Sensory, learning, and motor processes can support these behaviors, but generic neural development is a comparatively indirect connection.

## Application of the scale

Scores follow the requested five-level scale without a target distribution. A 5 identifies a process directly encompassing an observed host mechanism; 4 identifies a core component or strong physiological prediction; 3 indicates a plausible supporting process; 2 indicates an indirect or speculative link; and 1 indicates no specific link. Broad housekeeping processes were not upgraded merely because all responses require living cells. Reproduction, development, signaling, and metabolism were assessed at the specificity of each supplied term. Positive and negative regulation can both be relevant; regulatory direction alone does not determine relevance. Platelet-related terms were interpreted cautiously in the context of avian thrombocyte-mediated hemostasis.

The input gives GO:0160240 without a descriptive name. Its authoritative GO entry identifies RNA polymerase II transcription initiation surveillance; the supplied ID was preserved and scored as a general transcription-surveillance process [8].

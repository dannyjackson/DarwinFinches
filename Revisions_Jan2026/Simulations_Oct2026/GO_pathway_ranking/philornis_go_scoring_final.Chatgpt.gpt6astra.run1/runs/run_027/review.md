# Independent a priori review: Philornis downsi host responses

This assessment uses published ecological, behavioral, parasitological, and physiological evidence, together with general biological knowledge. No genomic, transcriptomic, or gene-expression results from Galápagos birds were used.

## Mechanisms informing the scores

**Blood and tissue injury.** Adult flies are free-living; their larvae parasitize birds. Early larvae occupy the nares and other tissues, while later larvae commonly shelter in nest material and feed on nestlings at night. Feeding produces wounds, blood loss, reduced hemoglobin, impaired growth, and substantial nestling mortality; nasal damage can persist in survivors [1, 2, 3]. Consequently, response to wounding is directly relevant. Hemostasis, erythrocyte production, iron handling, oxygen delivery and hypoxia responses, epithelial repair, extracellular-matrix remodeling, and angiogenesis are strong physiological predictions. These predictions are not claims that each molecular pathway has already been measured in this system.

**Immune resistance and its limits.** Experimental parasite removal demonstrated higher parasite-binding antibody levels in female medium ground finches attending parasitized nests. The inverse association between maternal antibody levels and parasite abundance was suggestive rather than definitive; the response did not rescue reproductive success. Males and nestlings did not show the same induced response in that experiment [4]. Adaptive immunity therefore has direct support, with B-cell activation/differentiation and supporting immune signaling strongly relevant. Innate inflammation, complement, leukocyte recruitment, phagocytosis, and inflammatory restraint are reasonable predictions from tissue injury and ectoparasitism, not uniformly demonstrated protective mechanisms. Antibacterial defenses have a plausible secondary wound-related role; virus-specific processes have no specific connection established by this review.

**Tolerance through nutrition and growth.** Parasitized Galápagos mockingbird nestlings increased begging, and parents increased provisioning; comparable parasite densities caused greater fitness costs in medium ground finches [2]. A multiyear manipulation showed that mockingbird tolerance deteriorated under low-rainfall conditions, consistent with resource limitation constraining compensatory provisioning [3]. Nutrient sensing, energy balance, nutrient utilization, growth maintenance, and recovery from blood loss are thus relevant. Generic metabolic machinery was scored below processes that more specifically express these functions.

**Behavioral resistance.** Nest observations documented self-preening, eating larvae, avoidance by standing on other chicks, and maternal probing of nares and nest material [5]. These support sensory detection, pain/irritation responses, motor behavior, and behavioral flexibility as potential host mechanisms. Nocturnal feeding also provides a plausible connection to activity timing. Neural development or a specific synaptic pathway is a much less direct connection than the behavior itself. Finch leaf-rubbing with Psidium galapageium is documented, and extracts repelled adult flies and inhibited larval growth experimentally; protective efficacy of the natural behavior in nests remains a hypothesis [6].

**Stress is not automatically a demonstrated mechanism.** A manipulation found no parasite-treatment difference in baseline or stress-induced corticosterone, body condition, or hematocrit of breeding female medium ground finches [7]. This limits strong claims about maternal endocrine stress. Cellular stress protection, redox balance, and autophagy remain plausible supporting processes, rather than direct demonstrations.

## Scoring interpretation

Each supplied term was assessed individually. Score 5 denotes direct correspondence to documented host responses; 4 denotes a core component or strong mechanistic prediction; 3 denotes a plausible supporting/downstream process; 2 denotes a weak or nonspecific connection; and 1 denotes no specific connection. Scores describe potential relevance, not proof of adaptation, protection, or selection. Broad housekeeping functions, unrelated embryonic patterning, and reproductive processes were not elevated merely because healthy birds require them. Both positive and negative regulation can be relevant where they control repair or immunopathology.

The input label for GO:0160240 repeated the identifier. AmiGO identifies it as “RNA polymerase II transcription initiation surveillance”; it was judged on that definition and retained under its original GO ID [8].

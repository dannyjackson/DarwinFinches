# Literature basis for the independent GO ratings

The ratings use published ecological, behavioral, parasitological and physiological evidence plus general biological knowledge. No host genomic, transcriptomic, gene-expression or selection results were used. Each of the 1,008 supplied terms was judged separately; no score distribution was targeted.

## Mechanisms and evidence

- **Blood and tissue loss.** Nonparasitic adult flies deposit eggs at bird nests. Early larvae invade nestling nares and other tissues; later larvae occupy nest material and feed externally, often at night. Tissue injury, enlarged or deformed nares, anemia, impaired growth and mortality are established consequences. This makes injury responses and blood replacement particularly relevant. Hemostasis, epithelial repair, extracellular matrix remodeling, vascular repair, iron handling and hypoxia compensation are strong physiological predictions, although their individual molecular pathways have generally not been demonstrated in these hosts. Source: Fessl, Sinclair and Kleindorfer (2006), *The life-cycle of Philornis downsi ... and its impacts on nestling survival*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16899139/

- **Erythropoietic compensation.** Reduced hemoglobin and increased reticulocytes in parasitized small ground finch nestlings support a compensatory red-cell response, even when compensation is insufficient to prevent mortality. The reticulocyte finding is reported in Dudaniec and Kleindorfer's published review, summarizing Dudaniec, Kleindorfer and Fessl (2006). Review: https://doi.org/10.1071/MU04040 ; original study metadata: https://researchnow.flinders.edu.au/en/publications/effects-of-the-introduced-ectoparasite-philornis-downsi-on-haemog/

- **Immune resistance, with limits.** Parasite-binding antibodies have been detected in medium ground finches. Experimental parasite removal showed higher antibody levels in exposed mothers; higher maternal levels tended to accompany fewer parasites. This did not rescue offspring survival, and comparable responses were absent in males and nestlings in that experiment. Thus adaptive immunity is directly relevant; B/T-cell processes and immune regulation are core predicted components. Inflammation, innate recognition, phagocytosis and complement are plausible resistance or repair mechanisms, not established effective defenses against this fly in every bird. Sources: Huber et al. (2010), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2799526/ ; Koop et al. (2013), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3930052/

- **Energetic tolerance and growth.** Parasitized mockingbird nestlings increased begging, associated with greater parental provisioning and compensation for parasite damage; medium ground finches did not show equivalent compensation in the comparison. Mockingbird tolerance subsequently proved sensitive to rainfall and provisioning opportunities, declining under dry conditions. Energy homeostasis, nutrient use and responses to food limitation are therefore relevant, while particular metabolic enzymes and growth-signaling cascades remain supporting predictions. Sources: Knutie et al. (2016), *Galapagos mockingbirds tolerate introduced parasites that affect Darwin's finches*, https://doi.org/10.1890/15-0119.1 ; McNew et al. (2019), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6408884/

- **Behavioral resistance and avoidance.** Nest videos recorded nestling self-preening, eating larvae and standing on nestmates at high infestation; these behaviors should not be assumed sufficient to protect survival. Finches also rub Psidium galapageium leaves on their feathers, and experiments support repellent properties, but the resulting protection in wild birds remains uncertain. Sensory detection, nociception, behavioral control and activity timing are relevant; generic neural development is much less specific. Sources: O'Connor, Robertson and Kleindorfer (2010), https://doi.org/10.1017/S0030605310000086 ; Cimadom et al. (2016), https://www.nature.com/articles/srep34559

- **Boundaries on inference.** An experiment found no effect of infestation on breeding female corticosterone or condition, so endocrine stress was not treated as a universally demonstrated mechanism: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4383185/ . Insecticide-treated nesting materials can reduce infestation through assisted self-fumigation, but this does not establish natural host xenobiotic detoxification as a central antiparasite defense: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7618593/

## Application of the scale

A score of 5 denotes a term directly encompassing a documented response mechanism; 4 denotes a core component or strong physiological prediction; 3 denotes plausible supporting or downstream physiology; 2 denotes an indirect or speculative relationship; and 1 denotes no specific relationship. Positive and negative regulatory terms were assessed as possible modulation of the process, not as claims about which direction selection would favor. Generic transcription, trafficking, cell-cycle and developmental terms were not promoted merely because all host responses require living cells. Antibacterial processes received consideration for secondary wound-associated challenges; virus-specific processes were not conflated with antiparasite immunity. Bird thrombocyte physiology informed the interpretation of platelet-related terms.

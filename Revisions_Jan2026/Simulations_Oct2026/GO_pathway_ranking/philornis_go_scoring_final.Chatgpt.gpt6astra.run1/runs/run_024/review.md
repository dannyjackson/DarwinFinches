# A priori assessment of host-response mechanisms

This independent rating used published ecological, physiological, immunological, and behavioral studies of Philornis downsi, together with general biological knowledge. No transcriptomic, gene-expression, or genomic results from Galápagos birds were used. Scores assess potential physiological relevance, not evidence that a particular GO process has changed genetically, and do not imply that a response is effective or beneficial.

## Host injury and physiological compensation

Early larvae occupy nestling nares and other tissues; later larvae generally shelter in the nest and feed externally on blood, particularly at night. Tissue damage, reduced hemoglobin, substantial blood loss, impaired growth, and nestling mortality are established effects. These observations provide strong a priori links to hemostasis, erythrocyte replacement and iron handling, oxygen supply, skin and epithelial repair, extracellular-matrix remodeling, and vascular repair. The detailed molecular pathways of these responses were usually not measured: such terms were normally scored as strong predictions (4), rather than documented mechanisms (5). Generic oxidative-stress, metabolic, and cell-survival processes were treated as supporting possibilities. Development of unrelated embryonic organs was not equated with post-injury repair.

Key sources:
- Fessl, Sinclair & Kleindorfer (2006), “The life-cycle of Philornis downsi ... and its impacts on nestling survival.” https://doi.org/10.1017/S0031182006001089
- Dudaniec, Kleindorfer & Fessl (2006), “Effects of the introduced ectoparasite Philornis downsi on haemoglobin level and nestling survival in Darwin's Small Ground Finch.” https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1442-9993.2006.01553.x

## Immune resistance and its limits

Experimental parasite removal showed that exposed female medium ground finches produced higher levels of fly-binding antibodies. Higher maternal antibody levels were associated with fewer parasites, but this correlation does not establish the causal efficacy of antibodies; the response did not rescue nestling survival in that experiment. Comparable induced responses were not detected in males or young nestlings. Accordingly, adaptive immunity is directly supported, while B- and T-cell functions, complement, inflammatory signaling, and antigen-handling mechanisms are core components or predictions. Inflammation and itching as means of impeding feeding or provoking grooming have been proposed. Antibacterial defenses are plausible secondary responses to wounds, not demonstrated evidence that the fly transmits bacteria. Virus-specific processes were not promoted merely because the parasite stimulates immunity.

Key source:
- Koop et al. (2013), “Experimental demonstration of a parasite-induced immune response in wild birds: Darwin's finches and introduced nest flies.” https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3930052/

## Behavioral resistance and nutritional tolerance

Nest-camera observations document grooming, probing of nestling nares and nest material, occasional larval consumption, and avoidance movements. These motivate sensory, motor, pain/irritation, and behavioral supporting processes, but do not specifically establish broad neural-development pathways. Applying guayabillo leaves to feathers is observed, and experimental repellency supports a possible self-medication function; reduced natural fly infestation or increased fitness from this behavior remains unproven.

Parasitized Galápagos mockingbird nestlings increased begging, with greater parental provisioning consistent with nutritional compensation. Equivalent compensation was absent in the medium ground finches studied. Mockingbird tolerance also varied among years and weakened in dry conditions. Thus, food acquisition, energy balance, nutrient sensing, and growth support are relevant, with species and resource dependence. Adult-female corticosterone, condition, and hematocrit did not differ significantly between treatments in a separate experiment; endocrine stress mechanisms remain plausible rather than universally demonstrated.

## Scoring conventions

Every term was judged separately without a target score distribution. Positive and negative regulation can both matter for response magnitude, resolution, and damage limitation. Broad housekeeping terms received little weight without a specific physiological bridge. Platelet-related terms were assessed for their corresponding avian thrombocyte/hemostatic relevance, not as evidence that birds possess mammalian platelets.

An official ontology lookup identifies it as RNA polymerase II transcription initiation surveillance; it received 1 because no specific link was identified.

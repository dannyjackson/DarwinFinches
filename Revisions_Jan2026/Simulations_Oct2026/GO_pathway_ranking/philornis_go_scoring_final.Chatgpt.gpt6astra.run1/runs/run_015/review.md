# A priori physiological relevance to Philornis downsi

## Evidence base and mechanisms

This assessment used published ecological, physiological, immunological, and behavioral evidence, together with general biological knowledge. No genomic, transcriptomic, or gene-expression findings from Galápagos birds were used. Scores address potential association with a host response, not whether a process is necessarily protective or under selection.

**Blood loss and compensation.** Early larvae occupy the nares and other tissues; older larvae live in nest material and feed externally on nestling blood. Fessl, Sinclair and Kleindorfer documented substantial blood loss and mortality [1]. Dudaniec, Kleindorfer and Fessl found lower hemoglobin with greater parasitism and increased immature erythrocytes associated with parasite intensity [2]. Thus erythrocyte production and hematopoiesis have direct physiological support. Iron handling, oxygen-deficit responses, hemostasis and circulatory compensation are strong predictions; their specific molecular pathways were not demonstrated in these studies.

**Tissue injury and repair.** Larval feeding produces wounds and damages nasal tissue. Naris deformation can persist into adulthood and affect foraging [1,3]. Repair of skin and epithelium, restoration of barriers and extracellular matrix, angiogenesis, and recruitment of repair cells are therefore strong a priori mechanisms. Specific growth-factor pathways are inferred components of repair, rather than measured Philornis responses. Oxidative-stress control, apoptosis regulation and cellular maintenance are plausible supporting processes, with less direct evidence.

**Immune resistance.** Medium ground finches can produce fly-binding antibodies. An experimental nest-fumigation study found elevated antibody responses in mothers exposed to flies, but not in fathers or young nestlings; the maternal response did not rescue fledging success [4]. B-cell activation, adaptive immunity and its supporting signaling are consequently relevant. Inflammation, innate recognition, complement and T-cell help are plausible defenses or components, not all established as effective resistance in this system. Antibody-mediated inflammation, itching and impairment of blood feeding were proposed mechanisms. The absence of a detected response in some age classes or experiments does not establish that immunity is irrelevant. Conversely, a fly association does not justify treating virus-specific processes as direct defenses.

**Tolerance and nutrition.** Experimental work showed Galápagos mockingbirds tolerated parasitism better than sympatric medium ground finches. Increased nestling begging and parental provisioning were associated with compensation, without a significant antibody response in either species in that comparison [5]. A multiyear study showed that mockingbird tolerance varied with environmental conditions, with provisioning and food availability implicated [6]. Nutrient sensing, energy balance, metabolic resource allocation and maintenance of growth can therefore support tolerance. This does not elevate every metabolic or developmental term equally.

**Behavioral defense.** Published observations include preening or removal of larvae, nest sanitation and altered brooding or posture, although their effectiveness and occurrence vary [4,6,7]. Finches have also been observed applying Psidium galapageium leaves to feathers; experiments showed repellent effects on adult flies and inhibition of larval growth, but protection of wild birds was not established [8]. Sensory detection, pain/itch responses, motor control, learning and behavioral flexibility are potential supporting processes. Generic synapse or embryonic brain-development terms remain indirect. Damage to song or foraging is an effect of injury, not proof of an adaptive neural defense.

## Scoring interpretation

Each of the 1,008 supplied rows was judged individually. Score 5 was reserved for terms directly encompassing documented responses, such as erythrocyte differentiation, immune response and response to wounding. Score 4 denotes core components or strong predictions, including repair, clotting and relevant immune signaling. Score 3 denotes plausible supporting physiology. Generic housekeeping, distant developmental processes and narrowly unrelated pathogen responses usually received 1 or 2 according to their specificity. Positive and negative regulation were assessed as regulation of a relevant process, without assuming that either direction is beneficial. Platelet terms were considered for their functional relevance to avian thrombocyte-mediated hemostasis, not as evidence that birds have mammalian platelets.

**Input limitation:** GO:0160240 was supplied without a term name (the name field repeats the identifier). Public ontology lookup attempts did not establish its identity. It is retained exactly and assigned 1 because no specific mechanism could be established from the supplied information; this is an uncertainty-limited rating, not evidence that an identified process is biologically unrelated.

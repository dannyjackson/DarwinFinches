# Independent a priori review: Philornis downsi host responses

The scores use published physiological and behavioral observations and general biological knowledge. No genomic, transcriptomic, gene-expression, or selection results from Galápagos birds informed the ratings. Each supplied term was judged independently, without a target score distribution.

## Mechanisms and evidence

- **Blood loss and replacement.** First and early second instar larvae invade nestling nares and other tissues; later larvae reside in nest material and feed externally, especially at night. Tissue damage and blood loss cause anemia, impaired growth, and mortality. In small ground finches, heavier infestation was associated with lower hemoglobin and more immature erythrocytes, supporting compensatory blood-cell production. Erythropoiesis is therefore directly relevant; iron handling, hemostasis, oxygen delivery, and hypoxia responses are strong physiological predictions. Sources: Fessl, Sinclair and Kleindorfer (2006), [life cycle and effects](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16899139/); Dudaniec, Kleindorfer and Fessl (2006), [hemoglobin and nestling survival](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1442-9993.2006.01553.x).

- **Injury, barriers, and repair.** Consumption of blood and soft tissue, including developing nasal tissue, makes epithelial integrity, wound closure, extracellular-matrix remodeling, vascular repair, and inflammatory regulation strong candidates. Persistent naris deformation can alter adult song and pairing outcomes. These observations support tissue repair, but do not demonstrate every individual signaling pathway. Generic embryonic patterning is less specifically connected than repair of injured epithelia. Source: Kleindorfer et al. (2019), [parasite effects on phenotype and song](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6571467/).

- **Immune resistance and its limits.** Experimental fly removal showed higher fly-binding antibody levels in exposed adult female medium ground finches. Higher maternal antibody levels tended to accompany fewer parasites, but did not rescue nestling survival. Males and young nestlings lacked the same detectable response. Adaptive immunity and the cellular machinery supporting antibody responses are consequently relevant, without assuming universal protection. Innate recognition, phagocytosis, complement, cytokine regulation, and inflammatory restraint are predictions from parasitism and injury, not claims that these specific pathways were measured. Virus-specific processes receive little weight merely because they are immune-related. Source: Koop et al. (2013), [experimental immune response](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3930052/).

- **Tolerance through nutrition and growth.** Parasitized Galápagos mockingbird nestlings increased begging; greater begging correlated with parental provisioning, apparently compensating for damage despite similar parasite densities to finches. Medium ground finches did not show the same compensation in that experiment. A later green warbler-finch study found lower maternal provisioning and lower nestling mass with high infestation, emphasizing species and context dependence. Energy balance, nutrient sensing, substrate use, and growth are plausible tolerance components; broad metabolic housekeeping is less specific. Sources: Knutie et al. (2016), [mockingbird tolerance](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27220210/); Pike et al. (2023), [provisioning and growth](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10188583/).

- **Behavioral resistance and avoidance.** Observations include occasional removal or consumption of larvae, probing nestling nares and nest material, preening, agitation, and repositioning. These behaviors are often insufficient, particularly in very young nestlings with limited motor abilities. Reduced maternal brooding may also impair nestling thermoregulation. Finch anointing with Psidium leaves is a proposed defense: extracts repel adult flies and inhibit larval growth, but this does not establish a fitness benefit of natural anointing. Sensation, motor control, learning, and daily timing can support defense, while generic neuronal development remains indirect. Sources: McNew and Clayton (2018), [review of Philornis biology and host defenses](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-ento-020117-043103); Cimadom et al. (2016), [natural-repellent experiments](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep34559). Birds' incorporation of supplied insecticide-treated nesting material also demonstrates a behavioral route for management, rather than an endogenous detoxification mechanism: [nest-treatment experiments](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7618593/).

- **Stress is not a universal endocrine finding.** A parasite-removal experiment found no treatment difference in baseline or acute stress-induced corticosterone, female condition, or hematocrit. This limits strong claims about adult endocrine stress. Oxidative stress, autophagy, and protein maintenance remain plausible downstream processes rather than directly demonstrated fly-specific defenses. Source: [experimental corticosterone study](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23892015/).

## Scoring interpretation

Scores distinguish direct mechanisms (5), their core components or strong predictions (4), plausible support (3), weak or nonspecific links (2), and no specific link (1). Both positive and negative regulation can matter: controlling an excessive response can aid tolerance. Platelet-labeled terms were assessed for the corresponding conserved hemostatic functions of avian thrombocytes, without asserting that birds have mammalian platelets. Generic transcription, RNA processing, meiosis, and anatomically unrelated embryogenesis were not elevated solely because all responses require cellular machinery.

The input supplied GO:0160240 without a descriptive name. Its official [AmiGO entry](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO%3A0160240) identifies RNA polymerase II transcription initiation surveillance; it was rated accordingly, retaining the supplied identifier unchanged.

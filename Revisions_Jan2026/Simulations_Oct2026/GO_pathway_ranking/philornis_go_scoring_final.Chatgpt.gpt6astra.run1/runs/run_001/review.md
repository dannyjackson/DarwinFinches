# A priori physiological relevance review

Scores reflect potential association with a host response to *Philornis downsi*, not evidence that a pathway is under selection or that its activation is beneficial. I used published ecological, behavioral, immunological and physiological evidence plus general biology. I did not use host genomic, transcriptomic or gene-expression results, other raters' work, or prior conversations. Every input term was judged individually; no target score distribution was imposed.

## Mechanisms and evidence

- **Blood removal and compensation.** Early larvae occupy the nares and other tissues; later larvae shelter in nest material and feed externally on nestlings. Blood loss and tissue damage contribute to severe nestling mortality. Small ground finch nestlings with more larvae had lower hemoglobin and more immature erythrocytes, providing direct evidence for a hematological response. This anchors high relevance for erythrocyte production and strong predictions for iron management and responses to reduced oxygen delivery. Sources: Fessl, Sinclair & Kleindorfer (2006), [life cycle and host damage](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16899139/); Dudaniec, Kleindorfer & Fessl (2006), [hemoglobin and immature erythrocytes](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1442-9993.2006.01553.x), with the [authors' institutional abstract](https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/effects-of-the-introduced-ectoparasite-philornis-downsi-on-haemog/).

- **Tissue injury, barriers and repair.** Larval feeding damages skin and nasal tissues and can leave lasting deformities. Hemostasis, epithelial repair, keratinocyte function, extracellular matrix repair and wound-associated vascular responses are therefore strong physiological predictions. Specific repair signaling pathways were not demonstrated by these studies and were scored as predictions or supporting processes rather than assumed experimental findings. Fessl et al. (2006), above, and Kleindorfer et al. (2019), [effects on phenotype and song](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6571467/), support the injury context. Effects on song do not establish broad neural-developmental pathways as parasite defenses.

- **Immune resistance, with important limits.** Manipulating parasites increased fly-binding antibodies in adult female medium ground finches. The inverse relationship between maternal antibodies and parasite abundance was suggestive, not conclusive, and the response did not rescue fledging success. Males and nestlings did not show the same induced response. Antibody production and adaptive immunity are documented mechanisms; B-cell activation and differentiation are core components. Inflammatory inhibition of blood feeding, itching-linked defenses and ingested defensive molecules were proposed mechanisms, supporting innate, cytokine and inflammatory processes without treating every immune pathway as equally relevant. Source: Koop et al. (2013), [experimental immune response](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3930052/).

- **Nutritional and behavioral tolerance.** Parasitized Galápagos mockingbird nestlings increased begging, associated with greater parental provisioning, and could retain reproductive success despite parasite burdens that harmed medium ground finches. This supports energy compensation, nutrient sensing and maintenance of growth. Such tolerance is conditional: it declined during dry, resource-limited years. The comparison did not find significant parasite-induced antibody responses in either species' nestlings, or compensatory begging, provisioning or plasma-glucose changes in finches. Sources: Knutie et al. (2016), [mockingbird–finch comparison](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27220210/); McNew et al. (2019), [environmental dependence of tolerance](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30963843/).

- **Avoidance and removal.** Nestling self-preening, eating larvae and standing on one another were observed in heavily parasitized finch nests; responses vary among studies and need not be effective enough to prevent death. These observations motivate sensory, motor and learning-related support processes, while generic synaptic or brain-development terms remain indirect. Source: O'Connor, Robertson & Kleindorfer (2010), [video observations](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0030605310000086). Birds also use supplied insecticide-treated cotton, demonstrating a nest-material route to parasite reduction, rather than endogenous chemical synthesis: Knutie et al. (2014), [self-fumigation](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24801182/).

- **Plant-assisted defense and stress hypotheses.** Finches apply *Psidium galapageium* leaves to feathers; laboratory assays showed repellent effects, but a protective fitness benefit in wild birds was not established: Cimadom et al. (2016), [natural repellent](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep34559). A separate maternal corticosterone experiment found no parasite-treatment effect on baseline or induced corticosterone, condition or hematocrit, so generalized endocrine stress is plausible rather than a demonstrated universal response: Knutie et al. (2013), [corticosterone experiment](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23892015/).

## Scoring interpretation

Documented responses receive 5; their core components and strong mechanistic predictions receive 4. Supporting metabolism, cellular injury control, movement and repair processes generally receive 3 when the connection is identifiable. Generic regulation and remote connections receive 2; processes without a specific connection receive 1. Positive and negative regulation were evaluated for relevance, not presumed benefit. Secondary bacterial defense is plausible after tissue injury, but I did not assume that the fly transmits viruses or enters host cells. Oxidative stress is a supporting physiological prediction, not a claimed measured result here. Platelet-labeled processes were considered for their functional correspondence to avian thrombocyte hemostasis.

The input supplied no name for GO:0160240. Its [official GO entry](https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO%3A0160240) identifies RNA polymerase II transcription initiation surveillance; I scored that process while preserving the original GO ID. No terms were merged or renamed in the score output.

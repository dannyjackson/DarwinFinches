# Independent a priori assessment of host responses to Philornis downsi

## Mechanisms and evidence

**Blood loss and tissue injury.** Early larvae occupy nestling nares and other tissues; older larvae occupy nest material and feed externally, predominantly at night. Blood feeding, myiasis, nasal deformation, impaired growth, and mortality are documented. Lower haemoglobin and increased immature erythrocyte counts in parasitized small ground finches support compensatory red-cell production. Thus erythropoiesis/haematopoiesis and responses to injury have especially direct relevance. Iron handling, hypoxia responses, haemostasis, epithelial integrity, extracellular-matrix repair, and vascular repair are strong physiological predictions from these injuries, rather than mechanisms all demonstrated experimentally in this system. [1,2]

**Immune resistance, with limited demonstrated efficacy.** Philornis-binding antibodies have been measured in medium ground finches. Experimental parasite manipulation increased antibody levels in mothers, but not fathers or young nestlings. Higher maternal antibody levels tended to accompany fewer parasites, yet did not prevent complete nestling mortality in that experiment. This supports adaptive/humoral immunity and its B-cell and lymphocyte machinery; it does not establish that every immune pathway protects birds. Innate recognition, inflammatory regulation, leukocyte recruitment, complement, and wound-associated microbial defense are predictions or supporting processes. Virus-specific mechanisms are generally remote from a blood-feeding fly. [3,4]

**Tolerance through nutrition and parental behavior.** Experimental comparison found that mockingbirds could maintain reproductive success despite parasitism, with increased nestling begging associated with increased parental provisioning. Finches did not show the same compensation. A multiyear mockingbird experiment found tolerance depended on environmental conditions, with provisioning and food availability implicated. Energy balance, nutrient responses, glucose availability, growth, and repair therefore have meaningful a priori links, without assuming that a particular metabolic pathway was measured or adaptive. [5,6]

**Behavioral resistance.** Video observations recorded self-preening, larval consumption, nestlings positioning themselves above nestmates, and maternal probing of nestling nares and nest material. These behaviors were limited and were not uniformly detected in subsequent experiments, particularly in very young nestlings. Finches also incorporate supplied insecticide-treated cotton into nests; this is demonstrated intervention-assisted parasite reduction. Rubbing Psidium galapageium leaves on feathers has been observed, and extracts repel adult flies and inhibit larval growth in experiments; protection of naturally self-treating birds remains a hypothesis. Sensation, motor control, learning, and cognition can support these behaviors, but generic neural development or synaptic machinery receives less weight than a specific defense mechanism. [4,7–9]

**Limits on stress-based inference.** An experimental study of breeding female finches did not find parasite-treatment effects on corticosterone, body condition, or haematocrit. Endocrine stress should therefore not be treated as a universally documented Philornis response. Redox balance, autophagy, apoptosis, and general cellular stress remain plausible supporting processes, with much weaker specificity than blood-loss compensation or adaptive immunity. [10]

## Scoring interpretation

All scores are individual qualitative judgments using the supplied scale, with no target distribution. A high score denotes physiological relevance, not proof of adaptation, protective direction, or selection. Both positive and negative regulation can be relevant to a mechanism. Broad housekeeping processes and unrelated embryonic/reproductive development were rated conservatively. Platelet-related labels were interpreted for their conserved haemostatic role, recognizing that birds use nucleated thrombocytes rather than mammalian platelets.

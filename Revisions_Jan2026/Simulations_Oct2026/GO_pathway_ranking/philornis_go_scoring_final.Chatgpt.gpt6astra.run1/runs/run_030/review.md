# Literature basis for independent GO ratings

The ratings concern potential host responses to *Philornis downsi*, not evidence that any particular GO term has been selected. I used ecological, behavioral and physiological literature and general biological knowledge. Genomic, transcriptomic, gene-expression and epigenomic findings were excluded. No other rater's files or results were consulted.

## Mechanisms and evidential limits

- **Blood and tissue feeding:** Early larvae occupy the nostrils and other tissues; older larvae live in nest material and feed externally, often at night. Injury includes blood loss, anemia, impaired growth, enlarged or damaged nares, and mortality. Adults are free-living flies, not the blood-feeding stage [1, 3]. Small ground finches with more larvae had lower hemoglobin and more immature erythrocytes [2]. Thus erythrocyte replacement is directly supported. Iron handling, oxygen-stress responses, circulation, hemostasis and nutrient supply are strong or supporting physiological predictions, depending on the specificity of the term.
- **Tissue protection and repair:** Skin and nasal damage make epithelial maintenance, keratinocyte responses, extracellular matrix remodeling, endothelial integrity and repair plausible host mechanisms. Inflammation, clearance of damaged cells, growth factors and angiogenesis can support them. These molecular pathways were generally not directly demonstrated by the ecological studies reviewed, so they were distinguished from documented responses. Secondary bacterial defense is plausible after barrier injury, but a specific bacterial or viral challenge was not assumed.
- **Immune resistance:** An experiment found elevated fly-binding antibodies in parasitized medium ground finch mothers, with stronger antibody responses associated with fewer parasites. Comparable induction was not detected in fathers or nestlings, and maternal immunity did not rescue reproduction in that experiment [4]. Adaptive immunity is therefore directly relevant; B-cell processes, helper T-cell functions and immune regulation are core components. Innate signaling, complement and inflammatory regulation are strong predictions rather than uniformly demonstrated fly-specific responses. Antiviral specificity was not equated with anti-fly defense.
- **Tolerance through compensation:** Parasitized mockingbird nestlings increased begging, associated with parental provisioning that apparently compensated for parasite damage; medium ground finches lacked that response in the comparison [5]. Mockingbird tolerance varied between years and declined with limited rainfall, linking it to resources and provisioning [6]. Energy homeostasis, metabolism, nutrient sensing and growth can therefore support tolerance, but generic metabolic machinery is less specific than the compensatory mechanism.
- **Behavioral resistance and avoidance:** Nest videos documented self-preening, eating larvae, repositioning or standing on other nestlings, and maternal probing of nares and nest material [3]. These observations justify links to sensory detection, motor function and learning, without making every neural-development or synaptic term a specific defense. Finch feather anointing with guayabillo leaves and experimentally observed repellent properties suggest chemical self-protection; a benefit against flies in natural nests was not established [7]. Reduced brooding and increased standing can also disrupt nestling thermoregulation, rather than necessarily being effective defense [9].
- **Stress is not a universal mechanism:** Experimental parasite reduction did not support the hypothesis that elevated maternal corticosterone mediated reproductive damage [8]. General stress physiology remains plausible, but specific hormonal responses were not treated as established.

## Rating conventions

Scores follow the supplied scale: 5 for a directly documented mechanism; 4 for a core component or strong mechanistic prediction; 3 for a plausible supporting process; 2 for a weak or indirect connection; 1 for no specific connection. Directional regulation was judged for relevance, not presumed benefit: either increased or decreased activity may matter. Broad housekeeping functions and unrelated embryonic, reproductive or sensory-organ processes were not promoted solely because healthy cells or birds need them. No score distribution was targeted. Avian thrombocytes provide the relevant functional context for platelet-associated hemostatic terms; these were treated as predicted components, not evidence of mammalian platelets in birds.

The input labels GO:0160240 only with its own identifier. AmiGO identifies it as RNA polymerase II transcription initiation surveillance; it received 1, while its supplied GO ID was preserved: https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO%3A0160240

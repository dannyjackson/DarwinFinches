# A priori physiological relevance to Philornis downsi

## Evidence and interpretation

The ratings assess potential host-response relevance, not evidence that a pathway has evolved, is under selection, or necessarily improves survival. Each supplied term was judged individually. No genomic, transcriptomic, or gene-expression findings from Galápagos birds were used; incidental search results from such studies were excluded. No other rater's files or results were consulted.

**Blood loss and tissue injury.** Adult flies are free-living; larvae attack nestlings and can feed on brooding females. Early larvae occupy the nares and other tissues, while later larvae reside in nest material and feed externally. Tissue destruction, nasal deformation, substantial blood loss, anemia, impaired growth, and nestling mortality are established effects. Small ground finches with heavier infestations had lower hemoglobin and more immature erythrocytes, supporting compensatory blood production. These observations directly motivate hematopoiesis and erythrocyte differentiation, with iron handling, oxygen sensing, hemostasis, epithelial repair, and vascular repair as strong physiological predictions.

**Immune resistance.** Experimental parasite removal showed elevated fly-binding antibodies in parasitized medium ground finch mothers, with an inverse association between maternal antibodies and parasite abundance. It did not demonstrate rescue of offspring survival, and comparable induced responses were absent in males and nestlings. Another comparison of finches and mockingbirds detected no significant antibody response. Thus, adaptive immunity is relevant, but effective immunity cannot be assumed universally. B-cell and T-cell functions support antibody responses. Inflammation, complement, leukocyte recruitment, phagocytosis, and tissue-barrier defenses are reasonable mechanistic predictions; specific cytokine pathways are not treated as directly demonstrated molecular responses.

**Tolerance and resources.** Galápagos mockingbird nestlings increased begging and received more parental provisioning in a parasite-removal experiment, apparently compensating for damage without reducing parasite density. Medium ground finches did not show this compensatory response. Mockingbird tolerance varied among years and weakened under drought conditions, consistent with resource limitation. Energy balance, nutrient acquisition and metabolism, growth, and parental or sensory behavior therefore have plausible roles. An experiment in adult female medium ground finches did not support a parasite-induced corticosterone response, so generic endocrine stress pathways were not assumed to be established defenses.

**Behavioral resistance and avoidance.** Occasional preening or removal of larvae has been observed, but its effectiveness is limited or unproven. Galápagos flycatcher observations support parental predation as a threat to adult flies visiting nests. Finch application of Psidium galapageium leaves to feathers is a possible avoidance mechanism: extracts repelled adult flies and inhibited larval growth in assays, but protection of birds under natural conditions was not established. Finch incorporation of supplied permethrin-treated cotton reduced nest parasites; this demonstrates a facilitated behavioral intervention, not endogenous pesticide synthesis or detoxification.

## Scoring conventions

Scores of 5 were reserved for processes directly encompassing documented host responses, including immunity and erythrocyte production. Scores of 4 identify core machinery or strong predictions from feeding injury, blood loss, and defense. Scores of 3 identify plausible supporting processes. Broad cellular housekeeping, generic signaling, unrelated pathogen-specific responses, and remote developmental links received 2 or 1 according to specificity. Directional regulation was evaluated as potential adjustment of a relevant mechanism, rather than assuming that increasing a process is always protective. Platelet-labelled terms were interpreted cautiously as hemostatic functions analogous to avian thrombocyte responses, not proof that birds possess mammalian platelets.

The supplied entry GO:0160240 repeated its ID instead of a name. The official GO record identifies it as RNA polymerase II transcription initiation surveillance; it was scored on that definition without changing the input identifier.

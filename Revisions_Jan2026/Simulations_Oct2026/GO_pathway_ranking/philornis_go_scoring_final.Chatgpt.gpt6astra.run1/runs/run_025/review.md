# Independent a priori review of host responses to Philornis downsi

## Evidence and mechanisms

**Blood loss and tissue injury.** Early larvae feed within nestling nares, consuming blood, tissue, and keratin; later larvae feed externally from the nest, often at night. Brooding females can also be exposed. Parasitism is associated with reduced hemoglobin, impaired nestling condition and growth, and mortality. Surviving birds may retain enlarged nares and altered beak function. These observations make wound responses, epithelial repair, extracellular-matrix reconstruction, hemostasis, erythrocyte replacement, iron balance, and compensation for reduced oxygen delivery strong physiological candidates. The latter cellular mechanisms are predictions from the observed injury, not claims that each has been measured in finches [1,2].

**Resistance and immune regulation.** An experimental study found increased P. downsi-binding antibodies in parasitized medium ground finch mothers, but not males or nestlings. Higher maternal antibody levels had a suggestive, marginal inverse association with parasite abundance, without rescuing fledging success. Inflammation and ingested host immune effectors were proposed as mechanisms impairing larval feeding. A separate finch–mockingbird comparison did not detect a significant parasite-induced antibody response. Thus adaptive immunity is directly relevant, while specific lymphocyte, cytokine, complement, phagocytic, and inflammatory pathways are generally core components or predictions, rather than established protective mechanisms in this system [3,4].

**Tolerance and resource allocation.** Galápagos mockingbirds can compensate through increased nestling begging and parental provisioning. Tolerance varies with environmental conditions and is reduced during resource-poor years; similar compensatory provisioning is not consistently observed in finches. Energy homeostasis and nutrient responses consequently have strong links. General carbohydrate, lipid, amino-acid, mitochondrial, and growth processes can support compensation but are less specific. Oxidant control and cell-survival pathways are plausible consequences of tissue damage, inflammation, and oxygen limitation, rather than demonstrated molecular responses here [4,5].

**Behavioral defenses.** Preening, scratching, flapping, and other nest behaviors may remove larvae or reduce contact, but observations do not establish consistently effective resistance. Their sensory, motor, learning, and circadian machinery is usually supporting or indirect. Finches apply Psidium galapageium leaves to feathers; extracts repel adult flies and inhibit larval growth experimentally, although a fitness benefit from natural application remains unconfirmed. Birds also incorporate human-provided insecticide-treated cotton into nests, reducing infestation. This demonstrates a management-assisted behavior, not an evolved host detoxification mechanism [2,6,7].

## Scoring interpretation

Each supplied term was judged individually. A score of 5 identifies a process directly encompassing a documented response; 4 identifies a core component or strong physiological prediction; 3 identifies a plausible supporting process; 2 indicates an indirect or speculative connection; and 1 indicates no specific connection. Relevance does not imply that activation is beneficial: negative regulators can limit immunopathology or allocate resources. Generic transcription, replication, transport, and cellular maintenance were not treated as specific fly defenses merely because all responses require them. Embryonic and reproductive processes received low scores unless a particular physiological connection was plausible. Platelet-related terms were considered in relation to analogous avian thrombocyte hemostasis.

The input labeled GO:0160240 only with its identifier. AmiGO identifies it as “RNA polymerase II transcription initiation surveillance”; this metadata informed its score without changing the supplied ID [8]. No Galápagos bird genomic, transcriptomic, or gene-expression study results were used. Scores were not adjusted to achieve a distribution.

# Independent literature review and scoring basis

This assessment uses published ecological, behavioral, and physiological evidence about *Philornis downsi*, together with general biological knowledge. It does not use results from genomic, transcriptomic, gene-expression, or epigenomic studies of Galápagos birds. Such results encountered incidentally during searching were excluded. No other rater's files or scores were consulted.

## Host effects and defenses

- **Blood loss and compensation.** Early larvae occupy the nares and other tissues; older larvae live in nest material and feed externally on nestling blood, often at night. Tissue injury and anemia contribute to mortality. Fessl, Sinclair & Kleindorfer (2006) describe this life cycle and pathology ([Parasitology](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0031182006001089)). Dudaniec, Kleindorfer & Fessl (2006) found lower hemoglobin and more immature erythrocytes with greater parasitism, directly supporting a compensatory blood-cell response ([Austral Ecology](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1442-9993.2006.01553.x)). Erythropoiesis and hematopoiesis therefore have direct links; iron handling, oxygen limitation responses, circulation, and hemostasis are strong physiological predictions rather than established molecular mechanisms in these birds.

- **Damage and repair.** Larval feeding damages skin and nasal tissues. Naris and beak abnormalities can persist into adulthood, although the 2009 study did not find reduced adult body condition in malformed birds (Galligan & Kleindorfer, [Biological Journal of the Linnean Society](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1095-8312.2009.01309.x)). Wound closure, epithelial restoration, extracellular-matrix remodeling, fibroblast activity, and angiogenesis are strong predictions from this injury. Specific repair signaling pathways were not established by these studies. General embryonic patterning or unrelated organ development is a much weaker link than repair of affected tissues.

- **Immune resistance.** Koop et al. (2013) experimentally detected higher fly-binding antibodies in mothers at parasitized nests; stronger maternal responses tended to accompany fewer parasites. Males and young nestlings lacked detectable induced responses, and maternal immunity did not rescue fledging in that experiment ([Ecology and Evolution](https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.651)). Knutie et al. (2016) did not detect a significant antibody response in their comparison ([Ecology](https://doi.org/10.1890/15-0119.1)). Thus adaptive immunity is documented but context dependent, not uniformly effective. B-cell and helper-T-cell functions are core candidate components. Innate recognition, inflammatory signaling, complement, phagocytosis, and inflammatory restraint are reasonable predictions from parasite exposure and injury; particular cytokines are not treated as directly demonstrated. Secondary bacterial defense is plausible after barrier damage, whereas virus-specific defense has a weaker link.

- **Tolerance through resources.** In Knutie et al. (2016), parasitized mockingbird nestlings begged more and received more food, apparently compensating for damage; medium ground finches did not show equivalent compensation. McNew et al. (2019) found that mockingbird tolerance declined in dry years, linking tolerance to resource availability ([Proceedings B](https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2019.0049)). Pike et al. (2023) found reduced female provisioning and lower nestling mass at highly infested Green Warbler-Finch nests ([Journal of Ornithology](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10336-023-02049-9)). Nutrient sensing and energy balance are strong candidates; individual metabolic, growth, and mitochondrial processes are generally supporting rather than fly-specific mechanisms.

- **Behavioral resistance and its limits.** Finches show preening, larval consumption, nest probing, and avoidance movements (O'Connor et al., 2010, [Oryx](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0030605310000086)). Flycatchers also remove larvae or catch adult flies, with observations limited to few nests (Pike et al., 2021, [Journal of Insect Behavior](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10905-021-09789-7)). These behaviors support sensory and motor contributions, but do not make all neuronal-development terms strong candidates. Leaf rubbing by four finch species and laboratory repellency/larval-growth inhibition support proposed plant-based protection; protection in wild nests remains unconfirmed (Cimadom et al., 2016, [Scientific Reports](https://doi.org/10.1038/srep34559)).

## Application of the scale

Scores represent physiological relevance, not evidence of selection or an expectation that any detected association is causal. A score of 5 is reserved for terms capturing documented responses; 4 denotes a core component or strong prediction; 3 denotes supporting processes; 2 denotes weak or indirect links; 1 denotes no specific link. Positive and negative regulators were assessed for their connection to the process, not assumed to be beneficial or harmful. Ubiquitous transcription, RNA processing, chromosome maintenance, and unrelated reproductive processes were not elevated merely because every host response requires cellular machinery. No target score distribution was imposed.

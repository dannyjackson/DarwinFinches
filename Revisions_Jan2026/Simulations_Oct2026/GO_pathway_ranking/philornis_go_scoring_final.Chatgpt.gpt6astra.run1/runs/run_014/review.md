# Independent a priori review: Philornis downsi host-response mechanisms

## Evidence used

**Larval injury and blood loss.** Adult flies are free-living; larvae parasitize birds. First and early second instars inhabit nostrils and other tissues, whereas older larvae live in nest material and feed externally. Tissue damage and blood loss cause anemia, impaired growth, and nestling mortality. This directly motivates responses to injury and blood replacement. Hemostasis, epithelial repair, extracellular-matrix remodeling, vascular repair, iron balance, and compensation for reduced oxygen delivery are strong physiological predictions; their individual molecular pathways have not thereby been demonstrated in finches. Source: Fessl, Sinclair & Kleindorfer (2006), *The life-cycle of Philornis downsi ... and its impacts on nestling survival*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16899139/

**Erythropoiesis.** Small ground finch nestlings with parasitism showed reduced hemoglobin and increased reticulocyte numbers, supporting compensatory red-cell production. Hemopoiesis and erythrocyte differentiation therefore have especially direct relevance. Source: Dudaniec, Kleindorfer & Fessl (2006), *Effects of the introduced ectoparasite Philornis downsi on haemoglobin level and nestling survival*: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1442-9993.2006.01553.x (author-posted article consulted at https://www.researchgate.net/publication/227817204_Haemoglobin_level_and_nestling_survival_in_Darwin%27s_Small_Ground_Finch_Geospiza_fuliginosa_effects_of_the_introduced_ectoparasite_Philornis_downsi).

**Immune resistance, with limited demonstrated effectiveness.** Experimental parasite removal showed higher fly-binding antibodies in parasitized medium ground finch mothers, but not fathers or young nestlings. Maternal antibody levels were inversely associated with parasite abundance; nevertheless, parasitized broods failed in that experiment. Antibody-associated inflammation and interference with blood feeding were proposed mechanisms, not demonstrated molecular pathways. Thus adaptive immunity has direct support, while lymphocyte activation, innate sensing, cytokines, phagocytosis, and inflammatory regulation are core or supporting predictions. Source: Koop et al. (2013), *Experimental demonstration of a parasite-induced immune response in wild birds*: https://darwin.biology.utah.edu/PDFs/118.pdf

**Nutritional tolerance.** A comparison found increased begging and parental provisioning in parasitized Galapagos mockingbirds, consistent with energy compensation, but not medium ground finches; neither species showed a significant antibody response in that study. Subsequent work found mockingbird tolerance varied with rainfall and provisioning, declining under resource limitation. Energy balance, nutrient mobilization, growth, and maintenance of damaged tissues are relevant; particular metabolic pathways are predictions rather than measured responses. Sources: Knutie et al. (2016), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27220210/ ; McNew et al. (2019), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30963843/

**Behavioral resistance.** Videos document preening, larval consumption, climbing over nestmates, and maternal probing of nostrils and nest material. These motivate sensory detection, motor behavior, and potentially learning, without establishing that generic neural-development pathways are fly-specific defenses. Longer female nest attendance correlates with fewer parasites, but causation is unresolved. Sources: O'Connor, Robertson & Kleindorfer (2010), https://doi.org/10.1017/S0030605310000086 ; Kleindorfer et al. (2021), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34905711/

**Proposed plant-based defense.** Finches apply Psidium galapageium leaves to feathers; laboratory extracts repel adult flies and inhibit larval growth. Subsequent field observations did not establish which parasites were targeted or a fitness benefit. Sources: Cimadom et al. (2016), https://www.nature.com/articles/srep34559 ; Tebbich et al. (2021), https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/eth.13153

## Scoring interpretation

Each term was judged independently, without a target score distribution. Scores distinguish a directly represented documented mechanism (5), core components or strong predictions (4), plausible support (3), weak indirect links (2), and no specific link (1). Regulation in either direction can be relevant; a high score does not assert that increased activity is beneficial. Generic housekeeping, embryonic patterning, gametogenesis, and virus-specific processes were not promoted simply because they could affect general fitness. Secondary bacterial defense and oxidative stress are plausible consequences of injury, not established fly-specific responses.

No genomic, transcriptomic, or gene-expression findings from Galapagos birds were used. Only the supplied input file was read locally. The unnamed input GO:0160240 was identified from the official ontology as RNA polymerase II transcription initiation surveillance and scored as housekeeping; its supplied ID was preserved: https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO%3A0160240

# A priori review: Philornis downsi and potential host responses

This assessment uses published parasite ecology, experimental parasitology, host physiology, and behavior, together with general biological knowledge. No host genomic, transcriptomic, or gene-expression results were used. Scores assess physiological relevance, not evidence of selection or the effectiveness of a defense.

## Mechanisms and evidence

- **Blood loss and tissue injury.** Larvae feed on nestling blood and tissue: early larvae occupy nasal/ear cavities, while larger larvae also feed externally, commonly at night. Damage includes wounds, enlarged/deformed nares, poor growth, and mortality. Small ground finches with more larvae had lower hemoglobin and more immature erythrocytes, supporting compensatory blood-cell production. Erythropoiesis and responses to wounding therefore have especially direct links; iron handling, oxygen limitation, hemostasis, and circulatory compensation are strong physiological predictions. [1,2]
- **Immune resistance, with important limits.** Experimental parasite reduction demonstrated elevated fly-binding antibodies in mothers at parasitized medium ground finch nests. Higher maternal antibody levels tended to accompany fewer parasites, but did not rescue reproductive success in that experiment; fathers and nestlings lacked comparable induced responses. Thus adaptive/humoral immunity is documented, while particular immune signaling, inflammatory, complement, and cellular mechanisms remain predictions or supporting processes, not demonstrated molecular responses. [3]
- **Nutritional tolerance and growth.** Mockingbird nestlings increased begging and parents increased provisioning, apparently compensating for parasite costs; medium ground finches did not show equivalent compensation in the comparative experiment. Mockingbird tolerance weakened in dry years and tracked provisioning. Green warbler-finch work likewise found poorer growth and reduced maternal provisioning with heavy infestation, rather than successful compensation. This supports nutrient sensing, energy balance, substrate metabolism, and growth as potentially relevant, without assuming every metabolic pathway is specific to the fly. [4–6]
- **Behavioral defenses.** Preening, scratching, movement, and occasional removal of larvae are observed, but their fitness benefit is not generally established. Birds can incorporate supplied insecticide-treated cotton into nests. Finches also apply Psidium leaves to feathers; extracts repel adult flies and inhibit larvae experimentally, although protection from natural application remains a hypothesis. Sensory, learning, circadian, and motor processes may support these behaviors, but generic neural development is an indirect link. [7–9]
- **Repair and stress predictions.** Epithelial barriers, keratinocytes, extracellular matrix, fibroblasts, and vascular repair are strong candidates given feeding wounds and lasting tissue damage. Oxidant control, proteostasis, autophagy, and apoptosis are plausible supporting responses. These are physiological inferences unless explicitly documented above. A parasite manipulation found no elevation of baseline or handling-induced corticosterone in adult female medium ground finches, so endocrine stress was not treated as a universally demonstrated response. [10]

## Scoring conventions and limitation

Each term was judged independently; no score distribution was targeted. Directly documented broad mechanisms received 5; core machinery or strong predictions 4; supporting processes 3; weak or nonspecific connections 2; unrelated functions 1. Opposing regulatory directions can both be relevant through resistance, damage control, or tolerance. Avian thrombocytes provide the functional hemostatic connection for platelet-labeled terms; this does not imply mammalian platelets in birds. Embryonic development and ubiquitous housekeeping were not automatically promoted merely because nestlings grow or injured cells repair themselves.

The input labels GO:0160240 only as “GO:0160240”. Its identity could not be verified through the available public ontology routes. It is retained unchanged and assigned 1 because no specific link could be established; this is an information limitation, not evidence of biological irrelevance.

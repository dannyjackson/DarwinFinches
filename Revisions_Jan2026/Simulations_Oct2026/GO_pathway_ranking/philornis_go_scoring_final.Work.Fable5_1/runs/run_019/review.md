# Literature review: host responses of Galápagos land birds to *Philornis downsi* (run_019)

## What the larvae do to the host
- *P. downsi* adults are free-living; larvae are obligate parasites of nestlings (and of brooding adult females). First and early second instars live inside the nares (nasal cavities), ear canals and other tissues (myiasis), consuming keratin, blood and soft tissue; late second and third instars live in the nest base and emerge at night to feed externally on blood (Fessl et al. 2006, Parasitology; Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016; Fessl et al. 2018).
- Consequences documented in finches: blood loss of ~18–55 %, reduced haemoglobin/haematocrit (anaemia), reduced growth rate and body mass, multiple body wounds with secondary infection, enlarged/deformed nares and beak that persist to adulthood, altered song and reduced pairing success, and very high nestling mortality (often >50 %, up to 100 % of nests in some years) (Dudaniec et al. 2006; Fessl et al. 2006; Huber 2008; Galligan & Kleindorfer 2009; O'Connor et al. 2010; Koop et al. 2011; Kleindorfer & Sulloway 2016; Kleindorfer et al. 2019, 2022).

## Host defences documented or proposed
1. **Resistance – adaptive (antibody) immunity.** Several finch species mount *P. downsi*-specific IgY antibody responses; mothers of parasitised nests have higher antibody levels, with some effect on parasite load/nestling survival (Huber et al. 2010; Koop et al. 2013). Mockingbirds and finches in another study did not show a measurable antibody response (Knutie et al. 2016). Antibody/B-cell biology and the T-cell help it requires are therefore the best-documented molecular defence.
2. **Resistance – inflammation/innate immunity at feeding sites.** Philornid myiasis produces local inflammatory lesions and secondary infection of wounds; innate immune, inflammatory, cytokine/chemokine, complement and antibacterial processes are core expected components.
3. **Tolerance – compensation for blood and tissue loss.** Anaemia (lower haemoglobin), haematocrit and hypoxia stress imply selection on erythropoiesis, iron homeostasis, haemostasis/coagulation and hypoxia responses. Wound healing and epithelial/keratinocyte repair of nares, skin and ear tissue are directly implied by documented lesions.
4. **Tolerance – energetic compensation.** Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate the fly by begging more and receiving more parental provisioning; tolerance collapses in dry years when food is limited; plasma glucose and growth were measured as tolerance metrics in finches (Knutie et al. 2016; McNew et al. 2019). Glucose/energy homeostasis, nutrient sensing, lipid storage/mobilisation and growth regulation are therefore relevant.
5. **Stress physiology.** Corticosterone (HPA-axis) responses of breeding finches to the fly were tested experimentally (Knutie et al. 2013), so glucocorticoid/steroid-hormone signalling is a plausible supporting axis.
6. **Behavioural defences.** Nestlings stand on top of each other and show behavioural types (docility) that predict naris damage; parents probe the nest base, preen nestlings and sometimes eat larvae; brooding females stand erect more (O'Connor et al. 2010; Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016; Katsis et al. 2021). Pain/nociception, cognition and neural processes are weakly related.
7. **Other.** Parasitism alters the nestling gut microbiota (Knutie et al. 2018); oxidative stress accompanies anaemia and inflammation; beak/craniofacial tissues (nares, palate) are the target of first-instar feeding.

## Scoring logic applied
- 5: antibody/B-cell and general immune response terms, inflammatory response, wound healing/response to wounding, erythrocyte differentiation.
- 4: innate/adaptive immune regulation, T-cell activation, cytokine/chemokine production and signalling, complement, defence against bacteria, haemopoiesis, coagulation/platelets, iron homeostasis, hypoxia response, keratinocyte differentiation, glucose/energy homeostasis.
- 3: supporting processes (oxidative stress, NF-kB/TLR/JAK-STAT, epithelial repair, ECM, angiogenesis, nutrient/starvation responses, insulin, lipid stores, steroid-hormone signalling, stress response, chemotaxis, phagocytosis, blood pressure, pain perception, roof-of-mouth development).
- 2: weak/indirect links (generic proliferation, adhesion, migration, apoptosis, autophagy, growth-factor cascades, skeletal/beak development, behaviour/cognition, circadian, thermogenesis, zinc, antiviral immunity).
- 1: housekeeping and unrelated processes (transcription, splicing, mitosis, cilia, synaptic, cardiac, renal, germline development, etc.).

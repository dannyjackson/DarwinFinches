# Literature review: host responses of Galápagos land birds to Philornis downsi (run_008)

## What the larvae do to the host
- Adult flies are free-living; females oviposit in active nests. First-instar larvae migrate into the nares and auditory canals of young nestlings and feed on blood from nasal vessels; second and third instars live in the nest base and emerge at night to feed externally (non-subcutaneously) on blood and tissue of nestlings (and occasionally brooding females). Average in-nest mortality ~55%, up to 100% in some years (Fessl et al. 2006; Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016; McNew & Clayton 2018).
- Documented pathology: blood loss with reduced haemoglobin and anaemia, elevated immature red blood cells (reticulocytosis) (Dudaniec et al. 2006; Fessl et al. 2006); skin lesions/wounds on abdomen, nares and ears (Koop et al. 2011); permanent naris and beak malformation that persists to adulthood and alters song and pairing success (Galligan & Kleindorfer 2009; Kleindorfer & Sulloway 2016; Kleindorfer et al. 2019); reduced nestling growth and mass (Huber 2008; Fessl et al. 2006); nocturnal energy drain. Myiasis wounds in other Philornis-host systems become secondarily infected and show inflammatory pathology (Uhazy & Arendt 1986).

## Host defenses documented or proposed
1. Resistance via adaptive (humoral) immunity: adult medium ground finches, especially brooding females, mount P. downsi-specific antibody (IgY) responses; antibody level in mothers was associated with fewer parasites in the nest in one study, but nestlings have negligible antibody responses (Huber et al. 2010; Koop et al. 2013; Knutie et al. 2016).
2. Innate/inflammatory responses at feeding sites (wound inflammation, cytokines, phagocytes, complement, mast-cell/basophil type anti-ectoparasite reactions) are the canonical vertebrate resistance mechanism to blood-feeding ectoparasites and are inferred from the lesions; secondary bacterial infection of wounds is a recognised complication of myiasis.
3. Tolerance via energetic compensation: Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate P. downsi by increasing nestling begging and parental provisioning, maintaining plasma glucose and haemoglobin; Darwin's finches generally do not compensate (Knutie et al. 2016; McNew et al. 2019; O'Connor et al. 2014; Cimadom & Tebbich 2021). This predicts a role for energy/glucose/lipid homeostasis, nutrient sensing and growth signalling in tolerance.
4. Haematological tolerance/compensation: replacement of lost erythrocytes (erythropoiesis, iron and haem handling, hypoxia response), haemostasis at wounds.
5. Tissue repair: wound healing, re-epithelialisation, keratinisation, extracellular-matrix deposition, angiogenesis; repair/regrowth of damaged nasal cartilage, bone and beak keratin.
6. Behavioural defenses: preening/removal of larvae by parents, nest sanitation, changes in nest attendance, nocturnal vigilance, begging; nociception/itch from larval feeding presumably drives these. Pox-like stress responses (corticosterone) are proposed to mediate begging and resource allocation.
7. Other documented physiological correlates: altered gut microbiota in parasitised nestlings (Knutie et al. 2018); reduced growth.

## Scoring logic
- 5: wound/response to wounding and wound healing, inflammatory response, adaptive immune response, erythrocyte differentiation.
- 4: humoral/B-cell, cytokine and innate immune regulation, complement, coagulation, immune-system-wide terms, hypoxia response, iron homeostasis, haematopoiesis, glucose and energy homeostasis, skin/epidermis/keratinocyte and ECM/collagen terms.
- 3: supporting processes: specific cytokine/NF-kB/TLR/JAK-STAT signalling, T cells, phagocytosis, chemotaxis, defense to bacteria, platelet/thrombocyte, angiogenesis, VEGF/TGF-b/EGF/FGF, epithelial/fibroblast proliferation, cartilage/palate/skeletal development (naris), glucose/insulin/lipid-mobilisation/nutrient-sensing, oxidative stress, stress and steroid-hormone response, nociception, blood pressure.
- 2: weak or speculative: generic MAPK/apoptosis/cell migration/adhesion/autophagy, circadian, cognition/learning, bone, BMP, thermogenesis, xenobiotic response, zinc, type I interferon.
- 1: housekeeping, neural, developmental and other terms with no specific link.

# Literature review: host responses to Philornis downsi (run_025)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- P. downsi adults are free-living; females oviposit in nests of Galapagos land birds (most Darwin's finch species, mockingbirds, flycatchers, warbler-finches). First-instar larvae live and feed inside the nares, ear canals and developing feather quills of nestlings; second/third instars live in the nest base by day and emerge at night to feed externally on nestling (and brooding female) blood and tissue (Fessl et al. 2006; Fessl, Heimpel & Causton 2018; McNew & Clayton 2018; O'Connor et al. 2010).
- Documented pathology: blood loss with reduced haemoglobin/haematocrit (anaemia), open wounds in skin, nares and ear openings, reduced mass gain and growth, in-nest mortality averaging ~50% and reaching 100% in some broods, and permanent naris/beak malformation in survivors that later alters song, foraging and hybridisation risk (Dudaniec, Kleindorfer & Fessl 2006; Fessl, Kleindorfer & Tebbich 2006; Huber 2008; Koop et al. 2011; Galligan & Kleindorfer 2009; Kleindorfer & Sulloway 2016; Kleindorfer et al. 2019, 2022; O'Connor et al. 2010).
- Parasitism changes nestling gut microbiota (mockingbirds more than finches), probably via altered parental provisioning (Knutie et al. 2018).

## Host defences documented or proposed
1. Acquired humoral immunity (resistance): medium ground finches and at least eight finch species produce P. downsi-binding antibodies (IgY); levels are higher in breeding adults, in females (who brood and are bitten) and in mothers at parasitised vs fumigated nests; nestlings show weak responses; the antibody response did not rescue nestling survival (Huber et al. 2010 PLoS ONE; Koop et al. 2013 Ecol Evol; Clayton NSF summary).
2. Innate/local responses to blood-feeding and wounding (a priori): inflammation at bite sites, cytokine/chemokine production, phagocyte recruitment, complement, coagulation/thrombocyte haemostasis, wound healing, re-epithelialisation, skin and keratinocyte repair, extracellular-matrix remodelling and angiogenesis; secondary bacterial infection of wounds is plausible (general ectoparasite immunology: Owen et al. 2010 "Ecological immunology of bird-ectoparasite systems"; Clayton et al. 2010 "How birds combat ectoparasites").
3. Haematological compensation (a priori, from documented anaemia): erythropoiesis/haematopoiesis, iron homeostasis, hypoxia response, blood-pressure/volume regulation.
4. Endocrine stress response: experimentally parasitised breeding adults show altered corticosterone and reduced haematocrit (Knutie et al. 2013, Gen Comp Endocrinol), implicating the HPA axis and glucocorticoid/steroid-hormone signalling.
5. Tolerance via energy compensation: Galapagos mockingbirds tolerate P. downsi because nestlings beg more and parents increase provisioning; finches do not compensate (Knutie et al. 2016 Ecology); tolerance collapses in dry years when food is limited (McNew et al. 2019 Proc B). Finch begging does not honestly signal need in parasitised nests (O'Connor et al. 2014). Predicts relevance of energy homeostasis, nutrient-sensing (insulin/IGF, TOR), lipid/glycogen storage and mobilisation, growth regulation, starvation responses and thermoregulation (nocturnal feeding on small, poorly insulated nestlings; larger-bodied hosts are more tolerant).
6. Behavioural defences: nestling preening of larvae and standing on nestmates (ineffective), parental nest sanitation, preening, incorporation of insecticide-treated cotton into nests; nocturnal attack suggests links to nociception, arousal and circadian processes (Koop et al. 2013; Knutie et al. 2014; O'Connor et al. 2010; Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016 BMC Zoology review).
7. Sensory/skeletal consequences: larvae in ear canals and nares and lasting beak/naris damage suggest a weak-to-moderate link to hearing, craniofacial/skeletal and cartilage/bone repair processes.

## Scoring logic applied
- 5: inflammatory, wound-healing, innate/adaptive/B-cell immune terms, generic defence/immune response.
- 4: cytokine/chemokine production and signalling, complement, coagulation/platelet, haematopoiesis/erythropoiesis, iron homeostasis, hypoxia, regulation of immune/inflammatory response, energy homeostasis, response to stress/cytokine.
- 3: downstream supporting processes: NF-kB/TLR/JAK-STAT/TNF/IL-1 signalling, T-cell processes, phagocytosis, chemotaxis, oxidative stress/ROS, angiogenesis, ECM/collagen, epidermis/skin/keratinocyte, hormone and glucocorticoid signalling, nutrient/starvation responses, glucose/glycogen/lipid energy metabolism, IGF signalling, thermogenesis, pain perception.
- 2: generic signalling with immune/metabolic or repair components (MAPK, PI3K, EGF/FGF/TGF-beta, insulin, autophagy, apoptosis regulation, adhesion, circadian, skeletal/bone, xenobiotic, antiviral).
- 1: housekeeping, DNA/RNA metabolism, cell cycle, cilia, neuronal, cardiac, reproductive and other developmental terms with no specific link.

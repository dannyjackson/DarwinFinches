# Literature review: host responses to *Philornis downsi* (run_005)

Scope: published ecological, physiological and behavioural literature on the avian vampire fly
and Galapagos land birds (Darwin's finches, Galapagos mockingbird, flycatchers). No genomic,
transcriptomic or gene-expression studies of Galapagos birds were used.

## What the larvae do to hosts

- Adult flies are free-living; eggs are laid in nests. First (and early second) instar larvae
  live as agents of myiasis inside the nares, ear openings and developing feather quills of
  nestlings, consuming keratin, blood and tissue; later instars live in the nest base and feed
  externally on nestling blood at night (Fessl, Sinclair & Kleindorfer 2006, Parasitology;
  McNew & Clayton 2018, Annu. Rev. Entomol.).
- Blood loss of 18-55% of nestling blood volume; reduced haemoglobin / anaemia; multiple skin
  wounds and lesions (abdomen, nares, ear canal) with risk of secondary infection; reduced growth
  and ~55% (up to 100%) in-nest mortality (Fessl et al. 2006; Dudaniec, Kleindorfer & Fessl 2006,
  Austral Ecol.; Koop et al. 2011, PLoS ONE; O'Connor et al. 2010; Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016,
  BMC Zool.).
- Permanent naris enlargement and beak malformation (upper nasal cavity may be destroyed), which
  carries into adulthood and alters song and foraging (Galligan & Kleindorfer 2009;
  Kleindorfer & Sulloway 2016, Glob. Ecol. Conserv.; Kleindorfer et al. 2019, Proc. B;
  Common et al. 2020).
- Brooding females are also bitten (sex-biased antibody levels; Huber et al. 2010).

## Documented or proposed host defences

1. Resistance (immune): *P. downsi*-binding antibody (IgY) responses in adult medium ground
   finches, higher in females and in parents at parasitised nests; at least eight finch species
   mount such responses, but they did not rescue nestling survival (Huber et al. 2010, PLoS ONE;
   Koop et al. 2013, Ecol. Evol.). Nestlings apparently do not mount a strong antibody response.
   General ectoparasite-bite biology predicts local inflammation, innate/adaptive responses to
   larval saliva, haemostasis at wounds, and antibacterial defence of wounds.
2. Tolerance: Galapagos mockingbirds fledge normally at the same parasite densities that kill
   finches, mediated by larger nestling size and parental food compensation (energy budget);
   tolerance collapses in dry years when provisioning cannot be increased (Knutie et al. 2016,
   Ecology; McNew et al. 2019, Proc. B). Haemoglobin loss is used as the physiological cost
   metric, so compensatory erythropoiesis, iron handling and hypoxia responses are strong a priori
   predictions. Nestling growth, energy metabolism and wound/tissue repair are the key
   tolerance axes. Parental compensation in finches is weak or absent (O'Connor et al. 2014;
   Knutie et al. 2016; Heyer et al. 2021; Green Warbler-Finch study 2023).
3. Behavioural: preening/removal of larvae by nestlings and brooding females (limited efficacy);
   nestling begging changes; finches anoint feathers with *Psidium galapageium* leaves whose
   volatiles repel adult flies and inhibit larval growth (Cimadom et al. 2016, Sci. Rep.;
   Tebbich et al. 2021, Ethology); uptake of insecticide-treated cotton into nests (Knutie et al.
   2014); larvae feed mainly at night, so nocturnal detection/pain and circadian factors are
   weakly plausible.

## How this guided scoring

- 5: adaptive immune response; erythrocyte differentiation; response to wounding / wound
  healing; inflammatory response.
- 4: regulation of inflammation and immune/innate processes, TLR signalling, cytokine
  production/signalling, B-cell activation/BCR, complement, hypoxia response, haematopoiesis,
  iron homeostasis, blood coagulation, energy homeostasis, epidermis/skin/keratinocyte
  development (barrier and repair).
- 3: downstream immune signalling (TNF, NF-kB, JAK-STAT, IL-1/IL-6/IL-17, T-cell processes,
  phagocytosis, chemotaxis, antibacterial defence), ROS/oxidative stress, tissue repair (ECM,
  collagen, angiogenesis, epithelial proliferation/migration, keratin filaments), energy and
  nutrient stress (glucose metabolism, gluconeogenesis, beta-oxidation, lipid storage,
  starvation responses), IGF growth signalling, palate (nasal cavity) development, platelet
  function, pain perception.
- 2: generic growth, metabolism, adhesion, apoptosis, MAPK/PI3K, autophagy, hormone/stress
  signalling, bone/cartilage (beak), circadian, thermogenesis, ear development.
- 1: everything with no specific link (neural, cell-cycle, RNA processing, transcription,
  organelle transport, development of unrelated organs, etc.).

# Philornis downsi – host response literature review (run_027)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- Adult flies are non-parasitic; larvae are semi-hematophagous obligate parasites of nestlings (and brooding females). First instars feed inside the nares/nasal cavity (keratin, blood, tissue); second and third instars live in nest material and feed externally at night on blood and tissue of nestlings (Fessl et al. 2006 Parasitology; O'Connor et al. 2010 Oryx video study).
- Consequences: blood loss and anaemia / reduced haemoglobin (Dudaniec et al. 2006; Fessl et al. 2006; Knutie et al. 2016), wounds and skin lesions (myiasis), reduced nestling growth and body mass (Huber 2008), naris and beak malformation that persists into adulthood (Galligan & Kleindorfer 2009; Kleindorfer & Sulloway 2016), altered song (Kleindorfer et al. 2019), and ~55% mean in-nest mortality, up to 100% in some years (Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016 review; O'Connor et al. 2010; Koop et al. 2011).

## Documented or proposed host defences
1. Resistance – humoral immunity: P. downsi-binding IgY antibodies in adult medium ground finches, higher in brooding females and in parasitised nests (Huber et al. 2010 PLoS ONE; Koop et al. 2013 Ecol Evol). Nestling antibody response weak/undetected. Antibody-mediated defence against ectoparasites is thought to integrate with inflammation and preening.
2. Tolerance – energy compensation: Galapagos mockingbird parents increase provisioning and nestlings keep haemoglobin and fledging success despite similar parasite densities; finches do not (Knutie et al. 2016 Ecology; McNew et al. 2019 Proc B, tolerance collapses in drought). Food availability/urban food increases finch nestling survival (Knutie et al. 2024). Implies energy, nutrient, growth and haematopoietic/iron physiology are core tolerance components.
3. Tissue repair / local responses: wound healing of lesions, inflammation at feeding sites, re-epithelialisation of skin and nasal tissue, haemostasis after blood loss; compensatory erythropoiesis and hypoxia responses to anaemia are strong a priori predictions.
4. Behavioural: preening/removal of larvae by nestlings and parents (limited; Koop et al. 2013; Pike et al. 2021), nestling flapping/movement, female brooding behaviour affecting larval numbers (Kleindorfer et al. 2021), self-anointing with Psidium galapageium leaves that repel adult flies (Cimadom et al. 2016 Sci Rep; Tebbich et al. 2021).
5. Secondary processes: bacterial infection of wounds, oxidative stress, stress-hormone responses, altered gut microbiota (Knutie et al. 2018 Ecosphere) – weaker links.

## Scoring logic
5: immune response, adaptive/B-cell humoral immunity, inflammatory response, wound healing, response to wounding.
4: regulation of immune/inflammatory processes, innate immunity, complement, BCR signalling, erythrocyte differentiation, coagulation, response to hypoxia, energy homeostasis, defense response.
3: cytokine/TNF/NF-kB/IL signalling, T-cell processes, phagocytosis, skin/epidermis/keratinocyte, ECM and epithelial proliferation (repair), iron homeostasis, hemopoiesis, nutrient/starvation responses, platelet activation, defense to bacteria, roof-of-mouth development (beak/nares), ossification.
2: angiogenesis, growth-factor/insulin/TOR signalling, glucose/lipid metabolism, oxidative stress, generic stress, learning/memory and pain (behavioural defences), craniofacial/cartilage development.
1: everything else (housekeeping, neural, cell-cycle, RNA processing, etc.).

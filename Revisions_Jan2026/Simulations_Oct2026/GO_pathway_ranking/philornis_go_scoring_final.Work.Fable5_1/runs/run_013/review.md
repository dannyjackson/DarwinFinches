# Literature review: host responses of Galápagos land birds to Philornis downsi (run_013)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- Adult P. downsi are free-living; larvae are obligate parasites of nestlings (and occasionally brooding females). First-instar (and early second-instar) larvae live inside the nares, ear canals and developing feather quills of nestlings as agents of myiasis; later instars live in the nest base and emerge at night to feed externally on blood and tissue (Fessl, Sinclair & Kleindorfer 2006, Parasitology; McNew & Clayton 2018, Annu. Rev. Entomol.).
- Documented pathology: blood loss of ~18-55% of nestling blood volume; anaemia with reduced haemoglobin and elevated immature red blood cell counts (regenerative anaemia); skin lesions/wounds on abdomen, nares and ears; reduced growth rates and body mass; permanent naris and beak malformation in survivors (enlarged naris, missing upper nasal cavity in extreme cases); ~55% mean in-nest mortality, up to 100% in some species/years (Dudaniec, Kleindorfer & Fessl 2006; Fessl et al. 2006; Huber 2008; Koop et al. 2011; Galligan & Kleindorfer 2009; Kleindorfer & Sulloway 2016; Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016 review).
- Naris malformation changes adult song (lower max frequency, greater vocal deviation) and pairing success, and blurs species mating signals (Kleindorfer et al. 2019, Proc. R. Soc. B) - a downstream phenotypic consequence rather than a defence.
- Parasitism alters gut microbiota in mockingbird nestlings (Knutie et al. 2018, Ecosphere); secondary bacterial infection of wounds is plausible.

## Host defences documented or proposed
1. Acquired (humoral) immune resistance: medium ground finches mount P. downsi-specific IgY antibody responses; levels are higher in females (who brood and are bitten) than males, higher in breeding than pre-breeding adults, and experimentally elevated in mothers at parasitized vs. fumigated nests. Antibody response did not rescue current reproductive success but may reduce parasite load in some contexts (Huber et al. 2010, PLoS ONE; Koop et al. 2013, Ecol. Evol.). Nestling antibody responses were weak/not protective. This implicates B-cell activation/differentiation, antigen presentation, T-helper signalling, cytokine and chemokine production, complement, and general adaptive/innate immune processes.
2. Inflammatory / innate response at feeding sites: lesions and wounds imply local inflammation, leukocyte chemotaxis, phagocytosis, oxidative burst, NF-kB/TLR signalling in response to larval saliva and wound-associated bacteria; regulation (damping) of inflammation is a candidate tolerance mechanism.
3. Haematological compensation (tolerance): reduced haemoglobin with increased immature erythrocytes indicates compensatory erythropoiesis; blood loss predicts selection on erythrocyte differentiation, haematopoiesis, iron homeostasis, hypoxia responses, haemostasis/coagulation at feeding wounds, and blood-pressure/fluid-volume regulation.
4. Tissue repair (tolerance): survivors heal wounds in skin, nares and beak; wound healing, epidermis/keratinocyte biology, ECM and collagen organisation, angiogenesis, epithelial proliferation and cartilage/craniofacial (palate/nasal) tissue biology are core candidates.
5. Energetic/nutritional tolerance: Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate P. downsi; nestlings beg more and parents increase provisioning, compensating for the energetic cost of blood loss; tolerance collapses in dry years when food is scarce (Knutie et al. 2016, Ecology; McNew et al. 2019, Proc. R. Soc. B). Finch parents generally fail to compensate (Knutie et al. 2016; Green warbler-finch study, J. Ornithol. 2023). Larger-bodied nestlings may withstand parasitism better. This implicates energy homeostasis, glucose/lipid metabolism and storage, nutrient-sensing (insulin/IGF/TOR), starvation responses, growth regulation and thermoregulation.
6. Endocrine stress: corticosterone of breeding females was not affected by experimental P. downsi manipulation, but nestling stress responses remain plausible (Knutie et al. 2013, Gen. Comp. Endocrinol.). Hormone-signalling terms are therefore plausible but not strongly supported.
7. Behavioural defences: parental preening of nestlings and probing of nest material, nestling flapping/preening to dislodge larvae (O'Connor et al. 2010, Oryx; Common et al. 2021, J. Insect Behav.), self-medication by rubbing Psidium galapageium leaves on plumage which repel P. downsi (Cimadom et al. 2016, Sci. Rep.), increased begging (mockingbirds), and nest-site/nest-structure effects. These implicate nociception/itch sensing, nervous-system processes, circadian biology (larvae feed nocturnally) and learning/cognition, with weaker links.

## Scoring approach
5: immune response/adaptive response/B-cell activation (documented IgY response), erythrocyte differentiation (documented regenerative anaemia), wound healing / response to wounding (documented lesions and healed malformations).
4: core components of those mechanisms or strong predictions: innate immune and inflammatory response and their regulation, cytokine/chemokine production, complement, B-cell differentiation/BCR signalling, haemopoiesis, iron homeostasis, hypoxia responses, blood coagulation, energy homeostasis, defence response, immune system process, pain perception (drives anti-larval behaviour).
3: plausible supporting/downstream processes: specific cytokine pathways (IL-1, IL-6, IL-8, IL-17, TNF, JAK-STAT, NF-kB, TLR), T-cell processes, phagocytosis and chemotaxis, oxidative stress/ROS, angiogenesis, ECM/collagen, skin/epidermis/keratinocyte biology, cartilage and palate development, glucose homeostasis, starvation/nutrient responses, lipid storage, IGF signalling, hormone responses, circadian rhythm, blood-pressure regulation, defence against bacteria/LPS (secondary infection), platelet (thrombocyte) activation.
2: weak/indirect links: general metabolism, growth-factor pathways involved in repair (EGF, Wnt, TGF-beta, FGF, VEGF), adhesion/migration, apoptosis regulation, autophagy, bone/skeletal development, zinc, thermogenesis, xenobiotic responses, learning/cognition, insulin signalling.
1: no specific link (housekeeping, neural, cardiac, renal, reproductive, cell-cycle, RNA/DNA processing, antiviral, etc.).

# Literature review: host responses to Philornis downsi (run_002)

## What the larvae do to the host
- Adult flies are free-living; eggs are laid in nests. First-instar (and early second-instar) larvae feed *inside* the nares and other tissues of nestlings (myiasis: keratin, blood, tissue); later instars live in the nest base and feed externally at night on blood of nestlings and brooding females (Fessl, Sinclair & Kleindorfer 2006, Parasitology; https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/parasitology/article/abs/lifecycle-of-philornis-downsi-diptera-muscidae-parasitizing-darwins-finches-and-its-impacts-on-nestling-survival/597D469905598A7105BBE59990009B32).
- Blood loss of 18-55% of nestling blood volume; reduced haemoglobin concentration and anaemia (Dudaniec, Kleindorfer & Fessl 2006, Austral Ecology; https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1442-9993.2006.01553.x).
- Open lesions/wounds on the body, naris and beak malformation that persists to adulthood (Galligan & Kleindorfer 2009; https://academic.oup.com/biolinnean/article-abstract/98/3/577/2532299 ; Kleindorfer & Sulloway 2016; https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2351989416300336). Severe cases lose the upper nasal cavity.
- Reduced growth (mass, tarsus, feather length), high in-nest mortality (~50%, up to 100%) (Koop et al. 2011, PLoS ONE; https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0019706 ; Huber 2008).
- Carry-over effects: naris enlargement alters adult song and mate signals (Kleindorfer et al. 2019, Proc B; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6571467/).
- Reviews: Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016, BMC Zoology (https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40850-016-0003-9); McNew & Clayton 2018, Annu Rev Entomol (https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-ento-020117-043103); Fessl et al. 2018 (colonization history/conservation); native-Philornis pathogenesis Uhazy & Arendt 1986 (https://research.fs.usda.gov/treesearch/61438).

## Host defences documented or proposed
1. **Adaptive (humoral) immunity / resistance**: P. downsi-binding IgY antibodies in medium ground finches, higher in females (brooders) and in mothers at parasitized vs fumigated nests; detected in at least eight finch species; antibody response did not rescue fledging success (Huber et al. 2010, PLoS ONE, https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0008605 ; Koop et al. 2013, Ecol Evol, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3930052/). Knutie et al. 2016 found no significant antibody response in finches or mockingbirds in another year. Resistance in Galapagos hosts is generally weak.
2. **Inflammatory / innate response at feeding sites**: wounds, lesions, myiasis of nasal tissue imply local inflammation, leukocyte recruitment, complement, cytokines, response to larval saliva/secretions and secondary bacterial infection (general ectoparasite biology; lesions documented by Huber 2010, Koop 2011; Knutie et al. 2018 Ecosphere microbiota study, https://esajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ecs2.2286).
3. **Haematological compensation**: documented anaemia/low haemoglobin implies compensatory erythropoiesis, iron handling, hypoxia responses, haemostasis/coagulation against blood-feeding larvae (Dudaniec et al. 2006; Fessl et al. 2006).
4. **Wound healing and tissue repair**: body lesions, naris/beak tissue loss and keratin consumption imply re-epithelialisation, keratinocyte/epidermal processes, ECM remodelling, angiogenesis, and bone/cartilage remodelling of the beak (Galligan & Kleindorfer 2009; Kleindorfer & Sulloway 2016).
5. **Endocrine stress response**: experimental test of corticosterone in breeding G. fortis (Knutie et al. 2013, Gen Comp Endocrinol; https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4383185/) - HPA/glucocorticoid axis, hematocrit and condition measured; oxidative stress and telomere length proposed as welfare indicators (Tebbich project; https://www.wildanimalinitiative.org/grantees/sabine-tebbich).
6. **Tolerance via energy compensation**: Galapagos mockingbirds tolerate P. downsi by increased begging and parental provisioning; finches do not compensate (Knutie et al. 2016, Ecology, https://darwin.biology.utah.edu/PDFs/126.pdf ; McNew et al. 2019, Proc B, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6408884/ ; O'Connor et al. 2014 Ethology; Heyer et al. 2021; Green Warbler-Finch study https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10188583). Implies energy homeostasis, nutrient sensing, growth regulation (IGF/insulin), thermoregulation of small nestlings fed on at night, lipid/glycogen stores.
7. **Behavioural defences**: parent and nestling preening/removal of larvae from nares and nest base, eating larvae, sibling jostling to avoid nest base (O'Connor et al. 2010 Oryx video, https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/oryx/article/video-analysis-of-hostparasite-interactions-in-nests-of-darwins-finches/BF956C9751763DAFAEE36345498A4A01); nestling behavioural type (docility) predicts naris deformation (Katsis et al. 2021, https://academic.oup.com/biolinnean/article/134/3/636/6332654); self-fumigation with treated cotton (Knutie et al. 2014). Nocturnal larval feeding suggests sleep/circadian and nociception links. These map only weakly to GO biological processes (nervous system process, pain perception, circadian rhythm, learning/memory).
8. **Reproductive compensation** (re-nesting, clutch adjustments) and song/mate-choice consequences - very indirect for GO terms.

## Scoring logic used
- 5: immune/inflammatory/defense/wound response terms, erythrocyte differentiation (direct response to documented anaemia and lesions).
- 4: core components or strong predictions: cytokine/TNF/IL-1/IL-6/chemokine production & signaling, NF-kB, TLR, complement, B/T cell activation, hemopoiesis, iron homeostasis, hypoxia response, coagulation, energy homeostasis.
- 3: supporting/downstream: angiogenesis, ECM/collagen, epidermis/keratinocyte, STAT/JAK, phagocytosis, chemotaxis, oxidative stress, nutrient/starvation responses, glucocorticoid/hormone signaling, platelet activation, IGF signaling, pain perception, palate (roof of mouth/nasal) development, thermogenesis.
- 2: weak/indirect: general metabolism and growth, adhesion/migration, bone/cartilage, autophagy, apoptosis regulation, circadian, learning/memory, MAPK, behaviour-adjacent neural terms.
- 1: housekeeping and unrelated processes (transcription, splicing, cell cycle, cilia, synaptic, organ-specific development, etc.).

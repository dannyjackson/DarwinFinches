# Literature review: host responses of Galápagos land birds to *Philornis downsi* (run_030)

## What the larvae do to the host
- Adult flies are free-living; larvae are obligate parasites of nestlings (and brooding females). First and early second instars live inside the nares, ear openings and feather quills as agents of myiasis, consuming nasal tissue and keratin; later instars live in the nest base and emerge at night to feed externally on blood (Fessl et al. 2006, Parasitology; McNew & Clayton 2018, Annu. Rev. Entomol.).
- Blood loss of 18-55% of nestling blood volume; reduced haemoglobin/anaemia (Dudaniec et al. 2006, Austral Ecology; Fessl et al. 2006; Knutie et al. 2016, Ecology: fumigated nestlings had 14-40% more haemoglobin).
- Skin lesions/wounds on abdomen, head, nares; permanent naris and beak malformation that persists into adulthood and alters song (Galligan & Kleindorfer 2009, Biol. J. Linn. Soc.; Kleindorfer et al. 2019, Proc. R. Soc. B; Koop et al. 2011, PLoS ONE).
- Reduced nestling growth and mass, ~55% average in-nest mortality (up to 100% in some years) (Huber 2008, Biol. Conserv.; Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016, BMC Zoology; O'Connor et al. 2010).
- Reduced brooding time by females; possible disruption of nest thermoregulation (McNew & Clayton 2018).
- Altered nestling gut microbiota (Knutie et al. 2018, Ecosphere) and elevated stress hormones (corticosterone) in breeding finches (Knutie et al. 2017, cited in McNew & Clayton 2018).

## Host defenses documented or proposed
1. **Resistance - adaptive (antibody) immunity.** Medium ground finches mount *P. downsi*-binding antibody (IgY) responses; levels are higher in females (who brood and are bitten) and in parents at parasitized nests; higher maternal antibody levels associated with fewer parasites in the nest (Huber et al. 2010, PLoS ONE; Koop et al. 2013, Ecol. Evol.; Knutie et al. 2016). Antibody response to a skin-feeding ectoparasite implies B/T cell activation, cytokine signaling, and the usual cutaneous inflammatory/hypersensitivity response (mast cells, basophils, eosinophils, IL-17/TNF/IL-1/IL-6 axis, complement, chemotaxis of leukocytes).
2. **Resistance - innate/local inflammatory and wound response.** Feeding lesions trigger inflammation, haemostasis (coagulation/thrombocytes), re-epithelialization and keratinocyte proliferation, angiogenesis and extracellular-matrix remodeling; secondary bacterial infection of wounds is a plausible downstream pressure.
3. **Tolerance - compensating blood loss.** Haemoglobin loss predicts erythropoietic/haematopoietic compensation, iron handling, hypoxia responses, and cardiovascular adjustment (heart rate/blood pressure under hypovolaemia).
4. **Tolerance - energetic/nutritional compensation.** Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate the same parasite density because parents increase provisioning; finches do not and lose fitness. Tolerance varies with rainfall/food (Knutie et al. 2016; McNew et al. 2019, Proc. R. Soc. B; Knutie et al. 2016 Oecologia on delayed costs). Host-side physiology of tolerance involves glucose/energy homeostasis, insulin/IGF growth signaling, nutrient sensing (TOR), lipid mobilization and thermogenesis in nestlings that are brooded less.
5. **Behavioral defenses.** Preening of larvae by parents, nestlings standing on nest mates, altered begging, reduced brooding, and possible self-medication/self-fumigation by incorporating treated or repellent material into nests (Koop et al. 2013; O'Connor et al. 2014; McNew et al. 2019; Cimadom et al. 2016 and Frontiers Conserv. Sci. 2025). Behavioral responses imply nociception/sensory detection of larvae but are mostly not captured by specific GO processes.
6. **Stress endocrine response.** Elevated corticosterone in breeding finches exposed to flies (Knutie et al. 2017); glucocorticoid signaling links to immune regulation and energy mobilization.
7. **Tissue repair/regeneration of nares and beak** (keratinization, craniofacial epithelial and skeletal tissue) is implied by the lasting naris malformation.

## Scoring logic applied
- 5: terms that literally name documented host mechanisms: adaptive/B-cell (antibody) immune response, immune response, inflammatory response, wound healing / response to wounding, defense response.
- 4: core components of those mechanisms or strong predictions: innate immune activation, T-cell help, cytokine/chemokine/IL-6/IL-8/IL-1/IL-17 signaling, complement, inflammasome, leukocyte adhesion, coagulation, haematopoiesis/erythropoiesis, iron homeostasis, hypoxia response, energy homeostasis, keratinocyte differentiation.
- 3: supporting/downstream processes: glucose and nutrient homeostasis, insulin/IGF signaling, starvation responses, thermogenesis, angiogenesis and ECM/collagen in wound repair, epithelial proliferation/migration, chemotaxis/phagocytosis, NF-kB/JAK-STAT/TNF signaling, oxidative stress/ROS, hormone/glucocorticoid signaling, skin/epidermis development, pain perception, blood pressure, defense to bacteria (wound infection).
- 2: weak/indirect links (general apoptosis, MAPK, TGF-beta/BMP, lipid/amino-acid metabolism, cell adhesion/migration, autophagy, circadian, learning/memory, bone/cartilage, ER/heat stress, interferon/antiviral).
- 1: no specific link (housekeeping, neural development/synaptic, cell cycle, RNA processing, organ development unrelated to skin/blood/immune, etc.).

# Literature review: host responses to Philornis downsi (run_023)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- Adult flies are free-living; females oviposit in nests during late incubation / early brooding. First (and early second) instar larvae live inside the nares and nasal cavity of nestlings as agents of myiasis, feeding on keratin, blood and soft beak tissue. Later second and third instars live in the nest base and emerge at night to feed externally on the blood (and some tissue) of nestlings and, less often, brooding females (Fessl et al. 2006, Parasitology; Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016, BMC Zoology; McNew & Clayton 2018, Annu Rev Entomol).
- Documented pathology: blood loss of 18-55% of nestling blood volume, lowered haemoglobin / anaemia (Dudaniec et al. 2006; Knutie et al. 2016: fumigated nestlings had 14-40% more haemoglobin), skin lesions on the abdomen and elsewhere, naris enlargement and beak malformation that persists into adulthood and alters song (Galligan & Kleindorfer 2009; Kleindorfer & Sulloway 2016; Kleindorfer et al. 2019), reduced growth and body mass, and on average ~55% in-nest mortality, up to 100% in some years/species (Koop et al. 2011; O'Connor et al. 2010; Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016).
- Brooding females are also fed on; parasitism raised corticosterone (HPA stress axis) and affected haematocrit/condition of breeding adults (Knutie et al. 2013, Gen Comp Endocrinol).
- Parasitism alters nestling gut microbiota, especially in mockingbirds (Knutie et al. 2018, Ecosphere).

## Host defences documented or proposed
1. Immune resistance: P. downsi-binding IgY antibodies rise in parasitized medium ground finch mothers (Koop et al. 2013) and in mockingbird and finch nestlings (Knutie et al. 2016); the response is interpreted as following cutaneous inflammation at bite/feeding sites (Grab et al. 2019). Antibody responses did not rescue fledging success in finches. Reviews note that Galapagos birds "appear to have no effective behavioural or immunological resistance" to date, but inflammation, complement, white blood cells and antibody binding to larvae are the proposed resistance mechanisms (Knutie et al. 2017, Ecosphere; McNew et al. 2019).
2. Tolerance: Galapagos mockingbirds fledge normally at similar larval densities; parasitized nestlings beg more and parents provision more (energy compensation), and tolerance collapses in drought years when food is limited (Knutie et al. 2016, Ecology; McNew et al. 2019, Proc B). Darwin's finches generally do not increase provisioning (O'Connor et al. 2014; Heyer et al. 2021; green warbler-finch, 2023). Body size / surface:volume and energy reserves are proposed to underlie tolerance of nightly blood loss. This points to energy homeostasis, nutrient-sensing, glucose/lipid metabolism and growth (insulin/IGF/TOR) as tolerance pathways, plus erythropoiesis/iron handling to replace lost blood and hypoxia responses to anaemia.
3. Behavioural: nestling self-preening, repositioning, standing on nestmates; more docile nestlings had less naris damage (Katsis et al. 2021); female in-nest attendance predicts parasite numbers; nocturnal feeding by larvae; begging intensity does not honestly signal need in parasitized nests.
4. Physiological stress: elevated corticosterone in parasitized adults; oxidative stress and telomere length have been proposed as welfare indicators in nestlings (Tebbich project). Wound repair of skin, nares and beak keratin is implicit in survivors with healed, enlarged nares.

## How this guided scoring
- 5: direct counters to documented pathology or documented responses: wound healing / response to wounding, inflammatory response and its regulation, innate and adaptive immune response, B-cell activation / BCR signalling (IgY), erythrocyte differentiation.
- 4: core components / strong predictions: T-cell activation and differentiation, cytokine production and signalling (IL-6, TNF, IL-1, IL-8, chemokines, IL-17), complement activation, leukocyte adhesion, haemopoiesis, iron homeostasis, hypoxia response, blood coagulation and platelet activation (haemostasis at feeding wounds), skin / epidermis development and keratinocyte differentiation (tissue consumed by larvae), energy homeostasis (tolerance via energy compensation).
- 3: supporting processes: NF-kB, JAK-STAT, TLR, inflammasome, chemotaxis, phagocytosis, ROS and oxidative stress, angiogenesis/ECM/collagen/fibroblast and epithelial proliferation and migration (repair), glucose/lipid/fatty-acid metabolism, starvation and nutrient responses, insulin/IGF/TOR signalling, defence against bacteria (wound infection), glucocorticoid (steroid nuclear receptor) signalling, neuropeptide signalling and nociception (begging/behaviour), blood pressure regulation.
- 2: weak or generic links (MAPK/JNK, apoptosis, autophagy, cell adhesion/migration generic, circadian rhythm, bone/cartilage/beak development, thermogenesis, antiviral terms as generic immunity).
- 1: no specific link (housekeeping, neural development, cell cycle, RNA processing, etc.).

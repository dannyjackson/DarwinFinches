# Literature review: host responses of Galapagos land birds to Philornis downsi (run_004)

Scope: web-based review of the ecological, physiological, behavioural and immunological literature on
P. downsi and its hosts (Darwin's finches, Galapagos mockingbirds, flycatchers). No genomic,
transcriptomic or gene-expression study of Galapagos birds was used (the Knutie et al. bioRxiv
2023.03.06.531275 / PubMed 38273516 "urban living" paper surfaced in searches and was deliberately
not opened or used).

## What the larvae do to the host

- Adult flies are free-living; larvae are obligate semi-haematophagous parasites of nestlings (and
  occasionally brooding females). First and early second instars live inside the nares and
  developing feather quills, consuming keratin, blood and tissue; later instars live in the nest
  base and feed externally on nestlings at night (Fessl, Sinclair & Kleindorfer 2006, Parasitology;
  McNew & Clayton 2018, Annu. Rev. Entomol.).
- Blood loss of 18-55% of nestling blood volume; reduced haemoglobin concentration / anaemia
  correlated with larval intensity (Dudaniec, Kleindorfer & Fessl 2006, Austral Ecol.; Fessl et al.
  2006).
- Naris and beak malformation (enlarged or encrusted nares, occasionally loss of upper beak) that can
  persist into adulthood and alter song and foraging (Galligan & Kleindorfer 2009; Kleindorfer &
  Sulloway 2016; Kleindorfer et al. 2019 Proc B).
- Wounds in skin, nares and ear openings; reduced nestling growth (mass, wing, beak depth); ~55%
  mean in-nest mortality, up to 100% in some years (Huber 2008; Koop et al. 2011 PLoS ONE;
  O'Connor et al. 2010; Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016 BMC Zoology).
- Histopathology of congeneric Philornis myiasis (P. deceptivus in pearly-eyed thrashers): larvae
  elicit a local inflammatory lesion dominated by macrophages and plasma cells with vascular
  congestion; the host response is an energetic drain that reduces growth (Uhazy & Arendt 1986,
  J. Wildl. Dis.).
- Welfare / physiology indicators proposed for parasitised nestlings include haematocrit, baseline
  corticosterone, oxidative stress, telomere length, breathing rate and sleep (Tebbich group,
  Univ. Vienna project description).

## Documented or proposed host defences

1. Antibody-mediated (adaptive, humoral) immunity. P. downsi-binding IgY antibodies detected in
   adult medium ground finches, higher in females (which brood) and in birds with active nests
   (Huber et al. 2010, PLoS ONE); experimentally elevated in mothers of parasitised nests, at least
   eight finch species show antibody responses, but the response did not reduce parasite numbers
   or rescue nestling survival (Koop et al. 2013, Ecol. Evol.). Nestling antibody responses were
   weak/absent in finches and mockingbirds (Knutie et al. 2016, Ecology). Maternal antibody
   transfer has been raised as a possible route.
2. Innate/inflammatory and wound responses. Local inflammation, phagocytes (macrophages),
   plasma cells, vascular congestion at larval feeding sites; wound healing / tissue repair is
   explicitly listed as a tolerance mechanism (Grab et al. 2019, Ecol. Evol.; Uhazy & Arendt 1986).
   Secondary bacterial infection of wounds and gut-microbiota shifts have been studied (Knutie et
   al. 2018, Ecosphere).
3. Haematological compensation. Haemoglobin loss and anaemia are the main measured physiological
   cost; recovery requires erythropoiesis, iron handling and hypoxia responses (Dudaniec et al.
   2006; Knutie et al. 2016).
4. Tolerance via energy compensation. Mockingbird nestlings beg more and parents provision more,
   maintaining fledging success; finches do not (Knutie et al. 2016). Tolerance collapses in drought
   years when food is limiting (McNew et al. 2019, Proc B). Larger body size and lower surface
   area:volume (nocturnal heat and blood loss) proposed as a tolerance factor. Plasma glucose was
   measured as an energetic indicator. Corticosterone is proposed to mediate begging.
5. Behavioural defences. Nestling preening of larvae and standing on nest mates (ineffective);
   parental preening; female brooding behaviour; Darwin's finches rub Psidium galapageium leaves
   into plumage, whose volatiles repel P. downsi (Cimadom et al. 2016, Sci. Rep.); finches
   incorporate permethrin-treated cotton into nests (Knutie et al. 2014). Larvae feed nocturnally
   and hide by day, avoiding host detection (McNew & Clayton 2018).
6. Growth and developmental consequences. Reduced growth of mass, wing and beak; beak/naris
   morphology (craniofacial epithelium and keratin) damaged; song learning/production altered.

## How this translated into scoring

- 5: processes directly documented as host responses: antibody/B-cell adaptive immunity, immune
  response, inflammatory response, wound healing / response to wounding, erythrocyte
  differentiation (recovery from anaemia).
- 4: core components or strong a priori predictions: regulation of immune system / cytokine
  signalling, T-cell help, NF-kB and JAK-STAT signalling, complement, phagocytosis, chemokine
  production, leukocyte adhesion, haematopoiesis, iron homeostasis, response to hypoxia, blood
  coagulation / thrombocyte activation (haemostasis after feeding), keratinocyte/epidermis/skin
  development and extracellular-matrix organisation (repair of nares, skin, feather follicles).
- 3: plausible supporting/downstream processes: specific pro-inflammatory cytokines (IL-1, IL-6,
  TNF, IL-8, IL-17, IFN-gamma), TLR/LPS responses (secondary infection), angiogenesis / VEGF,
  epithelial proliferation and migration, fibroblast proliferation, TGF-beta and EGF signalling,
  energy and nutrient homeostasis (starvation, glucose, IGF signalling, energy homeostasis),
  stress and glucocorticoid (steroid receptor) signalling, oxidative stress / ROS, blood pressure
  regulation (hypovolaemia), sensory perception of pain, roof-of-mouth development (naris/palate).
- 2: weak or indirect links: general metabolism of carbohydrate/lipid, apoptosis, cell adhesion and
  migration, MAPK/PI3K signalling, bone/cartilage (beak), circadian rhythm (nocturnal feeding),
  learning/memory/cognition (behavioural defences, song), xenobiotic response (plant repellents,
  insecticide), telomere/senescence, antiviral/IFN type I, ER stress (plasma-cell secretion), zinc.
- 1: no specific link (housekeeping transcription/translation/splicing/DNA repair, cell cycle,
  mitochondrial, neuronal wiring, organogenesis of heart/kidney/eye/gonad, cilia, etc.).

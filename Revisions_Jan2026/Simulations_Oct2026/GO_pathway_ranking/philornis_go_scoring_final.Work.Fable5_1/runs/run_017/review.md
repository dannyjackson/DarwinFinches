# Run 017 - Literature review: host responses to Philornis downsi

## What the larvae do to hosts
- Adult flies are free-living; larvae are obligate parasites of nestlings (and brooding females). First and early second instars live inside the nares and other tissues (myiasis); older second/third instars live in the nest base and feed externally on blood and tissue at night (Fessl, Sinclair & Kleindorfer 2006, Parasitology).
- Documented pathology: blood loss of 18-55%, reduced haemoglobin/anaemia (Dudaniec, Kleindorfer & Fessl 2006; Knutie et al. 2016), skin lesions/wounds on abdomen, ears and nares (Koop et al. 2011), permanent naris and beak malformation from feeding on keratin/tissue inside the beak (Galligan & Kleindorfer 2009; Kleindorfer & Sulloway 2016), reduced growth rate and body mass, and in-nest mortality averaging ~55% and up to 100% (Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016; Koop et al. 2011; O'Connor et al. 2010).
- Secondary bacterial exposure of wounds is plausible (larvae carry their own microbiota; Ben-Yosef et al. 2017).

## Host defenses documented or proposed
1. Acquired/humoral immunity: Philornis-binding antibody (IgY) levels are higher in nesting adults and in mothers at parasitized nests, and higher maternal antibody levels associate with fewer parasites (Huber et al. 2010; Koop et al. 2013; Knutie et al. 2016). This is the clearest documented resistance mechanism, implying B-cell activation, T-cell help, cytokine signalling and general adaptive/immune response.
2. Innate/inflammatory response at feeding sites: lesions imply local inflammation, leukocyte recruitment, complement, TLR/cytokine signalling (TNF, IL-1, IL-6, IL-8/chemokines, IL-17 as typical anti-ectoparasite skin responses); the host's ability to limit immunopathology (negative regulation of inflammation) is a tolerance component.
3. Haematological compensation: anaemia and haemoglobin loss predict selection on erythropoiesis, haematopoiesis, iron homeostasis, hypoxia responses, and haemostasis/coagulation (blood-feeding larvae; platelets/thrombocytes).
4. Tissue repair: wound healing, re-epithelialisation, keratinocyte/skin/epidermis processes, extracellular matrix and collagen, angiogenesis/VEGF; naris tissue damage.
5. Tolerance via energetics: Galapagos mockingbirds tolerate P. downsi by increasing nestling provisioning to compensate for lost energy, whereas finches do not (Knutie et al. 2016 Ecology; Knutie et al. 2018 Ecosphere; Heyer et al. 2023 on Green Warbler-Finch provisioning); therefore glucose/energy homeostasis, starvation/nutrient responses, growth (IGF/insulin), thermogenesis, and lipid stores are plausible tolerance processes.
6. Stress physiology: experimental tests of corticosterone (HPA axis) in breeding finches (Knutie et al. 2015 and related work); oxidative stress and telomere attrition proposed as welfare/physiological indicators (Tebbich project). Hormone and glucocorticoid receptor signalling therefore scored as supporting processes.
7. Behavioural defenses: nestling preening/consumption of larvae, standing on nestmates, altered begging, parental provisioning changes, female time budgets (Koop et al. 2013; O'Connor et al. 2014; Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016). These map weakly onto GO terms (nervous system process, nociception, learning/memory, circadian rhythm given nocturnal feeding).

## Scoring summary
- 5: wound healing / response to wounding, inflammatory response, immune response, adaptive immune response, B cell activation, erythrocyte differentiation.
- 4: core immune/inflammatory components (innate immune response, TLR, cytokine production/signalling, complement, T cell activation, IL-17, IL-1/IL-6/IL-8/TNF), coagulation, hypoxia response, haematopoiesis, iron homeostasis, regulation of inflammation.
- 3: supporting processes (angiogenesis, epidermis/skin/keratinocyte, ECM/collagen, phagocytosis, chemotaxis, oxidative stress, glucose/energy homeostasis, starvation/nutrient responses, IGF signalling, steroid hormone signalling, thermogenesis, nociception).
- 2: metabolic/growth/apoptosis/signalling terms with only indirect links; 1: no specific link.

# Literature review: host responses of Galápagos land birds to Philornis downsi (run_009)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- Adult flies are free-living; females oviposit in nests during incubation and nestling phases. First-instar (and some second-instar) larvae live inside the nares and ear canals of nestlings, consuming blood, keratin and tissue (internal myiasis); older second- and third-instar larvae live in the nest base by day and feed externally on nestling blood at night (Fessl et al. 2006; O'Connor et al. 2010; Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016).
- Blood loss of 18-55% of nestling blood volume, reduced haemoglobin, anaemia, impaired growth, reduced beak depth and high (~50%, up to 100%) nestling mortality (Fessl et al. 2006; Dudaniec et al. 2006; Huber 2008; Koop et al. 2011; O'Connor et al. 2009).
- Skin lesions/wounds on abdomen and body; naris and beak malformation (enlarged or encrusted nares, scarring) that persists to adulthood and alters song and foraging (Galligan & Kleindorfer 2009; Kleindorfer & Sulloway 2016; Kleindorfer et al. 2019, 2022).
- Larvae also feed on incubating/brooding females (Koop et al. 2013; Cimadom et al. 2016).
- Possible secondary consequences: bacterial infection of wounds/myiasis sites, altered gut microbiota, dehydration/energy depletion from nocturnal feeding (Knutie et al. 2018; McNew et al. 2019).

## Host defences documented or proposed
1. Antibody-mediated (adaptive, humoral) immunity: P. downsi-binding IgY detected in adults of at least eight finch species; higher in females (brooding exposure) and in mothers at parasitized vs fumigated nests; weak/no detectable response in nestlings; antibody response did not rescue fledging success (Huber et al. 2010; Koop et al. 2013; Knutie et al. 2016).
2. Innate/inflammatory response at feeding sites: inflammation, oedema and granulocyte recruitment are the general mechanism by which birds resist blood-feeding nest-fly larvae, and lesions/swelling are seen in P. downsi hosts (Koop et al. 2013; Grab et al. 2019; DeSimone et al. 2018). Darwin's finches appear to lack effective resistance (McNew et al. 2019).
3. Tolerance (limiting fitness loss per parasite): Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate P. downsi via increased nestling begging and parental provisioning (energy compensation, plasma glucose), larger body size; tolerance drops in drought years. Finches did not increase begging or provisioning (Knutie et al. 2016; McNew et al. 2019; O'Connor et al. 2014). Physiological predictions: compensatory erythropoiesis/haematopoiesis, iron handling, hypoxia response, energy/glucose homeostasis, wound healing and tissue (skin, keratin, nasal cavity, bone/cartilage of the beak) repair, haemostasis/coagulation after blood loss.
4. Behavioural defences: nestling preening/removal of larvae and standing on nest mates; parental nest maintenance; adult finches anoint feathers with Psidium galapageium leaves whose extracts repel adult flies and inhibit larval growth (Cimadom et al. 2016; Tebbich et al. 2021; Lincango/Common observations). These appear ineffective at improving fledging.
5. Stress physiology: corticosterone-mediated responses, pain/nociception from biting, sleep disruption from nocturnal feeding are plausible but little studied.

## How this guided scoring
- 5: terms directly naming documented mechanisms: immune/defense/innate/adaptive/inflammatory response, B cell activation (IgY), wound healing/response to wounding, erythrocyte differentiation (anaemia recovery).
- 4: core components or strong predictions: cytokine/chemokine production and signalling (TNF, IL-1, IL-6, IL-8), NF-kB and TLR signalling, inflammasome, complement, leukocyte adhesion/chemotaxis, T and B cell activation/differentiation, haemopoiesis, platelet/coagulation, iron homeostasis, hypoxia response, epidermis/skin/keratinocyte development, energy and glucose homeostasis.
- 3: supporting processes: apoptosis regulation, MAPK/JNK/p38, JAK-STAT, TGF-beta, ECM/collagen/angiogenesis, epithelial proliferation/migration, oxidative stress and ROS, starvation/nutrient responses, gluconeogenesis, skeletal/cartilage/bone development (beak), roof of mouth development, pain perception, blood pressure, hormone/steroid signalling, response to bacterium.
- 2: weak/indirect: generic signalling, adhesion, migration, growth, lipid/energy metabolism, insulin, autophagy, circadian, cognition/learning, sodium/zinc transport, interferon/antiviral terms.
- 1: no specific link (housekeeping, cell cycle, DNA repair, RNA processing, neural development, cilia, gametogenesis, organ development unrelated to beak/skin).

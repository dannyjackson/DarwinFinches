# Literature review: host responses of Galápagos land birds to Philornis downsi (run_010)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- Adult P. downsi are free-living; females oviposit in nests (and sometimes directly in nestling nares). First and early second instars live inside the nares and other tissues of nestlings (myiasis), consuming keratin, blood and tissue; later second and third instars live in the nest base and feed externally at night on nestling blood, and also on brooding females (Fessl et al. 2006, Parasitology; Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016; McNew & Clayton 2018).
- Documented host damage: blood loss of 18-55 % of blood volume, reduced haemoglobin and haematocrit (anaemia), skin lesions/wounds on abdomen, ears and nares, enlarged and permanently malformed nares and beak, lower growth and body mass, elevated corticosterone, and nestling mortality averaging ~55 % and reaching 100 % in some years (Dudaniec et al. 2006; Fessl et al. 2006; Huber 2008; Galligan & Kleindorfer 2009; Koop et al. 2011; Kleindorfer & Sulloway 2016; Knutie et al. 2016).
- Surviving birds carry naris deformation that alters song and mating signals (Kleindorfer et al. 2019).

## Host defences documented or proposed
1. Adaptive (humoral) immunity / resistance: Darwin's finches (at least eight species) mount P. downsi-specific IgY antibody responses; antibody levels are higher in breeding females and in mothers of parasitised nests, although this did not rescue nestling survival in the year studied (Huber et al. 2010; Koop et al. 2013). Ectoparasite bites activate inflammatory pathways leading to IgY production (Grab et al. 2019 review of mechanism). Immune/inflammatory and antibody-related processes are therefore the most directly documented physiological host response.
2. Inflammation and wound repair: larvae cause open lesions in skin and nasal tissue; wound healing, haemostasis, skin/keratinocyte biology, tissue regeneration and craniofacial (naris/beak) tissue development are directly implicated by the pathology.
3. Haematological compensation: anaemia from blood loss implies selection on erythropoiesis, iron handling, hypoxia responses and oxygen transport.
4. Tolerance via energy compensation: Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate P. downsi; parasitised mockingbird nestlings beg more and parents increase provisioning, whereas medium ground finches show no increase in begging, provisioning, or plasma glucose (Knutie et al. 2016; McNew et al. 2019). Tolerance is environment-dependent (rainfall/food). Processes of energy balance, glucose and lipid metabolism, growth/insulin-IGF signalling, nutrient-sensing and nestling growth are strong a priori candidates.
5. Stress physiology: parasitised nestlings/breeding adults show altered corticosterone (Knutie et al. 2013 Gen Comp Endocrinol; later nestling work), so glucocorticoid/steroid hormone signalling and stress responses are plausible.
6. Behavioural defences: nestlings preen off larvae and stand on nest mates; females reduce brooding/stand more when nests are heavily infested (possibly disrupting nestling thermoregulation); adults anoint feathers with Psidium galapageium leaves whose volatiles repel P. downsi and slow larval growth (Cimadom et al. 2016, Sci Rep; Tebbich et al. 2021); finches incorporate permethrin-treated cotton into nests (Knutie et al. 2014). Larvae feed nocturnally, so sleep/circadian and pain/itch perception are plausible but weak links. Learning/cognition links are speculative.
7. Native-range comparison: in Tobago, Philornis trinitensis affects grassquits similarly and no detectable antibody response was found in native hosts; tolerance via provisioning appears to be the main mockingbird defence (Knutie et al. 2017).

## How the scale was applied
- 5: terms naming immune/inflammatory/antibody responses, wound healing/response to wounding, erythrocyte differentiation.
- 4: core components of those mechanisms (T cell help, cytokine/TNF/IL-6/IL-1/IL-17/chemokine signalling, NF-kB, TLR, complement, phagocytosis, leukocyte adhesion, coagulation/platelets, hemopoiesis, iron homeostasis, hypoxia response, skin/epidermis/keratinocyte development, energy and glucose homeostasis, response to stress).
- 3: supporting processes (angiogenesis, ECM/collagen, TGF-b, epithelial proliferation/migration, bone/cartilage/craniofacial development for naris/beak, lipid/nutrient/starvation metabolism, oxidative stress, hormone/steroid signalling, circadian rhythm, pain perception, thermogenesis, JAK-STAT, antibacterial defence of wounds).
- 2: weak/indirect (general adhesion, MAPK/PI3K, autophagy, apoptosis, interferon/antiviral, learning, Wnt/BMP beak-patterning links, generic lipid metabolism).
- 1: no specific link (housekeeping, neural, cilia, cell cycle, RNA processing, etc.).

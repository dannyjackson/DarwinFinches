# Philornis downsi – host response mechanisms used for a priori GO-term scoring (run_011)

## What the larvae do to Galápagos land-bird hosts

- **Myiasis of the nares, ear canals and skin (1st / early 2nd instars).** Newly hatched larvae enter the nostrils (also ear canals, feather quills, subcutaneous sites) and consume keratin, blood and soft tissue inside the beak. This produces enlarged/deformed nares and beak malformation that persists into adulthood and alters song and foraging (Fessl et al. 2006 Parasitology; Galligan & Kleindorfer 2009; Kleindorfer & Sulloway 2016; Kleindorfer et al. 2019 Proc B; Common et al.).
- **Nocturnal haematophagy (late 2nd / 3rd instars).** Larvae live in the nest base by day and emerge at night to rasp the skin and ingest blood and serum, leaving open lesions on the abdomen, head and ears. Estimated blood loss 18–55 % of nestling blood volume; parasitised nestlings have lower haemoglobin, altered immature red-cell counts, reduced growth and body mass, and nestling mortality of ~50 % (3–100 %) (Fessl et al. 2006; Dudaniec et al. 2006 Austral Ecol; Huber 2008; Koop et al. 2011 PLoS ONE; O'Connor et al. 2010).
- **Adult females** brooding on the nest are also fed upon (Knutie et al. 2013 GCE; Cimadom et al. 2016).
- **Secondary effects**: altered nestling gut microbiota (Knutie et al. 2018 Ecosphere); larval feeding sites are open wounds susceptible to microbial infection; nocturnal feeding disturbs nestlings (begging/sleep); nest climate and host blood quality affect larval development.

## Host defences documented or proposed

1. **Antibody-mediated (humoral adaptive) immunity.** Medium ground finch mothers at parasitised nests have higher P. downsi-binding IgY than at fumigated nests; at least eight finch species mount Philornis-specific antibody responses, but the response does not rescue fledging success (Huber et al. 2010 PLoS ONE; Koop et al. 2013 Ecol Evol; Knutie et al. 2016 Ecology found no significant antibody effect in that year). This is the best-documented physiological defence.
2. **Local inflammatory / wound response.** Cutaneous lesions and naris tissue damage imply inflammation, leukocyte recruitment, haemostasis, re-epithelialisation, fibroblast/collagen repair and keratin regeneration; "tissue repair" is explicitly listed as a tolerance mechanism in the Philornis literature (Grab et al. 2019; McNew & Clayton 2018 Annu Rev Entomol).
3. **Tolerance via resource/energy compensation.** Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate equal parasite densities: parasitised nestlings beg more, parents provision more, and nestling plasma glucose and haemoglobin are maintained; finches do not show this compensation (Knutie et al. 2016 Ecology; McNew et al. 2019 Proc B). Hence energy/glucose homeostasis, nutrient sensing and growth pathways are a priori tolerance candidates.
4. **Haematological tolerance.** Blood loss / anaemia predicts selection on erythropoiesis, iron handling, hypoxia responses and haemostasis (Dudaniec et al. 2006 measured haemoglobin and immature erythrocytes; Knutie et al. 2016 haemoglobin).
5. **Stress physiology.** Corticosterone of breeding females was tested experimentally (Knutie et al. 2013 GCE; HPA axis / glucocorticoid signalling); oxidative stress, haematocrit and telomere length are being used as welfare indicators (Tebbich project).
6. **Behavioural defences.** Preening/removal of larvae by parents or nestlings (rare), nest sanitation, and anointing feathers with Psidium galapageium leaves whose volatiles repel P. downsi (Cimadom et al. 2016 Sci Rep; Tebbich et al. 2021 Ethology; Pike et al. 2021). Night-time feeding and begging responses link to circadian/sleep and nociception.
7. **Body size / growth** as a tolerance trait (larger mockingbird nestlings withstand parasitism; nestling growth is reduced by larvae).

## How mechanisms map to scores

- 5: terms that directly name documented mechanisms – immune/adaptive/B-cell/antibody-related processes, inflammatory response, response to wounding and wound healing, erythrocyte differentiation, defence response.
- 4: core components or strong predictions – innate immunity, cytokine/chemokine (IL-1, IL-6, IL-8, TNF) production and signalling, NF-kB and JAK-STAT, T-cell help, complement, inflammasome, leukocyte adhesion, haemopoiesis, iron homeostasis, hypoxia response, coagulation/platelet (thrombocyte) function, skin/epidermis/keratinocyte, collagen/ECM/fibroblast proliferation, glucose and energy homeostasis, response to stress, steroid (glucocorticoid) receptor signalling.
- 3: plausible supporting processes – MAPK/JNK/p38/PI3K signalling, TLR/LPS responses, phagocytosis and chemotaxis, apoptosis regulation, angiogenesis and epithelial migration/proliferation, oxidative stress handling, nutrient/starvation/insulin/TOR signalling, lipid energy stores, bone/cartilage/craniofacial repair (naris), circadian rhythm, nociception, protein secretion.
- 2: weak/indirect – generic adhesion/cytoskeleton/migration machinery, generic growth-factor and GPCR signalling, autophagy, ion transport linked to fluid balance, hormone responses other than glucocorticoids, cognition/learning (behavioural innovation), xenobiotic responses, sound perception (ear-canal myiasis).
- 1: no specific link – housekeeping (transcription, splicing, translation, DNA repair, cell cycle, cilia, mitochondria), neural development/synaptic function, embryonic patterning, organ development, antiviral processes.

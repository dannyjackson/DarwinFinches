# Literature review: host responses to Philornis downsi (run_012)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- Adult flies are free-living; females oviposit in nests of nearly all Galápagos land birds (12 of 17 Darwin's finch species, mockingbirds, flycatchers, warbler finches). First-instar larvae enter the nares, ear canals and developing feather quills of nestlings and feed internally on keratin, blood and soft tissue; second/third instars live in the nest base by day and feed externally on nestling (and brooding female) blood at night (Fessl et al. 2006 Parasitology; Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016 BMC Zool; McNew & Clayton 2018 Annu Rev Entomol).
- Documented host damage: large blood loss, reduced haemoglobin/haematocrit (anaemia), skin lesions/wounds on abdomen, nares and ears, reduced growth and body mass, naris and beak malformation persisting into adulthood (altering foraging and song), in-nest mortality averaging ~55% and up to 100% (Dudaniec et al. 2006; Fessl et al. 2006; Huber 2008; Koop et al. 2011; Galligan & Kleindorfer 2009; Kleindorfer & Sulloway 2016; Kleindorfer et al. 2019, 2022; Katsis et al. 2021).
- Parasitism raises corticosterone in brooding female finches (Knutie et al. 2013 Gen Comp Endocrinol) and imposes carry-over costs on future reproduction in mockingbirds (McNew et al. 2020 Oecologia).

## Host defenses documented or proposed
1. Immunological resistance (adaptive/humoral): P. downsi-binding antibodies (IgY) are elevated in breeding adults, especially brooding females, and in mothers at parasitized nests; females with higher antibody levels had fewer larvae in nests (Huber et al. 2010 PLoS One; Koop et al. 2013 Ecol Evol; Knutie et al. 2016 Ecology). A general inflammatory/innate response to ectoparasite saliva and wounds is the expected a priori counterpart (cytokines, TNF, IL-1/IL-6/IL-17, NF-kB, TLR, complement, leukocyte recruitment, phagocytosis), with secondary bacterial infection of lesions plausible.
2. Tolerance via haematological recovery: because the dominant insult is blood loss, erythropoiesis, haematopoiesis, iron homeostasis, hypoxia responses, haemostasis/coagulation (avian thrombocytes) and cardiovascular compensation are strong a priori predictions (Dudaniec et al. 2006; Koop et al. 2011; Knutie et al. 2016).
3. Tolerance via energetics / parental compensation: mockingbird nestlings beg more and parents provision more, which underlies their tolerance; tolerance collapses in drought years; larger-bodied hosts tolerate better; finches generally do not compensate (Knutie et al. 2016 Ecology; McNew et al. 2019 Proc B; O'Connor et al. 2014 Ethology; Heyer et al. 2021 Ibis). Predicts nutrient sensing, growth signalling (IGF/insulin/TOR), glucose/energy homeostasis, starvation responses, thermogenesis.
4. Tissue repair and integument: wounds in skin, nares (keratin/beak tissue) and ear canals imply wound healing, epidermis/keratinocyte biology, epithelial proliferation and migration, angiogenesis, ECM/collagen remodelling, and craniofacial/beak (palate) morphogenesis relevant to naris deformation.
5. Endocrine stress response: corticosterone/HPA activation (Knutie et al. 2013) implies glucocorticoid (nuclear receptor) signalling and general hormone responses.
6. Behavioural defenses: preening/removal of larvae from nares and nest base, nestling positioning, female in-nest attendance reducing larval numbers (Kleindorfer et al. 2021 Proc B), nestling behavioural type predicting naris damage (Katsis et al. 2021), use of repellent Psidium galapageium leaves and uptake of insecticide-treated nest material (Cimadom et al. 2016; Knutie et al. 2014 Curr Biol; Common et al. 2025). Behaviour maps only weakly onto specific GO biological processes (nervous system, learning, pain perception, circadian timing of nocturnal attack).

## Scoring logic
5 = terms naming the documented mechanisms directly (immune/adaptive/inflammatory/B-cell response and its regulation, wound healing/response to wounding, erythrocyte differentiation, defense response). 4 = core components (specific cytokine signalling, NF-kB/TLR, T-cell activation, complement, haemopoiesis, iron homeostasis, hypoxia, coagulation/platelets, glucocorticoid receptor signalling, epidermis/skin/keratinocyte development). 3 = plausible supporting processes (angiogenesis, epithelial proliferation/migration, ECM/collagen, chemotaxis/phagocytosis, oxidative stress, nutrient/starvation/TOR/IGF signalling, energy homeostasis, antibacterial defense, hormone responses, beak/palate morphogenesis). 2 = weak links (general metabolism, growth, adhesion, apoptosis, circadian, behaviour-related neural terms, bone/cartilage). 1 = no specific link (housekeeping, RNA/DNA metabolism, cilia, sperm, neuron wiring, cardiac morphogenesis, etc.).

Note: results of transcriptomic/genomic studies of Galápagos birds were deliberately not used.

# Literature review: host responses of Galápagos land birds to Philornis downsi (run_007)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- Adult flies are free-living; females oviposit in nests of nearly all Galápagos landbirds (12+ Darwin's finch species, mockingbirds, flycatchers, warbler finch). First-instar larvae enter the nares (also ear openings and feather quills) and feed internally on blood and soft keratinous beak tissue (myiasis); older 2nd/3rd instars live in the nest base and emerge at night to rasp the skin and feed externally on blood and serum of nestlings and brooding females (Fessl et al. 2006 Parasitology; O'Connor et al. 2010; Common et al. 2019; McNew & Clayton 2018).
- Documented host damage: blood loss of 18-55%, anaemia and reduced haemoglobin/hematocrit with elevated immature erythrocytes (Dudaniec et al. 2006; Fessl et al. 2006; Knutie et al. 2016); open lesions, swelling and subcutaneous bleeding on belly, wings, ears and nares (Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016); permanent naris/beak malformation affecting foraging and song (Galligan & Kleindorfer 2009; Kleindorfer & Sulloway 2016; Kleindorfer et al. 2019, 2022); reduced growth rate and body mass (Huber 2008; Koop et al. 2011; Katsis et al. 2021); in-nest mortality averaging ~55% (3-100%).

## Host defences documented or proposed
1. Resistance via adaptive immunity: Philornis-specific antibody (IgY) responses are detectable in adults of at least eight finch species; mothers at parasitized nests have higher antibody levels; nestlings have near-undetectable antibodies and the response does not rescue fledging success (Huber et al. 2010 PLoS ONE; Koop et al. 2013 Ecol Evol; Knutie et al. 2016). Local inflammatory/innate responses at bite sites (wounds, swelling) are described.
2. Tolerance: Galápagos mockingbirds fledge normally at equal parasite densities, apparently via behavioural/energetic compensation (more begging, more parental provisioning); finches do not increase begging, provisioning or plasma glucose (Knutie et al. 2016 Ecology; McNew et al. 2019). Energy balance, glucose homeostasis, nutrient/food supply and body size are therefore the proposed tolerance axes.
3. Haematological compensation: regenerative erythropoiesis (immature RBCs), iron handling and hypoxia responses are the physiological route for coping with anaemia.
4. Wound repair and tissue regeneration: healing of skin/naris lesions, haemostasis, re-epithelialisation, keratin/beak tissue regrowth.
5. Stress physiology: corticosterone/HPA axis responses to parasitism in breeding adults (Knutie et al. 2013 Gen Comp Endocrinol); oxidative stress and telomere attrition proposed as welfare/physiological indicators.
6. Behavioural defences: preening/removal of larvae (rare), self-fumigation with permethrin-treated cotton (Knutie et al. 2014 Curr Biol), anointing feathers with Psidium galapageium leaves that repel adult flies and inhibit larval growth (Cimadom et al. 2016 Sci Rep; Tebbich et al. 2021), nestling activity/behavioural type correlating with naris damage (Katsis et al. 2021), nocturnal feeding of larvae (circadian context), nociception.
7. Secondary infection: open wounds and larval microbiota plausibly expose nestlings to bacteria; gut microbiota altered in parasitized mockingbirds (Knutie et al. 2018 Ecosphere).

## Scoring logic applied
- 5: immune response, inflammatory response, adaptive/innate immunity, B cell activation/differentiation, wound healing/response to wounding, blood coagulation, erythropoiesis/hemopoiesis, iron homeostasis, defense response.
- 4: cytokine/TNF/IL-6/IL-1/NF-kB/TLR/complement signalling, T cell activation, leukocyte adhesion, skin/epidermis/keratinocyte processes, ECM/collagen (repair), hypoxia response, steroid hormone (glucocorticoid) signalling, glucose/energy homeostasis, response to stress, platelet activation/aggregation.
- 3: angiogenesis/endothelial processes, epithelial proliferation/migration, chemotaxis/phagocytosis, oxidative stress/ROS, nutrient/starvation and insulin/IGF signalling, JAK-STAT/IFN-gamma, antibacterial defence, hormone responses, pain perception, blood pressure.
- 2: general growth/metabolism/lipid/energy, apoptosis regulation, bone/cartilage/beak-related development, circadian, behaviour/cognition, generic MAPK/TGF-beta/EGF signalling, antiviral responses.
- 1: housekeeping, neural development/synaptic, cell cycle, RNA processing, transport, organ development unrelated to the above.

# Literature review: host responses of Galápagos land birds to Philornis downsi (run_029)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- Adult flies are free-living; larvae are obligate parasites of nestlings (and brooding females). First/early second instars live inside the nares and other tissues as agents of myiasis, consuming keratin, blood and soft tissue; later instars live in the nest base and feed externally (mostly nocturnally) on blood (Fessl, Sinclair & Kleindorfer 2006, Parasitology; McNew & Clayton 2018, Annu. Rev. Entomol.).
- Consequences: blood loss of 18–55%, reduced haemoglobin/anaemia, reduced growth and body mass, multiple skin wounds/lesions with secondary infection, grossly deformed nares that persist into adulthood, altered beak morphology and song, and ~55% (up to 100%) in-nest mortality (Dudaniec et al. 2006; Fessl et al. 2006; Huber 2008; Galligan & Kleindorfer 2009; Koop et al. 2011; O'Connor et al. 2010; Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016; Kleindorfer et al. 2019).

## Host defenses documented or proposed
1. Adaptive humoral immunity (resistance): Darwin's finches (≥8 species) mount P. downsi-specific IgY antibody responses; mothers at parasitised nests have higher antibody levels; nestlings generally do not (Huber et al. 2010; Koop et al. 2013; Knutie et al. 2016). Antibody response did not rescue current reproductive success in finches.
2. Innate/inflammatory and wound responses: feeding lesions, tissue damage and infections imply inflammation, haemostasis/coagulation, wound healing, re-epithelialisation and keratin regeneration in skin and nares (Fessl et al. 2006; Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016). Described as generally ineffective resistance in finches ("no effective behavioural or immunological resistance"; McNew et al. 2019).
3. Haematological tolerance: blood loss and anaemia are the main pathology; replacing red cells (erythropoiesis, iron handling, hypoxia responses) is the core physiological tolerance route; mockingbirds maintain hemoglobin and fledging success better than finches (Knutie et al. 2016, Ecology).
4. Energetic/nutritional tolerance: mockingbird nestlings beg more and parents increase provisioning, compensating for energy lost to the parasite; tolerance declines in drought; finches show little compensation (Knutie et al. 2016; McNew et al. 2019, Proc B; Cimadom et al. 2023 J. Ornithol.). Predicts selection on energy homeostasis, glucose/lipid metabolism, growth-factor and insulin signalling, starvation responses.
5. Behavioural defenses: self-anointing with Psidium galapageium leaves whose volatiles repel P. downsi and slow larval growth (Cimadom et al. 2016, Sci. Rep.; Tebbich et al. 2021); nestling behaviour (responsiveness) modulates naris deformation; beak morphology maintained for preening (Villa et al. 2018); use of insecticide-treated cotton in nests (Knutie et al. 2014). Mostly behavioural/learned, weakly mapped onto physiological GO terms.
6. Stress endocrine axis: corticosterone of breeding finches tested experimentally (Knutie et al. 2013, Gen. Comp. Endocrinol.); treated as plausible but weak.
7. Morphological consequences (naris/beak, song) are pathology rather than defense, so craniofacial/keratin development terms scored as plausible supporting processes.

## Scoring logic applied
- 5: immune response/adaptive/B-cell terms (documented IgY response), inflammatory response, wound healing, response to wounding, erythrocyte differentiation (anaemia/haemoglobin pathology), defense response.
- 4: innate immune activation, cytokine production/signalling (IL-6, TNF, IL-1, IL-17, chemokines), T-cell activation, TLR signalling, complement, haemopoiesis, iron homeostasis, hypoxia response, coagulation/platelet activation, skin/epidermis/keratinocyte development, energy homeostasis.
- 3: supporting processes: angiogenesis, ECM/collagen, epithelial proliferation/migration, phagocytosis, chemotaxis, NF-kB/JAK-STAT, oxidative stress/ROS, starvation/nutrient and insulin/IGF signalling, glucose/glycogen/fatty-acid energy metabolism, steroid hormone signalling, pain perception, bacterial defense (secondary infection).
- 2: indirect links (general cell migration/adhesion, apoptosis, autophagy, lipid metabolism, Wnt/TGF-b/BMP, bone/cartilage/craniofacial, circadian, thermogenesis, antiviral terms, xenobiotic responses).
- 1: housekeeping, neural, reproductive, cell-cycle, RNA/DNA processing and organ-development terms with no specific link.

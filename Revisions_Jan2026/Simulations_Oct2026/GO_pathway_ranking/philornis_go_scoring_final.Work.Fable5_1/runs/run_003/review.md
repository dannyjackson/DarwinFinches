# Literature review: host responses of Galápagos land birds to Philornis downsi (run_003)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- Adult flies are non-parasitic; females oviposit in nests during late incubation/early brooding. First (and some second) instars live inside the nares and feed internally on keratin, blood and soft beak tissue; later instars live in the nest base and emerge at dusk/dawn to feed externally (semi-hematophagous) on nestling blood and tissue, and on brooding females (Fessl et al. 2006 Parasitology; O'Connor et al. 2010; Common et al. 2020; McNew & Clayton 2018 Annu Rev Entomol).
- Documented host damage: blood loss and reduced haemoglobin/anaemia (Dudaniec et al. 2006; Fessl et al. 2006; Knutie et al. 2016 - hemoglobin 14-40% higher in fumigated nests), skin lesions/wounds on abdomen, ears and nares, naris and beak malformation that persists into adulthood and alters song and foraging (Galligan & Kleindorfer 2009; Kleindorfer & Sulloway 2016; Kleindorfer et al. 2019, 2022), reduced nestling growth (Huber 2008; Koop et al. 2011), ~55% mean in-nest mortality, up to 100% (Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016 review).

## Host defences documented or proposed
1. Resistance - adaptive (antibody) immunity: P. downsi-binding antibodies in G. fortis adults rise with exposure; mothers at parasitised nests have higher antibody levels (Huber et al. 2010; Koop et al. 2013). Nestling antibody responses are weak (Koop 2013; Knutie 2016 found no significant antibody response in either finches or mockingbirds), consistent with immature nestling immunity. Innate/inflammatory responses at feeding wounds and in nares are an obvious a priori prediction of skin-feeding larvae.
2. Tolerance - energy compensation: Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate the fly via increased parental provisioning that compensates for energy/blood lost (Knutie et al. 2016 Ecology; McNew et al. 2019 Proc B - tolerance varies with annual rainfall/food). Finches show limited compensation (Knutie 2016; green warbler-finch, 2023 J Ornithol). This implies host processes of nutrient/energy homeostasis, growth under nutritional stress, erythropoiesis/haemoglobin recovery, and wound repair.
3. Behavioural defences: preening of larvae by parents (Koop 2013), nestling activity/behavioural type predicts naris damage (Katsis et al. 2021), begging intensity changes in parasitised nests (O'Connor et al. 2014), self-fumigation with permethrin-treated cotton and incorporation of cigarette butts in urban nests (Knutie et al. 2014; later studies), preening with Psidium galapageium leaves that repel P. downsi (Cimadom et al. 2016, reviewed in Bush & Clayton 2018).
4. Beak/naris developmental consequences: larval feeding inside the developing beak causes permanent naris enlargement, implying tissue repair/keratinisation/craniofacial development processes in survivors.

## Mechanism categories used for scoring
- Score 5: immune/defense response, adaptive and B-cell/antibody processes, inflammatory response, wound healing/response to wounding, erythrocyte differentiation (anaemia recovery).
- Score 4: core components - cytokine production/regulation (IL-6, TNF, IL-1, IL-8, chemokines, IL-17), NF-kB/TLR/inflammasome/complement, T-cell activation, haemopoiesis, hypoxia response, iron homeostasis, coagulation/platelet (thrombocyte) activation, leukocyte adhesion, skin/epidermis/keratinocyte differentiation, energy homeostasis.
- Score 3: supporting/downstream - angiogenesis and endothelial processes in wound repair, ECM/collagen/basement membrane, epithelial proliferation/migration, phagocytosis, chemotaxis, antibacterial defence (secondary wound infection), oxidative stress and detoxification, nutrient/starvation responses, glucose homeostasis/gluconeogenesis, IGF/insulin signalling, JAK-STAT/TGF-beta signalling, pain perception, response to stress, palate (roof of mouth) development, intermediate filament (keratin) organisation.
- Score 2: weak/speculative - general metabolism, lipid storage/metabolism, TOR/autophagy, bone/cartilage/BMP (beak), circadian rhythm (nocturnal feeding), learning/cognition (behavioural defences), xenobiotic responses (nest fumigants), hormone signalling, zinc, apoptosis regulation, generic MAPK/PI3K.
- Score 1: no specific link (neural/synaptic, cell cycle, transcription/translation machinery, organelle biology, germ cells, development of unrelated organs, antiviral responses).

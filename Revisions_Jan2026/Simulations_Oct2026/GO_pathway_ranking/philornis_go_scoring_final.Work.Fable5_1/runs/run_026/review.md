# Literature review: host responses to Philornis downsi (run_026)

## What the larvae do to the host
- Adult flies are free-living; females oviposit in nests (and sometimes in nestling nares). First-instar larvae feed inside the nares/nasal cavity on keratin, blood and tissue; 2nd/3rd instars live in the nest base and emerge at night to feed externally on blood and tissue of nestlings and sometimes brooding females (Fessl et al. 2006 Parasitology; Kleindorfer & Sulloway 2016; O'Connor et al. 2010 Oryx video study).
- Documented pathology: blood loss and anaemia (reduced haemoglobin, elevated immature red blood cell counts, i.e., a regenerative/erythropoietic response), reduced growth and mass, wounds/lesions in nares, ears and skin, naris/beak malformation (sometimes loss of the upper nasal cavity), and in-nest mortality averaging ~55% and reaching 100% in some years (Dudaniec et al. 2006; Fessl et al. 2006; Huber 2008; Galligan & Kleindorfer 2009; Koop et al. 2011; Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016; Knutie et al. 2016).
- Nest climate/blood quality studies show larvae depend on nestling blood composition (Frontiers Ecol Evol 2024).

## Host defences documented or proposed
1. Acquired (antibody-mediated) immunity: P. downsi-binding antibodies (IgY) in adult medium ground finches, higher in brooding females and in mothers of parasitized nests; at least eight finch species show antibody responses; nestlings also assayed. The response does not rescue fledging success in finches (Huber et al. 2010 PLoS ONE; Koop et al. 2013 Ecol Evol; NSF grant summary). Mockingbirds did not mount a measurable antibody response yet tolerate the parasite (Knutie et al. 2016).
2. Innate/inflammatory and wound responses: feeding lesions in nares, ear canal and skin imply inflammation, haemostasis, wound healing/re-epithelialization and risk of secondary bacterial infection; keratin consumption in the nares points to keratinocyte/epidermal repair and craniofacial (beak) tissue regeneration (Galligan & Kleindorfer 2009; Kleindorfer et al. 2019 Proc B).
3. Haematological compensation: reduced haemoglobin with raised immature erythrocyte counts (Dudaniec et al. 2006) indicates compensatory erythropoiesis; anaemia implies hypoxia signalling, iron handling and coagulation as a priori relevant processes.
4. Tolerance via energetics: Galapagos mockingbirds tolerate P. downsi by increased begging and parental provisioning that compensates for energy/blood lost to larvae; tolerance collapses in dry years; finches generally do not compensate (Knutie et al. 2016 Ecology; McNew et al. 2019 Proc B; Heyer et al. 2023 J Ornithol). Larger nestling body size and lower surface-area:volume may aid withstanding nocturnal feeding (thermoregulation/energy balance).
5. Behavioural defences: nestlings and female parents preen and remove/consume larvae, probe nares, and nestlings stand on nest mates to avoid the nest base; these do not clearly improve fledging success (O'Connor et al. 2010; Koop et al. 2013; Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016).
6. Stress physiology: no corticosterone elevation detected in breeding females, but a nestling stress response was proposed (Knutie et al. 2013 Gen Comp Endocrinol).
7. Other proposed effects: altered gut microbiota of parasitized nestlings (Knutie et al. 2018 Ecosphere); nocturnal larval feeding (circadian context); beak/naris morphology changes affecting song and foraging (Kleindorfer et al. 2019).

## How this was used for scoring
- 5: immune/defense/inflammatory/adaptive immune responses, wound healing and response to wounding, erythrocyte differentiation (documented antibody response, lesions, and regenerative anaemia).
- 4: core components of those mechanisms: cytokine production/signalling (IL-6, IL-8, IL-1, TNF, chemokines), NF-kB, B-cell activation/differentiation/proliferation/BCR signalling, complement, leukocyte adhesion and chemotaxis, haemopoiesis, iron homeostasis, hypoxia response, coagulation/platelet activation, ECM/collagen organization, skin/epidermis development and keratinocyte differentiation, energy homeostasis.
- 3: plausible supporting processes: TLR/LPS and antibacterial responses (secondary infection), T-cell processes, JAK-STAT, IFN-gamma, angiogenesis and epithelial/endothelial proliferation or migration, TGF-beta/EGF/FGF/VEGF signalling, oxidative stress and ROS, glucose/lipid/energy metabolism, starvation and nutrient responses, insulin/IGF signalling, thermogenesis, pain perception, steroid hormone signalling, roof-of-mouth and cartilage development (naris/beak), tight junctions and barrier formation.
- 2: weak or indirect links (general adhesion/migration, apoptosis, MAPK/JNK, autophagy, antiviral immunity, circadian rhythm, bone development, hormone responses, hearing).
- 1: no specific link (housekeeping transcription/translation/RNA processing, cell cycle, neuronal/synaptic, developmental patterning, organelle transport, etc.).

# Run 016 – Literature review: host responses to *Philornis downsi*

## What the larvae do
- *P. downsi* is an invasive nest fly; adults are free-living, while the larvae live in the nest material and feed on the blood and tissue of nestlings (and sometimes brooding females). Second and third instars feed externally. Early instars get into the nares, which causes naris/beak deformation in surviving young.
- The result is blood loss and anemia (lower hemoglobin), tissue wounds, slower growth and high nestling mortality, often 50–100% in Darwin's finches.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
1. **Resistance via the adaptive immune system.** Finches make *P. downsi*-specific IgY antibodies. Experimental parasite removal showed the antibody response is caused by the parasite, and higher antibody levels in females go with fewer parasites. Nestlings make little antibody, which points to B-cell/antibody production, complement and inflammation as resistance mechanisms.
2. **Tolerance.** Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate the fly: their nestling survival is barely reduced despite heavy loads. Proposed tolerance routes are faster replacement of lost blood (erythropoiesis, iron homeostasis), compensatory growth and energy metabolism, more begging and parental provisioning, and environment-dependent (rainfall/food) tolerance.
3. **Tissue repair and hemostasis.** These are proposed responses to larval feeding wounds: wound healing, coagulation/platelets, keratinocyte/epidermal repair, collagen/ECM and angiogenesis. Secondary bacterial infection of wounds and oxidative stress are possible downstream effects.
4. **Behavior.** Nestling movement and begging are linked to naris deformation. Video studies record how hosts and parasites interact in the nest. Self-medication / nest fumigation (e.g., *Psidium galapageium* leaves) has been proposed. Behavioral GO terms (learning, pain perception) were scored as weak to moderate.

## Scoring logic
- 5: wound healing/response to wounding, adaptive immune/immune response, hemopoiesis and erythrocyte differentiation.
- 4: hemostasis, B-cell/antibody, complement, inflammation, innate immune, cytokine production, T-cell help, iron homeostasis, skin/epidermis.
- 3: cytokine signaling (TNF, IL-1/6/17, NF-kB, JAK-STAT), leukocyte chemotaxis/phagocytosis, antibacterial defense, ECM/collagen/angiogenesis, hypoxia/oxidative stress, growth/energy/insulin, pain, blood pressure.
- 2: broad signaling, metabolism, development and antiviral terms with only indirect links.
- 1: everything else.

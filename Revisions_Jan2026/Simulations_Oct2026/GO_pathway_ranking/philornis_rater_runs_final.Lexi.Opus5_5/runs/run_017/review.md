# Literature review: host responses to Philornis downsi (run_017)

## What the larvae do
- First-instar larvae live in nestling nares. Second- and third-instar larvae hide in nest material and come out at night to feed on the blood and tissue of nestlings, and sometimes of brooding females. This causes lesions, enlarged nares and beak deformation, which in turn affects song. It also causes blood loss (lower haemoglobin), slower growth and high nestling mortality.
- Parasitized nestlings have lower gut bacterial richness. Diet protein (δ15N) correlates negatively with parasite load, and nestlings with lower blood C:N survive longer.
- Corticosterone and haematocrit of adult female finches were not changed by parasitism in one experiment.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Resistance (adaptive immunity):** Adults, especially brooding females, produce P. downsi-specific antibodies (IgY). Nestlings produce antibodies as well. Maternal antibody transfer has been proposed.
- **Local inflammation:** Bites cause swelling and inflammation at the skin. Systemic immune responses have been proposed but not measured.
- **Tolerance:** Mockingbirds tolerate the parasite, and tolerance varies with rainfall and food. The proposed mechanism is compensating for blood and resource loss through parental provisioning, nestling size and nutrition. Haemoglobin loss is the main physiological cost.
- **Behavior:** Finches incorporate permethrin-treated cotton into nests ("self-fumigation"). Parasitized nestlings beg more. Female brooding and nest attendance affect nest microclimate.

## Scoring rationale
- **5:** inflammatory response; adaptive immune response.
- **4:** innate and general immune or defense response; B-cell and antibody components; complement; wound healing; coagulation; erythropoiesis and haematopoiesis; iron homeostasis (blood-loss tolerance).
- **3:** cytokine and NF-kB signaling, T cells, chemotaxis, platelets, skin and keratinocyte repair, collagen, hypoxia, starvation and nutrient or energy homeostasis, oxidative stress and respiratory burst, pain perception, IGF growth signaling.
- **2:** peripheral immune pathways (TLR, LPS, antibacterial, phagocytosis, JAK-STAT), angiogenesis and ECM, metabolic and hormone signaling, bone and skeletal terms (beak damage), circadian (nocturnal larval feeding), xenobiotic response, thermogenesis.
- **1:** everything else.

# Review: host responses to *Philornis downsi* in Galápagos land birds (run_030)

Note: my web search budget ran out after three searches, so this review is short. It draws on the sources I found plus general biological knowledge. I did not use any transcriptomic or genomic study of Galápagos birds.

## What the larvae do to hosts
- The fly lays its eggs in nests. Early-instar larvae live in the nestlings' nares (nostrils) and ear canals. Later instars move to the nest base and feed on nestlings' blood and tissue, mostly at night (dusk and dawn). Since 2012, larvae have also been found in nests during incubation.
- Effects on nestlings: blood loss and anaemia (lower haemoglobin), skin and tissue wounds, slower growth, deformed nares and beaks (which may later alter song), and high nestling mortality. The fly is described as the biggest threat to Galápagos land birds.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Resistance (immune):** brooding females and nestlings make *P. downsi*-specific antibodies (IgY), and higher antibody levels go with fewer larvae. Inflammation and wound repair at feeding sites are implied.
- **Tolerance:** Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate the parasite, and food availability or environmental variation shapes tolerance. Proposed routes are compensating for blood loss (making red blood cells, iron balance), keeping growth going through better nutrition or metabolism, and clotting.
- **Behavioral:** females brood standing upright in parasitized nests, nestlings change how they beg, and preening or anti-parasite behavior may help. Self-fumigation with repellent material is known in other systems. Nestling behavior predicts naris deformation.

# Literature review: host responses to *Philornis downsi* (run_021)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- First-instar larvae live in the nares and ear canals of nestlings. Second- and third-instar larvae stay in the nest base and come out at night to feed on nestling blood and tissue. They also feed on brooding females.
- The consequences are blood loss (parasitized nestlings have lower hemoglobin), tissue damage and lesions, and enlarged or deformed nares and beaks, which later affect song. Nestlings are smaller and grow less, and mortality is high (up to 100%). Effects depend on rainfall and food supply.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Antibody-mediated (IgY) resistance:** female medium ground finches produce *P. downsi*-specific antibodies, and higher titres go with fewer parasites in the nest. Nestlings show no detectable response (Koop et al. 2013).
- **Tolerance through energy compensation:** parasitized mockingbird nestlings beg more, and their parents provision more, which offsets the damage. Finches show no increase in begging, provisioning or plasma glucose (Knutie et al. 2016). Tolerance varies with the year's environment.
- **Behavioral defenses:** occasional removal of larvae or picking at them, and proposed self-fumigation by rubbing *Psidium* (guava) leaves on feathers. Females also change how long they brood.
- **No effect on maternal stress:** female corticosterone and condition do not change with parasitism.
- **Proposed physiological responses that follow from the damage:** hemostasis and coagulation (blood feeding), erythropoiesis and iron homeostasis (anemia), inflammation and leukocyte recruitment at feeding sites, wound healing, and re-epithelialization of skin and the naris.

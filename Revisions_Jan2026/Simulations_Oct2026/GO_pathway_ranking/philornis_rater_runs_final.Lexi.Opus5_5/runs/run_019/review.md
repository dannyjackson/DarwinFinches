# Literature review: host responses to Philornis downsi (run_019)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- P. downsi adults lay eggs in nests; early-instar larvae live in nestlings' nares/ear canals, and later instars live in nest material and feed externally on nestlings' blood and tissue at night. The result is blood loss, open wounds, lower hemoglobin, slower growth and high nestling mortality (often >50%, and up to 100% in some nests and years). Sources: Fessl & Tebbich 2002; Dudaniec & Kleindorfer 2006 review (Emu); Dudaniec et al. on haemoglobin.
- Tissue damage: naris/beak deformation in survivors changes vocal (song) performance and mating signals (Kleindorfer et al. 2019).
- Philornis myiasis more generally causes subcutaneous lesions with local inflammation, leukocyte infiltration and secondary infection (Uhazy & Arendt 1986, J Wildl Dis).

## Documented or proposed host defenses
- **Resistance (adaptive/humoral):** finches produce parasite-specific IgY antibodies; experimental parasite exposure raises the antibody response in nestlings and adults, and higher antibody titres were linked to fewer parasites (Huber et al. 2010 PLoS ONE; Koop et al. 2013).
- **Tolerance:** Galápagos mockingbird nestlings tolerate the fly. They beg more and get fed more, which offsets the blood loss. Finches showed no comparable compensation, and their plasma glucose did not rise significantly (Knutie et al. 2016 Oecologia). Food supply and resources affect resistance and tolerance (Knutie, food-supplementation studies). Plausible physiological routes are replacing lost red cells (erythropoiesis, iron balance), clotting and wound repair.
- **Behavioral:** parents brood, sanitize the nest and preen; finches will take up permethrin-treated cotton when it is offered ("self-fumigation", Knutie et al. 2014). Begging intensity in parasitized nests is not an honest signal of need.

## How this guided the scoring
- 5: antibody/B-cell adaptive immunity, immune response, inflammation, wound healing, blood clotting, and red-cell production (hemopoiesis).
- 4: innate/complement/T-cell processes, platelets, iron homeostasis, skin/epidermis repair.
- 3: cytokine/NF-kB/JAK-STAT signaling, phagocytosis, angiogenesis/ECM repair, response to hypoxia and oxidative stress, energy/glucose homeostasis, starvation response, pain.
- 2: speculative links such as growth signaling, beak/skeletal development, antiviral pathways, hormone, learning/memory and thermoregulation.
- 1: everything else.

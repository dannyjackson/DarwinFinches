# Literature review: host responses to Philornis downsi (run_025)

## What the larvae do
- Larvae of the avian vampire fly live in nests. They feed on the blood and tissue of nestlings (and also brooding females), and early instars enter the nares. The result is blood loss (lower hemoglobin/anemia), slower growth, high nestling mortality, and lasting deformation of the beak and naris, which changes song in adults.
  - https://knowablemagazine.org/article/living-world/2018/darwins-finches-fall-prey-blood-sucking-parasite
  - https://galapagosconservation.org.uk/?p=8221
  - https://www.sciencenews.org/article/parasites-ruin-songs-darwin-finches-galapagos-islands
  - https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/conservation-science/articles/10.3389/fcosc.2025.1591266/full

## Host defenses documented or proposed
1. **Resistance through an adaptive/humoral immune response.** Finches make P. downsi-specific IgY antibodies, and the antibody response is linked to fewer parasites (Huber et al. 2010 PLoS ONE; Koop et al. 2013).
   - https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3930052/
   - https://doaj.org/article/c0c1dd23b9f64ef2bc80d5833593b012
   - Proposed supporting processes: inflammation, complement, B/T-cell activation, cytokine signaling and leukocyte recruitment at feeding sites.
2. **Tolerance (limiting the damage from blood loss).** Tolerance depends on resources: in mockingbirds, food supplementation and wet years raised tolerance (Knutie et al. 2016).
   - https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.n11h95s
   - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6408884
   - Mechanisms predicted from blood feeding: hemostasis/coagulation, platelet function, replacing lost blood (hematopoiesis, erythropoiesis, iron homeostasis), response to hypoxia, wound healing and skin/keratin repair, energy and nutrient homeostasis, and growth signaling.
3. **Stress physiology.** Work on corticosterone in breeding finches and nestlings found little or mixed effect, so stress physiology gets only moderate support.
   - https://agris.fao.org/search/fr/records/65df89250f3e94b9e5d97be8
   - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4383185
4. **Behavior.** Proposed behavioral defenses include female in-nest attendance and brooding, changes in parental provisioning and nestling begging, and nest-material choice. Finches readily take up permethrin-treated cotton, and use of plant material such as Psidium leaves has been proposed.
   - https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7754105/
   - https://researchnow.flinders.edu.au/en/publications/darwins-finch-begging-intensity-does-not-honestly-signal-need-in-/
   - https://sicb.org/abstracts/darwins-finches-combat-introduced-nest-parasites-with-fumigated-cotton
   - https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24801182

## How the scores were assigned
- **5:** general and adaptive immune response, the documented antibody-mediated resistance.
- **4:** innate and inflammatory defense, complement, B-cell terms, coagulation and platelets, wounding and wound healing, hematopoiesis and erythropoiesis, iron homeostasis.
- **3:** T-cell and cytokine signaling, hypoxia, skin and extracellular-matrix repair, energy and nutrient homeostasis, IGF signaling, steroid-hormone signaling, pain perception.
- **2:** weak links, such as general pattern-recognition and NF-kB signaling, antibacterial defense (secondary wound infection), oxidative stress, angiogenesis, xenobiotic response, learning and memory, thermoregulation, beak and bone tissue.
- **1:** everything else, including antiviral terms.

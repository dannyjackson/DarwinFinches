# Run 011: Literature review of P. downsi host responses, used to score GO terms

## What the larvae do to hosts
- First-instar larvae live in the nestlings' nares. Second- and third-instar larvae live in the nest base and feed at night on blood and tissue from exposed skin. The result is blood loss (hemoglobin falls about 0.8 g/dL per larva), lower body mass and feather growth, and high nestling mortality, often 100% in parasitized nests.
- The tissue damage enlarges the naris and deforms the beak. Surviving birds can sing altered songs, which may affect mate choice and hybridization.
- Adults are mostly unaffected. Breeding females in parasitized nests show stress and corticosterone effects.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Resistance (adaptive immunity):** Female medium ground finches produce P. downsi-specific IgY antibodies. Antibody levels rise with parasitism and are negatively related to parasite load. There is little evidence that nestlings make antibodies. Inflammation and innate responses at feeding wounds are predicted from general myiasis and ectoparasite pathology.
- **Tolerance:** Compensating for blood loss through erythropoiesis, iron and heme supply, and hemostasis/coagulation against blood feeding. Repairing tissue (wound healing, angiogenesis, re-epithelialization, keratinization of beak and skin). Galápagos mockingbirds show energy compensation: parasitized nestlings beg more and parents feed them more. Finches showed no such increase and no rise in plasma glucose.
- **Behavior:** Females brood less and probe nest material. Females and nestlings preen and eat larvae. Nestlings stand on their siblings, which may relate to nociception. Birds may self-anoint with guava leaves. Hosts may adjust breeding timing and choose particular nest heights.

## How these mechanisms mapped to scores
- **Score 5:** immune response, adaptive immune response, B cell activation.
- **Score 4:** B cell proliferation, B cell receptor signaling, B cell differentiation, T cell activation, innate immune response, defense response, inflammatory response, wounding and wound healing, coagulation and platelet (thrombocyte) terms, complement, erythropoiesis and hemopoiesis, iron homeostasis, regulation of immune processes.
- **Score 3:** cytokine, NF-kB, toll-like receptor, phagocytosis and chemotaxis terms, hypoxia, blood pressure, angiogenesis, ECM and collagen, TGF-beta, skin and keratinocyte terms, glucose and energy homeostasis, IGF signaling, pain perception, ossification and bone (beak).

- **Score 1:** everything else.

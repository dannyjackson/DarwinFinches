# Literature review: Philornis downsi and host responses (run_003)

## What the larvae do
- Adult flies lay eggs in nests. The larvae feed on the blood and tissue of nestlings, and sometimes of brooding females. Early instars live in the nares and the later instars feed on the outside of the body.
- Effects documented in nestlings: blood loss, anemia and lower haemoglobin; wounds and deformation of the nasal openings (beak/naris); poorer growth; and high mortality (16-100% across host species). The fly has contributed to declines in several finch populations, including the small tree finch and the warbler finch.
- Naris deformation that lasts into adulthood changes vocal performance and song, which affects mating signals.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Resistance (adaptive/humoral immunity):** in medium ground finches, parasite-specific IgY antibody responses are induced by the larvae and are associated with fewer larvae (Huber et al. 2010; Koop et al. 2013).
- **Tolerance:** nestlings compensate for blood loss and recover haemoglobin through red-cell production. Tolerance depends on parental provisioning and food, and varies with rainfall from year to year (Knutie et al. 2016, 2017). Urban food resources can also rescue nestlings.
- **Wound response, inflammation and haemostasis:** these are a strong a priori prediction from tissue and blood feeding (wound healing, coagulation, platelet activity, inflammation, iron homeostasis). Responses to hypoxia and oxidative stress follow from the anemia.
- **Behavioral:** parents provision more food in parasitized nests, and nestling begging may not honestly signal need. Adults self-fumigate by adding insecticide-treated cotton or other materials to nests. Nest-building and preening behavior have also been proposed. These behaviors have little direct GO-process equivalent.
- **Developmental and metabolic support:** growth signaling (insulin/IGF), energy and nutrient homeostasis, and skin and epidermal repair are supporting processes.

## Scoring logic
- **5:** adaptive immune response, response to wounding, wound healing, erythrocyte differentiation.
- **4:** innate and general immune responses, B-cell processes, inflammation, haematopoiesis, iron homeostasis, coagulation and platelets, complement, cytokine responses.
- **3:** cytokine, NF-kB and T-cell signaling; hypoxia and oxidative stress; skin, extracellular matrix and angiogenesis repair; growth and nutrient-stress pathways.
- **2:** antiviral and antibacterial responses; bone and ossification (beak); hormones and behavior/learning; general metabolism.
- **1:** everything else.

# Run 018: literature review used for a priori GO scoring (host response to *Philornis downsi*)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- *P. downsi* adults lay eggs in nests of Galápagos land birds. Early-instar larvae live in the nestlings' nares and ear canals. Later instars live in the nest base and feed on nestling blood and tissue at night. The results are blood loss (lower haemoglobin), skin lesions and tissue damage, enlarged or deformed nares and beaks, slower growth, and very high nestling mortality. Brooding females can also be bitten.
  - https://www.darwininitiative.org.uk/documents/DAR15005/20871/15-005%20Philornis%20factsheet%202009.pdf
  - https://galapagosconservation.org.uk/?p=8221
  - https://discovermagazine.com/planet-earth/parasites-are-destroying-the-beaks-of-darwins-famous-finches
  - https://bmczool.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s40850-016-0003-9/tables/1
- Physiological costs reported include reduced haemoglobin and growth, and changes in glucose. Effects on corticosterone (stress) have been tested in breeding finches.
  - https://agris.fao.org/search/fr/records/65df89250f3e94b9e5d97be8

## Host defences documented or proposed
1. **Resistance (immune):** Medium ground finches mount a parasite-specific IgY antibody response, and higher antibody titres are associated with fewer larvae. Inflammatory and skin-site responses are proposed, as is interference with larval feeding (for example, clotting).
   - Huber et al. 2010, PLoS ONE: https://doaj.org/article/c0c1dd23b9f64ef2bc80d5833593b012
   - Koop et al., experimental immune response: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3930052/
2. **Tolerance:** Galápagos mockingbirds carry similar larval loads but have little fitness cost. They compensate through blood and haemoglobin recovery (erythropoiesis) and better energy and glucose homeostasis, helped by parental provisioning.
   - https://darwin.biology.utah.edu/PDFs/126.pdf
   - https://docta.ucm.es/entities/publication/70770688-559c-41e2-ba8b-ead6e9e97f7d/full
3. **Behavioural:** Parents change how they provision and brood, nestlings change begging, and in urban areas a human-food diet may rescue nestlings (resource-based tolerance). Gut microbiota may influence immunity.
   - https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10188583/
   - https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC7754105/
   - https://researchnow.flinders.edu.au/en/publications/darwins-finch-begging-intensity-does-not-honestly-signal-need-in-/
   - https://research.usfq.edu.ec/en/publications/urban-living-can-rescue-darwins-finches-from-the-lethal-effects-o/
   - https://par.nsf.gov/biblio/10491500

## How the scores map to mechanisms

- **4:** T-cell and innate immunity, cytokines and NF-kB, complement, platelets, iron homeostasis, phagocytosis, and epidermis and keratinocyte repair.

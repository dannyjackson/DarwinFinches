# Literature review: host responses to Philornis downsi (run_026)

## What the larvae do
- First instars live in nestlings' nares and ear canals. Second and third instars hide in the nest base and come out at night to feed on blood and tissue (semi-hematophagous).
- Effects on nestlings: blood loss and anemia (lower hemoglobin), open wounds, damage to the beak and keratin, and enlarged or deformed nares that persist into adulthood. Infested nestlings grow less and die at high rates (16-100% mortality across hosts). Incubating females can also be parasitized.
- Proposed effect: depletion of nestling protein.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
1. **Adaptive immunity / antibodies (resistance).** Finches tested during nesting had higher P. downsi-specific IgY than birds tested before nesting (Huber et al. 2010; Koop et al. 2013). Antibody levels in nestlings were low.
2. **Tolerance.** Galápagos mockingbirds fledge normally at fly loads similar to finches. Their parasitized nestlings had lower hemoglobin and lower glucose. This points to physiological tolerance of blood loss: recovering red cells and hemoglobin (erythropoiesis, iron use), hemostasis, and tissue and wound repair.
3. **Behavioral and parental compensation.** Parasitized mockingbird nestlings begged more and their parents fed them more. Finch begging does not honestly signal need in parasitized nests. Diet and protein intake are proposed to affect tolerance.
4. **Nest-related behavior.** Birds take up permethrin-treated cotton into their nests (self-fumigation). Nest microclimate and brooding affect how the larvae develop.
5. **General biology (prediction, not shown in Philornis).** Ectoparasite feeding sites are expected to trigger innate and inflammatory responses: cytokines, complement, leukocyte recruitment and the respiratory burst. Secondary skin repair is also expected (keratinocytes, collagen, angiogenesis).

## How scores were assigned
- **5:** immune response, adaptive immune response, response to wounding.
- **4:** core immune, inflammatory and B-cell processes, complement, coagulation and thrombocytes, erythropoiesis and iron homeostasis, wound healing.
- **3:** T-cell and cytokine signaling, NF-kB, chemotaxis, skin and ECM repair, angiogenesis, hypoxia, blood pressure, glucose homeostasis, pain.
- **2:** bacterial and TLR signaling, oxidative stress, growth factors, nutrition and starvation, thermogenesis, learning, circadian rhythm, hearing.
- **1:** everything else.

## Source excluded
Results from transcriptomic or genomic studies of Galápagos birds were excluded on purpose, including bioRxiv 2023.03.06.531275. This also covers the gene-expression findings in McNew et al. 2024 (Global Change Biology, "Urban living can rescue Darwin's finches..."). That abstract came up in the search, but its results were not used.

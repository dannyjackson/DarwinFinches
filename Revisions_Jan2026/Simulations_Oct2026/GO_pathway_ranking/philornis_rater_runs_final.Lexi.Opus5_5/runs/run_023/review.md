# Philornis downsi host-response review (run_023)

## What the larvae do
- Adult flies lay eggs in nests; larvae of the first instar live in the nestlings' nares (nostrils) and later instars live in nest material. They come out at night to feed on the blood and tissue of nestlings, and sometimes of brooding females.
- Effects: blood loss with lower haemoglobin (anaemia), open wounds and tissue damage, enlarged nares and beak deformation that persist into adulthood, slower growth or lower mass, and very high nestling mortality (often >50%, up to 100% in some species and years).

## Documented or proposed host defenses
1. **Resistance: antibody (IgY) response.** Nestlings and adults (medium ground finch) make P. downsi-specific antibodies. Antibody levels go up when parasites are present and are linked to fewer parasites. This is an adaptive/B-cell humoral response (Koop et al. 2013; Huber et al. 2010).
2. **Inflammatory and innate responses to feeding wounds** (proposed): inflammation, complement and cytokine signalling, leukocyte recruitment, and defense against secondary bacterial infection of wounds.
3. **Tolerance.** Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate the parasite: parents raise their provisioning rate, so nestling growth and survival hold up despite parasitism (Knutie et al. 2016). Proposed physiological tolerance includes replacing lost blood (haematopoiesis, erythropoiesis, iron homeostasis), stopping bleeding (coagulation, platelets), wound repair (re-epithelialisation, ECM and collagen, angiogenesis), and coping with hypoxia, oxidative stress and nutritional or energetic stress. Studies in warbler finches and others look at parental provisioning and nestling growth.
4. **Behavioural.** Finches add permethrin-treated cotton to nests (self-fumigation; Knutie et al. 2014). Video studies record nestling movement or begging and parental behaviour in parasitised nests (O'Connor et al.). Begging is not an honest signal of need in parasitised nests. Genetic links to behaviour are speculative, so neural and behaviour terms score 1–2.

## Scoring logic
- 5: adaptive immune response, B cell activation, response to wounding, wound healing.
- 4: core humoral, innate and inflammatory immunity; coagulation and platelets; haematopoiesis, erythropoiesis and iron homeostasis.
- 3: cytokine, NF-kB, TLR and JAK-STAT signalling; antibacterial defense; angiogenesis; ECM, skin and keratinocyte repair; hypoxia and oxidative stress; starvation and energy homeostasis.
- 2: metabolism, growth and hormone, bone and beak development, behaviour/cognition, antiviral and xenobiotic terms.
- 1: generic housekeeping, cell-cycle, RNA, neural-development and other unrelated terms.
- I did not use any genomic or transcriptomic studies of Galápagos birds.

# Run 013: literature review of host responses to *Philornis downsi* (a priori GO scoring)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- First-instar larvae get into the nestlings' nares. Second- and third-instar larvae live in the nest base and feed on the nestlings' blood and tissue, mostly at night. Incubating and brooding females can also be bitten.
- The effects on nestlings are severe:
  - Hemoglobin falls (about 28% on average, roughly 0.8 g/dL per larva).
  - Fatal blood loss can occur.
  - Mass gain and feather growth drop.
  - Nares become enlarged and the beak deformed. The damage persists into adulthood and changes song and mating signals.
  - In-nest mortality is high (mean about 55%, up to 100%).
- Adult females showed no corticosterone or hematocrit response to experimental parasite removal.

## Documented or proposed host defenses
- **Resistance (adaptive immunity):** Adult female medium ground finches made *P. downsi*-binding IgY antibodies, with higher titres at parasitized nests. Nestlings of finches and mockingbirds showed no measurable antibody response. Maternal antibodies have been proposed as a source of resistance in nestlings.
- **Tolerance:**
  - Galápagos mockingbirds fledge normally despite heavy infestation.
  - Parasitized mockingbird nestlings beg more and their parents provision more.
  - Parasitized mockingbird nestlings have higher plasma glucose.
  - Faster blood replacement (erythropoiesis) is a proposed but untested mechanism.
  - Food and condition may modulate tolerance.
- **Behavioral defenses:**
  - Nestlings stand on their siblings and preen off or eat larvae.
  - Females probe the nest base, preen nestlings, and brood less at parasitized nests (which changes nest temperature).
  - Proposed but untested: self-fumigation (use of treated cotton or guava leaves), nest site and nest size, and hybridization (hybrids carried fewer parasites).
- **Proposed physiological correlates:**
  - Hemostasis and coagulation at bite sites.
  - Inflammation, wound healing, and re-epithelialization of skin and naris tissue.
  - Iron and hypoxia responses to anemia.
  - Glucose and energy homeostasis, and growth under nutritional stress.
  - Thermoregulation.

## How the scores map onto these mechanisms

- **4:** innate immunity and inflammation, cytokine production, complement, platelets, hemopoiesis, iron homeostasis, hypoxia response, T-cell activation.
- **3:** cytokine and NF-kB pathways, leukocyte chemotaxis and phagocytosis, skin, keratinocyte and ECM repair, angiogenesis, oxidative stress, glucose and energy homeostasis, starvation response, IGF signaling, blood pressure, pain perception, thermogenesis.

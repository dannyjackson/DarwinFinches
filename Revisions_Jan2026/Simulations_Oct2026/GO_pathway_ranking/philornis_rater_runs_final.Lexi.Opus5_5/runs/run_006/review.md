# Literature review: host responses to Philornis downsi (run_006)

## What the larvae do
- P. downsi adults lay eggs in nests; larvae feed on nestlings' (and brooding females') blood and tissue. Early instars live in the nares/beak and cause tissue damage and permanent beak/naris deformation; later instars feed externally on blood at night.
- Effects: blood loss and lower hemoglobin, reduced growth, high nestling mortality (often near-total in heavily infested nests), deformed nares that later alter song.
- Wider myiasis literature on Philornis spp.: local inflammation, cutaneous lesions, anemia.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
1. **Resistance via immunity**: finches mount a P. downsi-specific antibody (IgY) response. In females it relates to fewer parasites (Huber et al. 2010; Koop et al. 2013). Inflammatory and innate responses at feeding sites are a strong prediction.
2. **Tolerance**: compensating for blood loss (hemoglobin recovery/erythropoiesis, iron homeostasis, hemostasis). Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate parasitism by raising parental provisioning (Knutie et al. 2016). Repairing tissue/wounds at the beak and nares.
3. **Behavioral**: nestling begging and parental feeding effort (begging is not an honest signal of need in parasitised nests); host-parasite behavior seen on nest video; self-fumigation by adding treated cotton to nests (Knutie et al. 2014); proposed preening and nest sanitation.
4. **Physiological stress/energetics**: corticosterone measured in breeding finches under fly exposure; costs to growth and energy budget.

## Scoring rationale
- 5: immune response, adaptive/antibody (B cell) response, inflammation, wounding/wound healing.
- 4: innate immunity, T cell help, cytokine responses, hematopoiesis/erythropoiesis, iron homeostasis, coagulation.
- 3: specific cytokine/NF-kB/TLR pathways, phagocytosis/complement, oxidative stress, angiogenesis/ECM/skin/keratinocyte (tissue repair), hormone/nutrient/energy responses, platelet function, response to stress.
- 2: general signalling, proliferation, apoptosis, migration/adhesion, bone/cartilage (beak), metabolism, learning/circadian (behavior).
- 1: housekeeping (RNA processing, DNA replication, cilia, synapse, organogenesis, etc.).

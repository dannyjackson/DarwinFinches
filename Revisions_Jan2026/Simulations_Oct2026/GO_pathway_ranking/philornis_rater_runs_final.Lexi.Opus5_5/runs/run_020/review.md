# Review: host responses to Philornis downsi (run_020)

## What the larvae do
- Larvae feed on nestling blood and tissue at night and shelter in the nest base during the day. First instars develop in the nasal cavities and ear canals. During incubation they also feed on brooding females.
- Effects on hosts: reduced hemoglobin (anemia from blood loss), smaller nestlings and slower growth, deformation of the nares and beak (which changes song, a mating signal), and high nestling mortality (up to 100% of a nest). Mortality generally rises with parasite intensity.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Resistance, humoral/adaptive:** adult female medium ground finches produce P. downsi-specific antibodies (IgY), and the response is induced by the parasite. It did not clearly improve nestling survival, and nestlings show little response.
- **Resistance, innate/inflammatory (proposed):** local inflammation, complement and leukocyte recruitment at feeding wounds; possible skin and epithelial barrier defenses.
- **Tolerance:** Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate the parasite: nestlings beg more, parents provision more, and they compensate for blood loss. Proposed tolerance mechanisms are erythropoiesis/hematopoiesis, iron homeostasis, hemostasis/coagulation (larvae feed on blood), wound healing and tissue repair, and dampening of inflammation.
- **Behavioral:** avoidance (nest timing, height and position), rare preening, possible use of repellent plants (guava leaves), and females' brooding and standing behavior.
- **Downstream:** response to hypoxia (from anemia), skeletal and bone development of the beak and naris, growth and energy and nutrient status, and stress and hormonal responses.

## Scoring logic
- **5:** adaptive and humoral immunity (B-cell activation) and response to wounding.
- **4:** immune and defense responses in general, B-cell pathways, innate immunity, inflammation, complement, coagulation, platelet and thrombocyte activation, hematopoiesis and erythropoiesis, iron homeostasis, and wound healing.
- **3:** T-cell and cytokine signaling, NF-kB, TLR, leukocyte chemotaxis, skin and epidermis, collagen and ECM, angiogenesis, hypoxia, pain perception, and bone and skeletal development.
- **2:** antibacterial and antiviral responses (secondary infection), xenobiotic and oxidative stress, growth, nutrient status, hormones, learning and circadian processes.
- **1:** everything else.

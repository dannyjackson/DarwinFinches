## What the larvae do to hosts
- First-instar larvae get into nestlings' nares (and ear canals) and feed on blood and tissue. Later instars live in the nest base and feed externally on nestlings' blood at night.
- The result is blood loss and anaemia (lower haemoglobin), open wounds, deformed or enlarged nasal openings, lower nestling mass and growth, and brood mortality of up to 100%. At least 16 to 21 endemic or native Galápagos landbird taxa are affected.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Resistance (immune):** a parasite-induced antibody response has been demonstrated experimentally in Darwin's finches (PMC3930052). Knutie et al. 2016, which used an IgG ELISA, found no significant antibody response in finches or mockingbirds. Inflammatory and wound responses at bite sites, and haemostasis against blood feeding, are core predictions.
- **Tolerance (physiological):** recovery from blood loss through erythropoiesis and iron homeostasis. Parasitized mockingbird nestlings had lower glucose, so energy and nutrient homeostasis and growth regulation are relevant.
- **Tolerance (behavioral):** Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate the fly through nestling begging and compensatory parental provisioning. Finches generally do not compensate (Knutie et al. 2016). In green warbler-finches, female provisioning fell and nestlings were lighter in high-infestation nests (J. Ornithol. 2023).

# Philornis downsi and host responses: summary of the review used for scoring

## What the parasite does
- Adult flies lay eggs in nests; the larvae feed on the blood and tissue of nestlings (and brooding females). First-instar larvae live in the nares and the later instars feed externally at night. The result is blood loss (lower haemoglobin), open wounds, naris and beak deformation, slower growth, and high nestling mortality.
- Effects on haemoglobin, haematocrit and growth: https://researchnow.flinders.edu.au/en/publications/effects-of-the-introduced-ectoparasite-philornis-downsi-on-haemog/ ; https://iucngisd.org/gisd/species.php?sc=1400 ; https://knowablemagazine.org/article/living-world/2018/darwins-finches-fall-prey-blood-sucking-parasite ; https://galapagosconservation.org.uk/?p=8221

## Host defenses documented or proposed
1. **Resistance (adaptive and humoral immunity):** parasitized finches produce P. downsi-specific IgY antibodies, and higher antibody levels are associated with fewer parasites (Huber et al. 2010; Koop et al. 2013). https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3930052/ ; https://sicb.org/?p=25430
2. **Inflammatory and innate responses to wounds and feeding sites (proposed):** these include cytokine signalling, leukocyte recruitment, complement, and the respiratory burst. Coagulation and thrombocyte (platelet) activity would oppose blood feeding. Wound healing, keratinocyte and epidermal repair, ECM and collagen remodelling, and angiogenesis would repair the tissue damage.
3. **Tolerance:** Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate parasite loads that harm finches. In finches, tolerance varies with annual resources. Both cases are consistent with compensating for blood loss through erythropoiesis and iron homeostasis, and with growth and energy compensation, including increased parental provisioning. https://docta.ucm.es/entities/publication/70770688-559c-41e2-ba8b-ead6e9e97f7d/full ; https://datadryad.org:443/stash/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.n11h95s
4. **Stress physiology:** corticosterone in parasitized breeding finches has been tested experimentally. https://agris.fao.org/search/fr/records/65df89250f3e94b9e5d97be8
5. **Behaviour:** nestlings beg more, and nestling behaviour predicts naris deformation. Video studies of host-parasite interactions exist. Urban finches incorporate permethrin-treated cotton into nests (self-fumigation), which suggests that learning and xenobiotic handling are weak links. https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/nestling-behaviour-predicts-naris-deformation-in-darwins-finches-/ ; https://researchnow.flinders.edu.au/en/publications/video-analysis-of-host-parasite-interactions-in-nests-of-darwins-/ ; https://research.usfq.edu.ec/en/publications/urban-living-can-rescue-darwins-finches-from-the-lethal-effects-o/ ; https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10063829
6. **Secondary effects:** hypoxia and oxidative stress from anaemia and inflammation, pain perception, and a possible bacterial infection of wounds.

## Scoring logic
- **5:** immune response, adaptive immunity, B-cell activation (IgY), response to wounding, and erythrocyte differentiation (blood-loss tolerance).
- **4:** innate, inflammatory and complement responses; coagulation and platelet terms; wound healing; B-cell differentiation and signalling; haemopoiesis; iron homeostasis.
- **3:** cytokine and NF-kB signalling, T-cell terms, phagocytosis, skin and epidermis repair, ECM and angiogenesis, hypoxia, oxidative stress, hormone and stress responses, pain, and energy homeostasis.
- **2:** antibacterial terms, generic tissue-repair signalling, metabolism and nutrient-stress terms, learning, and xenobiotic terms.
- **1:** all other terms, including housekeeping, developmental, neural, antiviral, and the placeholder term GO:0160240.

Transcriptomic and genomic studies of Galápagos birds (for example bioRxiv 2023.03.06.531275) appeared in search results. They were not opened or used.

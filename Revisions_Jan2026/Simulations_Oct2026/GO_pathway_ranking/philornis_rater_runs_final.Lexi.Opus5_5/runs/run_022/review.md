# Review: host responses to Philornis downsi (run_022)

## What the larvae do
- P. downsi adults lay eggs in nests; larvae are semi-hematophagous. Early instars enter nestling nares/ear canals, later instars feed externally on blood and tissue at night.
- Effects: blood loss and reduced hemoglobin (anemia), open wounds and tissue damage, enlarged/deformed nares and beak malformation persisting into adulthood (with effects on song), reduced growth, high nestling mortality; brooding females are also fed upon.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- Resistance: parasite-specific antibody (IgY) responses in finches, higher in adults/females and nestlings with fewer parasites; inflammation and innate immune responses at feeding sites (proposed).
- Tolerance: maintaining growth/survival despite blood loss, via blood replacement (erythropoiesis, iron), hemostasis, wound repair; Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate parasites, and tolerance is resource dependent (food supply, rainfall, parental provisioning).
- Behavioural: parental feeding/begging changes, nest sanitation, self-fumigation of nest material (experimental cotton), nestling movement/defensive responses.

## Scoring logic
5 = antibody/immune response, wound healing, coagulation, erythrocyte production.
4 = B/T cell activation, inflammation and its regulation, thrombocyte activation, hemopoiesis, iron homeostasis, innate immunity.
3 = cytokines, chemotaxis, NF-kB, complement, hypoxia (anemia), skin/keratinocyte/ECM repair, TGF-beta, blood pressure, oxidative stress, IGF growth, pain.
2 = generic signalling of immunity (TLR, JAK-STAT), secondary bacterial infection, angiogenesis, nutrient/starvation and hormone (stress) responses, ossification.
1 = everything else (housekeeping, neural development, meiosis, etc.).

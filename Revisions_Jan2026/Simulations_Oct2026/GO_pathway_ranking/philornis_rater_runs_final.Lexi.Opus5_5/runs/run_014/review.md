# Literature review: Philornis downsi and host responses (run_014)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- First-instar larvae live in nestling nares. Later instars feed on the blood and tissue of nestlings (and brooding females), mostly at night. Hosts lose blood (lower hemoglobin and hematocrit), grow more slowly, and die in large numbers: about 55% of nestlings die on average, and sometimes far more.
- Survivors can have enlarged or deformed nares (beak tissue damage), which changes their song and may affect mate choice and hybridization.
- The larvae leave skin lesions and wounds, which can let secondary infection in.

## Documented or proposed host defenses
- **Resistance (adaptive immunity):** Adult finches, especially the females that brood, produce P. downsi-specific antibodies (IgY) when exposed during nesting. Whether these antibodies protect them is unclear. Swallows that host related nest flies resist them through stronger antibody responses.
- **Resistance (innate):** Inflammatory responses at feeding sites have been proposed. Philornis myiasis in other hosts causes lesions that need tissue repair and wound healing.
- **Tolerance:** Hosts could tolerate the parasite by replacing lost blood (red blood cell production and iron for heme), by stopping bleeding (coagulation and platelets), and by repairing damaged tissue. Mockingbirds tolerate the flies through behavior: parasitized nestlings beg more and parents feed them more. This tolerance breaks down in dry, food-poor years, so energy and nutrition matter. A higher-protein diet is linked to lower parasite loads.
- **Physiological stress:** Stress-hormone (corticosterone) responses, body condition and hematocrit did not differ between females in parasitized and non-parasitized nests. Finches showed no rise in plasma glucose in response to the flies.
- **Behavioral:** Finches rub Psidium galapageium leaves on their feathers, and the leaves repel insects. Nestling preening and standing on nestmates were ineffective. Mockingbird nestlings beg more and their parents compensate with extra feeding.

## How these map to the scores
- **5:** blood-cell production and erythrocyte differentiation; adaptive immunity and B-cell activation (antibodies); inflammatory response; wound healing and response to wounding.
- **4:** coagulation and platelets; iron homeostasis; innate immunity; regulation of inflammation; B-cell differentiation and B-cell receptor signaling; complement activation.
- **3:** cytokines and NF-kB signaling; T-cell help; leukocyte recruitment; repair of skin, collagen and extracellular matrix; angiogenesis; response to hypoxia (from anemia); glucose and energy homeostasis; oxidative stress.
- **2:** weak links such as nutrient and starvation responses, hormone signaling, beak and bone development, thermogenesis, pain, and responses to bacteria and LPS.

Results of genomic, transcriptomic or gene-expression studies of Galápagos birds were deliberately excluded. This includes the gene-expression section of Knutie et al. 2024 (urban finches).

# Run 012: literature review summary (Philornis downsi and Galápagos land birds)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- First-instar larvae live in the nostrils (nares) and ear canals of nestlings. Later instars stay in the nest base during the day and come out at night to feed on nestling blood and tissue. They also feed on brooding/incubating females. The results are blood loss (lower hemoglobin and hematocrit in nestlings), skin and tissue lesions, enlarged or deformed nares (which alter beak shape and later song), slower growth, and high nestling mortality.
  - https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/ecology-and-evolution/articles/10.3389/fevo.2024.1398353/full
  - https://galapagosconservation.org.uk/?p=8221
  - https://knowablemagazine.org/article/living-world/2018/darwins-finches-fall-prey-blood-sucking-parasite
  - https://lifesciences.univie.ac.at/news-events/newsordner/einzelansicht/news/darwins-finches-sing-out-of-tune-call-after-parasites-deform-beaks/
  - https://publish.csiro.au/MU/MU04040 (Philornis effects on birds in general)

## Host defenses documented or proposed
1. **Resistance through an antibody (IgY) response.** Experiments showed that P. downsi induces a parasite-specific antibody response in medium ground finches. Higher antibody levels were linked to fewer parasites and better fitness. This is the strongest documented host mechanism. (Score 5: adaptive immune response, immune response, B cell activation.)
   - https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3930052/ (Koop et al. 2013)
   - https://doaj.org/article/c0c1dd23b9f64ef2bc80d5833593b012 (Huber et al. 2010, PLoS ONE)
   - https://sicb.org/?p=25430
2. **Tolerance through behavioral and nutritional compensation.** Parasitized Galápagos mockingbird nestlings begged more and received more food from their parents, which offset the parasite's cost. Finches did not show this compensation, and plasma glucose did not change. This points to nutrition, energy balance and growth regulation (scored 3).
   - https://docta.ucm.es/entities/publication/70770688-559c-41e2-ba8b-ead6e9e97f7d/full (Knutie et al. 2016)
   - https://datadryad.org:443/stash/dataset/doi:10.5061/dryad.n11h95s (environmental variation in tolerance)
3. **Stress physiology.** Corticosterone was tested in breeding females and did not change with parasitism. This is a weak but plausible axis for nestlings (steroid signaling scored 3, other hormone terms 2).
   - https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4383185
4. **Behavioral and nest-based defenses.** Finches incorporate permethrin-treated cotton into nests ("self-fumigation"). This depends on human-provided material and has no clear physiological GO counterpart, so learning, cognition and pain perception were scored 2–3.
   - https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/darwins-finches-stamp-out-deadly-parasite-with-help-from-cotton-balls
   - https://sicb.org/?p=17441
5. **Predictions from hematophagy and tissue damage (scored 4).** Coagulation/hemostasis, replacement of red cells (erythropoiesis, hemopoiesis, iron homeostasis), wound healing, inflammation, innate immunity and complement (which works with antibodies). Supporting processes were scored 3: cytokine and T-cell help, phagocytosis, the oxidative burst, hypoxia response, skin/epidermis and ECM repair, angiogenesis, and growth/energy homeostasis.

## Scoring notes
- Generic housekeeping, developmental, neural, synaptic, DNA, RNA and cell-cycle terms scored 1.
- Antiviral- and bacterial-specific immunity scored 2 (secondary wound infection is only an indirect link).
- Genomic and transcriptomic studies of Galápagos birds were not used.

# Philornis downsi host-response review (run_029)

## What the larvae do
- Adult flies lay eggs in nests; 1st-instar larvae enter nestlings' nares/nasal cavity, and later instars live in nest material and feed on nestling blood and tissue at night. This causes wounds, blood loss (lower haemoglobin/anaemia), slower growth and high nestling mortality (often >50%; up to ~100% in some years). Brooding females may also be bitten.
- Larvae in the nares cause lasting beak/naris malformation in survivors, which alters song (vocal performance) and may affect mate choice/hybridisation.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Resistance (adaptive immunity):** Darwin's finches mount a P. downsi-specific IgY antibody response; nestlings and females with higher antibody titres had fewer parasites (Koop et al. 2013). Food supplementation / urban diet and resource availability modulate resistance.
- **Tolerance:** Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate the fly: parasitized nestlings beg more and parents provision more, compensating for losses; tolerance is labile and drops in dry years (Knutie et al. 2016; McNew et al. 2019). Larger hosts tolerate better. Finches did not show begging/glucose increases.
- **Physiology:** Parasitism lowers haemoglobin and growth; corticosterone in breeding females was not changed (Knutie et al. 2015 / Koop-related work); haematocrit not affected in adults.
- **Behavioural:** Finches incorporate permethrin-treated cotton into nests ("self-fumigation", Knutie et al. 2014); nest sanitation/grooming behaviours proposed; video analysis showed limited parental removal of larvae.
- Related-host work (Protocalliphora): antibody levels negatively related to parasite load; parasitized nestlings had lower haemoglobin.

## Scoring rationale
5 = adaptive/humoral immune response (antibody/B cell, immune response). 4 = inflammation, wound healing, haemostasis/coagulation, erythropoiesis/haematopoiesis (blood-loss compensation), innate immunity, T-cell activation, complement, iron homeostasis. 3 = cytokine/NF-kB signalling, phagocytosis, leukocyte adhesion, skin/keratinocyte/collagen repair, angiogenesis, hypoxia response. 2 = generic stress/oxidative stress, nutrient/energy/glucose metabolism (growth, tolerance via provisioning), hormone signalling, pain, learning/memory (behaviour), antibacterial/LPS responses (secondary infection), growth-factor tissue remodelling. 1 = everything else (housekeeping, development, neural, DNA/RNA processing, etc.). Genomic/transcriptomic studies of Galápagos birds were not used.

# Literature review: host responses to *Philornis downsi* in Galápagos land birds

## What the larvae do to hosts
- Adult flies lay eggs in nests. First-instar larvae live in the nestlings' nares (nostrils) and feed inside them, which can cause permanent beak and naris deformation. Later instars live in the nest material and feed on nestlings' blood and tissue at night, and on brooding females to a lesser degree.
- In nestlings this causes blood loss (lower haemoglobin), open skin lesions and wounds, slower growth, and high mortality (often more than 50% in Darwin's finches). Adult females show little measurable physiological cost: their hematocrit, body condition and corticosterone do not differ between fumigated and control nests.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
1. **Resistance through antibodies (documented):** Ground finches produce *P. downsi*-specific IgY antibodies. Experimental parasitism induced an antibody response, and antibody levels in adult females and nestlings were negatively related to parasite load (Huber et al. 2010; Koop/Knutie et al. 2013). A humoral or adaptive immune response is therefore the main documented physiological mechanism.
2. **Tolerance (documented in mockingbirds and in some finch years):** Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate the parasite. They lose less fitness than finches do, apparently because parents increase provisioning, which helps nestlings make up for blood loss. How well hosts tolerate the parasite varies with food and rainfall (Knutie et al. 2016). The proposed physiological bases are compensatory erythropoiesis and haemoglobin recovery, hemostasis, and wound repair.
3. **Inflammation, wound healing and hemostasis (proposed):** Larval feeding creates lesions. So clotting and platelet function, complement, inflammatory and cytokine signalling, skin and epidermal repair, and the oxidative stress or hypoxia that follows blood loss are plausible components of resistance and tolerance.
4. **Behavioral defenses (documented or proposed):** Females spend more time in the nest, which is associated with fewer parasites. Finches rub themselves with *Psidium galapageium* leaves (which repel *P. downsi*) and line nests with treated cotton ("self-fumigation"). Nestlings may also beg more. Few GO biological-process terms correspond directly to these behaviors.
5. **Stress and condition:** Corticosterone did not respond in adult females. Effects on nestling energy balance and growth are plausible but indirect.

## Scoring logic
- **5:** Adaptive or humoral immunity: immune response, adaptive immune response, B cell activation, B cell receptor signalling.
- **4:** Hemostasis and platelets, complement, wound healing, inflammation, erythropoiesis and hemopoiesis, innate immunity, B cell differentiation and proliferation.
- **3:** T cell processes, cytokine signalling, phagocytosis, oxidative stress, hypoxia, iron homeostasis, epidermal repair, pain.
- **2:** Generic immune signalling (NF-kB, JAK-STAT, TLR), angiogenesis and ECM remodelling, stress and hormone responses, nutrient and energy homeostasis, learning and memory.
- **1:** All other terms.

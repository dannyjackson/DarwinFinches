# P. downsi host-response review (run_022)

## What the larvae do
- First-instar larvae enter nestling nares; 2nd/3rd instars live in the nest base and feed at night (and by day) on blood and tissue from skin of abdomen, wings, ears. Larvae also feed on brooding adult females.
- Effects: blood loss (hemoglobin drops by about 28%, and death can follow loss of about 25% of blood volume), lower body mass and growth, shorter feathers, enlarged or deformed nares/beaks that can last into adulthood, and in-nest mortality averaging about 55%. These costs are worse in dry years (lower provisioning) and lighter in urban nests (higher-protein diet).

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Adaptive/humoral immunity (resistance):** P. downsi-specific IgY antibodies rise in adult female medium ground finches during nesting and in parasitized nests. These antibodies are proposed to confer resistance. Nestlings show no detectable antibody response. In a related nest-fly system (bluebirds/Protocalliphora), food-boosted IgY tracked lower parasite load, while haptoglobin (acute phase) did not.
- **Tolerance:** Mockingbirds tolerate the flies through compensation (more begging, more parental provisioning). Tolerance depends on rainfall and food. Hemoglobin/blood replacement (erythropoiesis) and energy reserves (glucose, glycogen) are physiological axes, with low glucose in parasitized, high-mortality nestlings. Urban/protein diet partially rescues survival.
- **Behavioral:** Nestlings stand on siblings, preen and eat larvae. Females probe the nest base, preen nestlings and change brooding posture. Parents remove dead nestlings. Begging and provisioning shift. No corticosterone stress response was found in females.
- **Inferred a priori:** inflammation and wound healing at feeding sites, skin/epidermal barrier, hemostasis, hypoxia and oxidative stress from anemia, and iron homeostasis.

## Scoring logic
- 5: immune response, adaptive immunity, B-cell activation (antibody resistance).
- 4: B-cell receptor/differentiation/proliferation, immune regulation, inflammation, wound healing, coagulation, hemopoiesis/erythropoiesis (blood-loss tolerance).
- 3: innate immunity, T cells, cytokines, NF-kB/JAK-STAT, chemotaxis, platelets, epidermis/keratinocytes/collagen, hypoxia/oxidative stress, iron, glucose/energy homeostasis, starvation, pain.
- 2: antibacterial/antiviral signaling, angiogenesis/ECM, insulin/hormone, bone/beak skeletal, general stress.
- 1: everything else.

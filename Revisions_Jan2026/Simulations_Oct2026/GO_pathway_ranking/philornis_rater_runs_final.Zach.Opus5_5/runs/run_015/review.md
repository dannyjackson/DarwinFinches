# Philornis downsi: host effects and defenses (review summary for GO scoring)

## What the larvae do
- First-instar larvae enter the nares of nestlings and feed inside them. Second- and third-instar larvae hide in nest material and come out at night to feed externally on blood and tissue. They also feed on brooding females.
- Documented effects on hosts: blood loss, with lower haemoglobin in parasitized nestlings (Galápagos mockingbirds, small ground finch); slower growth in mass, tarsus and feathers; tissue damage and wounds; enlarged or deformed nares and beaks in survivors, which change song and mating signals; and high nestling mortality, often above 50%.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Adaptive (antibody) resistance:** adult female medium ground finches produced P. downsi-binding IgY in parasitized nests. Antibody levels were marginally linked to lower parasite load. Nestlings showed no response at the age sampled. In a related Protocalliphora–bluebird system, food-supplemented nestlings had higher IgY and fewer parasites.
- **Inflammation and innate defense (proposed):** antibodies may speed local inflammation that blocks larval access to blood vessels, exposes larvae to host defensive or lytic molecules, and causes itch that prompts defensive behaviour. Urban finches with access to more protein survived parasitism much better.
- **Tolerance:** Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate the parasite. Tolerance varies by year and depends on food supply and parental provisioning. Candidate physiological routes are replacing blood (erythropoiesis, iron balance), clotting and wound repair, and energy and nutrient balance.
- **Behavioural defense:** nestlings preen off larvae and stand on nest mates. Females adjust time spent in the nest. Parents may raise provisioning, as mockingbirds do, while small tree finches do not. Finches incorporate permethrin-treated cotton into nests. Behavioural resistance has been largely ineffective.
- **Stress physiology:** corticosterone has been proposed as a cost of parasitism in parents.

## How scores were assigned
- **5:** adaptive and humoral immunity, immune response, inflammation, wound healing and response to wounding, erythrocyte differentiation, defense response.
- **4:** core parts of those mechanisms: B-cell signalling, innate immunity, coagulation and platelets, haematopoiesis, iron homeostasis, complement.
- **3:** supporting or downstream processes: T cells, cytokines and NF-kB/JAK-STAT, chemotaxis, phagocytosis, hypoxia and angiogenesis, skin and epidermis, ECM and TGF-beta, oxidative stress, nutrient and energy balance, pain.
- **2:** weak or indirect links, such as anti-bacterial and TLR signalling, general signalling, metabolism and bone or beak development.
- **1:** everything else.

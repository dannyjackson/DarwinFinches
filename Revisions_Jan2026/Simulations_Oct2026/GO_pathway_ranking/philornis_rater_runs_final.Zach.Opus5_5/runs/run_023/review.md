# Philornis downsi and host responses: literature summary for GO scoring (run_023)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- Larvae feed on the blood and tissue of nestlings in a semi-haematophagous way. First and early second instars live under the skin and inside the nares or developing feather quills. Later instars hide in the nest base and feed externally, mostly at night.
- Effects on nestlings: 18-55% blood loss, lower hemoglobin (anemia), multiple wounds and secondary infections, and naris and beak deformation, which later alters song and mating signals. Parasitized nestlings also grow more slowly, have lower mass and shorter tarsi, and suffer high mortality (up to 100%).
- In adult females, parasitism had no effect on corticosterone, hematocrit or body condition.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Resistance (adaptive/humoral):** female medium ground finches at parasitized nests had higher P. downsi-binding IgY antibody levels, and these were marginally linked to fewer parasites. Nestlings showed no detectable antibody response. The proposed mechanisms are antibody-accelerated inflammation at bite sites, which blocks access to vessels, plus lytic and defensive molecules. Antibody-driven itching might also trigger preening.
- **Tolerance:** Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate the fly in wet years. Parasitized nestlings beg more, parents provision more, and resource availability and food supplementation affect the outcome. Compensation for blood loss (erythropoiesis, hemoglobin recovery) and for growth costs is the implied physiology. Urban finches with richer diets survive parasitism far better (gene-expression results were deliberately not used).
- **Behavioral:** heavily parasitized females brood less and stand more. Preening larvae off is rare. Finches incorporate permethrin-treated cotton into nests (self-fumigation), and rubbing guava leaves onto feathers has been proposed.
- **Avoidance/life history:** breeding timing, nest height and clutch size have been proposed for other Philornis hosts.

## Scoring rationale
- **5:** immune response and adaptive immune response, B cell activation (antibodies), and response to wounding.
- **4:** defense and innate immunity, B/T cell processes, inflammation, complement, wound healing, coagulation and platelets, and hemopoiesis/erythropoiesis (anemia compensation).
- **3:** cytokine, NF-kB and TLR signaling, antibacterial defense (secondary infection), phagocytosis and chemotaxis, iron homeostasis, hypoxia, skin and epidermis, ECM and collagen repair, angiogenesis, oxidative stress, pain, and energy homeostasis and nutrient response (tolerance and growth).
- **2:** weak or indirect links, such as antiviral immunity, generic stress, metabolism, hormones, thermoregulation and bone/cartilage (beak).
- **1:** everything else.

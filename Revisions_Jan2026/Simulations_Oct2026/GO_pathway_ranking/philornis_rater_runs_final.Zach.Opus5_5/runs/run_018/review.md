# Philornis downsi and Galápagos land birds: mechanisms review (run_018)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- First-instar larvae get into nestling nares and ear canals and feed on keratin, tissue and blood. Later instars hide in the nest base and come out at night to feed on blood and tissue. During incubation they also feed on brooding females. Heavy infestation causes high nestling mortality (about 50 to 90%), and over 2004 to 2013 hosts died younger as parasite intensity rose (Fessl et al. 2006; Kleindorfer et al. 2014; O'Connor et al. 2010).
- **Blood loss and anemia:** parasitized nestlings have lower hemoglobin. In one study each extra larva lowered hemoglobin by about 0.8 units. Parasitism also reduces body mass and feather growth (Knutie et al. 2016).
- **Tissue damage and wounding:** larvae enlarge the nares and deform the beak. This lowers song performance and pairing success and may raise hybridization risk (Kleindorfer et al. 2019).
- **Nutrient and protein depletion** has been hypothesized (Pike et al. 2024).

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Resistance through adaptive humoral immunity:** breeding female medium ground finches make *P. downsi*-binding antibodies (IgY). Females with higher titers tended to carry fewer parasites. Nestlings showed little response. The authors propose that antibodies speed up inflammation and so impair larval feeding (Huber et al. 2010; Koop et al. 2013). In related nest-fly systems, food supplementation raised nestling antibody levels and lowered parasite load (Knutie 2020, bluebirds).
- **Tolerance:** mockingbirds tolerate the parasite. Their parasitized nestlings beg more and get fed more, so fledging success holds up despite blood loss and lower glucose. Finches do not show this compensation. Proposed tolerance mechanisms include replacing lost blood (erythropoiesis), repairing tissue and wounds, and adjusting energy and glucose metabolism (Knutie et al. 2016).
- **Stress physiology:** female corticosterone and hematocrit did not differ with parasitism (Knutie et al. 2013), so the HPA axis has only weak support.
- **Behavioral defenses:** females probe nares and the nest base and preen nestlings, and nestlings preen themselves and reposition through the night. Finches also self-fumigate by adding permethrin-treated cotton to nests (O'Connor et al. 2010; Knutie et al. 2014).

## Scoring logic
- **5:** adaptive and humoral immunity, B cells, blood production and erythrocytes, hemostasis and coagulation, wounding and wound healing, general immune response.
- **4:** inflammation, complement, innate defense, T cells, cytokines, platelets, iron homeostasis, skin and keratinocyte development, hypoxia (from anemia).
- **3:** specific cytokine and NF-kB and TLR signaling, phagocytosis, chemotaxis, ECM and collagen repair, angiogenesis, oxidative stress, energy and glucose and starvation responses, pain.
- **2:** antiviral immunity, apoptosis, hormones, lipid and energy metabolism, bone and beak development, learning, broad signaling.
- **1:** housekeeping processes, neural, reproductive and other unrelated terms.

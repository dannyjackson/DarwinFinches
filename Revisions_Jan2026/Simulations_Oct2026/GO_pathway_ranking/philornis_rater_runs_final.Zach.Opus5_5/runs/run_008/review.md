# P. downsi host-response review (run_008)

## What the larvae do
- First-instar larvae live in nestling nares/beak cavities; later instars live in nest material and feed nocturnally on nestling blood and tissue. Hosts suffer blood loss (lower hemoglobin), tissue damage/wounds, naris and beak deformation, reduced growth, and high nestling mortality.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Resistance (adaptive/humoral immunity):** Nestlings and adults produce P. downsi-specific antibodies (IgY); higher antibody titre associated with lower parasite load; immune response experimentally shown to be parasite-induced.
- **Tolerance:** Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate parasitism (maintain growth/survival despite load), linked to increased parental provisioning and environmental (food/rainfall) variation; compensation for blood loss (erythropoiesis, hemoglobin recovery, iron) and wound repair are implied tolerance mechanisms.
- **Inflammation/innate immunity and haemostasis:** proposed downstream of feeding wounds (blood coagulation, platelet activation, wound healing, skin/keratinocyte repair, cytokine signaling).
- **Behavior:** nestling behaviour (begging, movement, defensive preening) predicts naris deformation; adults self-anoint feathers with Psidium galapageium leaves that repel larvae; parental nest attendance/provisioning.

## Scoring logic
5 = immune response / adaptive immunity / B cell activation (antibody resistance). 4 = B cell differentiation/receptor signaling, wound healing, coagulation, erythropoiesis/hemopoiesis, inflammation, innate/defense response, complement. 3 = T-cell, cytokine, NF-kB, phagocytosis, iron homeostasis, skin/ECM repair, angiogenesis, hypoxia/oxidative stress, pain. 2 = more peripheral immune (bacterial/TLR), nutrition/energy stress, learning/memory, general stress. 1 = everything else.

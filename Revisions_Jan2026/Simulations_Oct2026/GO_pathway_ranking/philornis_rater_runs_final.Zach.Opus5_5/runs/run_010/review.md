# Philornis downsi and Galápagos land birds: summary of host-response mechanisms

## What the larvae do
- Adult flies lay eggs in nests. First-instar larvae enter the nares and ear canals of nestlings and feed inside them. Later instars hide in the nest base by day and come out at night to feed externally on the blood and tissue of nestlings, and of brooding females. (Fessl et al. 2006, *Parasitology*; Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016, *BMC Zoology*)
- Consequences reported in the literature:
  - High nestling mortality, often 100% at high intensity.
  - Lower hemoglobin, meaning blood loss and anemia (Dudaniec et al. 2006, *Austral Ecology*).
  - Smaller body mass and tarsus.
  - Tissue lesions, and enlarged or deformed nares and beaks that later change song (Kleindorfer et al. 2019).
  - No effect found on adult female corticosterone, body condition or hematocrit (Knutie et al. 2013).

## Host defenses documented or proposed
1. **Antibody-mediated (adaptive) resistance**: Breeding female *G. fortis* produce P. downsi-binding IgY. Antibody titre was marginally negatively correlated with fly number. The authors proposed that antibodies speed up local inflammation, which would stop larvae feeding, and cause itching that prompts behavioral defense. Nestlings showed no detectable antibody response by day 5. (Koop et al. 2013, *Ecology & Evolution*; Huber et al. 2010)
2. **Inflammatory and innate responses at the feeding site**: proposed as the effector arm (cytokines, leukocyte recruitment, complement, hemostasis).
3. **Tolerance through blood replacement and resource compensation**: Hosts may recover from blood loss through erythropoiesis, iron handling and hemostasis. Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate the parasite: parasitized nestlings beg more and parents provision more. Finches did not raise begging, provisioning or plasma glucose (Knutie et al. 2016, *Ecology*). Dietary protein from urban food is linked to lower parasite load and better survival (Knutie et al. 2024, *Global Change Biology*). This points to nutritional and metabolic processes (glucose and energy homeostasis, starvation responses, growth signaling).
4. **Tissue repair**: wound healing, re-epithelialisation, skin and keratinocyte biology, ECM and collagen, angiogenesis, and possibly remodeling of the beak and nares.
5. **Behavioral defenses**:
   - Females stand in the nest instead of brooding, which risks the brood's thermoregulation.
   - Rare preening of larvae.
   - Nestlings crawl over nestmates.
   - Adults rub guayabillo leaves on their feathers.
   - Birds incorporate permethrin-treated cotton into nests (Knutie et al. 2014, *Current Biology*).
   - Changes in nesting time and height.
   These implicate sensory or pain perception, learning and cognition only weakly at the level of GO processes.
6. **Secondary stress**: oxidative stress, hypoxia from anemia, glucocorticoid signaling (weak support), and the risk of bacterial infection of lesions.

## Scoring approach
- **5**: core processes for antibody, immune or inflammatory response; wounding and wound healing; erythrocyte differentiation.
- **4**: core components such as T-cell help, cytokines, innate activation, complement, coagulation and platelets, hemopoiesis and iron homeostasis.
- **3**: supporting processes such as NF-kB, JAK-STAT, TLR, oxidative stress, hypoxia, glucose and nutrient homeostasis, skin and ECM, angiogenesis, phagocytosis, and pain.
- **2**: weak links such as lipid metabolism, bone and beak development, thermogenesis, behavior and cognition, and generic stress.
- **1**: housekeeping, neural development and other unrelated processes.

The excluded genomic and transcriptomic study (bioRxiv 2023.03.06.531275), and its published version (Knutie et al. 2024, *GCB*), contributed no gene-expression results. From that work, only the ecological findings on diet (δ15N) and survival were used.

# Philornis downsi and host responses: summary of the review

## What the larvae do to hosts
- First-instar larvae get into nestlings' nares (nostrils) and developing feather quills. Second- and third-instar larvae live in the nest material and feed externally on blood and tissue, mostly at night. Nestlings lose a lot of blood (lower hemoglobin), wounds form, and the nares and beak can be deformed. Growth is slower (mass, tarsus), and death rates are high, up to 100% of nestlings in some nests. Incubating females are also bitten, but researchers have seen no clear loss of condition in adults.
- In heavily parasitized nests, nestlings have lower blood glucose and lower body condition.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Resistance through antibodies (adaptive or humoral):** adult female medium ground finches make antibodies (IgY) specific to *P. downsi*, and these are linked to fewer parasites. No such response has been detected in nestlings. Better nutrition (food supplementation) raises antibody levels and lowers parasite load in a related Philornis–bluebird system.
- **Tolerance:** Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate parasitism. Parasitized nestlings beg more and parents bring more food, so the cost of blood loss is offset. Urban finches have higher hemoglobin and better fledging despite parasites, and their diet is richer in protein. The likely physiological routes are making new red blood cells (erythropoiesis), iron balance, clotting, wound repair and nutrient or energy metabolism.
- **Inflammation and innate immunity:** these are expected as a first response to skin wounds and to larval saliva (proposed rather than measured directly).
- **Behavior:** nestlings preening larvae off themselves is rare. Females pick at nestlings' nares, nestlings crawl over nestmates, and nest site and breeding timing may reduce exposure. Finches may rub insect-repelling plants into their feathers (proposed, untested). Begging and provisioning are part of tolerance.
- **Stress and endocrine:** females' corticosterone response did not change with parasitism (a weak link).

## How the scores were assigned
- **5:** adaptive, humoral and B-cell immunity, and immune response in general.
- **4:** red blood cell and blood cell production, iron balance, clotting and platelets, wound healing, inflammation, innate immunity, T-cell activation, complement.
- **3:** cytokine and NF-kB signaling, leukocyte chemotaxis and phagocytosis, skin and extracellular matrix repair, angiogenesis, low oxygen (hypoxia), glucose and gluconeogenesis, starvation and nutrient responses, oxidative stress, steroid hormone signaling, pain.
- **2:** more indirect supporting processes (growth signaling, bone and beak development, learning and behavior, defense against bacteria from secondary infection, and others).
- **1:** everything else.

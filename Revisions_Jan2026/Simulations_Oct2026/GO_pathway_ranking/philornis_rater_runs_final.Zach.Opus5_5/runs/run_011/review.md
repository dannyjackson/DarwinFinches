# Philornis downsi and host responses: literature summary for GO term scoring

## What the larvae do to their hosts
- Adult flies lay eggs in nests. Early-instar larvae live in the nestlings' nares and ear canals. Later instars live in the nest material and feed at night on the blood and tissue of nestlings, and also of brooding females.
- Effects on nestlings: blood loss and lower hemoglobin (anemia), slower growth (mass, tarsus, primary feathers), skin wounds and lesions, enlarged nares and deformed beaks, and high mortality. Fledging success can drop to about 17–19%.
- Adults whose beaks were deformed as nestlings later sing with lower vocal performance. This changes a mating signal and may contribute to hybridization.
- Costs are higher in dry years when food is short.

## Documented or proposed host defenses
- **Immune resistance:** Medium ground finches produce P. downsi-binding antibodies (IgY) when parasitized, and higher antibody levels are associated with fewer larvae (Huber et al. 2010; Koop et al. 2013). Mounting this response is likely to involve B cell and T cell activation, inflammation, and response to skin wounds. Other Philornis species cause inflammatory lesions at the larval feeding site.
- **Tolerance:** Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate the parasite better than finches (Knutie et al. 2016). Proposed tolerance mechanisms include replacing lost blood (erythropoiesis, iron homeostasis), wound repair, hemostasis, and keeping growth going through better nutrition and energy balance. Finch tolerance rises with parental provisioning and falls in dry years (McNew et al. 2019). Urban finches that eat human food are partly rescued from parasite effects.
- **Behavioral defenses:** Nestlings preen off larvae and stand on their nest mates. Parasitized nestlings beg more, but begging is not an honest signal of need. Parents may adjust provisioning. Behavioral resistance appears weak.

## How the review maps onto the scoring scale
- **5:** adaptive and B-cell/antibody immune processes.
- **4:** inflammation, wound healing, coagulation and platelet processes, erythropoiesis and iron homeostasis, T cell processes, innate defense and complement.
- **3:** cytokine and NF-kB signalling, oxidative stress, hypoxia, skin and keratinocyte development, starvation and nutrient responses, angiogenesis, chemotaxis.
- **2:** metabolic, endocrine, bone and beak, and behavioral or learning terms with indirect links.
- **1:** all remaining cellular housekeeping and developmental terms.

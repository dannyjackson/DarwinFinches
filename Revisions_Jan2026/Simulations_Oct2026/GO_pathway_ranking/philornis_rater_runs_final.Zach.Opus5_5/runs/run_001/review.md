# Philornis downsi and Galápagos land-bird hosts: mechanism review (run_001)

## What the parasite does
- *P. downsi* (Muscidae) is an introduced nest fly. Adults are non-parasitic. Females lay eggs in nest material or in nestling nares. 1st/early 2nd instars live subcutaneously, in the nares; later instars live free in the nest base and feed at night on the blood and tissue of nestlings, and also of brooding females. Larvae spend about 5–6 days in the nest.
- Effects on hosts: heavy blood loss (estimated 18–55%), lower haemoglobin (anaemia), many body wounds and secondary infections, enlarged or deformed nares (which persist in adults and change beak shape and song), slower growth, and high nestling mortality (often above 50%, up to 100%).

## Host defences documented or proposed
- **Resistance (immune):** breeding female medium ground finches produce *P. downsi*-specific IgY antibodies. Females with higher titres tended to have fewer parasites, but this did not save broods. Antibody responses were not detected in 5-day-old nestlings or in mockingbirds. Inflammatory and innate responses at feeding sites are a strong a priori prediction.
- **Tolerance:** Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate the fly. Parasitised nestlings beg more and parents feed them more, which maintains plasma glucose and fledging success despite lower Hb. Tolerance falls in dry years when food is limited. Diet or protein (urban nests) and red-blood-cell replacement are proposed tolerance routes. Predicted physiological components: erythropoiesis, iron homeostasis, haemostasis/coagulation, hypoxia responses, wound repair, and energy and nutrient homeostasis.
- **Behavioural:** female in-nest attendance and brooding, nestling begging and parental provisioning, and possibly self-medication. Breeding-female corticosterone was not altered by parasitism.
- Nest temperature affects larval development rate (an ecological factor, not a host mechanism).

## Scoring logic
5 = wounding and wound healing, immune response and adaptive immunity (antibodies), erythrocyte differentiation (anaemia compensation). 4 = general defence, innate and inflammatory immunity, B-cell processes, haematopoiesis, coagulation and platelets, hypoxia, and iron homeostasis. 3 = cytokine, NF-kB, TLR and T-cell signalling, phagocytosis and complement, oxidative stress, angiogenesis, epidermis and ECM repair, and energy and glucose homeostasis. 2 = antiviral and other peripheral immunity, growth and metabolic signalling, learning and behaviour, bone, and xenobiotic responses. 1 = everything else.

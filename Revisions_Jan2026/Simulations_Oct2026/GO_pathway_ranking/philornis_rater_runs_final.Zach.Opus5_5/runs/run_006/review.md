# P. downsi host-response review (run_006)

## What the larvae do
- First-instar larvae enter nestling nares and ear canals, causing enlarged nares, lesions, swelling, internal bleeding and lasting beak deformation (which affects song). Later instars live in the nest base and feed at night on the blood and tissue of nestlings and brooding females.
- Effects measured in hosts: heavy overnight blood loss (18-55%), lower hemoglobin as larvae increase, slower growth (lower mass, shorter feathers) and high in-nest mortality (often more than 50%). Costs are worst in dry, food-poor years.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Resistance (immune):** females at parasitized nests show higher P. downsi-binding antibody (IgY) levels, and those levels are marginally linked to fewer parasites. The authors propose that antibodies speed inflammation at feeding sites and so block blood feeding. Nestlings showed no antibody response.
- **Tolerance:** mockingbirds tolerate the larvae better than finches, and tolerance varies with food supply and parental provisioning. Possible routes are replacing lost blood (erythropoiesis, iron), wound repair and hemostasis, and keeping up energy and growth under nutrient stress. Some urban nestlings with a protein-rich diet do better.
- **Behavior:** females brood less and stand more. Nestlings preen, eat larvae and stand on siblings. Begging and provisioning change. Some finches put permethrin-treated cotton into nests (a human-supplied remedy). Corticosterone did not change in breeding adults.

## Scoring basis
- **5:** immune response, B-cell/adaptive (antibody) processes.
- **4:** innate/defense/inflammatory responses, wound healing, coagulation and platelets, hemopoiesis and erythrocyte differentiation (predicted from the documented hemoglobin loss), iron homeostasis, response to hypoxia (anemia).
- **3:** cytokine/NF-kB/T-cell signaling, skin and epidermis repair, angiogenesis, extracellular matrix, oxidative stress, nutrient/energy homeostasis, pain perception.
- **2:** more distant immune, metabolic, hormonal, learning and stress terms.
- **1:** everything else.

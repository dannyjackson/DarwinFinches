# P. downsi and host responses: literature review (run_019)

## What the larvae do
- First-instar larvae live in the nestlings' nares (nostrils). Second and third instars live in the nest base and come out at night to feed on blood and tissue. They feed on the abdomen, wings, ears and other exposed body parts. The wounds cause swelling, open lesions and internal bleeding, and they enlarge or deform the nares and beak. Nestlings can lose 18–55% of their blood overnight. Hemoglobin falls with each added larva, and parasitized nestlings grow more slowly. Average in-nest mortality is about 55%, with a range of 3–100%. Naris deformation can last into adulthood and changes vocal performance (song).
- In 2013, stress-induced corticosterone and hematocrit in breeding females did not differ by parasitism. Parasitism also shifts the composition of the nestling gut microbiota.

## Host defenses
- **Resistance (immune):** Adult medium ground finches produce antibodies (IgY) specific to *P. downsi*. Levels are higher in parasitized nests and during nesting, and they may give partial resistance. Defense against wounds and inflammation is predicted.
- **Tolerance:** Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate the fly better than finches. Tolerance depends on rainfall, food and the parents' provisioning rate. Urban finch nestlings, with diets higher in protein, keep higher hemoglobin. This implies recovery from blood loss (erythropoiesis, iron balance, hemostasis) and compensation through nutrition.
- **Behavior:** Nestlings beg more and stand on siblings. Adults preen nares and nest bases, eat larvae and remove dead nestlings. Finches "self-fumigate" by adding permethrin-treated cotton to nests. Some species choose nest sites that work as ecological traps.

## Scoring rationale
- **5:** Antibody, B-cell and adaptive immunity; erythropoiesis and hemopoiesis.
- **4:** Inflammation, wound healing, coagulation and platelets, innate defense, T-cell activation, cytokines, iron homeostasis.
- **3:** Specific cytokine and TLR/NF-kB pathways, phagocytosis, oxidative stress, hypoxia, epidermis and keratinocytes, extracellular matrix, angiogenesis.
- **2:** Weak links: nutrition and starvation responses, glucose homeostasis, growth signaling, learning, pain, bone and beak remodeling.
- **1:** All other terms. Gene-expression results from Galápagos birds were deliberately excluded, including Knutie et al. bioRxiv 2023.03.06.531275 and the transcriptomic parts of the 2024 urban study.

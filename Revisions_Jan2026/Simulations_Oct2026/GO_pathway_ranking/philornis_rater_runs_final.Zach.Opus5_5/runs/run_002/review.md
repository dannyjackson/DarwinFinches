# Literature review: host responses to *Philornis downsi* (run_002)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- Adult flies lay eggs in nests. First-instar larvae enter the nasal cavities (nares) and ear canals of nestlings. Second- and third-instar larvae live in the nest base and come out at night to feed on the blood and tissue of nestlings (and of brooding females). The result is open wounds, tissue damage and naris (beak) deformation that persists into adulthood.
- Parasitized nestlings lose blood: hemoglobin falls by about 28%, by about 0.8 g/dL per larva. They also have lower body mass and shorter feathers, beg less strongly, and in many finch species most of them die. Lower blood glucose and lower body condition have also been reported.
- One experiment found no fly effect on adult female hematocrit, corticosterone or body condition.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Resistance (immune):** Adult female medium ground finches make *P. downsi*-specific IgY antibodies. Antibody levels rise during nesting, are higher in parasitized nests, and are linked to fewer flies (Koop et al. 2013). Other proposed mechanisms are acquired/humoral immunity, inflammation at feeding sites, and cytokine-mediated responses to ectoparasite saliva. Urban finch nests had fewer flies, which the authors read as resistance (this rests on non-expression data only).
- **Tolerance:** Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate parasitism, with no density-dependent mortality, and parents compensate by feeding more (Knutie et al. 2016). Urban small ground finch nestlings kept their hemoglobin up and survived high fly loads, which points to tolerance through blood replacement (erythropoiesis/hematopoiesis, iron homeostasis), wound repair, clotting, and diet/energy (protein intake, glucose homeostasis).
- **Behavioral:** Nestlings stand on siblings, and nestlings and adults preen and eat larvae. Adults preen the nares and nest base and remove dead nestlings. Some birds self-fumigate with permethrin-treated cotton when it is offered. Females at parasitized nests brood less. Nestling behavior predicts naris deformation.

## Scoring logic
- 5: immune/defense response, B-cell/antibody (adaptive) immunity, inflammation, wound healing/response to wounding, hemopoiesis/erythropoiesis, blood coagulation.
- 4: core components such as cytokines (IL-6, TNF, IL-1, IL-17, IFN-gamma), NF-kB, T-cell activation, complement, phagocytosis, platelets, iron homeostasis, hypoxia response, epidermis/keratinocyte development.
- 3: supporting processes such as TLR/JAK-STAT, angiogenesis, ECM/collagen remodeling, oxidative stress, starvation/nutrient and glucose homeostasis, TGF-beta, hormone response, pain, blood pressure.
- 2: generic signaling, metabolism, cell migration/adhesion, apoptosis, autophagy, development of skeleton, muscle or growth.
- 1: no specific link, e.g. RNA processing, DNA replication/repair, cell cycle, cilia, neural/synaptic development, organ morphogenesis, antiviral.

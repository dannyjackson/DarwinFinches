# P. downsi host-response review (run_009)

## What the larvae do to the host
- First-instar larvae live in the nestlings' nares. Second- and third-instar larvae hide in the nest base and come out at night to feed on the nestlings' blood and tissue.
- The feeding causes swelling, bleeding under the skin, open lesions and enlarged or deformed nares and beaks. Damaged beaks can impair song later in life.
- Parasitism lowers hemoglobin (about 0.8 g/dL per larva in one study), reduces body mass and feather growth, and causes high nestling mortality (55% on average).

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Resistance (immune).** Adult female medium ground finches produce antibodies specific to P. downsi, and the antibody levels go up when nests are parasitized. Nestlings mount weak responses. Inflammation is proposed as a mechanism.
- **Tolerance.** Mockingbirds tolerate parasitism, through more food from the parents, larger nestling size and more begging. Proposed tolerance mechanisms include replacing lost blood (erythropoiesis, iron), wound repair, clotting, and nutrition or energy reserves.
- **Behavior.** Nestlings and females preen and eat larvae, and nestlings stand on their nest mates. Females brood less and probe the nest base. Finches take up permethrin-treated cotton.
- **Stress hormones.** No corticosterone response was found in females at parasitized nests.

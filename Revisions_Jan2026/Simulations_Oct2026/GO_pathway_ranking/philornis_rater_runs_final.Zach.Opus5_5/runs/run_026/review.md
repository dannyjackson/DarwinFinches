# P. downsi host-response literature review (run_026)

## What the larvae do
- First-instar larvae live in the nares and beak cavity of nestlings. Older larvae live in the nest base and feed on blood and tissue at night. This causes blood loss (lower hemoglobin and anemia), wounds, deformed nares and beaks, slower growth, and high nestling mortality (often above 50%). Adult females are also bitten. (Fessl et al. 2006 Parasitology; Dudaniec et al., Flinders)

## Documented or proposed host defenses
1. **Resistance (adaptive humoral immunity):** Medium ground finches make *P. downsi*-specific IgY antibodies. The response is induced by parasitism (experimental fumigation). Antibody levels relate to larval feeding and load, and are higher in females and during nesting (Huber et al. 2010; Knutie et al. 2013; Koop et al.). Mockingbird nestlings showed no specific antibodies.
2. **Tolerance:** Mockingbirds tolerate the parasite through higher nestling begging and parental compensation (Knutie et al. 2016). Tolerance varies with environmental and food conditions, and urban or food-supplemented finches do better. Parasitized nestlings must make up for blood loss through erythropoiesis, iron metabolism, hemostasis and wound repair. Small tree finches did not raise their provisioning.
3. **Behavioral defenses:** Parents preen nestling nares and the nest base. Nestlings stand on top of each other to avoid larvae. Brooding behavior changes. Finches fumigate their nests with permethrin-treated cotton, and anoint their feathers with *Psidium* repellent.
4. **Stress physiology:** Experiments have tested parasite effects on corticosterone in breeding finches (stress-response and hormonal processes).

## Scoring logic
- **5:** B cell and antibody-mediated adaptive immunity (the documented IgY response) and erythrocyte differentiation (compensation for documented blood loss).
- **4:** Core immune, inflammatory, complement, wound healing, hemostasis (coagulation and platelets), hemopoiesis, and iron homeostasis.
- **3:** T cell and cytokine signaling, NF-kB, oxidative stress, phagocytosis, hypoxia, pain, epidermal and skin repair, collagen, angiogenesis, and steroid hormone (corticosterone) signaling.
- **2:** Broad innate pattern-recognition signaling (antibacterial and LPS, linked to secondary infection), JAK-STAT, nutrient and energy balance (food limitation and tolerance), insulin and IGF growth signaling, learning and behavior, xenobiotic response (permethrin), thermogenesis (brooding), and generic stress.
- **1:** Housekeeping, developmental patterning, neural, reproductive and other processes with no specific link. Antiviral terms were also scored 1.

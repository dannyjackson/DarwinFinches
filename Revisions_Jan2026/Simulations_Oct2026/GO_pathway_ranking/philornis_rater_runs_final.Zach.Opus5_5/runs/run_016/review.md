# P. downsi host-response review (run_016)

## What the larvae do
- First-instar larvae live in the nares and ear canals of nestlings. Later instars hide in the nest base by day and feed on blood and tissue at night. They also feed on brooding females. The result is open wounds and enlarged or deformed nares that can persist into adulthood. (Fessl et al. 2006, Parasitology; Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016 BMC Zoology)
- Hosts lose a lot of blood. Hemoglobin falls about 0.8 g/dL per larva, and losing roughly 25% of blood volume is usually fatal. Nestlings also have lower mass, shorter feathers and 35–88% lower survival. (Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016)
- Protein depletion (higher blood C:N) is proposed as a cost. (Frontiers Ecol Evol 2024)

## Host defenses: documented or proposed
- **Resistance (adaptive humoral immunity):** Adult females make P. downsi-specific IgY, and levels rise with exposure during nesting. Experiments show the response is induced by the parasite. Nestlings may not mount it before fledging; maternal antibodies are proposed. (Huber et al. 2010 PLoS ONE; Koop et al. 2013 Ecol Evol)
- **Tolerance (blood recovery / erythropoiesis):** Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate the fly through more begging, more parental provisioning and more brooding, which compensate for blood and mass loss. (Knutie et al. 2016 Ecology) Urban finches lose less hemoglobin and resist better. The proposed reasons are a protein- and iron-rich diet and erythropoietin-driven recovery of red blood cells. (Knutie et al. 2024 Global Change Biology)
- **Behavior:** Nestlings stand on siblings to get away from larvae. Females and nestlings preen and remove larvae. Finches build fumigated cotton into nests (self-fumigation). Nestling responsiveness predicts naris damage. (Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016; Knutie et al. 2014 Curr Biol; Common et al. 2023)
- **Stress axis:** Corticosterone in adult females did not change with parasitism. (Knutie et al. 2013 Gen Comp Endocrinol)
- **General inference:** Larval feeding wounds skin, so innate and inflammatory responses, wound healing, coagulation, and skin and epidermal repair are reasonable a priori predictions. So are hypoxia responses and iron homeostasis that follow from anemia.

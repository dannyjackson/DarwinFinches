# Literature review: host responses to Philornis downsi (run_014)

## What the larvae do
- P. downsi lays its eggs in nests. First-instar larvae feed inside the nestlings' nares (nostrils). Later instars live in the nest material and come out at night to feed on nestling blood and tissue.
- This causes blood loss and lower hemoglobin (anemia), open wounds and lesions, enlarged or deformed nares, and beaks that are permanently malformed. Deformed beaks change vocal output and song.
- Infested nestlings grow less, and nestling mortality is very high, often more than half and up to 100% in some species and years.
- Females that brood in infested nests can also be bitten.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Resistance (adaptive humoral immunity):** finches mount a P. downsi-specific IgY antibody response. In adult females, higher antibody titres are linked to lower parasite load. Nestlings show weaker responses.
- **Resistance (innate and inflammatory):** inflammation and immune-cell recruitment at feeding sites and in the skin. This is proposed by analogy with other Philornis and myiasis systems.
- **Tolerance:** Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate the parasite, and in some finches tolerance depends on resources. The mechanisms involve keeping growth going and hemoglobin levels up, through more parental provisioning and nestling begging, and through compensatory blood production (erythropoiesis, iron homeostasis). Repair of blood vessels and tissue (coagulation, wound healing, skin regeneration) is predicted from the damage the larvae cause.
- **Behavioral:** parents increase or adjust provisioning and nestlings beg differently. Adults rub leaves of Psidium galapagium on their feathers (self-medication with repellent or larvicidal compounds), and parents may sanitize the nest or brood differently.
- **Secondary:** bacterial infection of wounds, physiological stress from hypoxia or anemia, and oxidative stress.

## Scoring logic
- **5:** antibody, B-cell and adaptive humoral response terms; immune response; wound healing; blood coagulation.
- **4:** erythropoiesis and hemopoiesis, iron homeostasis, inflammation, innate immunity, complement, B-cell proliferation and differentiation, regulation of immune response.
- **3:** cytokine, T-cell, chemotaxis, phagocytosis, platelet and angiogenesis terms; skin and keratinocyte repair; collagen and extracellular matrix; hypoxia; oxidative burst.
- **2:** weak links to growth, metabolism, behavior, stress, xenobiotic and antibacterial processes.
- **1:** everything else.

# P. downsi host-response review (run_017)

## What the larvae do
- Adult flies lay eggs in nests. First-instar larvae enter the nestlings' nares and ear canals. Second- and third-instar larvae hide in the nest base by day and come out at night to feed on blood and tissue. Some also feed on brooding females.
- Effects on hosts: blood loss and lower hemoglobin (anemia), tissue wounds, enlarged or deformed nares (beak and nasal-cavity damage), slower growth and body mass gain, possible protein loss, and very high nestling mortality.

## Documented or proposed host defenses
- **Resistance (adaptive immunity).** Adult female medium ground finches at parasitized nests produced P. downsi-binding IgY antibodies, measured by ELISA. Higher antibody levels were marginally associated with fewer parasites. Nestlings showed no measurable antibody response.
- **Resistance (innate immunity and inflammation).** The proposed mechanism is that antibodies speed up local inflammation at the feeding site. This blocks larval access to blood vessels, and the larvae ingest defensive or lytic host molecules. Other Philornis myiasis work describes inflammatory lesions.
- **Tolerance (blood replacement).** Hosts that tolerate the fly appear to make up for blood loss through red blood cell production (erythropoiesis), iron homeostasis and hemostasis or coagulation.
- **Tolerance (energy compensation).** Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate the fly. Their parasitized nestlings beg more and receive more food from parents. Better diet and protein (urban food) are linked to higher nestling survival.
- **Other proposed routes.** These are more speculative: wound repair of skin and epidermis, behavioral responses such as preening, begging and nest sanitation, and glucocorticoid stress physiology (corticosterone has been tested in breeding finches).

## How the scores map to these mechanisms
- **Score 5.** Inflammation, humoral or B-cell and adaptive immunity, erythrocyte production, wound healing.
- **Score 4.** Innate immunity, cytokines, complement, coagulation and platelets, iron homeostasis, phagocytosis, epidermis or skin, T cells.
- **Score 3.** Supporting processes: interferon, NF-kB and JAK-STAT signaling, oxidative stress, hypoxia, angiogenesis, extracellular matrix and collagen, nutrient and energy homeostasis, pain.
- **Score 2.** Weak links: metabolism, hormones, apoptosis, autophagy, growth, craniofacial skeleton, learning.
- **Score 1.** Everything else.

# Philornis downsi host-response review (run_025)

## What the larvae do
- First-instar larvae live in nestling nares and ear canals. Later instars live in nest material and feed at night on nestling blood and tissue. Effects include open skin lesions, swelling, internal bleeding, enlarged nares and beak deformation that can persist into adulthood.
- Blood loss can reach 18-55% overnight. Haemoglobin falls by about 28% and drops with each additional larva. Parasitized nestlings show reduced mass and feather growth, and in-nest mortality is high (mean about 55%).

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Resistance (adaptive humoral immunity):** P. downsi-specific IgY antibodies are elevated in nesting adult females and in parasitized nests (Huber et al. 2010; Koop et al. 2013). Higher antibody levels were associated with fewer parasites. In other nest-fly systems, antibody-mediated, inflammation-based damage to larvae (granulocytes, edema) is proposed. No corticosterone stress response was found.
- **Tolerance:** Mockingbirds tolerate parasitism better than finches (Knutie et al. 2016; McNew et al. 2019), and tolerance is linked to parental provisioning and rainfall or food. Proposed tolerance mechanisms are blood and haemoglobin recovery (erythropoiesis), tissue repair or wound healing, and nutrition (urban protein-rich diet; Knutie et al. 2024). The gene-expression results of that 2024 study were not used.
- **Behavioral:** Adults preen the nares and nest base, female brooding and in-nest attendance changes, nestlings stand on nestmates, and finches self-fumigate with permethrin-treated cotton (Knutie et al. 2014). These behaviors have weak links to any specific GO process.

## Scoring logic
- **5:** adaptive immune response.
- **4:** immune and defense responses, B cell and antibody processes, inflammation, erythropoiesis and iron homeostasis, wound healing, hemostasis and platelets.
- **3:** innate immunity, T cell and cytokine signaling, NF-kB, leukocyte chemotaxis, hypoxia response (from anemia), skin and keratinocyte repair, ECM and collagen, angiogenesis.
- **2:** antibacterial and TLR pathways (secondary infection), nutrient and energy status, oxidative stress, beak bone and cartilage, pain, blood pressure.
- **1:** all other terms.

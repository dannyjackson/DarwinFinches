# Philornis downsi and Galápagos land birds: mechanisms used for scoring

## What the parasite does to hosts
- Adult flies lay eggs in nests. First-instar larvae get into nestlings' nares and ear canals and eat tissue there. Later instars live in the nest material and feed on nestling blood and tissue at night.
- Documented effects on nestlings:
  - blood loss: lower hemoglobin, i.e. anemia
  - open wounds
  - lower growth and fledging success, and high mortality
- Damage to the nares can permanently enlarge or deform them in survivors. This changes song (vocal performance), which affects mating signals and possibly hybridization (Kleindorfer, Galligan and colleagues).

## Host defenses documented or proposed
1. **Resistance (immune).** Adult female Darwin's finches make P. downsi-specific IgY antibodies, a humoral/adaptive response, and antibody levels go down when nests are fumigated (Koop et al. 2013). Antibody response has been linked to lower parasite loads. Innate and inflammatory responses at feeding sites, complement, cytokine signaling and leukocyte recruitment are strong predictions from this. Protection against secondary bacterial infection of wounds is a plausible further step.
2. **Tolerance (physiological).** Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate parasite loads that kill finch nestlings (Knutie et al. 2016). Tolerance is linked to resource availability: anthropogenic or extra food rescues finch nestlings in urban areas. Proposed mechanisms:
   - blood replacement (erythropoiesis, hemopoiesis, iron homeostasis)
   - hemostasis and coagulation against blood feeding
   - wound healing and skin repair (keratinocytes, collagen, extracellular matrix, angiogenesis)
   - coping with hypoxia and oxidative stress from anemia
   - nutrient and energy homeostasis
3. **Behavioral.** Nestlings and brooding females move and preen in response to larvae (video studies, O'Connor et al. 2010). Parents may adjust feeding and brooding, and nests are abandoned under heavy infestation. Neural, pain, stress-hormone, learning and circadian processes (the larvae feed at night) are only weakly linked, so they were scored 2 to 3.
4. Bone and beak development, hormonal signaling, and thermoregulation of nestlings while brooding is disrupted were treated as speculative and scored 2.

Housekeeping, developmental-patterning, neural-development, DNA, RNA and cell-cycle terms were scored 1. Following the instructions, no genomic or transcriptomic results from Galápagos birds were used, including Knutie et al. bioRxiv 2023.

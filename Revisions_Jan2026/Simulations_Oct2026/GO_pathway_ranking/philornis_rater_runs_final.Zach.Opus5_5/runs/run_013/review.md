# Review: host responses to Philornis downsi (avian vampire fly)

## What the larvae do
- Free-living adult flies lay eggs in nests; 1st-instar larvae enter nestlings' nares/ear canals, and later instars feed externally on nestling blood and tissue at night from the nest base. They cause blood loss (lower haemoglobin), open wounds, high nestling mortality (often >50%), and reduced growth.
- Internal feeding by young larvae deforms the beak/naris (enlarged nares) in survivors, which changes vocal (song) performance and possibly feeding later in life.
- Nestlings increase begging; parental provisioning changes; females brooding at night also get bitten.

## Documented / proposed host defenses
- Resistance: P. downsi-specific IgY antibodies in adult and nestling finches, with experimental evidence that parasitism induces an antibody response; higher antibody titres associated with fewer parasites (Huber et al. 2010; Koop et al. 2013). Innate inflammatory/complement responses are a strong a priori prediction.
- Tolerance: Galapagos mockingbirds and some finches tolerate parasitism, e.g. by compensating for blood loss (erythropoiesis, haemoglobin maintenance) and by increased parental provisioning; tolerance is linked to food availability/resource state (Knutie et al. 2016). Wound healing, coagulation, iron homeostasis and stress/energy metabolism are plausible supporting physiology.
- Behavioural: nestling and parental behaviour (preening, nest sanitation, begging, movement) proposed to reduce larvae; nestling behaviour predicts naris deformation. Self-fumigation with cotton treated with permethrin is a human intervention, not a natural defense.
- Morphological/ontogenetic: beak/naris tissue repair and craniofacial development; song learning affected downstream.

## Scoring logic
5 = adaptive (antibody/B-cell) immune response; 4 = innate/inflammatory immunity, coagulation, haematopoiesis/erythropoiesis, iron homeostasis, wound healing, complement; 3 = cytokine, NF-kB, TLR, T-cell, leukocyte migration, oxidative stress, angiogenesis, epidermis/collagen repair, hypoxia; 2 = metabolism/energy, hormonal stress, bone/beak development, learning/hearing, generic signalling cascades; 1 = housekeeping (transcription, translation, DNA repair, cell cycle, synapse, cilia, gametogenesis, etc.).

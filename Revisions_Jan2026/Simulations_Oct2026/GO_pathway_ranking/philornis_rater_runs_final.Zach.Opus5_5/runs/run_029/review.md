# Philornis downsi and host responses: literature summary (run_029)

## What the parasite does
- Larvae of *P. downsi* live in the nest base and feed at night on nestlings' blood and tissue. Brooding females are also fed on. Early instars enter the nares and ear canals and cause tissue damage, which can enlarge or deform the naris and beak and later affect song (Fessl et al. 2006; Galligan and Kleindorfer 2009; Kleindorfer et al. 2019).
- In parasitized nests, nestling mortality is high and fledging success falls. Growth slows: in experiments, primary feathers were shorter and mass tended to be lower (Koop et al. 2011).
- Parasitized nestlings lose blood. Their hemoglobin is lower and they appear anemic; parasitized non-urban nestlings had the lowest hemoglobin and lower blood glucose (Koop et al. 2011; Knutie et al. 2024, physiological data only).
- Breeding females showed no difference in corticosterone or hematocrit (Knutie et al. 2013).
- The authors propose that parasitized nestlings may lose protein or nutrients (Frontiers in Ecology and Evolution 2024).

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Resistance through antibodies:** Adult finches produce IgY specific to *P. downsi*, and higher titers go with fewer parasites. Nestlings make a weak response (Koop et al. 2013). In Knutie et al. 2016, neither finches nor mockingbirds showed a significant antibody response.
- **Tolerance:** Mockingbirds tolerate the fly. Parasitized nestlings beg more and parents provision more (Knutie et al. 2016). Urban finch nestlings kept higher hemoglobin, which suggests they replace lost blood, and the protein-rich diet in urban areas is linked to defense (Knutie et al. 2024).
- **Behavioral:** Video shows nestlings preening, moving at night to avoid larvae, trampling nestmates and eating larvae. Females preen nestlings, probe the nares and nest base, and remove dead young (O'Connor et al. 2010). Proposed but not documented: self-fumigation with *Psidium* leaves.
- **Hemostasis, inflammation and wound repair at feeding sites:** These are strong predictions from blood-feeding and tissue damage, by analogy with other ectoparasite systems.

## How the mechanisms map to scores
- 5: adaptive or antibody immune response, and erythrocyte production (replacing lost blood).
- 4: B-cell processes, innate immune, inflammatory and defense responses, complement, coagulation and platelets, wound healing and response to wounding, hemopoiesis, iron homeostasis.
- 3: cytokine and immune signaling (TLR, NF-kB, JAK-STAT), T cells, phagocytosis and chemotaxis, epidermis, keratinocyte and collagen repair, oxidative stress, hypoxia (from anemia), nociception (drives preening and avoidance), glucose and energy homeostasis.
- 2: angiogenesis, barrier function, responses to bacteria and LPS, hormone and stress responses, learning, memory and circadian rhythm, starvation and nutrient responses, skeletal or beak development, cardiovascular compensation.
- 1: all other terms.

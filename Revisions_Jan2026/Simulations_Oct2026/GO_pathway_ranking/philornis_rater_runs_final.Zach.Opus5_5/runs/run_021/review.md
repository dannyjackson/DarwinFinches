# Review: host responses to Philornis downsi (run_021)

## What the larvae do
- First instars live in the nares and ear canals of nestlings and eat tissue and keratin, which causes enlarged or deformed nares that persist into adulthood and degrade song (Common et al. 2019; Kleindorfer and colleagues, 2019).
- Later instars live in nest material and feed at night on nestlings' and brooding females' blood and tissue by piercing the skin. This causes blood loss (lower haemoglobin), slower growth and high nestling mortality, averaging about 50% and reaching 100% (Fessl et al. 2006; McNew et al. 2019).

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Resistance (immune):** Adult female medium ground finches produce P. downsi-specific IgY antibodies. Higher titres go with lower parasite intensity but not with higher fledging success (Huber et al. 2010; Koop et al. 2013). This is a humoral, B-cell response that probably targets the larval feeding interface, i.e. skin and wound inflammation, coagulation, and cytokines.
- **Tolerance:** Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate the parasite. Nestlings lose haemoglobin but still fledge, and tolerance depends on parental provisioning and food availability in a given year (McNew et al. 2019; Knutie et al. 2016). This points to compensating for blood loss through erythropoiesis and iron homeostasis, wound repair, and energy and nutrient status.
- **Stress physiology:** Fumigation did not change female corticosterone or haematocrit (Knutie et al. 2013).
- **Behavior:** Females brood standing upright more, nestlings preen off larvae and stand on nest mates, and finches self-fumigate with Psidium galapageium leaves. None of these clearly improves fledging success.

## Scoring logic
- **5:** Antibody/B-cell activation, adaptive immune response, immune response.
- **4:** Haematopoiesis and erythropoiesis, iron homeostasis, wound healing, coagulation and platelets, inflammation, innate and general immune processes, T-cell help.
- **3:** Cytokine and NF-kB/JAK-STAT signalling, phagocytosis and chemotaxis, oxidative stress and hypoxia (anaemia), angiogenesis, keratinocyte and epidermis development (skin and beak keratin), ECM and collagen repair, pain, starvation responses and energy and glucose homeostasis (growth and tolerance), steroid hormone signalling.
- **2:** Weaker supporting processes.
- **1:** Generic cellular, developmental and neural processes.

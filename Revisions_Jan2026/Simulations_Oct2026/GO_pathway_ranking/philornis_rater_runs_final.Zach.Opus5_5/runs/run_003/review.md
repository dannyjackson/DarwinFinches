# Philornis downsi and Galápagos land birds: literature review used for scoring

## What the larvae do to hosts
- Adult flies lay eggs in host nests. First-instar larvae get into nestlings' nares and ear canals, and later instars live in the nest material and feed on nestlings' blood and tissue at night (Fessl et al. 2006, Parasitology).
- The effects documented on nestlings are blood loss, lower haemoglobin and anaemia, slower growth, high mortality (often more than 50% of nests), and a deformed naris/beak. A deformed beak changes adult song, which can affect mate choice and hybridization (Dudaniec et al. 2006; Fessl et al. 2006; Kleindorfer et al. 2019 news releases).
- The larvae cause open wounds and tissue damage. This is consistent with pathology described for other *Philornis* myiasis (J. Wildl. Dis. 22:224).

## Host defenses
- **Resistance (immune).** Nestlings and adult females make P. downsi-specific IgY antibodies. Higher antibody titres in females go with fewer parasites, which points to an acquired humoral response (Huber et al. 2010; Koop et al. 2013).
- **Tolerance.** Mockingbirds tolerate the parasite better than finches, and this is linked to brood provisioning and resources (Knutie et al. 2016). Food supplementation and urban resources improve tolerance and nestling survival (Knutie 2020; urban-living study). Recovery from blood loss through erythropoiesis, plus wound repair, are the obvious physiological tolerance mechanisms.
- **Stress physiology.** Breeding finches show corticosterone responses to parasitism (Experimental test of effects on corticosterone).
- **Behaviour.** Female in-nest attendance and brooding predict parasite numbers, and video studies show nestlings and adults responding to larvae (preening, moving) (Kleindorfer group; O'Connor et al. video analysis). Nestling begging and parental provisioning also matter.

## How the review was turned into scores
- **5:** humoral/adaptive immunity, B-cell activation, immune and inflammatory response, wounding and wound healing, blood coagulation, haemopoiesis and erythropoiesis, broad defence response.
- **4:** innate immunity, regulation of immune or inflammatory response, complement, platelets, iron homeostasis, hypoxia, epidermis and skin.
- **3:** cytokines, T cells, NF-kB, oxidative stress, angiogenesis, ECM and collagen, hormone (glucocorticoid) signalling, starvation and energy homeostasis, pain perception.
- **2:** general stress, metabolism, growth, bone (beak), apoptosis, autophagy, broad signalling pathways, learning and memory.
- **1:** housekeeping, neural development, reproduction and other unrelated processes.

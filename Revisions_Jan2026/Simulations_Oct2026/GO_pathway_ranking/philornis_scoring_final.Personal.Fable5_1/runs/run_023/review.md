# Run 023 — Literature review: host responses to *Philornis downsi*

## What the larvae do to hosts
- First-instar larvae live in the nares and ear canals of nestlings; later instars live in the nest base and emerge at dusk/dawn/night to feed on blood and tissue (semi-haematophagous). Infestation can begin within ~24 h of hatching; brooding females are occasionally fed upon.
- Direct effects: blood loss and anaemia (each larva lowers nestling haemoglobin; ~25% blood loss is lethal), open lesions/swelling of nares and beak (nares ~39% larger; beak malformation that can persist to adulthood and affects song/foraging), reduced body mass (~50%), reduced feather growth, lower plasma glucose in dying nestlings, and in-nest mortality averaging ~50–55% (3–100%).

## Documented or proposed host defenses
1. **Immune resistance** — Adult (brooding) medium ground finches mount *P. downsi*-binding IgY antibodies, higher in nesting females than in pre-nesting birds and higher in parasitized nests; antibody level is associated with lower parasite intensity but not clearly with fledging. Nestling antibody responses are not detectable before fledging; maternal antibody transfer is proposed. Inflammation of skin at feeding sites, skin antimicrobial/immune cells and reactive oxygen/nitrogen species are proposed resistance routes; secondary bacterial infection of lesions is a plausible correlated process.
2. **Tolerance / blood recovery** — Galápagos mockingbirds (and larger-bodied hosts) fledge young despite similar larval loads; tolerance tracks parental provisioning and rainfall (labile), and parasitized mockingbird nestlings beg more, are fed more and have higher plasma glucose. Tolerance therefore implicates energy/nutrient homeostasis, glucose metabolism, growth regulation and, above all, replacement of lost red blood cells (haematopoiesis/erythropoiesis, iron handling, hypoxia responses) and haemostasis/wound repair at feeding lesions.
3. **Behavioral defenses** — Nestlings stand on nest-mates to avoid larvae, nestlings and females preen off and eat larvae, parents allopreen, females stand more and brood less in parasitized nests; begging changes with parasitism. These are weakly represented by nervous-system GO terms (nociception, learning/memory, circadian timing of nocturnal attack scored 2–3).
4. **Evolutionary/other** — Increased tree-finch hybridization with lowest parasite loads in hybrids; nest fumigation/self-fumigation with permethrin-treated cotton (behavioral exploitation, not physiology).

## Scoring logic
- 5: immune/inflammatory response, adaptive & innate immunity, B-cell activation/BCR signaling, wound healing/response to wounding, blood coagulation, haemopoiesis/erythrocyte differentiation.
- 4: cytokine production/signaling (IL-1, IL-6, IL-8, IL-12, IL-17, TNF, chemokines), TLR/NF-kB/JAK-STAT signaling, complement, phagocytosis, T-cell activation/differentiation, leukocyte adhesion, inflammasome, oxidative burst, platelet activation, iron homeostasis, response to hypoxia, ECM/collagen organization, skin/epidermis/keratinocyte development, glucose & energy homeostasis.
- 3: supporting processes — chemotaxis, angiogenesis/VEGF/TGF-beta, epithelial proliferation/migration, oxidative/ROS and general stress responses, nutrient/starvation responses, insulin/IGF/TOR signaling, glycolysis/gluconeogenesis/glycogen, lipid storage, cartilage (beak), heart rate/blood pressure, pain perception, interferons, antibacterial defense.
- 2: generic signaling, proliferation/apoptosis, metabolism, hormone responses, circadian, cognition, adhesion.
- 1: housekeeping, mitosis, RNA processing, neural/organ development, cilia, gametogenesis etc.

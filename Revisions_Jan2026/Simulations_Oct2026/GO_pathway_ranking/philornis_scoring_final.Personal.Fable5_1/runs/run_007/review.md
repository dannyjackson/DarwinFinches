# Literature review: host responses to Philornis downsi (run_007)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- *P. downsi* adults are free-living; females oviposit in nests (sometimes in nestling nares). First-instar larvae feed subcutaneously inside the nares/nasal cavity; later instars live in the nest base and emerge at night to feed semi-hematophagously on blood and tissue fluid of nestlings (and brooding females).
- Consequences documented in Darwin's finches and other Galápagos passerines: blood loss of ~18–55% of blood volume, reduced haemoglobin/anaemia, multiple skin wounds and secondary infection, grossly deteriorated/enlarged nares (persisting into adulthood and altering song and mate choice), reduced growth (mass, tarsus), and nestling mortality of ~16–100% (mean ~55%).

## Host defenses documented or proposed
1. **Resistance – adaptive immunity.** Adult female medium ground finches (and at least eight finch species) mount *P. downsi*-binding IgY antibody responses after exposure; higher maternal antibody correlated with fewer nest parasites, but the response did not protect nestlings; nestling antibody responses were negligible in finches and mockingbirds. Proposed maternal antibody transfer via yolk.
2. **Resistance/inflammation at feeding sites.** Larval feeding creates wounds; innate inflammatory responses, cytokine signalling, leukocyte recruitment, phagocytosis and defence against secondary bacterial infection are the obvious a priori local responses, as is wound healing/re-epithelialisation, haemostasis (coagulation, thrombocyte activation) and skin/epidermal barrier integrity.
3. **Tolerance – haematological compensation.** Because blood loss and anaemia are the main pathology, erythropoiesis/hemopoiesis, iron homeostasis and responses to hypoxia are core predicted tolerance mechanisms.
4. **Tolerance – energetic compensation.** Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate the fly: parasitized nestlings beg more and parents provision more, offsetting energy loss; tolerance collapses in dry years when provisioning is low. Finches do not compensate (Green Warbler-Finch females even provisioned less). Higher-protein diet (urban finches) correlates with better survival. Hence nutrient sensing, glucose/energy homeostasis, insulin/IGF/TOR signalling, starvation responses and growth regulation are plausible tolerance components.
5. **Stress physiology.** Adult female corticosterone, body condition and haematocrit were unaffected by experimental parasite treatment; glucocorticoid/hormone signalling is a plausible but weak link.
6. **Behavioural defences.** Nestling preening of larvae and standing on nest-mates (no fitness benefit), adult self-fumigation with permethrin-treated cotton, nest-material choice, brooding changes; nociception is the plausible physiological substrate for larval detection. Neural/behavioural GO terms were therefore scored low (1–3).

## Scoring rationale summary
- 5: immune system process, innate/adaptive/immune response, defense response, inflammatory response, wound healing (and positive regulation), response to wounding, erythrocyte differentiation (and positive regulation).
- 4: cytokine/TNF/IL-1/IL-6/IL-8/chemokine production and signalling, NF-kB, TLR, inflammasome, complement, B/T cell activation, hemopoiesis, hematopoietic progenitor differentiation, iron homeostasis, hypoxia responses, coagulation, platelet activation/aggregation, skin and epidermis development, energy homeostasis, regulation of immune/inflammatory processes.
- 3: supporting processes (chemotaxis, phagocytosis, ROS generation, angiogenesis/VEGF, fibroblast and epithelial proliferation, ECM/collagen, keratinocyte differentiation, glucose/insulin/IGF/nutrient and starvation responses, hormone signalling, JAK-STAT, defence against bacteria, response to stress, nociception).
- 2: indirect or speculative links (general metabolism/lipid, MAPK/TOR/autophagy, growth regulation, skeletal/cartilage development for naris morphology, antiviral terms, xenobiotic response, cell migration/adhesion, neuropeptide signalling).
- 1: housekeeping and unrelated development/neuro terms.

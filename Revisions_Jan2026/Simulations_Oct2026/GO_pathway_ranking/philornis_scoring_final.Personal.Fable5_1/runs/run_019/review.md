# Run 019 - Literature review: host responses to Philornis downsi

## What the larvae do to hosts
- Larvae (3 instars) feed on blood and tissue of nestlings (and brooding females). First/second instars live subcutaneously inside the nares/beak, consuming keratin, blood and tissue; later instars feed externally at night from the nest base (ears, belly, wings, nares). Lesions, swelling and subcutaneous bleeding are documented.
- Blood loss and anemia: parasitized nestlings have ~28% lower hemoglobin (~0.8 units per larva); nestlings losing ~25% of blood volume die within hours. Hemoglobin reduction is seen in both finches and mockingbirds, every year studied.
- Growth: ~56% lower body mass, ~30% shorter feathers; slower growth, worse in dry years.
- Mortality: mean ~55% in-nest nestling mortality (3-100%); survival falls ~2% per larva.
- Naris/beak deformation persists into adulthood (~34% of adults with enlarged nares), altering song pitch, pairing success, foraging niche and body condition.
- No measurable corticosterone response in females at parasitized nests.

## Documented or proposed host defenses
1. **Adaptive/humoral immunity (resistance)**: adult medium ground finches mount P. downsi-specific antibody (IgY) responses, higher in nesting females; nestling antibody responses are weak/undetectable in several studies. Innate defenses are proposed where antibody responses are absent. Pro-inflammatory responses and immunoglobulin production are discussed as resistance mechanisms (Knutie et al. 2024 abstract; only the qualitative abstract framing was used, no genomic results).
2. **Tolerance of blood loss**: compensatory erythropoiesis / red blood cell production and energy compensation; Galapagos mockingbirds tolerate the parasite (no fitness loss) via increased nestling begging and parental provisioning, which offsets energy loss; tolerance depends on food availability (rain, diet protein/δ15N). Plasma glucose differences noted between treatments.
3. **Wound repair / tissue integrity**: tissue, skin, keratin and beak damage imply wound healing, coagulation, epidermal/keratinocyte processes and extracellular matrix repair as predicted responses.
4. **Behavioral defenses**: nestlings standing on siblings, preening/eating larvae, females probing nest base, reduced brooding; none shown to improve fledging.
5. **Energetic/metabolic**: hypoxia tolerance (low hemoglobin), glucose/energy homeostasis, nutrient-dependent tolerance.

## Scoring logic
- 5: immune system, innate/adaptive/humoral (B cell, antibody) responses, inflammation, wound healing/response to wounding, erythrocyte differentiation.
- 4: cytokine/TNF/IL/NF-kB/JAK-STAT signaling, complement, TLR, T-cell activation, hemopoiesis, iron homeostasis, coagulation, hypoxia response, skin/epidermis/keratinocyte development, energy homeostasis, regulation of immune processes.
- 3: supporting processes: angiogenesis, ECM/collagen, phagocytosis, chemotaxis, oxidative stress, glucose/nutrient homeostasis, platelet function, epithelial proliferation, general stress response, pain perception.
- 2: broad metabolic/signaling/stress/apoptosis/autophagy terms, antiviral/antibacterial-specific defenses, growth/proliferation, lipid/energy metabolism.
- 1: everything else (neural, developmental, cell-cycle, RNA processing, cilia, etc.).

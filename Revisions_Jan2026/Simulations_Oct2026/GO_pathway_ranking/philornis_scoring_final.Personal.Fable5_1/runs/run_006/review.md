# Literature review: host responses to *Philornis downsi* (run_006)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- Adult flies lay eggs in nests; first-instar larvae live in the nasal cavities and ear canals of nestlings, later instars retreat to the nest base by day and feed at night on nestling blood and tissue ("semi-hematophagous"). Larvae also feed on the brood patch/underside of incubating and brooding females (Common et al. 2024, Frontiers Ecol Evol; Huber et al. 2010).
- Consequences for nestlings: blood loss and anemia (hemoglobin reduced ~14-40% in parasitized finch and mockingbird nestlings; up to 28% reductions reviewed), reduced mass (up to 56%), reduced feather growth, lower plasma glucose in mockingbirds, nestling mortality averaging ~50-55% (3-100%), each extra larva ~2% lower survival; nestlings losing ~25% of blood die within hours (Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016 review; Knutie et al. 2016 Ecology; Dudaniec et al. 2006).
- Tissue destruction in the nares: larvae chew keratin and soft tissue, producing permanently enlarged nares / deformed beaks in fledglings and adults; deformed beaks lower song pitch, delay pairing and are linked to hybridization in tree finches (Kleindorfer et al. 2019, Proc R Soc B; Science News coverage).
- Open wounds in skin, nares and ear canal imply tissue injury, haemorrhage, local inflammation and exposure to secondary microbial infection.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
1. **Acquired (antibody-mediated) immunity**: medium ground finches produce *P. downsi*-binding antibodies; nesting birds had 1.7x higher levels than pre-nesting, breeding females higher than males (brood-patch exposure); experimentally parasitized females mount a parasite-induced antibody response, although this did not increase nestling survival; nestling antibody response was weak/undetectable (Huber et al. 2010 PLoS ONE; Koop et al. 2013 PLoS ONE; Knutie et al. 2016). Maternal transfer of antibodies to eggs was proposed. Cost of immune response (energy) discussed.
2. **Hematological compensation / anemia response**: lowered hemoglobin is the central physiological cost; erythropoiesis, iron handling and hypoxia responses are the obvious a priori compensatory mechanisms.
3. **Tolerance through energy compensation**: Galapagos mockingbirds tolerate the fly; parasitized nestlings beg more and parents feed more, offsetting damage; tolerance varied with rainfall/provisioning. Finches (medium ground finch, small tree finch, green warbler-finch) did not show compensatory provisioning (Knutie et al. 2016; McNew et al. 2019 Oecologia; Heimpel et al. 2017; Pike et al. 2023 J Ornithol). Growth, nutrient/energy homeostasis and glucose regulation are therefore relevant tolerance mechanisms.
4. **Wound healing / tissue repair**: necessary for recovery from larval feeding lesions and naris damage; keratin/beak tissue regeneration.
5. **Behavioral defenses**: nestlings stand on siblings, preen and eat larvae; females brood less, stand erect, probe nest base; finches rub *Psidium galapageium* leaves (repellent terpenoids) on feathers (Cimadom et al. 2016 PLoS ONE); acceptance of permethrin-treated cotton. Nocturnal larval feeding and nestling thermoregulation are also discussed. These behavioral and sensory traits have only indirect links to GO biological-process terms (nervous system, pain perception, circadian rhythm, cognition).
6. **No stress hormone response**: female corticosterone did not respond to parasitism (reviewed in Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016).

## Scoring logic applied
- 5: adaptive/humoral immunity (B cells, antibody signalling), inflammatory and general immune response, erythrocyte differentiation, wound healing / response to wounding.
- 4: innate immune activation and regulation, cytokine production/signalling, complement, platelets/coagulation, hemopoiesis, iron homeostasis, hypoxia response, epidermis/skin/keratinocyte processes, T-cell help.
- 3: supporting processes: chemotaxis, phagocytosis, NF-kB/TLR/JAK-STAT signalling, specific cytokines, ECM/collagen/epithelial repair, fibroblast and endothelial proliferation/angiogenesis, energy/glucose/nutrient homeostasis, oxidative stress, pain perception, defense to bacteria (secondary infection), general response to stress, roof-of-mouth/beak-relevant development.
- 2: weak/speculative: generic development (skeletal, cartilage, bone, growth), hormone/steroid signalling, xenobiotic response, circadian rhythm, learning/memory/cognition, sound perception (song), thermogenesis, zinc homeostasis, amino-acid/lipid metabolism, apoptosis regulation, cell migration/adhesion, MAPK/PI3K/TGF-beta/Wnt signalling.
- 1: housekeeping cell biology (transcription, splicing, ribosome, cilia, mitosis, DNA repair, neural/synaptic, cardiac, renal, gonad development etc.).

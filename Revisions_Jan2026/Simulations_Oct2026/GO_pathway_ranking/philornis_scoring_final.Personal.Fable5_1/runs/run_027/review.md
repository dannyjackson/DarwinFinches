# Literature review: host responses to Philornis downsi (run_027)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- P. downsi is semi-hematophagous. First-instar larvae live inside the nares (and feather quills) of nestlings; second and third instars attach externally, feeding mainly at night on blood and tissue of nestlings and brooding females (McNew & Clayton 2018; Fessl et al. 2006).
- Feeding causes blood loss: parasitized nestlings have lower haemoglobin (about 14% lower in older medium ground finch nestlings, ~40% in mockingbirds), reduced growth (mass, tarsus, primary feather), and nestling mortality averaging ~50% and up to 100% (Knutie et al. 2016; Dudaniec et al. 2006; Fessl et al. 2006).
- Feeding in the nares produces wounds and permanent naris/beak deformation, which carries into adulthood and alters song pitch, mate attraction and hybridization in tree finches (Kleindorfer et al. 2019).
- Parasitism may deplete nestling blood protein (C:N ratio) (Pike et al. 2024).

## Documented or proposed host defenses
1. Humoral immunity (resistance): adult medium ground finches mount a P. downsi-binding antibody (IgY) response during nesting, stronger in brooding females (Huber et al. 2010); Koop et al. 2013 showed an experimentally induced antibody response in females, inversely related to parasite load but not linked to nestling survival. Nestlings of finches and mockingbirds show no detectable antibody response (Knutie et al. 2016; McNew & Clayton 2018).
2. Innate / inflammatory / wound responses: wounds at the nares and skin, blood feeding and tissue damage predict inflammation, cytokine signalling, leukocyte recruitment, complement, phagocytosis, haemostasis (coagulation / thrombocyte activation), wound healing, re-epithelialization, keratinocyte differentiation, ECM/collagen remodelling and angiogenesis. Skin/scale barrier is suggested to block larval penetration in adult legs (Huber et al. 2010).
3. Haematological compensation: recovery from anaemia via erythropoiesis/haematopoiesis, iron homeostasis and hypoxia responses are strong a priori predictions from the haemoglobin loss.
4. Tolerance via compensatory feeding: Galapagos mockingbird nestlings beg more and parents provision more, offsetting damage; finches do not (begging, provisioning, plasma glucose unchanged). Tolerance is lower in dry, food-poor years (Knutie et al. 2016; McNew et al. 2019). This implicates nutrient/energy homeostasis, glucose, insulin/IGF/TOR signalling, starvation responses and lipid/protein metabolism.
5. Stress physiology: corticosterone did not differ between fumigated and parasitized breeding females (Knutie et al. 2013), so hormonal stress responses are only weakly supported.
6. Behavioural defenses: preening/standing on nest mates (rare, ineffective); females standing more may impair nest thermoregulation; self-medication with Psidium galapageium leaves that repel flies and slow larval growth (Cimadom et al. 2016); nest timing/height avoidance (McNew & Clayton 2018).

## Scoring rationale
- 5: wound response, wound healing, inflammatory response, innate/adaptive/immune response, defense response, B cell activation (antibody response documented), erythrocyte differentiation (direct compensation for documented haemoglobin loss).
- 4: core components of these (cytokines, NF-kB, TLR, TNF/IL-1/IL-6/IL-8/IL-17/IFN-gamma signalling, complement, phagocytosis, T/B cell processes, leukocyte adhesion, chemotaxis, coagulation/platelets, hypoxia, iron homeostasis, haematopoiesis, skin/epidermis, keratinocytes, ECM/collagen) and strong predictions from tolerance (nutrient/starvation responses, energy and glucose homeostasis).
- 3: supporting processes (growth-factor and angiogenic signalling, epithelial proliferation/migration, oxidative stress, metabolic/insulin/TOR signalling, hormone responses, thermogenesis, pain, lipid/amino acid metabolism, antibacterial defense of wounds).
- 2: weak/indirect (general signalling, adhesion, cytoskeleton, apoptosis, autophagy, circadian, cognition/learning, skeletal/cartilage development, antiviral responses).
- 1: no specific link (housekeeping, neural, developmental patterning, RNA/DNA processing, cell cycle, cilia, etc.). GO:0160240 had no term name and was scored 1.

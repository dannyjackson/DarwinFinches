# Literature review: host responses to Philornis downsi (run_008)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- Adult flies oviposit in nests of nearly all Galapagos land birds (12 of 17 Darwin's finch species, mockingbirds, warbler finches, etc.). First-instar larvae live in the nares and ear canals of nestlings; later instars live in the nest base and feed at night on blood and serous fluid/tissue of nestlings and brooding females.
- Documented consequences: nestling mortality of ~16-100% (often >50%), reduced haemoglobin/haematocrit (anaemia), reduced body mass and feather growth, open wounds, enlarged/deformed nares that persist to adulthood and alter male song (lower peak frequency, blurred species mating signals, hybridisation risk), and reduced fledging success.

## Host defences documented or proposed
1. Resistance via adaptive immunity: medium ground finch adults and nestlings/mothers mount P. downsi-specific antibody (IgY) responses, higher during nesting and in brooding females; in finches the antibody response did not clearly protect fledging. Mockingbirds show weak antibody response.
2. Innate/inflammatory response: proposed as the earlier, more effective defence against blood-feeding larvae; wound and skin-barrier biology at bite/naris sites.
3. Tolerance via compensation of blood loss: haematopoiesis/erythropoiesis, iron handling, coagulation and hypoxia responses (anaemia is the main documented pathology).
4. Tolerance via energetics/nutrition: mockingbird nestlings beg more and parents provision more; plasma glucose and body condition link to survival; better-fed (urban/protein-rich diet) nestlings survive parasitism far better. Predicts glucose/nutrient homeostasis, growth (IGF/insulin) and starvation responses.
5. Stress physiology: corticosterone tested in breeding females (no significant effect detected, but glucocorticoid/hormone signalling remains an a priori candidate).
6. Behavioural defences: nestling avoidance of larvae and nestling reactivity (predicts naris damage), begging, parental brooding/allopreening changes, nest-building/material choice (self-fumigation with treated cotton), pain/irritation perception; nervous-system terms are scored as plausible but indirect.
7. Secondary infection of wounds: antibacterial defence a plausible supporting process.

## Scoring logic
5: wound healing/response to wounding, inflammatory and immune responses (innate, adaptive), erythrocyte differentiation.
4: core components of these (cytokine/TNF/IL production and signalling, NF-kB, TLR, JAK-STAT, complement, T/B cell activation, hemopoiesis, iron homeostasis, hypoxia response, coagulation).
3: supporting processes (glucose/energy/nutrient homeostasis, starvation response, hormone/glucocorticoid signalling, skin/epidermis/keratinocyte, ECM/collagen/angiogenesis, chemotaxis/phagocytosis, oxidative stress, stress response, IGF growth signalling, pain perception).
2: weak/indirect (general lipid/metabolic, cell migration/adhesion, MAPK/PI3K/TOR, apoptosis regulation, nervous-system behaviour, circadian, bone/cartilage).
1: no specific link (cell cycle, transcription machinery, RNA processing, cilia, organ development, neural development, etc.).

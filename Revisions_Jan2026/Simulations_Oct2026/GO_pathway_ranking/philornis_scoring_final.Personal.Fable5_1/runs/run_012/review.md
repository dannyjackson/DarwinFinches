# Run 012 — literature review: host responses to *Philornis downsi*

## What the larvae do to hosts
- *P. downsi* adults are free-living; larvae live in the nest and are semi-hematophagous. First-instar larvae feed inside the nares of nestlings; later instars feed externally at night on blood and tissue (ears, belly, wings, nares), burrowing into skin and causing swelling, open lesions and internal bleeding (Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016 review).
- Consequences: substantial blood loss and anemia (hemoglobin ~28% lower in parasitized nestlings; ~0.8 g/dL drop per larva; Dudaniec et al. 2006, Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016), reduced body mass and feather growth, naris enlargement/beak deformation that can persist to adulthood, and in-nest mortality averaging ~55% (range 3–100%) across Darwin's finch species; contributes to declines of small tree finch, green warbler finch and mangrove finch. Twelve of 17 finch species are hosts; nearly all Galápagos land birds are attacked (Fessl et al. 2018; Frontiers 2025 review).
- Adults may also be bitten (Koop et al.), but nestlings carry the main burden.

## Documented or proposed host defenses
1. **Immune (resistance):** Adult medium ground finches mount a *P. downsi*-specific antibody (IgY) response, higher during nesting than before and higher in experimentally parasitized nests (Koop et al. 2013; Huber et al. 2010). Whether antibodies reduce larval numbers or improve fledging is unclear; Knutie et al. 2016 found neither finches nor mockingbirds mounted a significant antibody response in their experiment. Local inflammatory/wound responses at feeding lesions (innate immunity, cytokines, inflammasome, complement) are the predicted first-line response to a blood-feeding ectoparasite, and secondary bacterial infection of lesions is a plausible downstream problem.
2. **Tolerance (compensation):** Galápagos mockingbirds suffer no fitness cost at similar parasite densities: parasitized nestlings beg more, parents provision more, offsetting blood loss (Knutie et al. 2016). This predicts hematopoietic/erythropoietic recovery, iron homeostasis, hypoxia responses, energy/nutrient metabolism and growth signaling as tolerance components. Urban finches with richer diets show improved survival under parasitism (Knutie et al. 2024 — only the general finding, not genomic results, was used).
3. **Behavioral:** nestlings stand on siblings to escape larvae at the nest base and preen/eat larvae; females spend less time brooding, stand erect, probe the nest base and eat larvae (video studies, Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016; O'Connor et al.). Self-fumigation with insecticide-treated cotton reduces larvae (Knutie et al. 2014); finches also rub *Psidium galapageium* leaves on feathers (Cimadom et al. 2016). Begging intensity may not honestly signal need in parasitized nests. These give modest a priori weight to cognition/learning/neuropeptide terms, but the GO terms are not specific.
4. **Physiology:** no corticosterone stress response was detected in females; plasma glucose did not rise in finches (Knutie et al. 2016). Wound healing, skin/epidermal repair, hemostasis/coagulation at bite sites, and pain/nociception are reasonable supporting predictions.

## How these mapped to scores
- 5: innate/adaptive/immune and inflammatory response terms, B-cell activation/differentiation and BCR signaling (antibody response), response to wounding and wound healing.
- 4: cytokine production and signaling (TNF, IL-1, IL-6, IL-8, IL-17, IL-12, chemokines), NF-κB and TLR pathways, inflammasome, T-cell terms, complement, coagulation/platelets, erythrocyte differentiation and hemopoiesis, iron homeostasis, hypoxia response.
- 3: interferon/JAK-STAT, antibacterial defense, phagocytosis, chemotaxis, oxidative-stress/ROS, general stress response, skin/epidermis and keratinocyte terms, ECM/collagen/basement membrane, angiogenesis and endothelial repair, blood-pressure regulation, nutrient/starvation and glucose homeostasis, pain perception, steroid-hormone receptor signaling.
- 2: metabolic/energy terms (tolerance via compensation), growth-factor and MAPK/PI3K signaling, apoptosis, cell adhesion/migration, epithelial and bone/cartilage development (beak deformation), ion/zinc homeostasis, antiviral terms (generic immunity), learning/memory/cognition (behavioral defenses), autophagy, xenobiotic response.
- 1: everything else (housekeeping, neural development, cilia, cell cycle, transcription, RNA processing, etc.).

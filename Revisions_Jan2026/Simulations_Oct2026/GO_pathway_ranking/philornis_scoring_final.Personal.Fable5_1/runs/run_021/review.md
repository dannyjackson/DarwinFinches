# Literature review: host responses to *Philornis downsi* (run_021)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- *P. downsi* adults are free-living; females oviposit in nests. First-instar larvae live inside nestling nares (and ear canals), feeding on tissue and blood; 2nd/3rd instars live in the nest base and emerge at night to feed on nestling blood (and occasionally on brooding adults, possibly via the brood patch).
- Documented pathology: blood loss and reduced hemoglobin/anemia (c. 0.8 g/dL per added larva in small ground finches; nestlings losing ~25% blood volume die within hours), open skin lesions, subcutaneous bleeding and swelling, enlarged/deformed nares and beak (nares ~39% larger; in extreme cases loss of the upper beak), ear-canal and orbit scarring in other Philornis systems, reduced body mass, tarsus and feather growth, lower plasma glucose (mockingbirds), and in-nest mortality averaging ~55% (3-100%). Survivors can carry permanent naris deformation that alters song and mate choice.
- No clear effects on adult condition or corticosterone have been found, though adult females spend less time brooding and more time standing/probing nest material when heavily parasitized.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
1. **Immune resistance (humoral/adaptive).** Adult female medium ground finches raise *P. downsi*-specific antibodies (IgY) during nesting and more so in parasitized nests (Huber et al. 2010; Koop et al. 2013), showing an acquired immune response to a novel parasite. Nestlings of finches and mockingbirds generally show weak or no detectable antibody response (Knutie et al. 2016); immune response in adults was not clearly linked to nestling survival. Immune activation is proposed to carry an energetic cost.
2. **Innate/inflammatory response at feeding sites.** Lesions, swelling and subcutaneous bleeding are documented; by general knowledge of ectoparasite myiasis this entails inflammation (cytokines, chemokines, heterophil/macrophage recruitment, complement, pattern-recognition signaling, respiratory burst), hemostasis/coagulation and subsequent wound healing, re-epithelialization, extracellular-matrix/collagen deposition and scar formation.
3. **Tolerance via energetic compensation.** Galápagos mockingbird nestlings beg more when parasitized and parents provision more, offsetting blood/energy loss with no loss of fledging success; Darwin's finches do not show this (Knutie et al. 2016; McNew & Clayton 2018). This predicts involvement of appetite/neuropeptide signaling, glucose and energy homeostasis, nutrient-sensing, insulin/IGF growth signaling and compensatory growth.
4. **Tolerance via hematological compensation.** Reduced hemoglobin and anemia predict compensatory erythropoiesis/hematopoiesis, iron homeostasis, and hypoxia responses as core tolerance mechanisms.
5. **Behavioral defenses.** Nestlings standing on siblings to avoid larvae at the nest base; occasional preening/eating of larvae by nestlings and females; females probing nest material; possible use of insect-repellent guava leaves; nest-site choice (height, mixed-species aggregations as ecological trap); seasonal timing. Behavioral responses to larval bites plausibly involve pain/itch perception.
6. **Beak/naris development.** Larval damage to nares/beak and later repair of nasal and facial tissue implicates craniofacial skin, cartilage and bone development/repair.
7. **Stress physiology.** Glucocorticoid (corticosterone) responses were examined in adults with no clear effect; still a plausible host axis.

Not used: transcriptomic/genomic studies of Galápagos birds (e.g., bioRxiv 2023.03.06.531275) were excluded per instructions.

## Scoring logic
- 5: humoral/adaptive immunity (B cell activation, adaptive and general immune response), inflammatory response, wound healing/response to wounding, erythrocyte differentiation.
- 4: core components of these (T cell help, B cell differentiation/BCR signaling, cytokine production and signaling, complement, innate immunity, regulation of inflammation, coagulation, hemopoiesis, iron homeostasis, hypoxia response, glucose/energy homeostasis, IGF signaling, skin/epidermis/keratinocyte differentiation, ECM and collagen organization).
- 3: supporting processes (specific cytokine/TLR/NF-kB/JAK-STAT signaling, phagocytosis, chemotaxis, angiogenesis, epithelial proliferation/migration, oxidative stress/ROS, insulin signaling, starvation/nutrient responses, gluconeogenesis, pain perception, neuropeptide signaling, steroid hormone signaling, hemostasis via thrombocytes, TGF-beta signaling).
- 2: weak/indirect (general MAPK/PI3K signaling, adhesion and migration, apoptosis regulation, lipid/energy metabolism, autophagy, bone/cartilage development, circadian rhythm, thermogenesis, zinc transport, general stress responses).
- 1: no specific link (neural development, synaptic transmission, cell cycle, DNA repair, RNA processing, translation, organelle organization, reproduction, embryonic patterning, viral defense, etc.).

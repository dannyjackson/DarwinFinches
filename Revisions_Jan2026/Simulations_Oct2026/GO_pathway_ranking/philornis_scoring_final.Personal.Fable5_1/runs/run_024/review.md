# Literature review: host responses of Galápagos land birds to *Philornis downsi* (run_024)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- *P. downsi* adults are free-living; larvae are obligate nest parasites. First instars feed inside the nares of nestlings; later instars live in the nest base and feed at night on blood and tissue of the abdomen, wings, ear canals and other exposed skin (Fessl et al. 2006; Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016 review).
- Damage: enlarged / deformed nares (persisting into adulthood, sometimes with loss of the upper beak), open skin lesions, swelling and subcutaneous bleeding, anemia (hemoglobin ~28% lower; ~0.8 g/dl per larva; Dudaniec et al. 2006), reduced mass (~56%) and feather growth (~30%), and in-nest mortality averaging ~55%; survival falls ~2% per larva (Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016; Koop et al. 2011, 2013).
- Mortality is driven by blood loss and tissue damage; larvae also impair begging.

## Host defences documented or proposed
1. **Immune (resistance)**: Adult female medium ground finches show elevated *P. downsi*-binding antibody (IgY) during nesting and at parasitized nests (Huber et al. 2010; Koop et al. 2013 "Experimental demonstration of a parasite-induced immune response in wild birds"). Nestling antibody responses are low and do not ameliorate effects (Knutie et al. 2016, mockingbirds vs finches). Inflammation/swelling at feeding sites is reported. Thus: adaptive (B-cell / antibody) response, innate inflammatory response, cytokine signalling, complement, leukocyte recruitment and phagocytosis are the core immune axes.
2. **Tolerance**: Galápagos mockingbirds (*Mimus parvulus*) maintain fledging success at similar parasite loads; tolerance is linked to compensatory feeding (increased nestling begging and parental provisioning), is lower in dry years, and is mitigated by rainfall/food (Knutie et al. 2016; McNew et al. 2019 "Annual environmental variation influences host tolerance"). Urban finches with better food access also fare better. Physiological correlates of tolerance: compensation for blood loss (erythropoiesis, hemopoiesis, iron homeostasis, hypoxia responses, hemostasis/coagulation), tissue repair (wound healing, epithelial/keratinocyte proliferation, ECM/collagen organisation, angiogenesis), and energetic/nutritional buffering (growth signalling, insulin/IGF, glucose/energy homeostasis, response to starvation/nutrient levels).
3. **Behavioural**: nestlings stand on nest-mates to avoid larvae, preen and eat larvae; parents probe the nest base, preen nestlings, brood less at parasitized nests; begging changes. These are proposed/observed but with little evidence of efficacy in finches (Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016; O'Connor et al. 2010).
4. **Stress physiology**: no corticosterone/body condition/hematocrit effect in adult females (Knutie et al. 2013, PMC4383185), but glucocorticoid/hormone-mediated responses remain a plausible a priori axis.
5. **Other**: hybrid nestlings had lower parasite intensity (proposed genetic basis); pain/sensory perception of larval feeding may underlie behavioural responses.

## Scoring logic
- 5: wound healing / response to wounding, inflammatory response, adaptive immune response, B cell activation (antibody response is the one experimentally demonstrated immune mechanism).
- 4: core components: innate immune response, immune response/regulation, cytokine production and signalling (IL-6, IL-1, TNF, chemokines), complement, NF-kB, JAK-STAT, T/B cell activation and differentiation, leukocyte adhesion, hemopoiesis and erythrocyte differentiation, iron homeostasis, blood coagulation, hypoxia response, ECM organisation.
- 3: supporting processes: epithelial/skin/keratinocyte development, angiogenesis, collagen/basement membrane, phagocytosis, chemotaxis, TLR/PRR signalling, oxidative stress/ROS, energy/glucose homeostasis, starvation/nutrient responses, insulin/IGF signalling, steroid hormone signalling, platelet activation, pain perception, general stress responses.
- 2: weak/indirect: general cell migration/adhesion/proliferation, growth and metabolic housekeeping, skeletal/beak development, apoptosis regulation, behaviour-related neural terms (learning, memory, neuropeptide signalling), antiviral terms.
- 1: no specific link (housekeeping, development of unrelated organs, cell cycle, RNA processing, etc.).

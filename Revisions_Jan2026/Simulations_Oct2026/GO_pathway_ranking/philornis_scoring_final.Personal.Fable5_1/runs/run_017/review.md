# Run 017 — literature review: host responses to *Philornis downsi*

## What the larvae do to hosts
- *P. downsi* larvae are obligate nocturnal blood- and tissue-feeders on nestlings (and brooding females). First instars live inside the nares; later instars feed from the nest base on the belly, wings, ears and nares, causing swelling, open lesions, internal bleeding and naris/beak deformation (nares ~39% larger; upper beak sometimes nearly absent; deformity can persist to adulthood and affect song/mate choice).
- Documented costs: ~28% lower haemoglobin (anaemia; ~0.8 units per larva), lower haematocrit, ~56% lower body mass, ~30% shorter feathers, slower growth (worse in dry years), and 35–100% nestling mortality; nestlings losing ~25% of blood die within hours.

## Host defences documented or proposed
1. **Immune resistance**: adult female medium ground finches mount *P. downsi*-specific antibody (IgY) responses that increase with experimental parasitism (Koop et al. 2013; cited in Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016). Nestlings show little effective response; Knutie et al. 2016 found no significant antibody response in either finches or mockingbirds. Inflammatory/innate skin responses are proposed but their efficacy is uncertain; resistance to blood-feeding ectoparasites in other systems relies on cutaneous inflammation, cytokines, leukocyte recruitment, complement and antibody.
2. **Tolerance**: Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate the fly with little loss of fledging success. Mechanism: parasitised nestlings beg more and parents provision more, offsetting blood/energy loss (Knutie et al. 2016; McNew et al. 2019 show tolerance scales with provisioning rate and rainfall). Finches did not increase begging, provisioning or plasma glucose. Thus energy/nutrient homeostasis, glucose regulation, growth signalling (insulin/IGF/TOR) and blood regeneration (erythropoiesis, iron handling, hypoxia response) are the a priori physiological basis of tolerance.
3. **Tissue repair**: wound healing, haemostasis/coagulation, re-epithelialisation, skin/epidermis barrier, ECM/collagen and angiogenesis are the direct repair responses to larval lesions.
4. **Behavioural defences**: nestlings preen and eat larvae, stand on nest-mates to avoid larvae; females spend less time brooding, probe the nest base and eat larvae; these have not been shown to improve fledging. Nocturnal feeding may disturb sleep (circadian link is speculative). Nociception/itch plausibly triggers these behaviours.
5. **Stress physiology**: breeding females show no corticosterone or body-condition response to parasitism (Knutie et al. 2013), so stress-hormone pathways are a measured but weakly supported axis.

## Scoring logic
- 5: inflammatory/innate/adaptive immune response and cytokine production/signalling (incl. TNF, IL-1, IL-6, IL-8, chemokines, TLR), B-cell/antibody processes, wound healing/response to wounding, erythrocyte differentiation.
- 4: NF-kB/JAK-STAT/IFN-gamma/TNF signalling, T-cell processes, phagocytosis, complement, haemopoiesis, coagulation/platelets, iron homeostasis, hypoxia response, epidermis/skin/keratinocyte development, collagen organisation, leukocyte adhesion, energy homeostasis, glucose homeostasis, general stress response.
- 3: MAPK/TGF-beta/EGF/VEGF/angiogenesis and epithelial proliferation/migration (repair), ROS/oxidative stress and detoxification, insulin/IGF/TOR/nutrient signalling, lipid/glycogen storage, amino-acid transport, hormone signalling (glucocorticoid), circadian rhythm, pain perception, cell–cell junctions/ECM/basement membrane, blood pressure.
- 2: generic metabolism, adhesion, migration, apoptosis, proliferation, craniofacial/skeletal/cartilage development (naris/beak malformation is damage rather than defence), sensory/ear development, exocytosis, autophagy.
- 1: housekeeping, neural, reproductive, developmental patterning, DNA repair, RNA processing, etc.

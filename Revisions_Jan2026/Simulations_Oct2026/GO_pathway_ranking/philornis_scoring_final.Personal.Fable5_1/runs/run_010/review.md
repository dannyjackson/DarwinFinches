# Literature review: host responses to *Philornis downsi* (run_010)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- First-instar larvae enter the nares and ear canals of nestlings and feed on blood and tissue inside the nasal cavity; second/third instars live in the nest base and emerge at night to feed on nestling (and brooding female) blood through the skin.
- Documented effects on nestlings: substantial blood loss and anaemia (haemoglobin falls ~28%, ~0.8 g/dl per larva; losses of ~25% blood volume are lethal), skin wounds/lesions, swelling and internal bleeding, enlarged/deformed nares and sometimes malformed upper beaks that persist into adulthood, reduced body mass (~56%) and feather growth, and in-nest mortality averaging ~55% (3-100%). Secondary infection of wounds is plausible.
- Adults: females brooding on the nest are bitten and mount anti-*P. downsi* antibody responses.

## Host defences documented or proposed
1. **Resistance - immune**: adult (especially female) medium ground finches produce *P. downsi*-specific antibodies (anti-IgY assay), higher during nesting (Huber et al. 2010); experimental parasite manipulation showed a parasite-induced antibody response (Koop et al. 2013). In mockingbirds vs finches no significant antibody response was detected (Knutie et al. 2016). Inflammatory responses at bite/feeding sites and innate pathways (cytokines, NF-kB, TLR, complement, phagocytes, leukocyte recruitment) are the strong a priori expectation for a blood-feeding ectoparasite causing wounds.
2. **Tolerance - recovery from blood loss**: compensating haemoglobin/red-cell loss (erythropoiesis, haematopoiesis, iron homeostasis, hypoxia signalling), haemostasis (coagulation, thrombocyte activation) and wound healing/tissue repair (epidermis, ECM, angiogenesis, keratinocytes, fibroblasts).
3. **Tolerance - energetic/nutritional compensation**: Galapagos mockingbirds tolerate the fly via increased begging and parental provisioning; finches generally do not compensate (Knutie et al. 2016; Pike et al. 2023 green warbler-finch; small tree finch). Hence nutrient sensing, energy/glucose homeostasis, growth signalling (TOR, insulin/IGF) are plausible supporting processes.
4. **Behavioural defences**: nestlings standing on siblings to avoid larvae at the nest base, preening and consuming larvae, females probing the nest base, altered brooding; begging intensity does not honestly signal need in parasitised nests. No corticosterone stress response detected. Beak/naris deformation may affect song, mate choice, foraging and preening (proposed, untested).
5. Craniofacial/skeletal processes (nares, upper beak, palate) are relevant to the lesion phenotype but not to a documented defence; scored low-moderate (2).

## Scoring logic
5: inflammatory/immune response, B-cell/antibody processes, innate immunity, erythrocyte differentiation, coagulation, wound healing/response to wounding.
4: core components (cytokine signalling/production, NF-kB, TLR, JAK-STAT, TNF/IL-1/IL-6/IL-8, complement, leukocyte adhesion, haematopoiesis, iron homeostasis, hypoxia response, thrombocyte activation, T-cell activation).
3: supporting processes (oxidative stress/ROS, chemotaxis, phagocytosis, antibacterial defence, ECM/epidermis/angiogenesis/TGF-beta repair, nutrient/energy homeostasis, TOR/insulin/IGF, response to stress).
2: weak links (generic adhesion/migration/proliferation/apoptosis, metabolism, skeletal/cartilage, circadian, cognition, hormone signalling, antiviral).
1: no specific link (housekeeping, neurodevelopment, RNA processing, cell cycle, etc.).

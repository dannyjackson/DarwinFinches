# Run 025: literature review used for a priori scoring of GO terms (host response to *Philornis downsi*)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- First-instar larvae live inside the nares (and ear canals) of nestlings; second/third instars live in the nest base and emerge at dusk/night to feed on blood and tissue of the abdomen, wings, ears and nares. Larvae also feed on brooding females through the brood patch (Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016 review; Koop et al. 2011).
- Damage: open lesions, subcutaneous bleeding and swelling; enlarged nares and malformed upper beak that can persist to adulthood (naris deformation ~39% larger); beak damage affects song, foraging and preening.
- Blood loss and anaemia: haemoglobin ~28% lower in parasitized nestlings, ~0.8 g/dl lower per larva; nestlings losing ~25% of blood die within hours. Lower hematocrit in parasitized nests. Hb reduction was also confirmed experimentally in mockingbirds and finches (Knutie et al. 2016).
- Growth: body mass and feather length markedly reduced (primary feathers ~30% shorter; mass ~13-56% lower); protein loss / lower C:N in heavily parasitized nestlings suggested.
- Mortality: in-nest mortality 3-100% (mean ~55%); fledging success 4% unlined vs 33% lined nests (Koop et al. 2011); each larva reduces survival ~2%. Drought exacerbates mortality.
- Secondary consequences: wounds are open to microbial infection; sleep disruption and energetic drain from night feeding; mother broods less.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
1. **Immune (resistance)**: Adult female *G. fortis* mount *P. downsi*-specific IgY antibody responses (higher during nesting and at parasitized nests; Huber et al. 2010), and nestlings in parasitized nests show elevated antibody responses (Koop et al. 2013). Antibody levels were not linked to reduced parasite load; several studies found no effective antibody-mediated resistance in finches or mockingbirds and suggested innate defenses (complement, white blood cells) and the costs of immune activation should be studied. -> adaptive/humoral immunity, B cell processes, innate immunity, complement, cytokine/inflammatory signalling, leukocyte chemotaxis/phagocytosis.
2. **Inflammation and wound repair**: lesions, swelling, bleeding -> inflammatory response, response to wounding, wound healing, coagulation/platelets, epidermis/skin and keratinocyte processes, ECM/collagen organisation, angiogenesis, epithelial proliferation, defence against bacteria at wound sites.
3. **Blood loss / anaemia compensation**: haemoglobin and hematocrit losses -> erythropoiesis/erythrocyte differentiation, hemopoiesis, iron homeostasis, hypoxia response, energy homeostasis.
4. **Tolerance via energetic/nutritional compensation**: Galapagos mockingbirds tolerate the fly by increased nestling begging and parental provisioning; tolerance collapses in dry years when food is scarce (Knutie et al. 2016; McNew et al. 2019). Nestling plasma glucose differed with treatment in mockingbirds. -> response to nutrient levels/starvation, glucose and lipid homeostasis, insulin/IGF and TOR signalling, growth-related processes, thermogenesis (reduced brooding at night).
5. **Behavioural**: nestlings stand on siblings to avoid larvae, preen and eat larvae; parasitized finch nestlings beg less; females brood less, probe nest base, preen nestlings; nest-site choice. -> pain/itch perception, nervous system processes scored as supporting/speculative.
6. **Stress physiology**: females showed no corticosterone response to parasitism (Knutie et al. 2013); body condition and hematocrit of adults unaffected -> hormonal stress terms scored low/moderate.
7. **Beak/naris tissue**: naris and upper-beak deformation reflects tissue loss during development -> cartilage/bone/craniofacial development scored as plausible supporting processes.

Scores: 5 = terms directly naming documented mechanisms (immune response, innate/adaptive immunity, inflammatory response, response to wounding, wound healing, erythrocyte differentiation); 4 = core components (B cell activation, cytokine production, NF-kB, TLR, complement, coagulation, iron homeostasis, hypoxia response, skin/epidermis, ECM, nutrient/starvation response, energy homeostasis, hemopoiesis); 3 = plausible supporting processes (angiogenesis, T cell, chemotaxis, phagocytosis, oxidative stress, glucose/lipid homeostasis, TOR/insulin signalling, skeletal/cartilage, pain perception); 2 = weak/indirect (general signalling cascades, apoptosis, cell migration, adhesion, autophagy, hormone response, antiviral/interferon); 1 = no specific link (housekeeping, neural development, cardiac, cilia, gametogenesis, RNA processing, etc.).

# Run 015 — literature review: host responses to *Philornis downsi*

## What the larvae do to hosts
- First-instar larvae develop inside the nares and ear canals of nestlings; 2nd/3rd instars live in the nest base by day and feed on nestling (and brooding-female) blood and tissue at night ("semi-hematophagous"). Feeding causes open wounds, blood loss, anemia (hemoglobin falls ~0.8 g/dL per larva), reduced body mass and feather growth, and enlarged/deformed nares that can remove much of the upper beak and persist into adulthood (effects on song, foraging, preening). In-nest nestling mortality averages ~55% (range 16–100%) and can reach 100% in some site-years; several finch populations have declined.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
1. **Resistance — immune**: An experimentally induced *P. downsi*-specific antibody (IgY) response in medium ground finch females (Koop et al. 2013); nesting females have higher anti-*Philornis* antibody levels. Antibody responses were weak/absent in mockingbirds and finches on Santa Cruz (Knutie et al. 2016). Innate pro-inflammatory responses (wound/skin inflammation, cytokine signalling, pattern-recognition signalling) are the a priori expectation for a tissue-feeding ectoparasite; adaptive humoral (B cell / antibody) responses are the documented specific mechanism.
2. **Tolerance — hematological/energetic**: Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate flies (no fledging cost) while finches do not; tolerance is argued to work through compensation for blood/energy loss (erythropoiesis/hemoglobin recovery, provisioning, nutrient/protein intake, glucose/energy homeostasis). Urban small ground finches with richer diets fledge far more parasitized young (Knutie lab, 2024). Nutritional status (blood C:N) predicted nestling survival (Frontiers Ecol Evol 2024).
3. **Wound healing / hemostasis**: Repeated puncture wounds and blood loss make coagulation, platelet activation, wound healing, skin/keratinocyte and epidermal repair, and craniofacial/beak tissue (nares, bone/cartilage) repair core predicted mechanisms.
4. **Behavioral**: Females at parasitized nests brood less, stand erect, probe nest material and sometimes eat larvae; nestlings stand on siblings to avoid larvae; nestling reactivity predicts naris deformation; parents of parasitized mockingbird nestlings adjust provisioning; finches incorporate insecticide-treated cotton into nests (self-fumigation). No corticosterone stress response detected in females. Behavioural/neural terms were scored low (1–2) because GO process terms for neuron development etc. are not specific to these behaviours.

## Scoring logic
- 5: immune/inflammatory/wound-healing/coagulation/erythropoiesis core terms (e.g., inflammatory response, wound healing, blood coagulation, B cell activation, erythrocyte differentiation, defense response).
- 4: core components of these (TNF/IL-1/IL-6/IL-8/chemokine production, TLR/NF-kB signalling, phagocytosis, T/B cell signalling, hemopoiesis, iron homeostasis, hypoxia response, nutrient/starvation responses, glucose/energy homeostasis, epidermis/skin development, chemotaxis).
- 3: supporting processes (metabolic/lipid/glycogen reserves, angiogenesis, ECM/basement membrane, bone/cartilage/craniofacial development relevant to beak deformation, oxidative stress, JAK-STAT, growth-factor signalling in repair).
- 2: weak/indirect (general apoptosis, cell migration, adhesion, generic stress responses, endocytosis).
- 1: no specific link (neural, cilia, mitosis, RNA processing, developmental patterning, etc.).

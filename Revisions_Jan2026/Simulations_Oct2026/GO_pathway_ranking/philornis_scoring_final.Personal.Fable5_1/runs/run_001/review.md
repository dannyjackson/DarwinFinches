# Literature review: host responses to *Philornis downsi* (run_001)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- *P. downsi* larvae live in the nest; 1st/2nd instars feed inside the nares of nestlings (subcutaneous, tissue/fluid feeding), later instars emerge at night and feed semi-haematophagously on nestling blood and tissue, burrowing into skin and causing swelling, open lesions, internal bleeding and wounds on ears, belly, wings and nares (Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016 review; Dudaniec & Kleindorfer 2006).
- Documented physiological costs: anaemia (haemoglobin reduced ~28%, ~0.8 units per larva), loss of ~25% blood volume is lethal within hours; reduced body mass (~56%) and feather growth (~30%); naris/beak deformation (nares ~39% larger) that persists to adulthood and alters song and mate choice; average nestling mortality ~55%, each larva reducing survival ~2%.
- Reduced haemoglobin in parasitized nestlings is consistent across finches and mockingbirds; older parasitized nestlings are lighter.

## Host defences documented or proposed
1. **Immune (resistance)**: adult female finches show elevated *P. downsi*-binding antibodies (IgY/IgG) during nesting (Huber et al. 2010); experimental parasite-induced antibody response in medium ground finches (Koop et al. 2013); nestling antibody responses are weak/undetectable (Knutie et al. 2016). Innate inflammatory responses at larval feeding sites (swelling, lesions) and secondary bacterial infection of wounds are described/proposed. Overall, Galápagos birds appear to have no effective immunological resistance.
2. **Tolerance**: Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate the parasite (similar parasite density, little fitness loss) via increased nestling begging, increased parental provisioning, and apparent compensation for blood loss (rapid blood/erythrocyte replacement); tolerance declines in dry years when provisioning falls (Knutie et al. 2016; McNew et al. 2019). Finches largely do not compensate; small tree finches do not increase provisioning under parasitism (Common et al. 2020). Diet quality/protein (nitrogen isotopes) and nutritional state have been proposed to mediate tolerance and defence.
3. **Behavioural**: nestlings stand on nest-mates, preen and eat larvae; parents probe nest base, preen nestlings, brood less and consume larvae; nestling behavioural type (responsiveness to handling) relates to naris deformation under high parasite load (Kleindorfer et al.). No corticosterone stress response in females at parasitized nests.
4. **Energetics/growth**: parasitism reduces growth and mass; compensation depends on food availability, nest microclimate and energy balance.

## Mechanism categories used for scoring
- Score 5: immune/defence/inflammatory response and their regulation; B cell/antibody responses; wound/response to wounding and healing; erythrocyte differentiation (anaemia compensation).
- Score 4: core components/strong predictions: cytokine and pattern-recognition signalling (TLR, NF-kB, TNF, IL-1/6/8/17, chemokines, inflammasome, JAK-STAT), T cell activation, phagocytosis, complement, leukocyte adhesion/chemotaxis, haemopoiesis, iron homeostasis, hypoxia response, blood coagulation, oxidative stress/respiratory burst, skin/epidermis/keratinocyte, energy homeostasis and nutrient response, general stress response.
- Score 3: supporting processes: tissue repair (ECM, collagen, angiogenesis, epithelial proliferation/migration, EGFR/TGF-beta, integrin, barrier/tight junctions, membrane repair), starvation/glucose homeostasis, growth signalling (IGF, TOR), hormone responses (glucocorticoid), MAPK/JNK/p38, bone/cartilage/craniofacial (naris/beak deformation), pain perception, blood pressure, defence against bacteria/LPS, T cell subsets, interferon-gamma.
- Score 2: weak/indirect: metabolism (lipid, carbohydrate, amino acid), insulin, apoptosis, autophagy, general development, adhesion/migration, growth-factor pathways, cognition/behaviour, thermogenesis, xenobiotic/viral responses, ER stress.
- Score 1: no specific link (RNA processing, translation, DNA repair, cell cycle, cilia, neural/synaptic, cardiac, reproduction, organelle transport, etc.).

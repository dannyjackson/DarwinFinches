# Literature review: host responses to *Philornis downsi* (run_003)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- *P. downsi* larvae are nocturnal, haematophagous nest parasites. First and some second instars feed inside the nares of nestlings; later instars live in the nest base and feed through the skin on blood and tissue of nestlings (and sometimes brooding females), causing lesions on the belly, wings, ears and nares (Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016 review).
- Documented effects: blood loss and reduced haemoglobin (~28% lower, ~0.8 g/dL per larva; nestlings losing ~25% blood volume die), reduced body mass (~56%) and feather growth, enlarged nares (~39%) and in severe cases erosion of the upper beak keratin and soft tissue that persists into adulthood, high in-nest mortality (mean ~55%, range 3–100%) (Fessl et al. 2006; Dudaniec et al. 2006; Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016; McNew et al. 2019 for mockingbirds).
- Naris/beak deformation lowers song pitch, alters the mating signal and prolongs time to pairing; promotes hybridisation between tree finch species (Kleindorfer et al. 2019, Proc R Soc B).
- Parasitised nestlings have lower plasma glucose; dying nestlings appear to lack glycogen reserves (Knutie et al. 2016 and later work).

## Host defenses documented or proposed
1. **Resistance – adaptive immunity.** Adult medium ground finches produce *P. downsi*-specific antibodies (IgY), ~1.7x higher during nesting than before (Huber et al. 2010, PLoS ONE); an experimental study (Koop et al. 2013, Proc R Soc B) demonstrated a parasite-induced antibody response in breeding birds, though its protective value for nestlings is unclear. Knutie et al. 2016 found no significant antibody response in either finches or mockingbirds. The system is nevertheless framed as a question of whether an antibody-mediated (B-cell) response can reduce larval feeding/survival.
2. **Resistance – innate/inflammatory response at feeding sites.** Larvae cause skin wounds and tissue lesions; inflammation at bite sites and anti-ectoparasite cytokine/complement responses are a standard a priori prediction (discussed in Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016 and Knutie et al. 2016). This predicts cytokine production (IL-6, TNF, IL-1, IL-17, chemokines), NF-kB/TLR signalling, leukocyte chemotaxis, phagocytosis, complement, inflammasome, and regulation of inflammation.
3. **Tolerance – compensating for blood loss and tissue damage.** Mockingbirds tolerate the parasite: parasitised nestlings beg more, parents provision more, and fledging is not reduced; tolerance varies with rainfall/food availability (Knutie et al. 2016 J Anim Ecol; McNew et al. 2019 Proc R Soc B). Finches generally show little compensation, and green warbler finch females actually fed less at infested nests (Ornithology 2023). Physiologically, tolerance implies erythropoiesis/haematopoiesis, iron homeostasis, hypoxia response, haemostasis/coagulation to limit blood loss, wound healing (re-epithelialisation, keratinocyte differentiation, collagen/ECM, angiogenesis), energy and glucose homeostasis, glycogen/lipid reserves, nutrient-sensing (insulin/IGF/TOR, starvation responses) and growth regulation.
4. **Behavioural defenses.** Nestlings stand on siblings to avoid larvae at the nest base, preen and eat larvae; females probe nest bases, preen nestlings, brood less and stand erect more; nestling reactivity/struggling relates to naris deformation (O'Connor et al. 2010; Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016; Macquarie/Flinders 2022 nestling-behaviour study). Four finch species rub *Psidium galapageium* leaves on their feathers; extracts repel adult flies and slow larval growth (Cimadom et al. 2016, PLoS ONE). Finches readily incorporate novel (permethrin-treated) nest material (Knutie et al. 2014). These imply only weak, indirect links to GO terms (pain/itch perception, learning/cognition, hormone signalling, circadian rhythm).
5. **Stress physiology.** No corticosterone stress response of breeding females was detected (Knutie et al., SICB abstract; Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016), so glucocorticoid-related terms are rated low-to-moderate.
6. **Thermal/nest environment.** Nest base temperature during brooding influences larval development (Frontiers Ecol Evol 2024); thermoregulation terms rated weak.

## Scoring logic
- 5: immune response, adaptive immune response, inflammatory response, wound healing / response to wounding, erythrocyte differentiation (direct counterparts of documented antibody response, tissue damage and anaemia).
- 4: core components of those mechanisms: innate immune response and its activation, cytokine production/regulation (TNF, IL-6, IL-8, chemokines), NF-kB, TLR, complement, inflammasome, B- and T-cell activation, haematopoiesis, iron homeostasis, hypoxia response, blood coagulation, keratinocyte differentiation/epidermis/skin development, collagen fibril organisation, energy and glucose homeostasis.
- 3: plausible supporting processes: cytokine-signalling pathways (JAK-STAT, TNF/IL-1/IFN responses), chemotaxis, phagocytosis, oxidative stress/ROS, angiogenesis and endothelial/epithelial migration or proliferation, ECM organisation, TGF-beta, fibroblast proliferation, nutrient-starvation responses, gluconeogenesis/glucose metabolism, glycogen and lipid storage, insulin/IGF signalling, platelet activation/aggregation, blood pressure, pain perception, keratin/intermediate filaments.
- 2: weak/indirect: generic stress and hormone responses, apoptosis regulation, cell adhesion/migration, lipid and amino-acid metabolism, TOR signalling, circadian rhythm, learning/cognition, song/hearing, bone/beak development, xenobiotic response, antiviral terms (pox co-infection).
- 1: no specific link (neural development, cell cycle, transcription/RNA processing, organelle biology, organ morphogenesis, etc.).
- Note: a transcriptomic study of Galápagos finches (Knutie et al. 2024, Global Change Biology; related bioRxiv 2023) was encountered during searching; its gene-expression results were not used.

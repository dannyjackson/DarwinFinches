# Literature review: host responses to *Philornis downsi* (run_009)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- First-instar larvae live inside the nares and ear canals of nestlings, feeding on blood and tissue; later instars live in the nest base and emerge at night to feed externally by penetrating the skin (semi-haematophagous). Larvae rarely feed on brooding adults.
- Blood loss causes severe anaemia: each larva lowers nestling haemoglobin by ~0.8 g/dL; parasitised nests show ~28% lower haemoglobin, and nestlings losing ~25% of blood volume die within hours. Lower plasma glucose has also been reported in parasitised nestlings.
- Tissue damage: swelling, open lesions, internal bleeding on nares, ears, belly and wings; opportunity for secondary bacterial infection and altered microbiota.
- Naris deformation: nares ~39% larger in parasitised nestlings, persisting in surviving adults, with beak malformation in severe cases (affects song/mate choice later).
- Growth: ~56% lower body mass and ~30% shorter feathers in heavily parasitised nestlings; in-nest mortality averages ~55% (range 3–100%), survival falling ~2% per larva.

## Host defences documented or proposed
1. **Resistance (immune)**: brooding female medium ground finches carry *P. downsi*-specific antibodies (IgY) that rise during nesting and are associated with lower parasite intensity (but not higher fledging). Nestlings mount weak/variable antibody responses; innate inflammatory responses at feeding sites are presumed. Hence: adaptive (B cell/antibody, T cell), innate immunity, inflammatory signalling (cytokines, TLR, NF-kB), complement, phagocytosis, chemotaxis.
2. **Tolerance (physiological)**: Galapagos mockingbirds tolerate the same parasite density that kills finches; proposed mechanisms are compensatory parental provisioning driven by increased begging, erythropoietic recovery of haemoglobin (iron, red blood cell production), and energy/glucose homeostasis under blood loss. Growth compensation and nutrient-sensing pathways are plausible correlates.
3. **Wound/tissue repair**: haemostasis against blood-feeding (coagulation, platelets), wound healing, re-epithelialisation, skin/keratinocyte differentiation, extracellular matrix and collagen remodelling, angiogenesis; tissue regeneration around nares and beak (craniofacial/palate development).
4. **Behavioural defences**: nestlings stand on top of siblings to avoid larvae; nestlings and females preen and eat larvae; parasitised females brood less and stand more and probe nest material; finches incorporate repellent plant material (*Psidium galapageium* leaves) and fumigated cotton into nests. Nociception and nervous-system processes are only weakly linked at the gene level. Corticosterone (stress response) showed no clear change in parasitised females.
5. **Hypoxia / oxidative stress**: anaemia reduces oxygen transport, predicting hypoxia-response, energy-metabolism and redox pathways as downstream processes.

## Scoring logic
- 5: immune response, inflammatory response, defence response, adaptive/innate immunity, B cell activation/differentiation, erythrocyte differentiation, wound healing, response to wounding.
- 4: cytokine production/signalling (IL-6, IL-8, IL-12, TNF, IFN-gamma, IL-1, IL-17), TLR/NF-kB/NLRP3, T cell activation, complement, hemopoiesis, iron homeostasis, hypoxia response, coagulation/platelets, skin/epidermis/keratinocyte, ECM/collagen organisation, energy homeostasis, leukocyte adhesion, nociception.
- 3: angiogenesis/VEGF, chemotaxis/phagocytosis, oxidative stress, glucose/insulin/TOR/IGF nutrient-growth signalling, starvation responses, steroid hormone signalling, JAK-STAT, type I IFN, epithelial differentiation/migration, palate (beak) development.
- 2: generic apoptosis, adhesion, lipid/energy metabolism, skeletal development, behaviour-related neural terms, circadian rhythm, generic stress.
- 1: housekeeping, neural, reproductive, developmental and cell-cycle terms with no specific link.

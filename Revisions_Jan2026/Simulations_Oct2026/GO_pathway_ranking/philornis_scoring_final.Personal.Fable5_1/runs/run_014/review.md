# Literature review: host responses to *Philornis downsi* (run_014)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- First/second-instar larvae live inside the nestling's nares and feed on keratin, blood and tissue inside the beak; later instars live in the nest base and emerge at night to feed on blood through the skin (ears, belly, wings, nares). Feeding causes swelling, open lesions, internal bleeding, and permanent naris/beak deformation (enlarged nares in ~34% of adults on Floreana). Adult females brooding on the nest are also bitten (bare brood patch).
- Blood loss: haemoglobin falls ~0.8 units per larva (~28% lower overall); nestlings losing ~25% of blood volume die within hours. Parasitised nestlings show lower body mass (~56%), shorter feathers (~30%), lower plasma glucose, slower growth, and in-nest mortality averaging ~55% (3-100%). Beak deformation shifts adult foraging niche and lowers body condition.

## Documented or proposed host defences
1. **Immune resistance (humoral/adaptive):** Adult medium ground finches produce *Philornis*-specific antibodies (ELISA, IgY) during nesting, females more than males (Huber et al. 2010). Experimental parasite manipulation showed a parasite-induced antibody response in nests but no clear improvement in fledging (Koop et al. 2013; Knutie et al. 2016 found low nestling antibodies). Innate inflammation at bite sites is implied by swelling and lesions.
2. **Tolerance (haematological/energetic):** Compensating for blood loss via erythropoiesis/iron handling, hypoxia responses, and maintaining growth and energy balance (glucose, nutrient intake). Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate the fly by increased nestling begging and parental provisioning, and tolerance rises with food/rainfall; finches generally do not compensate (Knutie et al. 2016; McNew et al.; Pike et al. 2023; small tree finch study).
3. **Tissue repair:** Wound healing, haemostasis/coagulation after blood feeding, re-epithelialisation and keratin/skin repair of lesions and nares.
4. **Secondary infection defence:** Open lesions and nostril damage expose hosts to bacterial infection; antibacterial responses plausibly co-selected. Avian pox co-infection is a confound.
5. **Behavioural defences:** Sibling stacking (standing on nest mates to avoid larvae at the nest base), preening and eating larvae, altered female brooding posture, nest-base probing; nestling agitation/pain response correlates with naris damage; larvae feed nocturnally so circadian activity and sleep may matter. Nestling begging and parental provisioning behaviour are components of tolerance. No evidence of a corticosterone stress response in females.

## Scoring logic
5: immune/inflammatory response, B-cell activation, haematopoiesis/erythropoiesis, wound healing/response to wounding, regulation of immune/inflammatory response, adaptive/innate immune response. 4: cytokine production and signalling (IL-6, IL-8, IL-12, IL-17, TNF, IL-1, IFN-gamma), NF-kB, TLR, chemotaxis of leukocytes, T/B-cell processes, complement, phagocytosis, coagulation/platelets, iron homeostasis, hypoxia response, glucose/energy homeostasis, nutrient/starvation response, skin/epidermis/keratinocyte, ECM/collagen, fibroblast proliferation, antibacterial defence, pain perception. 3: supporting processes (angiogenesis, epithelial proliferation/migration, growth-factor signalling, MAPK/JAK-STAT, oxidative stress, lipid/energy metabolism, insulin, circadian rhythm, neuropeptide signalling, blood pressure/heart rate, cardiac/respiratory compensation). 2: weak or speculative (general adhesion, apoptosis, autophagy, hormone responses, antiviral, bone/beak development, learning/memory). 1: no specific link (housekeeping, development of unrelated organs, cell cycle, RNA processing, neural wiring).

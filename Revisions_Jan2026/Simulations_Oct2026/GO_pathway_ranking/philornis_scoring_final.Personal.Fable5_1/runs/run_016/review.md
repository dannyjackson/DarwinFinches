# Run 016 – Literature review: host responses to *Philornis downsi*

## What the larvae do to hosts
- *P. downsi* larvae are obligate nest parasites of Galápagos land birds. First instars live in the nares/ear canals of nestlings; later instars live in the nest base and feed externally at night, penetrating skin and feeding on blood and tissue (Fessl et al. 2006; Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016 review).
- Documented effects: haematophagy and blood loss leading to anaemia (hemoglobin ~28–40% lower in parasitized nestlings; ~0.8 g/dL drop per larva), open skin lesions, swelling and internal bleeding, naris enlargement/beak deformation (nares ~39% larger, sometimes persisting into adulthood), reduced body mass (up to ~56%), shorter feathers, slower growth, and in-nest mortality averaging ~55% (range 3–100%). Fledging success is strongly reduced in finches.

## Host defences documented or proposed
1. **Acquired (antibody) immunity**: medium ground finches produce *P. downsi*-specific IgY antibodies; levels higher during nesting and in females (brooding contact). Experimental work reports a parasite-induced antibody response; antibody level associated with lower parasite intensity but not clearly with fledging. Knutie et al. 2016 found no significant antibody response in either finches or mockingbirds (assay sensitivity caveats). Non-urban parasitized nestlings showed immunoglobulin-associated responses (adaptive resistance) in the urban-living study abstract (I did not use gene-level results).
2. **Innate / inflammatory resistance**: pro-inflammatory responses at larval feeding sites are proposed to deter feeding or kill larvae; the urban-living study abstract frames inflammation as innate resistance. Wound healing, coagulation/platelet responses, and skin barrier integrity follow directly from the external feeding wounds.
3. **Tolerance of blood loss**: Galápagos mockingbirds are tolerant hosts (fledging unaffected at similar parasite density), compensating via increased begging and parental provisioning (higher nestling glucose); tolerance is labile and lost in dry years. Erythropoiesis / red blood cell production and iron handling, hypoxia responses, and energy/glucose homeostasis are the physiological basis of tolerating anaemia and the energetic drain. Parental food compensation was not supported in green warbler-finches (Tebbich et al. 2023).
4. **Behavioural defences**: nestlings standing on top of siblings to avoid larvae in the nest base, preening and eating larvae; adult females brooding less, standing erect, probing the nest base; no corticosterone stress response detected in females. Nest-material/self-fumigation defences have been proposed (repellent plant use, fumigated cotton).

## Scoring logic
- 5: adaptive/innate immune response, inflammation, wound healing/response to wounding, blood coagulation, B-cell/erythrocyte differentiation.
- 4: immune-system regulation, cytokine production, TLR/complement, hemopoiesis, hypoxia response, iron homeostasis, glucose/energy homeostasis, platelet activation/aggregation, hormone and nutrient-level responses (tolerance/compensation).
- 3: specific cytokine/NF-kB/JAK-STAT/TNF/IL signalling, phagocytosis, chemotaxis, T-cell processes, oxidative-stress and ROS, skin/epidermis and ECM/angiogenesis (wound repair), insulin/glucose/lipid mobilisation, growth regulation, heat/protein-folding stress, zinc handling.
- 2: generic signalling, apoptosis, autophagy, cell cycle, development, proliferation, metabolism, transport and other processes with only an indirect possible link.
- 1: neural, ciliary, meiotic, cardiac, RNA processing and similar processes with no specific link.

# Literature review: host responses to *Philornis downsi* (run_026)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- *P. downsi* is an introduced muscid fly whose larvae live in nests of Galápagos land birds. First instars sit inside nestlings' nares from ~day 2; second and third instars live in the nest base and feed externally at night (and by day when the female is absent) on blood and tissue of the abdomen, wings, ears and other bare skin. Larvae also feed on brooding females (brood patch).
- Documented effects on nestlings: ~50% average in-nest mortality (3–100% across studies); reduced haemoglobin (anaemia; each larva lowers Hb), reduced body mass and feather growth, enlarged/deformed nares and in severe cases loss of the upper beak. Deformation persists in survivors and alters song, mate choice and foraging. Secondary infection of wounds and blood loss leading to death within hours are described.
- Adult females: brood-patch exposure, elevated parasite-specific antibodies; no corticosterone stress response, no haematocrit or body-condition change in breeding females (Knutie et al. 2013).

## Host defenses documented or proposed
1. **Humoral adaptive immunity (resistance)**: medium ground finches mount *P. downsi*-specific IgY antibody responses (ELISA), higher during nesting and in females (Huber et al. 2010; Koop et al. 2013). Nestling antibody responses are weak or absent (Knutie et al. 2016). Fitness benefit of antibodies unresolved; costs (energy, autoimmunity) proposed. This is the only immunological mechanism measured directly, so immune-response, adaptive immunity and B-cell terms score highest.
2. **Innate/inflammatory response to skin wounds and larval feeding**: strongly predicted from blood-feeding tissue damage (inflammation, wound healing, complement, phagocytes, cytokines, keratinocyte/epidermal repair, ECM/collagen, coagulation). Not directly measured but core to any ectoparasite response.
3. **Blood loss compensation**: haemoglobin reduction is the best-documented physiological cost; erythropoiesis, haemopoiesis, iron homeostasis, coagulation/platelet function and hypoxia responses are strong a priori predictions. Knutie et al. 2016 found no evidence of rapid blood replacement in tolerant mockingbirds, but it remains a central candidate.
4. **Tolerance via energy/nutrient compensation**: tolerant Galápagos mockingbirds beg more, are provisioned more and have higher plasma glucose; tolerance depends on rainfall/food (McNew et al. 2019). Energy homeostasis, glucose homeostasis, insulin signalling, lipid/glycogen storage, starvation and nutrient-level responses therefore score 3–4. Growth reduction (reduced mass, feathers) links growth-factor (IGF/insulin) signalling.
5. **Behavioural defenses**: nestlings preen and eat larvae, stand on nest mates; females brood less, stand, probe the nest base and preen nestlings; nestling handling responsiveness predicts naris deformation (Kleindorfer group). Behaviours imply nociception/sensory and nervous-system processes; these are given modest scores (2–3) because the GO terms are generic.
6. **Other**: nest temperature affects larval development (thermoregulation terms 2); larvae feed nocturnally (circadian 2); no stress-hormone response detected (hormone terms 2).

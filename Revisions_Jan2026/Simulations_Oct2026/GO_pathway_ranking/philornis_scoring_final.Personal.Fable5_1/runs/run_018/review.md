# Run 018 — Literature review: host responses to *Philornis downsi*

## What the larvae do to hosts
- First-instar larvae feed inside the nares of nestlings; later instars live in the nest base and feed at night on blood and tissue of the abdomen, wings, ears, nares and other exposed skin (Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016 review).
- Documented pathology: open lesions, subcutaneous bleeding and swelling, enlarged/deformed nares (naris size ~39 % larger; upper beak nearly absent in severe cases), which persist into adulthood and alter song and mate choice (Kleindorfer et al. 2019).
- Blood loss and anaemia: haemoglobin ~28 % lower in parasitised nestlings, ~0.8 units lower per larva; 14–40 % higher haemoglobin after fumigation (Fessl et al. 2006; Knutie et al. 2016; McNew et al. 2019).
- Reduced growth (mass, feather length), in-nest mortality averaging ~55 %, with reduced survival per additional larva. Adult brooding females are also fed on.

## Host defences documented or proposed
1. **Adaptive (antibody) immune response** — *Philornis*-binding antibody levels rise during nesting and are higher in adult females than males (Huber et al. 2010); females at parasitised nests have higher antibody levels (Koop et al. 2013). Nestling antibody levels are near-undetectable and not treatment-dependent (Knutie et al. 2016). Resistance via immunity appears limited.
2. **Inflammation / wound response** — swelling, subcutaneous bleeding and lesions at feeding sites are documented; healing of wounds, scarring of nares and re-epithelialisation are implied by surviving fledglings with deformed nares. Secondary microbial infection of wounds is plausible; parasitism also lowers nestling gut bacterial richness (Molecular Ecology 2024, San Cristóbal small ground finches).
3. **Haematological compensation** — anaemia from blood loss predicts erythropoiesis, iron handling, hypoxia responses and haemostasis/coagulation at wounds.
4. **Tolerance (energetic compensation)** — Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate the fly: parasitised nestlings beg more, parents provision more, nestling glucose is higher; tolerance is labile and depends on rainfall/food (Knutie et al. 2016; McNew et al. 2019). Finches (medium ground finch, small tree finch) do not show parental compensation. Energy/glucose/nutrient homeostasis and growth regulation under resource limitation are therefore a priori relevant.
5. **Behavioural defences** — nestlings climb on nest-mates to avoid larvae at the nest base, preen and eat larvae; females brood less, stand, probe the nest base, preen nestlings and eat larvae; finches incorporate insecticide-treated nest material (self-fumigation). Pain/irritation perception plausibly triggers these behaviours.
6. **Stress endocrinology** — experimentally manipulated parasite load did not alter baseline or stress-induced corticosterone, body condition or haematocrit of breeding females (Knutie et al. 2013), so glucocorticoid-related terms were scored low.

## Scoring logic
- 5: immune response, adaptive immune response, B cell activation, innate immune response, inflammatory response, wound healing, response to wounding, erythrocyte differentiation (+ positive regulation).
- 4: immune-system regulation, T/B cell activation and differentiation, TLR signalling, complement, cytokine production, haemopoiesis, hypoxia response, iron homeostasis, coagulation, defence response.
- 3: specific cytokine/NF-kB/JAK-STAT pathways, chemotaxis/phagocytosis, oxidative stress, ECM/collagen/epidermis/keratinocyte, angiogenesis, glucose/energy/starvation/nutrient responses, insulin/IGF signalling, nociception.
- 2: generic signalling, adhesion, migration, proliferation, apoptosis, lipid/energy metabolism, autophagy, antiviral terms.
- 1: housekeeping, neural, developmental, cell-cycle, RNA/DNA processing terms with no specific link.

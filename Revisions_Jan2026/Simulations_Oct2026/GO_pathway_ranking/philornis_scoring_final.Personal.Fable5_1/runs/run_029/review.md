# Run 029 — literature review: host responses to *Philornis downsi*

## What the larvae do to hosts
- Larvae are obligate blood/tissue feeders on nestlings (and occasionally brooding females). First instars live inside the nares and feed subcutaneously; later instars live in the nest base and feed externally at night on the abdomen, wings, ears and other exposed skin.
- Documented damage: open lesions, swelling, subcutaneous bleeding, secondary infection of wounds, enlarged/deformed nares and beak malformation (persisting in survivors); haemoglobin ~28% lower in parasitized nestlings (about 0.8 units per larva), blood loss estimated at 18–55% of blood volume; lower body mass (~56%) and shorter feathers; in-nest mortality averaging ~55% (range 3–100%).

## Host defences documented or proposed
1. **Resistance (immune)**: nesting females have higher *P. downsi*-binding IgY, and more so in parasitized nests (Huber et al. 2010); but in experimental work neither finches nor mockingbirds showed a significant antibody response, and nestling IgY is near-undetectable before fledging (Knutie et al. 2016; Addesso et al. 2020). Inflammatory/innate responses in the skin (ROS, skin immune cells) are proposed but untested.
2. **Tolerance (physiological)**: mockingbirds, vegetarian finches and other larger species suffer little fitness loss at similar parasite loads. Proposed mechanisms: compensatory energy intake (parasitized mockingbird nestlings beg more, parents provision more; plasma glucose differences), recovery from blood loss, body-size/growth buffers, and resource/nutrition dependence (tolerance collapses in dry years; higher protein intake in urban nests associates with survival).
3. **Behavioural**: nestlings stand on siblings, preen and eat larvae; females brood less, stand more, probe nest base, preen nestlings; begging changes; no detectable corticosterone stress response in females; nest-site effects are mainly encounter-rate effects.
4. **Other**: hybridization/lower parasite loads in hybrid tree finches (proposed); parental compensation is absent in several finch species (green warbler-finch females actually reduce provisioning).

## Mechanism classes used for scoring
- Blood loss / anaemia: erythropoiesis, haematopoiesis, iron homeostasis, hypoxia response, haemostasis/coagulation (5–4).
- Tissue damage: response to wounding, wound healing, inflammation, skin/epidermis repair, ECM/collagen, angiogenesis, epithelial migration (5–3).
- Immunity: innate/adaptive immune response, cytokine production and signalling (IL-6, TNF, IL-1, IL-17, chemokines), TLR/NF-kB, complement, B-cell/antibody, T-cell, phagocytosis, ROS, defence against wound bacteria (5–3).
- Energetic tolerance: glucose/energy homeostasis, insulin/IGF signalling, nutrient/starvation responses, growth regulation, lipid/carbohydrate metabolism (4–2).
- Behaviour/stress: nociception, hormone responses (3–2); generic neural development terms scored 1.
- Beak/naris deformation: bone/cartilage/ossification terms scored 2 (damage rather than developmental response).
- Housekeeping, cell-cycle, cilia, neurodevelopment, transcription/RNA processing, organ development: 1.

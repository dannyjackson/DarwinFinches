# Literature review: host responses to Philornis downsi (run_004)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- First-instar larvae live in the nares and ear canals of nestlings; later instars live in the nest base and emerge at night to pierce the skin and feed on blood and tissue fluids of nestlings (and, to a lesser extent, brooding females). Wounds/lesions occur on ears, belly, wings and nostrils (Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016; Fessl et al. 2018).
- Blood loss causes anemia: hemoglobin ~28% lower in parasitized nestlings (~0.8 units per larva); nestlings losing ~25% of blood volume often die within hours. In-nest mortality averages ~55% (range 3-100%).
- Reduced growth: ~56% lower body mass and ~30% shorter feathers in parasitized nestlings; reduced nestling mass also documented in green warbler-finch (Pike et al. 2023).
- Naris malformation: nares ~39% larger, in severe cases upper beak nearly absent; deformity persists into adulthood and alters song and mating signal.
- No detectable corticosterone stress response or hematocrit change in brooding females (Knutie et al. 2013).

## Documented or proposed host defenses
1. Humoral immunity (resistance): brooding females mount a P. downsi-specific antibody (IgY) response; antibody levels associated with lower parasite intensity but not higher fledging success (Huber et al. 2010; Koop et al. 2013). Knutie et al. 2016 found no significant antibody response in finches or mockingbirds. Antibody production implies B cell activation/differentiation, T cell help, cytokine signaling, lymphoid/hematopoietic processes.
2. Inflammatory / wound response: skin penetration, open lesions, bleeding under the skin and nares tissue destruction predict wound healing, coagulation/platelet (thrombocyte) activation, inflammatory cytokines and chemokines, innate immune activation (NF-kB, TLR, IL-1/IL-6/IL-8/IL-17/TNF), leukocyte chemotaxis, epidermis/keratinocyte repair, ECM/collagen organization, angiogenesis and epithelial proliferation.
3. Anemia compensation: blood loss predicts erythropoiesis/hemopoiesis, iron homeostasis, hypoxia response and blood-pressure regulation.
4. Tolerance via energy compensation: Galapagos mockingbirds tolerate the fly through increased nestling begging and parental provisioning (Knutie et al. 2016; mockingbird tolerance fails in dry years, McNew et al. 2019). Plasma glucose was measured as a tolerance proxy; finches showed no increase. This predicts glucose/energy homeostasis, insulin signaling, nutrient/starvation responses and growth (IGF) signaling as tolerance-related processes.
5. Behavioral defenses: nestlings preen/eat larvae and stack on nest-mates; females stand upright more, probe nest base, preen nestlings, brood less (Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016; O'Connor et al. 2010). Larval feeding is nocturnal. These give weak-to-moderate links to nociception/pain perception, circadian rhythm and thermoregulation (reduced brooding).
6. Hybrid tree finches have ~half the parasite load of parental species; mechanism unknown (immune, nest traits or parental behavior proposed) (Peters et al. 2019).
7. Oxidative stress: blood loss, inflammation and tissue damage plausibly induce ROS/oxidative-stress responses (supporting, not documented).

## Scoring logic
5: immune/inflammatory/wound-healing processes directly documented (antibody response, inflammation, wound healing, defense response).
4: core components (B/T cell activation, cytokine production/signaling, NF-kB, TLR-type innate activation, coagulation/platelet activation, erythropoiesis/hemopoiesis, iron homeostasis, epidermis/skin development, glucose/energy homeostasis).
3: supporting/downstream (angiogenesis, ECM/collagen, epithelial proliferation/migration, phagocytosis, hypoxia/oxidative stress, insulin/nutrient/starvation responses, JAK-STAT, pain perception, blood pressure).
2: weak/indirect (general growth, adhesion, lipid/energy metabolism, circadian, thermogenesis, bone/cartilage for beak, MAPK/PI3K, apoptosis regulation).
1: no specific link (neural development, cell cycle, RNA processing, cilia, etc.).

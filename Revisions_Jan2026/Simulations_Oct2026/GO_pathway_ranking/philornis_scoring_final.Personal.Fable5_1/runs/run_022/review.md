# Literature review: host responses to *Philornis downsi* (run_022)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- *P. downsi* is an invasive muscid fly in Galapagos. Adults lay eggs in nests; first-instar larvae
  live inside nestlings' nares (and ear canals), later instars live in the nest base and emerge at
  night to feed externally on blood and tissue of nestlings and, sometimes, brooding females.
- Documented consequences: heavy blood loss, anaemia (reduced haemoglobin / haematocrit), reduced
  plasma glucose (mockingbirds), reduced growth and body mass, wounds and tissue damage, enlarged
  / deformed nares and beak malformation (with later effects on song and foraging), nestling
  mortality of ~16-100%, population declines of small tree-finch, mangrove finch, medium tree-finch.
- Secondary consequences proposed: protein depletion, susceptibility of wounds to infection.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
1. **Immune (resistance)**: adult medium ground finches mount *P. downsi*-specific antibody
   responses, higher in nesting females; nestlings show weak/undetectable antibody responses.
   Immune-mediated resistance is thus plausible (B-cell/antibody, innate/inflammatory, cytokine,
   complement, pattern-recognition, phagocytosis) but Galapagos hosts are described as lacking
   effective immunological resistance.
2. **Haematological compensation**: anaemia from blood loss implies erythropoiesis, iron
   homeostasis, hypoxia responses, haemostasis (coagulation, platelets) as core a priori responses.
3. **Tissue repair**: wounds in skin, nares, ear canals -> wound healing, epidermis/keratinocyte,
   extracellular matrix, collagen, angiogenesis, epithelial migration/proliferation.
4. **Tolerance (energetic compensation)**: Galapagos mockingbirds tolerate the parasite; finch
   tolerance varies with rainfall/food. Tolerance depends on nestling begging, parental
   provisioning, growth and energy/glucose/lipid metabolism, nutrient-sensing (insulin/IGF/TOR),
   response to starvation.
5. **Behavioural**: nestling preening of larvae, standing on nestmates, increased begging; parents
   provisioning more, brooding less, standing on nest rim, allopreening; nest-material choice
   (self-fumigation). Sensory/pain pathways plausibly underlie larval detection.
6. **Stress endocrinology**: corticosterone tested in breeding females (no parasite effect found);
   hormone-response terms kept at moderate scores.

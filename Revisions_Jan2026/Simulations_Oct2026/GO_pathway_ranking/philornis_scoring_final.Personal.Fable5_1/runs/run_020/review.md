# Literature review: host responses to *Philornis downsi* (run_020)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- *P. downsi* larvae are obligate hematophagous ectoparasites of nestlings (and brooding females). First instars live inside the nares and ear canals; later instars sit in the nest base and feed on blood through the skin at night (Fessl et al. 2006; O'Connor et al. 2010; McNew & Clayton 2018).
- Documented effects: blood loss, reduced haemoglobin/haematocrit (~28% lower; ~0.8 g/dL per larva), reduced body mass (up to ~56%), shorter primary feathers (~30%), skin lesions/wounds, enlarged or deformed nares that persist into adulthood and alter song and pairing, nestling mortality averaging ~55% (up to 100%), and reduced fledging success (Kleindorfer & Dudaniec 2016; Koop et al. 2011; Kleindorfer et al. 2019; McNew & Clayton 2018).
- Parasitized nestlings may lose sleep (nocturnal feeding), be weakened in begging, and have depleted blood protein (lower survival with low C:N) (Koop et al. 2011; Pike et al. 2024).

## Host defenses documented or proposed
1. **Acquired (antibody) immunity** – adult medium ground finches (especially nesting females) mount a *Philornis*-specific IgY antibody response (Huber et al. 2010; Koop et al. 2013); the response weakens with parasite load and does not clearly improve nestling survival; nestlings show little antibody response, and mockingbirds and finches showed no significant antibody response in Knutie et al. 2016. Still, this is the only directly documented immunological mechanism -> adaptive/B-cell/immunoglobulin, T-cell help, cytokine and lymphocyte terms scored high.
2. **Innate/inflammatory and wound responses** – larvae chew through skin and burrow in nares, producing lesions; wound healing, inflammation, haemostasis/coagulation, epidermal/keratinocyte repair, ECM/collagen, angiogenesis, chemotaxis and phagocytosis are strong a priori predictions. Secondary bacterial infection of wounds (larval gut bacteria) makes antibacterial defense plausible.
3. **Haematological tolerance** – blood loss/anaemia predicts selection on erythropoiesis, haematopoiesis, iron homeostasis, hypoxia response, redox homeostasis.
4. **Energetic/nutritional tolerance** – mockingbird nestlings beg more and parents provision more, compensating for parasite damage; tolerance tracks food availability and rainfall (Knutie et al. 2016; McNew et al. 2019). Finches show no compensation (Pike et al. 2023 found reduced female provisioning). Predicts selection on growth (IGF/insulin signalling), energy and glucose homeostasis, nutrient/starvation responses, metabolism, thermoregulation (reduced female brooding).
5. **Stress physiology** – female corticosterone not altered by parasitism (Knutie et al. 2013); glucose not elevated in finches; hormone/stress terms get moderate scores.
6. **Behavioural defenses** – nestlings stand on nestmates, occasionally remove/eat larvae; females brood less and may eat larvae; possible guava-leaf self-medication; altered begging; song/pairing effects of naris deformity. Neural/behavioural/cognition/sound-perception terms get weak-to-moderate scores; skeletal/craniofacial (naris, beak) terms weak-moderate.
7. **Evolutionary responses proposed** – smaller clutches, hybrid tree finches with lower loads, trade-off between resistance and tolerance (McNew & Clayton 2018).

## Scoring logic
5 = documented mechanism (adaptive immune/antibody, wound response, erythrocyte differentiation); 4 = core components (B/T cells, cytokines, inflammation, coagulation, hypoxia response, iron homeostasis, haemopoiesis, skin/epidermis); 3 = supporting (innate signalling cascades, growth/energy/nutrient responses, oxidative stress, chemotaxis, phagocytosis, ECM, angiogenesis, thermogenesis); 2 = weak/speculative (general metabolism, generic signalling, bone, behaviour, circadian); 1 = no specific link (cell cycle, DNA repair, RNA processing, neuronal development, cilia, etc.).

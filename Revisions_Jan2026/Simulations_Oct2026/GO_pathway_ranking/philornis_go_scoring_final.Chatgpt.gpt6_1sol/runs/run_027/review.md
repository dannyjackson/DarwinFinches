# Independent a priori mechanism review

The ratings concern potential bird responses, not functions of the fly, and do not imply that selection or any particular molecular response has been demonstrated. Each supplied GO ID is retained in its original order. No genomic, transcriptomic or gene-expression findings were used. A retrieved urban-finch paper included gene-expression results; that paper was excluded from the evidence used for scoring.

## Injury and physiological compensation

First and early second instars occupy nestling nostrils and tissues, causing myiasis; older larvae shelter in nest material and feed on nestling blood and tissue. Tissue destruction, anemia, impaired growth and death are established consequences. This supports hemostasis and blood-cell replacement, oxygen delivery and hypoxia responses, and epithelial repair as strong prior mechanisms. The distinction matters: blood loss and tissue damage are documented, while molecular coagulation, repair and hypoxia pathways are generally predictions rather than demonstrated Philornis-specific defenses.

- Fessl, Sinclair & Kleindorfer (2006), *The life-cycle of Philornis downsi ... and its impacts on nestling survival*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16899139/
- Dudaniec, Kleindorfer & Fessl (2006), *Effects ... on haemoglobin level and nestling survival ...*: higher parasite intensities accompanied lower hemoglobin and increased reticulocyte numbers, directly motivating regenerative erythropoiesis. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1442-9993.2006.01553.x
- Koop et al. (2011), *Experimental demonstration of the fitness consequences ...*: experimental evidence for harmful effects on nestling growth and survival. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3092749/

## Resistance and damage limitation

Adult female medium ground finches have experimentally demonstrated fly-binding antibody responses. This does not establish effective protection: immune responses differ by age and species, and nestling responses were not demonstrated in several early studies. Adaptive immunity and its B-cell machinery therefore have a strong prior relationship. Innate recognition, inflammatory recruitment, phagocytosis, complement and cytokine regulation are strong physiological predictions for tissue-invasive feeding and damaged barriers, but are not treated as specifically established fly-defense pathways. Oxidant handling, apoptosis and cellular stress protection are supporting predictions. Antiviral-specific processes receive weaker ratings than broad defenses because the fly is not a virus.

- Koop et al. (2013), *Experimental demonstration of a parasite-induced immune response in wild birds*: https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.651
- McNew & Clayton (2018), *Alien Invasion: Biology of Philornis Flies ...*: review distinguishing resistance, tolerance and behavioral defense and discussing limitations of evidence for nestling immunity. https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-ento-020117-043103

## Tolerance and behavior

Mockingbirds can compensate through increased nestling begging and parental provisioning. Nutrient sensing, fuel mobilization, glucose regulation and energy balance consequently have supporting physiological relevance. Finches did not show the same compensatory changes in the comparative experiment. Nervous-system processes are not uniformly upgraded: sensory detection, pain, learning and timing are more plausible than specific synaptic or embryonic neural construction pathways.

Preening, larval consumption, probing nest material or nestling nares, and avoidance movements have been observed, although their protective efficacy can be limited. Flycatchers also capture adult flies and remove larvae. Incorporation of insecticide-treated cotton demonstrates behavioral access to a protective resource; it does not establish endogenous pesticide detoxification as a natural defense.

- Knutie et al. (2016), *Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate introduced parasites that affect Darwin's finches*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27220210/
- O'Connor et al. (2010), *Video analysis of host-parasite interactions in nests of Darwin's finches*: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0030605310000086
- Pike et al., *Behavior of the Avian Parasite Philornis downsi ... in and Near Host Nests*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8813692/
- Knutie et al. (2014), *Darwin's finches combat introduced nest parasites with fumigated cotton*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24801182/

A hormonal stress pathway remains a plausible general mechanism, but a manipulation found no parasite-treatment difference in adult female baseline or stress-induced corticosterone. Generic steroid and stress pathways therefore are not presumed established defenses. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23892015/

## Scoring boundaries

5 denotes a directly represented documented host response; 4 denotes core immune, regenerative, barrier or repair machinery or a strong prediction from injury; 3 denotes plausible support such as energy metabolism, cellular damage control or repair-related signaling; 2 denotes an indirect connection; 1 denotes no specific mechanism, including many housekeeping, reproductive and embryonic patterning processes. Regulation in either direction can matter and was not assumed beneficial. Generic signaling is less specific than a named injury or defense mechanism.

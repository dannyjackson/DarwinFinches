# Literature basis and scoring rationale

Ratings reflect potential host responses, not evidence of selection. I used published natural-history, experimental, behavioral and physiological studies and general biological knowledge. No genomic, transcriptomic or gene-expression results from Galápagos birds informed these ratings; genetic-study search hits were excluded. No target score distribution was imposed.

**Blood loss and tissue damage.** Early larvae invade nares and other tissues; older larvae shelter in nest material and feed externally on blood. Tissue injury and anemia contribute to mortality. Declining hemoglobin and increasing immature erythrocytes with infestation directly support compensatory erythropoiesis. Iron regulation, hypoxia responses and circulatory compensation are strong predictions. Hemostasis and epithelial/keratinocyte repair are strong predictions from feeding wounds, rather than demonstrated effective resistance.

- Fessl, Sinclair and Kleindorfer (2006), *The life-cycle of Philornis downsi ... and its impacts on nestling survival*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16899139/
- Dudaniec, Kleindorfer and Fessl (2006), *Effects ... on haemoglobin level and nestling survival*: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1442-9993.2006.01553.x

**Immune resistance.** An experimental study found elevated fly-binding antibodies in parasitized medium ground finch mothers, with higher antibodies associated with fewer parasites, but no rescue of reproduction. Fathers and young nestlings lacked detectable induced responses. This supports adaptive immunity and B-cell mechanisms; innate defenses, complement, phagocytosis, inflammatory signaling and cytokines are plausible components or supporting responses, without claiming demonstrated efficacy. Viral defenses have only weak relevance. Bacterial defenses are plausible at injured barriers, but bacterial disease is not assumed to explain mortality.

- Koop et al. (2013), *Experimental demonstration of a parasite-induced immune response in wild birds*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3930052/

**Nutritional and energetic tolerance.** Parasitized mockingbird nestlings begged more and received more food, apparently compensating for damage; finches lacked this pattern. Tolerance varies with rainfall and provisioning resources. Green warbler-finch females reduced provisioning under high infestation, and nestling mass was lower. Energy/nutrient homeostasis and glucose regulation therefore have clear relevance. Nutrient sensing, lipid/amino-acid metabolism and mitochondrial energy pathways support compensation. Molecular changes in those pathways are predictions, not findings from these studies. General growth processes can support recovery; unrelated embryonic patterning programs have no specific connection.

- Knutie et al. (2016), *Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate introduced parasites that affect Darwin's finches*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27220210/
- McNew et al. (2019), *Annual environmental variation influences host tolerance to parasites*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6408884/
- Pike et al. (2023), *Parental food provisioning and nestling growth ... Green Warbler-Finch*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10188583/

**Behavioral defenses and costs.** Observations include preening, larval removal or consumption, agitation and climbing on nestmates. Responses vary with age and need not improve survival. Reduced brooding may limit maternal exposure while compromising nestling thermal support. Nest location and breeding timing can affect exposure. Sensory, motor, learning and circadian pathways are generally indirect; pain detection is more closely tied to feeding injury. Insecticide-treated cotton demonstrates a nest-building route to assisted control. Leaf anointing is documented, but its occurrence outside breeding cautions against assuming a specific anti-Philornis function.

- McNew and Clayton (2018), *Alien Invasion: Biology of Philornis Flies ...*: https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-ento-020117-043103
- Pike et al., *Behavior of the Avian Parasite Philornis downsi ...*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8813692/
- Knutie et al. (2014), *Darwin's finches combat introduced nest parasites with fumigated cotton*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24801182/
- Tebbich et al. (2021), *Darwin's finches habitually anoint their feathers ...*: https://doi.org/10.1111/eth.13153

**Applying the scale.** Score 5 directly matches documented host responses, including antibody immunity, erythrocyte replacement and energy compensation. Score 4 covers core components or strong physiological predictions; 3 covers plausible support; 2 covers weak or indirect links; 1 indicates no specific link. Basal information-processing machinery, chromosome segregation, specialized reproduction and unrelated embryonic structures usually receive 1. Regulatory directions can participate in controlling responses and are not automatically classified as beneficial or harmful.

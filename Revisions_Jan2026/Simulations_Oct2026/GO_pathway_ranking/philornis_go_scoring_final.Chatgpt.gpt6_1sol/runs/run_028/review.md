# Independent a priori mechanism review

## Biological basis

Adult Philornis downsi are free-living; their larvae attack nestlings and sometimes brooding females. Early larvae occupy the nares and other tissues, whereas later larvae shelter in nest material and feed externally, often at night. Feeding removes blood and damages tissue. Reported consequences include anemia and reduced hemoglobin, impaired growth, enlarged or malformed nares and beaks, and high nestling mortality. These findings motivate host blood replacement, maintenance of oxygen delivery, hemostasis, epithelial repair and energy compensation. The cellular details of these compensatory processes are predictions from the injury, rather than established Philornis-specific molecular mechanisms.

## Resistance, tolerance and behavior

Experimental parasite removal demonstrated higher P. downsi-binding antibody levels in parasitized medium ground finch mothers. Stronger maternal responses tended to accompany fewer larvae, but the association was marginal and did not rescue reproductive success. Males and young nestlings did not show the same detectable induced response. This supports adaptive antibody defense as a directly observed mechanism; B-cell functions and immune regulation are core supporting predictions. Inflammation that impedes feeding and host defensive factors ingested by larvae are proposed explanations, not proven mechanisms of larval killing in these birds. Innate defenses, leukocyte recruitment and cytokine signaling are strong or supporting predictions depending on specificity. Antiviral processes receive little credit merely because they belong to immunity.

Mockingbirds can tolerate parasitism through increased nestling begging and parental food provisioning, compensating for damage despite infestation. This capacity varies with environmental conditions; drought can constrain compensation. Medium ground finches did not exhibit comparable compensation in the comparative experiment. Nutrient acquisition, energy homeostasis and resource allocation therefore have clear relevance, while particular metabolic pathways generally remain supporting predictions. Urban/nonurban finch comparisons also link diet and parasite effects, but their observational associations do not establish a specific nutrient-mediated defense.

Nest observations document agitation, repositioning, preening and flapping, with some larval removal. These responses are age-dependent and their protective efficacy is limited or uncertain. Reduced female brooding can expose chicks to thermal costs. Learning, sensory detection, motor control, circadian timing and thermoregulation are consequently relevant at different distances from the actual behavior; generic neuronal or synaptic machinery is only indirectly linked. Leaf rubbing with guayabillo and experimental repellency support a proposed self-medication behavior. Incorporation of supplied insecticide-treated cotton illustrates behavioral exploitation of nesting material; it does not establish an endogenous detoxification defense.

## Scoring interpretation

Each input term was evaluated independently without a target score distribution. Five denotes an observed host mechanism, four a core component or strong physiological prediction, three a credible supporting process, two an indirect connection, and one no specific connection. Specific embryonic, reproductive, viral and nucleic-acid housekeeping processes generally lack a strong parasite-specific rationale. Broad housekeeping processes were not promoted solely because all responses require them. Positive and negative regulation can both contribute to defense or damage tolerance, so direction alone did not determine relevance. The input gives GO:0160240 without a descriptive term; its identity could not be verified in the targeted lookup and it received 1 rather than an invented function.

Only the designated input file was read locally. No bird genomic, transcriptomic, gene-expression or epigenomic findings were used, including irrelevant indexed results encountered during searches. Evidence was obtained from published abstracts and indexed article text; several direct PubMed/PMC opens presented access challenges.

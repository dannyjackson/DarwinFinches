# A priori review: host responses to Philornis downsi

Scoring used the non-genomic literature below and general vertebrate physiology. Every term was considered individually, without targeting a score distribution. Resistance reduces parasite burden; tolerance reduces damage without necessarily reducing burden. A response can be relevant despite being insufficient or costly. No genomic, transcriptomic or gene-expression findings from Galapagos birds informed the ratings; incidental genomic search results were excluded.

## Mechanisms

**Blood and tissue loss.** First and early second larval instars invade nostrils and other tissues; later instars live in nest material and feed externally, especially at night. Tissue destruction, nasal damage, anemia, reduced growth and nestling mortality are established effects; brooding females can also be exposed. These effects make erythrocyte replacement and injury responses particularly relevant. Hypoxia sensing, iron homeostasis, hemostasis, epithelial repair and angiogenesis are strong physiological predictions, without implying that all have been demonstrated as adaptive defenses. General cardiac and metabolic processes are supporting rather than specific mechanisms.

**Immune resistance, with limits.** Experimental manipulation increased fly-binding antibodies in medium ground finch mothers at parasitized nests. Higher antibodies were associated with fewer parasites, suggesting possible resistance, but did not rescue reproductive success. Fathers and nestlings lacked a corresponding induced response in that experiment; comparative work also failed to demonstrate a nestling antibody defense. Humoral/adaptive immunity is directly relevant. B-cell and T-cell processes are core components; innate recognition, cytokines, inflammation and phagocytosis are plausible predicted defenses or injury responses, not established molecular mechanisms against this fly. Antiviral pathways have weak indirect relevance. Wound-associated antibacterial defense is plausible, without assuming proven pathogen transmission by the fly.

**Tolerance and compensation.** Mockingbirds tolerated infestation in a comparative field experiment; parasitized nestlings begged more and received greater parental provisioning, unlike medium ground finches. Replacement of lost blood was also investigated as a possible tolerance mechanism, not a universal established explanation. Later work demonstrated environmental dependence of mockingbird tolerance. Energy homeostasis, nutrient sensing, fuel mobilization and growth therefore have plausible supporting roles. Specific metabolic machinery is less informative than the integrated organismal response.

**Behavior.** Observations include rare larval removal/consumption, probing nest material, and nestling positioning to avoid larvae; widespread effective preening resistance is not established. Altered brooding can affect heat balance. Finches rub endemic guava leaves on plumage, supporting a self-medication hypothesis, with field protection against flies still uncertain. Cognition, sensation, motor control and daily timing can support these behaviors, but generic synaptic processes are weakly specific, and embryonic neural patterning still less directly connected.

**Stress.** Experimental parasite reduction did not support parasite-induced corticosterone changes in breeding female medium ground finches. Stress physiology remains a plausible response domain; that experiment does not establish a general endocrine defense. Oxidative stress and cell-survival processes were treated as supporting predictions rather than demonstrated fly-specific mechanisms.

## Applying the scale

5: direct mechanism represented in this literature; 4: core component or strong prediction; 3: supporting/downstream role; 2: weak or nonspecific connection; 1: no specific link. Generic transcription, translation, protein turnover, transport and organelle processes usually score low despite being necessary for any response. Many embryonic patterning and germ-cell terms lack a specific link. Injured epithelia and compensatory growth have stronger relevance than unrelated organ development. Platelet terms were interpreted cautiously in birds, whose hemostatic cells are thrombocytes. Both positive and negative regulation may matter; regulatory sign alone does not imply benefit.

# A priori biological review: Philornis downsi host responses

## Evidence and mechanisms

**Blood and tissue injury.** Early larvae occupy nestling nares and other tissues; later larvae shelter in nest material and feed externally, principally at night. Blood loss, anemia, reduced hemoglobin, damaged nasal tissues, impaired growth, and nestling mortality are established consequences. These support strong predictions for hemostasis, erythrocyte replacement and iron balance, oxygen delivery/hypoxia responses, epithelial integrity, and wound repair. Those compensatory pathways are biological predictions; the injury evidence does not demonstrate each molecular pathway.

**Resistance through immunity.** Experimental parasite removal showed parasite-binding antibody responses in adult female medium ground finches, with stronger responses tending to accompany fewer parasites. Adult males and nestlings did not show the same induced response, and female immunity did not prevent reproductive failure in that experiment. Thus adaptive immunity and antibody-producing B-cell mechanisms have a particularly direct connection. Innate immunity, complement, inflammatory regulation, leukocyte recruitment, and T-cell support are reasonable mechanistic predictions, not demonstrated universal protection. Antiviral pathways are not equivalent to defenses against a blood-feeding fly.

**Behavioral defenses.** Video observations documented nestling preening, larval consumption, and avoidance, plus females probing nest material and nestling nares. Responses differ among studies; increased preening was not universally detected. Leaf anointing with Psidium galapageium and laboratory repellency of its extracts suggest possible self-medication, without proving field protection from this fly. Sensory detection, pain/irritation, motor behavior, learning, parental care, and nest attendance can contribute, but highly specific neural developmental processes have much weaker links. Reduced brooding could also worsen nestling thermal conditions rather than provide an effective defense.

**Tolerance and resource compensation.** An experimental comparison found mockingbirds maintained reproductive performance under parasitism while medium ground finches did not. Parasitized mockingbird nestlings begged more, associated with greater provisioning; finches lacked this compensation in that study. This supports energy acquisition and allocation, nutrient handling, growth maintenance, and repair as tolerance mechanisms. It does not establish every metabolic pathway as directly fly responsive. A corticosterone experiment found no treatment effect on baseline or acute stress-induced corticosterone; endocrine stress remains plausible rather than established as the causal explanation.

## Scoring interpretation

Each supplied term was considered for host physiology, not fly physiology. Scores distinguish directly demonstrated response classes (5), core components or strong predictions (4), supporting mechanisms (3), weak indirect connections (2), and processes without a specific connection (1). Broad cellular maintenance was not automatically treated as a core defense. Developmental timing and taxonomic applicability matter: reproductive, embryonic patterning, and many specialized neural development terms generally lack a specific connection; avian thrombocytes make mammalian platelet terminology an imperfect but potentially relevant hemostatic analogy. No score distribution was targeted. No genomic, transcriptomic, or gene-expression study results were used. GO:0160240, supplied without a descriptive name, was identified as RNA polymerase II transcription initiation surveillance using the official GO term report, without changing the supplied identifier: https://current.geneontology.org/reports/go-cam-stats/go-cam-unique-go-bp-terms.html

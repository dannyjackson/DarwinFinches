# A priori physiological rationale

I scored the 1,008 supplied biological-process terms individually for their specificity to host responses, without consulting bird genomic, transcriptomic or expression results. Ratings distinguish documented mechanisms (5), their core components or strong predictions (4), supporting processes (3), weak indirect connections (2), and processes with no specific link (1). A high rating means plausible association if later implicated; it does not assert that the process provides effective protection.

## Mechanisms and evidence

- **Blood and tissue loss:** early larvae occupy nostrils and other tissues; later larvae reside in the nest and feed on hosts externally. Tissue injury and anemia are major causes of nestling mortality. This directly motivates blood replacement and erythrocyte differentiation, and strongly predicts hemostasis, tissue repair, vascular repair and oxygen-stress responses. General energy metabolism and cell survival are supporting processes rather than specific fly defenses. Fessl, Sinclair & Kleindorfer (2006), *The life-cycle of Philornis downsi ...*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16899139/ . Dudaniec, Kleindorfer & Fessl (2006), *Effects ... on haemoglobin level and nestling survival ...*, reports lower hemoglobin with greater infestation and increased immature erythrocytes: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1442-9993.2006.01553.x .

- **Immune resistance, with limits:** experimental removal of flies demonstrated parasite-binding antibody responses in female medium ground finches, but no detectable induced response in males or young nestlings. Higher maternal antibody levels were only marginally associated with fewer parasites and did not rescue reproductive success. Adaptive immunity and B-cell processes therefore have strong relevance; inflammatory, innate, cytokine and leukocyte mechanisms are reasonable predictions or supporting pathways, not uniformly demonstrated responses. Defense against bacteria is a secondary-wound possibility; virus-specific processes have no direct support here. Koop et al. (2013), *Experimental demonstration of a parasite-induced immune response ...*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3930052/ .

- **Tolerance and resource compensation:** parasitized mockingbird nestlings increased begging, associated with parental provisioning that could compensate for damage. Finches did not show the same compensation in that experiment. This supports energy homeostasis and resource allocation, with nutrient sensing and metabolic mobilization as plausible supporting processes. Tolerance is context dependent: a later multi-year experiment found lower mockingbird tolerance under limited rainfall, implicating food availability and provisioning. Knutie et al. (2016), *Galapagos mockingbirds tolerate introduced parasites that affect Darwin's finches*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27220210/ . McNew et al. (2019), *Annual environmental variation influences host tolerance to parasites*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6408884/ .

- **Behavioral resistance:** observations include occasional larva removal/preening and nestlings moving above nestmates; these are limited and age dependent. Nest placement, breeding timing and nest maintenance are proposed routes to avoiding exposure. Treated cotton experimentally enabled nest self-fumigation, demonstrating use of nest materials but not an evolved chemical-detoxification mechanism. Sensory, motor and learning processes have indirect links; generic neural development and particular synaptic machinery are much less specific. McNew & Clayton (2018), *Alien Invasion: Biology of Philornis Flies ...*: https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-ento-020117-043103 . Knutie et al. (2014), *Darwin's finches combat introduced nest parasites with fumigated cotton*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24801182/ .

- **Persistent structural injury:** naris deformation can affect adult song, supporting a distinction between damage to existing barriers and generic embryonic patterning or neural development. Kleindorfer et al. (2019), *Introduced parasite changes host phenotype, mating signal and hybridization risk ...*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6571467/ .

## Scoring boundaries

Repair, epithelial integrity, inflammatory regulation, erythropoiesis and compensation are relevant even when improved host survival reflects tolerance rather than reduced parasite burden. Secondary oxidative stress is plausible but not assumed experimentally established. Broad housekeeping, trafficking and regulatory terms generally received weak ratings unless they identify a clearer supporting mechanism. Reproductive cell processes, chromosome mechanics, specific embryonic structures, virus-specific responses and many narrowly defined neural processes lack a specific fly-response link.

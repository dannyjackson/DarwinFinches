# Independent a priori review: Philornis downsi host responses

## Literature basis

**Injury and blood loss.** Early larvae occupy nestling nostrils and sometimes other tissues; later larvae live in nest material and feed externally, predominantly at night. Tissue consumption, open feeding injuries, anemia, impaired growth and high nestling mortality establish the central physiological challenges. Fessl, Sinclair and Kleindorfer (2006), *The life-cycle of Philornis downsi ...*, reports substantial blood loss and distinguishes the tissue-damaging and blood-feeding stages. These observations motivate repair of skin and mucosal barriers, hemostasis, restoration of blood cells and iron balance, and compensation for reduced oxygen delivery. These predictions should not be confused with demonstrated protective effectiveness of every pathway.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16899139/

**Hematological compensation.** Dudaniec, Kleindorfer and Fessl (2006), *Effects ... on haemoglobin level and nestling survival in Darwin’s Small Ground Finch*, examined hemoglobin and immature erythrocytes in relation to parasitism. Lower hemoglobin and altered immature red-cell counts support anemia and erythrocyte replacement as relevant responses. The publisher abstract was retrieved in search results; direct access to its full page failed, so no additional full-text findings were assumed.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1442-9993.2006.01553.x

**Immune resistance, with important limits.** Koop et al. (2013), *Experimental demonstration of a parasite-induced immune response ...*, experimentally found greater fly-binding antibody levels in mothers at parasitized nests. Higher maternal antibody levels were associated with fewer parasites, although causality for protection was not established and the response did not rescue nest success. Males and nestlings did not show equivalent induced responses. The paper proposes inflammation, defensive molecules ingested by feeding larvae, and interactions between itching and grooming as possible resistance mechanisms. Antibody production/adaptive immunity therefore have direct support; B-cell pathways and inflammatory regulation are strong mechanistic predictions. Specific cytokines, innate receptors and phagocytic routes are less directly established.

Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3930052/

**Behavioral resistance.** O’Connor, Robertson and Kleindorfer (2010), *Video analysis of host–parasite interactions in nests of Darwin’s finches*, observed nestling preening, larval ingestion and positioning on nestmates, and maternal probing of nares and nest substrate. These make sensory detection, discomfort/pain responses and coordinated movement plausible supporting processes. They do not make all neural development or synaptic pathways specifically associated with the fly.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0030605310000086
Source record: https://researchnow.flinders.edu.au/en/publications/video-analysis-of-host-parasite-interactions-in-nests-of-darwins-/

**Tolerance through food and energy compensation.** Knutie et al. (2016), *Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate introduced parasites that affect Darwin’s finches*, experimentally compared mockingbirds and medium ground finches. Parasitized mockingbird chicks increased begging, associated with increased parental food provisioning, and maintained reproductive performance despite parasites. Finches did not show comparable compensation in that experiment. This supports energy homeostasis, nutrient availability and growth maintenance as tolerance candidates, while individual metabolic enzymes, insulin pathways or mitochondrial processes remain supporting predictions rather than demonstrated defenses.

Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28792593/

**Plant use and nest construction.** Cimadom et al. (2016) observed finches applying Psidium galapageium leaves to feathers and experimentally showed repellent effects of extracts on adult flies and inhibition of larval development. Protection of wild birds through this behavior remains a hypothesis. Knutie et al. (2014) demonstrated that birds incorporate experimentally supplied insecticide-treated cotton into nests. This establishes a behavioral route to parasite reduction, not endogenous insecticide production or a general strong link to xenobiotic metabolism.

Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5056383/
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24801182/

The synthesis by McNew and Clayton (2018), *Alien Invasion: Biology of Philornis Flies Highlighting Philornis downsi ...*, provided context on resistance, tolerance, nest placement and the limited effectiveness of reported defenses.

Source: https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-ento-020117-043103

## Scoring decisions

Each supplied term was considered as a host process, rather than a fly process. Direct responses received 5; cell-specific machinery of antibody responses, erythropoiesis, inflammatory control, repair, hemostasis and hypoxia compensation generally received 4 when strongly predicted. Supporting metabolism, redox control, matrix organization and relevant signaling generally received 3. Generic transport and cellular maintenance with only indirect links received 2, while unrelated reproductive, embryological or highly specific housekeeping processes generally received 1. Regulatory direction alone did not determine relevance: suppressing excessive inflammation can provide tolerance, and both positive and negative regulators can affect a relevant mechanism.

Species, age and experimental variation were retained: maternal antibody responses are not assumed universal, and absence of a detectable nestling antibody response does not exclude potential relevance of immune pathways. Blood loss does not establish actual hypoxia or oxidative stress in every host; these were treated as predictions. Mammalian-style platelet terminology was interpreted functionally as avian hemostasis, with no claim that birds possess mammalian platelets. The input entry GO:0160240 had no descriptive name and could not be resolved in the search; it received 1 because no specific mechanistic relationship could be justified from available information.

No genomic, transcriptomic or gene-expression findings from Galápagos birds, previous conversations, personal context or other raters’ files were used. No score distribution was targeted. All 1,008 input GO IDs were preserved in order in scores.tsv.

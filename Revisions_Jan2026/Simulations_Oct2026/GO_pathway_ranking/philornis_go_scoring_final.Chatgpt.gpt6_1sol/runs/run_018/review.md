# Literature basis and scoring rationale

The ratings concern host processes, not fly biology. They were assigned from the literature below and general biological knowledge, without genomic, transcriptomic, or gene-expression results from Galápagos birds. Only this run’s supplied term list was read locally. No score distribution was targeted.

## Mechanisms

**Blood loss and tissue injury.** First and early second instars invade the nares and other tissues; later larvae reside in nest material and feed externally on nestlings. Fessl, Sinclair and Kleindorfer documented substantial blood loss, anemia, tissue damage, and high mortality. Thus erythrocyte replacement, hematopoiesis, iron homeostasis, oxygen limitation, hemostasis, skin/epithelial repair, extracellular matrix repair, and vascular repair have strong mechanistic relevance. The specific compensatory cellular pathways are a priori predictions rather than demonstrated molecular responses. Naris deformation can persist in survivors and affect song; this supports local tissue repair and developmental effects more strongly than unrelated embryonic patterning or generalized synaptic development.

**Immune resistance.** Koop and colleagues experimentally demonstrated increased fly-binding antibodies in parasitized medium ground finch mothers. Higher maternal antibody levels were associated with fewer parasites, but did not rescue current nesting success; fathers and nestlings did not show equivalent induced defenses. This directly supports adaptive immunity and strongly supports B-cell mechanisms. Innate sensing, inflammatory regulation, leukocyte recruitment, cytokines, complement, and phagocytic clearance are mechanistically plausible predictions, not demonstrated fly-specific molecular pathways. T-cell processes are supporting predictions; antiviral-specific processes have little specific connection to this extracellular blood-feeding fly. Bacterial defenses could support damaged barriers but are not established direct responses to the fly.

**Nutritional tolerance.** Experimental comparisons found that parasitized mockingbird nestlings increased begging, associated with greater parental provisioning, whereas medium ground finches did not show corresponding compensation. Mockingbird tolerance varied across years and decreased with limited rainfall/resources. Energy balance, nutrient sensing, fuel mobilization and growth maintenance are therefore plausible compensatory mechanisms. Specific glucose, insulin or lipid pathways receive supporting rather than direct-mechanism ratings. The studies do not establish that every parasitized host increases these responses.

**Behavioral defense and stress.** Video observations documented self-preening, larvae consumption, nest probing, and positional avoidance. Sensation, learning, motor activity and behavioral timing may support these defenses, but generic neuronal development or synaptic machinery remains indirect. Incorporation of insecticide-treated cotton demonstrated assisted nest self-fumigation, not an evolved insecticide-detoxification mechanism. Experimental maternal corticosterone measurements did not support a parasite-induced stress response mediating reduced reproductive success; stress/endocrine pathways were not treated as proven defenses.

## Application of the scale

Scores of 5 identify processes directly encompassed by documented immunity/defense. Scores of 4 identify core immune components or strong predictions from blood loss, injury and nutritional compensation. Scores of 3 identify plausible supporting or downstream processes. Scores of 2 indicate indirect links through general cellular maintenance, signaling, metabolism or behavior; necessity for cell function alone does not make a process a specific defense. Scores of 1 indicate no specific connection, especially unrelated reproduction, embryonic patterning, nucleic-acid housekeeping, radiation responses and selected specialized neural processes. Regulation in either direction can be relevant; a negative-regulation term was not automatically discounted. Host bird biology was considered when assessing mammalian-named platelet processes: avian thrombocytes provide the relevant hemostatic analogy.

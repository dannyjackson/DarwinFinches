# Independent a priori review: Philornis downsi and host responses

The scoring used published ecological, behavioral, parasitological and physiological literature plus general biology. No genomic, transcriptomic, gene-expression or selection results from Galapagos birds were used. Scores indicate mechanistic relevance, not evidence that a particular GO process changes in these birds or confers an effective defense.

## Mechanisms and key evidence

- **Blood loss and tissue injury.** Nonparasitic adults produce parasitic larvae. Early instars occupy nostrils and other tissues; later instars shelter in nest material and feed externally on nestlings. Tissue destruction and anemia contribute to mortality. Blood replacement, iron regulation, oxygen delivery, hemostasis, epithelial repair and vascular repair therefore have strong mechanistic relevance; detailed molecular implementations remain predictions. Fessl, Sinclair & Kleindorfer (2006), *The life-cycle of Philornis downsi ... and its impacts on nestling survival*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16899139/ . The hematological study by Dudaniec, Kleindorfer & Fessl (2006), *Effects ... on haemoglobin level and nestling survival*, further links parasitism to blood physiology: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1442-9993.2006.01553.x .

- **Immune resistance and its limits.** Experimental parasite removal showed parasite-binding antibody responses in adult female medium ground finches, without a corresponding detectable response in males or the young nestlings studied. Maternal antibody levels tended to correlate inversely with parasite abundance, but did not rescue reproduction. The paper discusses antibody-associated inflammation, itching and defensive molecules as possible mechanisms rather than demonstrated effective pathways in finch nestlings. This supports adaptive immunity strongly, with B/T-cell support, cytokines, innate recognition and inflammatory regulation as core or supporting predictions. Koop et al. (2013): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3930052/ .

- **Resource-dependent tolerance.** Galapagos mockingbirds can compensate through increased nestling begging and parental provisioning, whereas medium ground finches did not show the same response in the comparative experiment. Energy and glucose homeostasis, nutrient use, growth maintenance and parental behavior are relevant. Tolerance varies with conditions; drought can limit compensation. Knutie et al. (2016), *Galapagos mockingbirds tolerate introduced parasites that affect Darwin's finches*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27220210/ . McNew et al. (2019), *Annual environmental variation influences host tolerance to parasites*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6408884/ .

- **Behavioral resistance and avoidance.** Observations include occasional preening/removal of larvae, nestling agitation and altered brooding; robust effective behavioral resistance is not generally established. Reduced brooding may compromise thermoregulation. Leaf rubbing with insect-repellent plants is a proposed self-medication mechanism. Finches also incorporate experimentally supplied insecticide-treated nest material; this is assisted control, not proof of an evolved chemical defense. McNew & Clayton (2018), *Alien Invasion: Biology of Philornis Flies Highlighting Philornis downsi*: https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-ento-020117-043103 .

- **Developmental consequences.** Naris/beak damage can persist and alter song, connecting localized repair and sensory/behavioral consequences to the fly without making all neural or embryonic development directly relevant. Kleindorfer et al. (2019): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6571467/ .

## Scoring interpretation

Directly matching immune and erythrocyte responses received the highest relevance. Strong physiological predictions such as wound healing, coagulation and hypoxia responses were distinguished from documented responses. Generic metabolism, stress pathways, cell migration and tissue maintenance were generally supporting or indirect; specialized unrelated organogenesis, germ-cell processes and generic information-processing machinery often lacked a specific link. Antiviral specificity was not treated as evidence of anti-fly specificity. Mammalian platelet terminology was interpreted cautiously in the avian thrombocyte context. Opposite regulatory directions were assessed for relevance, not assumed to be beneficial or harmful.

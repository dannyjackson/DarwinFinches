# Independent a priori review: Philornis downsi host responses

## Evidence and mechanisms

**Blood and tissue loss.** First and early second instars occupy the nostrils and other tissues; older larvae shelter in nest material and feed externally on nestling blood. Fessl, Sinclair and Kleindorfer (2006) describe this life cycle and substantial blood loss. Dudaniec, Kleindorfer and Fessl (2006) associate heavier infestation with lower haemoglobin and altered immature red-cell counts. Thus erythrocyte replacement and haematopoiesis have a particularly direct physiological connection. Iron handling, oxygen sensing, hypoxia responses, circulatory compensation and energy supply are strong or supporting predictions; they need not have been demonstrated as effective defenses.

**Tissue injury and repair.** Larval feeding damages nasal and other tissues, and naris/beak malformations can persist into adulthood (Galligan and Kleindorfer 2009). Wound responses are directly relevant. Haemostasis, epithelial maintenance, extracellular matrix repair, fibroblast activity and vascular repair are predicted components rather than demonstrations that these processes confer resistance. Specific embryonic patterning and unrelated organogenesis receive little weight simply because a growing nestling is affected. Platelet-related GO terms are treated as mechanistically related to haemostasis, with the qualification that birds have nucleated thrombocytes rather than mammalian platelets.

**Immune resistance.** Koop et al. (2013) experimentally detected increased P. downsi-binding antibodies in adult female medium ground finches, but not males or nestlings. The inverse association with parasite abundance did not establish effective protection or improved offspring survival. Other experiments found no significant antibody response in the compared nestlings. Adaptive immunity and its B-cell components therefore remain justified a priori candidates despite inconsistent or absent responses in particular ages/species. Innate recognition, inflammation, cytokine signaling and leukocyte functions are strong or supporting biological predictions from feeding injuries and parasite exposure, rather than molecular results from these birds. Antiviral-specific processes have only indirect links; the fly is not assumed to transmit a virus.

**Tolerance and resource compensation.** Knutie et al. (2016) found that parasitized mockingbird nestlings increased begging, associated with greater parental provisioning, whereas medium ground finches did not show comparable compensation. McNew et al. (2019) found that mockingbird tolerance declined in low-rainfall years, alongside evidence implicating resource limitation. These results motivate energy homeostasis, nutrient responses and metabolic support, growth maintenance and physiological repair. General metabolism is less specific than an explicitly compensatory mechanism.

**Behavioral resistance and avoidance.** The behavioral literature and McNew and Clayton's review describe occasional larval removal/preening, agitation and altered parental posture or brooding; evidence for effective behavioral resistance in finches is limited. Nestling repositioning, parental attendance and nest-site or breeding-time differences may influence exposure. Sensory and motor processes can support these responses, but a specific synaptic or neuronal-development term is not automatically a parasite-defense mechanism. Larval feeding is mainly nocturnal, giving circadian processes a plausible supporting connection.

## Scoring approach

Each term was judged for a potential host response, including damage compensation and susceptibility, without predicting the direction of selection or requiring an already effective defense. Scores distinguish documented host mechanisms (5), their core components or strong predictions (4), plausible support (3), weak/indirect links (2), and no specific link (1). Broad housekeeping processes generally receive 2; unrelated reproductive, developmental or exposure-specific processes generally receive 1. No target score distribution was imposed. Only non-genomic physiological, ecological and behavioral evidence informed the ratings. A genomic/expression search hit was excluded and none of its results was used. GO:0160240 was independently identified as RNA polymerase II transcription initiation surveillance; its supplied identifier was retained.

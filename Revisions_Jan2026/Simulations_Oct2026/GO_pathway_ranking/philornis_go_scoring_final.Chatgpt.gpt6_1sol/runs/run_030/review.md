# A priori physiological relevance to Philornis downsi

This independent assessment used the supplied biological-process terms, a fresh web literature review and general biological knowledge. No bird genomic, transcriptomic or gene-expression results were used. An epigenetic/expression paper appeared incidentally among search results and was excluded from the evidence and scoring.

## Mechanisms and evidence

* **Blood loss and injury.** Early larvae inhabit nestling nares and other tissues; later larvae feed externally, mainly at night, from the nest. Blood feeding and tissue consumption cause anemia, reduced hemoglobin, skin lesions, persistent naris/beak deformation, impaired growth and mortality. Erythropoiesis, iron handling, responses to hypoxia, hemostasis, epithelial repair and wound healing are therefore strong physiological predictions; their prediction status is distinguished from direct demonstrations of an effective defense. Sources: Fessl et al. (2006), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16899139/ ; Koop et al. (2011), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3092749/ .

* **Immune resistance.** Experimental nest-fly reduction demonstrated parasite-binding antibody responses in medium ground finch mothers; higher maternal antibody levels were associated with fewer parasites, but parasitized broods still failed. Fathers and nestlings lacked a detectable induced response in that experiment. Antibody/adaptive immune processes have direct support; B-cell differentiation/activation and receptor signaling are core components. Inflammation is discussed as a potential means of interfering with blood feeding. Innate immunity, cytokines, complement, leukocyte recruitment and phagocytic clearance are reasonable predictions, not established protection in all host species or ages. Source: Koop et al. (2013), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3930052/ .

* **Tolerance and energy compensation.** Parasitized Galapagos mockingbird nestlings increased begging, with greater parental provisioning apparently compensating for damage. Comparable compensation was absent in medium ground finches. Tolerance varied among years and declined under food-limited drought conditions. Energy balance, nutrient transport, glucose and lipid metabolism, growth and resource allocation are consequently plausible supporting processes; this evidence does not establish a particular intracellular metabolic pathway as a specific fly response. Sources: Knutie et al. (2016), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27220210/ ; McNew et al. (2019), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6408884/ .

* **Behavioral resistance and exposure.** Occasional larval removal by preening, scratching, parental picking and avoidance movements have been observed, but their effectiveness is limited or unproven. Nest construction can be used for human-assisted self-fumigation with insecticide-treated cotton. Sensation, pain, learning, motor function and circadian activity may support these behaviors, but generic synaptic or neural-development processes are usually only weakly linked. Sources: McNew and Clayton (2018), https://www.annualreviews.org/doi/pdf/10.1146/annurev-ento-020117-043103 ; Knutie et al. (2014), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24801182/ ; nest observations, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8813692/ .

* **Stress and limitations.** Manipulating fly abundance did not alter maternal corticosterone, condition or hematocrit in a breeding-female experiment. General hormonal/stress mechanisms remain possible, particularly in nestlings, but the null adult results do not justify a high score for every hormone response. Source: Knutie et al. (2013), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4383185/ .

## Scoring interpretation

Each term was judged by its own specificity and its distance from these mechanisms, without a target distribution. Score 5 identifies a directly matching documented response (e.g. parasite-induced immune response or response to injury); score 4 includes core immune components and strong predictions from bleeding and tissue damage; score 3 denotes plausible supporting or downstream physiology; score 2 denotes weak generic cellular, metabolic or behavioral support; score 1 denotes no specific connection, particularly narrowly specified germline, embryonic-patterning, radiation, viral and RNA-processing processes. General immune processes are not assumed to be protective in every life stage. Similarly, relevance to damage tolerance does not mean that the process was measured or shown adaptive in this system. Avian platelet-labelled processes received only supporting scores because avian hemostasis involves nucleated thrombocytes rather than mammalian platelets. Developmental processes were distinguished from post-injury repair, and virus-specific immunity was not treated as anti-fly immunity.

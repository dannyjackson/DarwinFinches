# A priori mechanistic review: Philornis downsi and Galápagos land birds

This independent rating uses the supplied GO terms, published ecological and physiological literature, and general biological knowledge. No selection, transcriptomic, gene-expression, or genomic results from Galápagos birds were used. A potentially relevant process need not be an effective defense or show a positive response in every species or age class.

## Host injury and predicted physiological compensation

Early larvae occupy nestling nares and other tissues; later larvae shelter in nest material and feed externally, mainly at night. Tissue destruction, wounds, blood loss and anemia reduce growth and survival. Nasal and beak damage can persist. Consequently, blood replacement, iron handling, hypoxia responses, hemostasis, epithelial repair and vascular repair are strong mechanistic predictions. Specific erythropoietic, clotting and repair pathways were generally scored 4 rather than claiming that their molecular activation has been demonstrated. More general growth, metabolic and stress processes were scored 3 or below.

Key sources:
- Fessl, Sinclair & Kleindorfer (2006), *The life-cycle of Philornis downsi (Diptera: Muscidae) parasitizing Darwin's finches and its impacts on nestling survival*. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16899139/
- Dudaniec, Kleindorfer & Fessl (2006), *Effects of the introduced ectoparasite Philornis downsi on haemoglobin level and nestling survival in Darwin's small ground finch (Geospiza fuliginosa)*. https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1442-9993.2006.01553.x
- Galligan & Kleindorfer (2009), *Naris and beak malformation caused by the parasitic fly, Philornis downsi ... in Darwin's small ground finch*. https://academic.oup.com/biolinnean/article-abstract/98/3/577/2532299

## Immune resistance

An experimental nest treatment study detected greater fly-binding antibody levels in parasitized female medium ground finches, but not males or nestlings. Greater maternal antibodies were associated with fewer parasites only marginally; effective protection of offspring was not established. The literature proposes inflammatory skin responses and ingested immune effectors as potential resistance mechanisms. Adaptive immunity therefore has a direct link; B-cell mechanisms, innate immunity, inflammatory recruitment and cytokine pathways are strong predictions, without asserting that any particular signaling pathway has been measured in these birds. Antibacterial defenses have a more indirect link through damaged barriers; virus-specific responses have little parasite-specific justification.

Key source: Koop et al. (2013), *Experimental demonstration of a parasite-induced immune response in wild birds: Darwin's finches and introduced nest flies*. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3930052/

## Tolerance and energy compensation

In experimental comparisons, parasitized Galápagos mockingbird nestlings increased begging, associated with greater parental provisioning and apparent compensation for parasite damage. Medium ground finches did not show equivalent compensation. Mockingbird tolerance varied among years and declined in dry conditions, supporting resource-dependent tolerance rather than universal immunity. Nutrient sensing, energy balance and metabolic support are consequently relevant; generic cellular energy metabolism is supporting rather than specific evidence of defense.

Key sources:
- Knutie et al. (2016), *Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate introduced parasites that affect Darwin's finches*. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27220210/
- McNew et al. (2019), *Annual environmental variation influences host tolerance to parasites*. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6408884/

## Behavioral resistance and avoidance

Preening, larval removal, scratching and repositioning have been observed, although protection is often limited. Parent attendance can affect opportunities for flies to visit nests. Nest timing and placement are possible avoidance mechanisms. Finches can incorporate supplied insecticide-treated cotton into nests. They also anoint feathers with Psidium galapageium leaves: laboratory extracts repel adult flies and inhibit larval growth, but field protection and the behavior's exact target remain uncertain. Sensory, learning, motor and circadian processes can support these behaviors; generic synaptic machinery receives only a weak link, not the score of the behavior itself.

## Scoring conventions

Each of the 1,008 terms was considered individually, retaining its supplied GO ID. Scores reflect mechanistic specificity, not whether a process is generally essential to survival. Broad signaling, transport, cytoskeletal and neural terms usually receive 2; plausible injury, metabolic or repair-supporting processes 3; core immune, blood-loss and barrier-repair mechanisms 4; directly represented documented responses 5. Unrelated reproduction, embryonic patterning and many housekeeping processes receive 1. Positive and negative regulation can both be relevant to controlling damage and defense costs. Mammalian platelet terminology was interpreted cautiously as the conserved hemostatic function relevant to avian thrombocytes, not evidence that birds have mammalian platelets. The unlabeled GO:0160240 was verified as RNA polymerase II transcription initiation surveillance and scored 1; the output ID was preserved.

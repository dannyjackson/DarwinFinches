# Literature basis and scoring

Ratings are independent a priori judgments using natural-history, experimental parasitology, behavioral and physiological literature plus general biological knowledge. No genomic, transcriptomic, gene-expression or selection results were used. All 1,008 supplied terms were considered without a target score distribution.

## Host effects and defenses

**Blood loss and injury.** Early larvae occupy nares and tissues; later larvae reside in nest material and feed on blood, principally at night. Anemia and tissue injury contribute to mortality. Higher infestation is associated with lower hemoglobin and more immature erythrocytes, supporting compensatory erythropoiesis. Nares and beak damage can persist, and nestling growth is impaired. Blood replacement is directly relevant; iron handling, hemostasis, hypoxia responses, epithelial repair, matrix remodeling and vascular repair are core or strong predictions rather than individually established defenses.

**Immune resistance.** Experimental work found elevated fly-binding antibodies in parasitized adult female medium ground finches, with higher antibody levels accompanying fewer parasites. This did not rescue nestling survival. Adult males and nestlings did not show equivalent induced responses. Another comparative experiment found no significant antibody response, so immunity is context-dependent. Adaptive immunity and B-cell functions have a close link; innate recognition, inflammation, cytokines and phagocytic clearance are predictions or supporting processes, not demonstrated molecular pathways. The fly is not assumed to transmit viral or bacterial pathogens.

**Tolerance and nutrition.** In one experiment, parasitized mockingbird nestlings begged more and parents provisioned more, consistent with energy compensation. Medium ground finches did not compensate similarly. Compensation varies among studies and species; heavily infested green warbler-finch nests received less provisioning by females. Energy homeostasis is directly relevant, with metabolism, nutrient acquisition, growth and tissue replacement supporting tolerance. These observations do not identify particular metabolic pathways as adaptive.

**Behavior.** Preening, scratching, larval removal and avoidance movements have been observed, sometimes rarely or without demonstrated efficacy. Altered brooding may reduce exposure while compromising nestling temperature control. Nest position and breeding timing are proposed avoidance routes. Finches anoint feathers with Psidium leaves; extracts repel adult flies and inhibit larval growth, but protection of wild nests remains a hypothesis. Birds also incorporate experimentally supplied insecticide-treated cotton into nests. These support behavioral flexibility and motor/sensory processes indirectly, rather than all neural-development terms. Xenobiotic handling has only a weak indirect connection through plant chemicals or supplied insecticide.

**Stress and allocation.** Resource allocation between growth, repair, defense and reproduction is plausible. An experiment did not find elevated corticosterone in parasitized breeding females; a universal endocrine stress response is therefore not assumed. General oxidative-stress and metabolic processes are supporting predictions.

## Rating interpretation

5 identifies a documented mechanism, including acquired immunity, erythrocyte replacement or compensatory energy balance. 4 identifies core machinery or strong predictions from anemia, injury or immune defense. 3 identifies plausible supporting physiology. 2 identifies weak, indirect connections through broad signaling, transport or other nonspecific functions. 1 identifies no specific connection, including many specialized embryonic, reproductive, transcriptional and chromosome-maintenance processes. Relevance does not imply demonstrated protection or selection. Regulation in either direction can be relevant. GO:0160240 has no descriptive name in the input; the GO report identifies it as RNA polymerase II transcription initiation surveillance, with no specific connection here.

# Independent a priori mechanism review

Scores were assigned from ecological, behavioral, parasitological and physiological literature and general biological knowledge. No genomic, transcriptomic or gene-expression results from Galápagos birds were used. Each input term was considered independently, without a target score distribution. The supplied GO IDs and order are retained.

## Host injury and physiological compensation

Early larvae inhabit nestling nares and other tissues; later larvae generally live in nest material and emerge to feed externally, especially at night. Blood and tissue feeding causes wounds, nasal damage, anemia, reduced development and substantial nestling mortality. Adult brooding females can also be exposed. Fessl, Sinclair & Kleindorfer (2006), *The life-cycle of Philornis downsi ...*, documents the life cycle and combined tissue damage and blood loss:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16899139/
https://doi.org/10.1017/S0031182006001089

Dudaniec, Kleindorfer & Fessl (2006), *Effects ... on haemoglobin level and nestling survival ...*, reports lower hemoglobin with increasing parasite intensity and increased immature erythrocytes, consistent with regenerative compensation:
https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1442-9993.2006.01553.x

Accordingly, erythropoiesis is directly relevant. Hypoxia responses, iron availability and hemostasis are strong physiological predictions; vascular repair, epithelial repair, extracellular matrix remodeling, energy metabolism and oxidative protection are supporting predictions. Specific cytokines, oxidative mechanisms and cellular repair pathways are not thereby established as measured Philornis responses.

## Resistance and behavioral defenses

Koop et al. (2013), *Experimental demonstration of a parasite-induced immune response ...*, experimentally found increased fly-binding antibodies in adult female medium ground finches. Higher antibodies were associated with a tendency toward fewer parasites, but did not rescue fledging. Males and young nestlings did not show comparable induced responses. Antibody-facilitated inflammation, impaired feeding and immune-linked itching are proposed explanations, rather than demonstrated molecular mechanisms:
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3930052/
https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.651

O'Connor, Robertson & Kleindorfer (2010), *Video analysis of host–parasite interactions ...*, documents grooming, larval consumption and avoidance, including nestlings standing on one another. These behaviors need not provide sufficient protection. Koop et al. also found altered female brooding/standing behavior and little effective grooming in very young nestlings:
https://doi.org/10.1017/S0030605310000086

Cimadom et al. (2016), *Darwin's finches treat their feathers with a natural repellent*, observed leaf application and experimentally demonstrated repellency of Psidium leaf extracts to flies and inhibition of larval growth. Natural protection of nests remains a hypothesis:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27721475/
https://doi.org/10.1038/srep34559

## Tolerance and limitations

Knutie et al. (2016) found mockingbird tolerance associated with increased begging and provisioning, unlike medium ground finches; neither species showed a significant induced antibody response in that study. Thus immune responses vary by context:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27220210/
https://doi.org/10.1890/15-0119.1

McNew et al. (2019) found tolerance declined in dry years, with resource-dependent provisioning implicated:
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6408884/
https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2019.0049

An experimental adult female finch corticosterone study did not support parasite-driven endocrine stress as the mediator of reproductive failure; generic hormone terms therefore receive only indirect scores:
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23892015/

## Scoring interpretation

5 denotes direct correspondence to documented immunity, blood-cell compensation or energy compensation; 4 denotes a core component or strong prediction; 3 a plausible supporting process; 2 a weak or nonspecific physiological connection; 1 no particular host-response link. Generic housekeeping was not elevated merely because defense requires living cells. Embryonic, reproductive, viral and specialized developmental terms were not assumed to reflect antiparasite adaptations. Platelet terms receive supporting scores because birds use nucleated thrombocytes rather than mammalian platelets. The input's unnamed GO:0160240 was checked against the GO report (RNA polymerase II transcription initiation surveillance) and scored without renaming:
https://release.geneontology.org/2026-08-05/reports/go-cam-stats/go-cam-unique-go-terms.html

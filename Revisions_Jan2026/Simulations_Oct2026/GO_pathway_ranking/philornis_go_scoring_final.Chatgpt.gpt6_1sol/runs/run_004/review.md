# A priori review: Philornis downsi host responses

## Scope and scoring
I scored the 1,008 supplied biological-process terms individually using non-genomic natural-history, experimental ecological, immunological and behavioral evidence plus general vertebrate biology. Scores measure physiological relevance, not evidence of selection, efficacy, or whether a pathway necessarily increases in activity. No target distribution was imposed. Genomic, transcriptomic and gene-expression findings from Galapagos birds were excluded; an incidental search result mentioning such work was disregarded. Only this run's input file was read locally.

## Mechanisms and evidence

**Blood loss and injury.** Larvae, rather than the free-living adult flies, parasitize birds. Early instars occupy nostrils and tissues; later instars shelter in nest material and feed externally, largely at night. Myiasis, wounds and blood feeding cause anemia and substantial mortality. Fessl, Sinclair & Kleindorfer (2006), *The life-cycle of Philornis downsi ... and its impacts on nestling survival*, documents tissue damage and anemia: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16899139/ . Accordingly, coagulation, erythrocyte differentiation, hematopoiesis, iron balance, hypoxia responses, tissue repair, epithelial integrity and vascular repair have strong a priori relevance. The latter pathways are physiological predictions from injury and anemia, not demonstrated molecular mechanisms in this system.

**Immune resistance.** Koop et al. (2013), *Experimental demonstration of a parasite-induced immune response in wild birds*, experimentally found elevated parasite-binding antibodies in mothers at parasitized medium-ground-finch nests, but not males or young nestlings. Higher maternal antibody levels tended to accompany fewer larvae, without rescuing fledging success. The paper proposes inflammation and ingested defensive molecules as routes by which antibodies could inhibit blood feeding; these routes should not be described as established in these finches. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3930052/ . Adaptive immune response therefore directly matches documented host activity; B-cell processes and T-cell help are core components. Innate recognition, cytokines and inflammatory control are strong predictions or supporting processes. Antiviral-specific pathways have little specific relevance; antimicrobial defenses have plausible secondary relevance to breached tissues without assuming that secondary infection was demonstrated.

**Tolerance and energy compensation.** Knutie et al. (2016), *Galapagos mockingbirds tolerate introduced parasites that affect Darwin's finches*, found increased begging and parental provisioning in parasitized mockingbirds, unlike the finches studied. Neither species mounted a significant antibody response in that experiment, emphasizing context dependence. Source: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27220210/ . McNew et al. (2019), *Annual environmental variation influences host tolerance to parasites*, found that mockingbird tolerance declined during resource-poor dry years and linked tolerance to provisioning: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6408884/ . Energy homeostasis directly captures this compensatory mechanism. Nutrient sensing, fuel metabolism and transport, growth and cellular stress protection are plausible supporting processes rather than specific demonstrated pathways.

**Behavioral resistance.** Preening, scratching, shaking, larval removal, nest sanitation and adult-fly predation have been observed, with incomplete or age-dependent protection. Pike et al., *Behavior of the Avian Parasite Philornis downsi ... in and Near Host Nests*, documents flycatcher predation and larval removal: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8813692/ . Sensory perception, pain detection, motor function and behavioral plasticity can support these responses; generic synapse development or embryonic neural patterning is substantially less specific. Knutie et al. (2014), *Darwin's finches combat introduced nest parasites with fumigated cotton*, shows that birds incorporate experimentally supplied treated fibers: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24801182/ . This is assisted behavioral control, not evidence for an endogenous insecticide pathway.

## Interpretation limits
Broad cellular housekeeping, transcription and RNA processing do not receive high scores merely because all defenses require them. Embryonic patterning, gametogenesis and unrelated stimulus-specific responses generally lack a specific connection. Repair and inflammatory terms are assessed in both regulatory directions because restraint of damage can support tolerance. Developmental effects of parasitism do not make every developmental term a direct defense.

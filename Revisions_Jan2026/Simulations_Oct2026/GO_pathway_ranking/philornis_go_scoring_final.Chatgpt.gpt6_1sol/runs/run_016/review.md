# Independent a priori review: Philornis downsi and avian host responses

The scores use parasite natural history, experimental ecology, physiology, and general biological knowledge. No bird genomic, transcriptomic, or gene-expression findings were used. I read only the supplied input file locally. Scores describe potential association with a host response, not demonstrated selection, benefit, or effect direction.

## Documented effects and defenses

**Blood loss and tissue injury.** First and early second instars inhabit the nares and other tissues; later larvae reside in nest material and feed externally on host blood. Fessl, Sinclair and Kleindorfer (2006), *The life-cycle of Philornis downsi ... and its impacts on nestling survival*, reported substantial blood loss. This establishes strong prior links to hemostasis, damaged epithelial barriers, wound responses, and anemia-associated oxygen limitation; the specific wound-repair pathways remain predictions.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16899139/

Dudaniec, Kleindorfer and Fessl (2006), *Effects ... on haemoglobin level and nestling survival in Darwin's Small Ground Finch*, documented reduced hemoglobin and associations between infestation, immature erythrocytes, and hemoglobin. Compensatory erythrocyte production therefore has particularly direct support; iron handling and hematopoietic progenitors are closely related predictions.
https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1442-9993.2006.01553.x

**Immune resistance.** Koop et al. (2013), *Experimental demonstration of a parasite-induced immune response in wild birds*, found higher fly-binding antibodies in mothers at parasitized nests, with evidence suggestive of reduced parasite abundance at higher antibody levels. Males and nestlings did not show equivalent induced responses, and maternal responses did not rescue nestling survival. Antibody/adaptive immunity is documented; B-cell processes are core components. Innate inflammation, complement, cytokines, and leukocyte recruitment are biologically motivated predictions rather than established effective defenses against this fly.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3930052/

**Tolerance through compensation.** Knutie et al. (2016), *Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate introduced parasites that affect Darwin's finches*, experimentally showed mockingbird tolerance accompanied by increased nestling begging and parental provisioning. Medium ground finches lacked corresponding changes in begging, provisioning, or plasma glucose. Energy balance and food-dependent compensation have direct support; individual metabolic pathways remain supporting hypotheses.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27220210/

McNew et al. (2019), *Annual environmental variation influences host tolerance to parasites*, found mockingbird tolerance varied among years and declined under limited rainfall, consistent with provisioning and resource limitation affecting compensation.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6408884/

**Behavioral resistance and avoidance.** McNew and Clayton's 2018 review describes occasional larva-removal/preening and nestling positioning behavior, with limited evidence of effectiveness. Nest location and timing can influence exposure, but documented associations do not establish evolved avoidance.
https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-ento-020117-043103

Cimadom et al. (2016), *Darwin's finches treat their feathers with a natural repellent*, observed leaf anointing; extracts repelled adult flies and inhibited larval growth experimentally. Protection of wild nests was proposed, not established.
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5056383/

Knutie et al. (2014), *Darwin's finches combat introduced nest parasites with fumigated cotton*, demonstrated incorporation of experimentally provided insecticide-treated nest material. This supports behavioral possibilities without showing natural insecticide production by hosts.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24801182/

**Stress remains conditional.** Knutie et al. (2013), *Experimental test ... corticosterone levels of breeding Darwin's finches*, did not detect parasite-treatment differences in maternal corticosterone, condition, or hematocrit. Thus generalized endocrine stress is plausible, not a universally demonstrated response.
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23892015/

## Scoring interpretation

Scores of 5 emphasize documented adaptive immune responses, erythrocyte compensation, wound response, and energy balance. Scores of 4 cover core immune machinery and strong injury, anemia, and nutritional predictions. Scores of 3 cover supporting repair, metabolism, signaling, oxidative stress, growth, and behavior. Scores of 2 indicate remote or nonspecific support. Scores of 1 indicate no specific mechanistic link, including much basal information-processing machinery, unrelated developmental systems, and irrelevant exposures. Negative regulation can be relevant through limiting immunopathology or balancing resource allocation; a high score does not imply that increasing a pathway is beneficial. Avian hemostasis uses thrombocytes: platelet terms are interpreted for their conserved hemostatic functions, not as evidence that birds possess mammalian platelets.

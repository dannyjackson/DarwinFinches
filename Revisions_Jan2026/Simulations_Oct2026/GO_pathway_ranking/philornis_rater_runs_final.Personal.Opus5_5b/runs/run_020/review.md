# Philornis downsi host-response review (run_020)

## What the larvae do
- First-instar larvae live in the nares of nestlings and feed on tissue; later instars live in nest material and feed on blood at night. Results include blood loss (lower haemoglobin), tissue damage and wounds, lower growth, and high nestling mortality (often over 50%, sometimes 100%).
- Damage to the nares and beak in early life leaves adult beaks deformed (enlarged nares), which changes song (vocal performance) and can affect mate choice and hybridisation.
- Adult females that brood also face stress, and the stress-hormone (corticosterone) response has been tested.

## Host defenses
- **Resistance (immune):** finches make parasite-specific IgY antibodies (a humoral, B-cell response). Higher antibody levels go with fewer or smaller larvae, so the adaptive immune response is documented. Innate and inflammatory responses at bite and wound sites are a strong prediction.
- **Tolerance:** Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate the fly. They lose blood but keep growth and survival, probably by replacing blood (making new red cells) and by more parental feeding. Tolerance of blood loss also implies roles for clotting and platelets, iron balance, wound repair and tissue regeneration.
- **Behavioural:** female in-nest attendance predicts parasite load. Proposed defenses include brooding, nest sanitation, preening and begging or parental feeding. Living in urban areas and having more food reduce the cost of the fly.

## How the scores map to mechanisms
- **5:** humoral or adaptive immunity, B-cell activation, red cell formation, clotting, wound healing and response to wounding.
- **4:** immune and inflammatory processes, haemopoiesis, platelets and iron balance, cytokines, phagocytosis.
- **3:** cytokine and NF-kB/JAK-STAT signalling, oxidative stress, hypoxia (anaemia), skin and keratinocyte processes, angiogenesis, extracellular matrix and collagen, beak and bone growth, nutrient and energy balance, stress hormones.
- **2:** general metabolic, growth, hormone, neural and behavioural processes.
- **1:** everything else.

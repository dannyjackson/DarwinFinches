# Review: host responses to *Philornis downsi* in Galápagos land birds

## What the larvae do to hosts
- First-instar larvae live in the nestlings' nares and ear canals and feed there, which can enlarge or deform the naris. Later instars live in the base of the nest and feed at night. They pierce the skin and take blood and other fluids.
- Hosts lose blood, have lower haemoglobin and suffer high in-nest mortality. Larvae have also been found feeding on brooding females. Naris deformation lasts into adulthood and alters song (vocal performance), which affects mate choice and hybridization.
- Hosts include Darwin's finches (*Geospiza*, *Camarhynchus*, *Certhidea*), Galápagos mockingbirds and other passerines.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
1. **Resistance (adaptive humoral).** Brooding female medium ground finches produce *P. downsi*-specific antibodies (IgY). Higher antibody titres go with fewer parasites (Huber et al. 2010; Koop et al. 2013).
2. **Resistance (innate/inflammatory, proposed).** Larvae pierce the skin again and again, so local inflammation, immune-cell recruitment and cytokine signalling at the feeding site are strong a priori predictions. In other bird systems, *Philornis* larvae trigger inflammatory responses in the skin.
3. **Tolerance (blood-loss compensation, predicted).** Parasitism lowers haemoglobin and causes blood loss (Dudaniec et al. 2006). Faster red-blood-cell replacement (erythropoiesis), haemostasis, iron homeostasis and wound repair are therefore strong a priori tolerance mechanisms.
4. **Tolerance (behavioural and nutritional).** Parasitized mockingbird nestlings beg more and their parents feed them more, which offsets the damage; finches do not do this (Knutie et al. 2016). Food supply and energy balance plausibly support tolerance. The physiological stress axis (corticosterone) was not changed by parasitism in breeding females.
5. **Behavioural avoidance.** Finches rub their feathers with *Psidium galapagaeum* leaves, which repel the fly (Cimadom et al. 2016). Female in-nest attendance predicts parasite numbers. Parasitized females also change their brooding posture.

## How terms were scored
- **5:** B-cell activation and the adaptive immune response, the only host mechanisms with direct documentation (antibodies).
- **4:** Erythropoiesis, wound healing and response to wounding, general immune and inflammatory processes, cytokine signalling, T-cell help, haematopoiesis, coagulation and platelets, iron homeostasis, skin/epidermis.
- **3:** Downstream signalling (NF-kB, TLR, JAK-STAT, TNF), chemotaxis, phagocytosis, antibacterial defence for secondary infection, keratinocytes and ECM in tissue repair, hypoxia, oxidative stress, nutrient/energy balance, pain.
- **2:** Speculative links such as antiviral pathways, angiogenesis, cell migration, metabolism, hormones, circadian rhythm, learning, and bone/beak development.
- **1:** Housekeeping, neural and developmental processes with no specific link.

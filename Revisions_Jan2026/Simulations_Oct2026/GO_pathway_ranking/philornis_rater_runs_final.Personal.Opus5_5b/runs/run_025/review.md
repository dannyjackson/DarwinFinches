# P. downsi host-response review (run_025)

## What the larvae do
- First-instar larvae live in the nares and ear canals of nestlings, under the skin. They feed on keratin and soft tissue, which enlarges the nares and deforms the beak. Adults with deformed beaks sing at lower pitch and pair less often.
- Later instars live in the nest base and feed at night on nestling blood and tissue fluids. Nestlings lose 18-55% of their blood, hemoglobin drops, and they get wounds and secondary infections. Growth falls, and mortality is high (up to about 76-95% of broods). Incubating and brooding adult females are also bitten.

## Documented or proposed host defenses
- **Adaptive (humoral) resistance.** Adult medium ground finches produce *P. downsi*-specific IgY antibodies, and levels rise with exposure. Antibody responses in nestlings are weak or undetectable.
- **Tolerance.** Mockingbirds tolerate the parasite. Their nestlings beg more, their parents provision more, and the nestlings keep plasma glucose up, although hemoglobin and mass still fall. Tolerance depends on food and rainfall. Recovery from blood loss (erythropoiesis, iron homeostasis) and resource and energy homeostasis are therefore key components.
- **Behavior.** Adults self-fumigate by carrying permethrin-treated cotton to nests. Brooding, begging and provisioning are also behavioral responses. Finches were not able to compensate by provisioning more.
- **Inferred from general biology.** Inflammation and cytokines, innate immunity (TLR and NF-kB), coagulation and platelets for hemostasis at feeding sites, wound healing, epidermal and keratinocyte repair, and responses to hypoxia and oxidative stress from anemia.

Scoring followed the 1-5 scale. Immune response and humoral or B-cell terms scored 5. Inflammation, innate immunity, coagulation, erythropoiesis, wound healing and iron homeostasis scored 4. Downstream signaling, skin repair, stress responses and energy homeostasis scored 3. Metabolism, angiogenesis and behavior-related neural terms scored 2. Everything else scored 1. I did not use any results from genomic or transcriptomic studies of Galápagos birds.

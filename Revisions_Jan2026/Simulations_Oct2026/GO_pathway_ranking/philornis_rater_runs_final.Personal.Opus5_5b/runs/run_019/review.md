# Philornis downsi host-response review (run_019)

## What the larvae do
- Free-living larvae live in nest material and feed on nestling blood and tissue (hematophagous; early instars enter nares/body). Effects: blood loss, reduced haemoglobin/anaemia, skin and tissue lesions (open wounds), naris enlargement and beak malformation that persists into adulthood (altered song and mating signals), reduced growth, and high nestling mortality.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Resistance (immune):** Parasite-specific IgY antibody responses in adult female and nestling finches; higher antibody levels associated with fewer larvae (Koop et al. 2013). Adaptive immunity (B/T cell activation), complement, innate/inflammatory responses at feeding sites are core predictions.
- **Tolerance:** Galapagos mockingbirds tolerate parasitism; tolerance in finches varies with food availability. Tolerance mechanisms: erythropoiesis/haematopoiesis and iron homeostasis to compensate blood loss, haemostasis/coagulation/platelet function at bite sites, wound healing and skin/epidermal repair, energy reallocation and growth maintenance.
- **Behavioral:** Increased nestling begging and parental provisioning, self-medication with Psidium galapageium (anti-larval) leaves, nest sanitation; not well captured by GO process terms (learning/cognition scored low).

## Scoring logic
5 = antibody/adaptive immune response, B cell activation; 4 = immune/inflammatory/complement/T cell, haemostasis/coagulation/platelets, erythropoiesis/haematopoiesis, iron homeostasis, wound healing; 3 = cytokine signalling, NF-kB, phagocytosis, keratinocyte/epidermis, angiogenesis, ECM/collagen in repair, oxidative stress, nutrient/starvation responses; 2 = tangential (bone/beak development, metabolism, general stress, growth signalling); 1 = everything else.

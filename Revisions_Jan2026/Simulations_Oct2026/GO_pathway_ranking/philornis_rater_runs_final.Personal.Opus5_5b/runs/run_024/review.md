# P. downsi host-response literature review (run_024)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- First-instar larvae live in nestlings' nares and ear canals. Later instars live in the nest base and come out at night to feed on blood and tissue by piercing the skin. The result is severe blood loss (an estimated 18-55% overnight), lower haemoglobin (about 2% less per larva), lower mass, shorter primary feathers and high in-nest mortality.
- Damage to the nares leaves enlarged or malformed beaks in adults (about 36% of adult small ground finches), which changes song.
- Since about 2012, larvae are also found in nests with eggs, which suggests they may feed on incubating females.

## Host defences documented or proposed
- **Resistance (adaptive immunity):** brooding females carry P. downsi-specific antibodies (IgY). Higher titres go with lower parasite intensity but not with higher fledging success. Nestlings showed no significant antibody response.
- **Innate, inflammatory and wound responses:** these are a strong a priori prediction from repeated skin penetration and tissue feeding, as with other myiasis. They include inflammation, cytokines, complement, phagocytes, coagulation, platelets and wound healing or re-epithelialisation.
- **Tolerance:** compensating for blood loss through erythropoiesis, iron homeostasis and haemostasis. Mockingbirds tolerate the flies through more begging and more parental provisioning, which finches lack; finches showed no change in plasma glucose. Diet and protein availability affect parasite load.
- **Behavioural defences:** adults preen the nares and nest base, change brooding posture (standing upright) and remove dead nestlings. Nestlings stack on one another. Finches rub leaves of *Psidium galapageium* into their feathers, which repels the flies.

## How the scale was applied
- **5:** antibody or adaptive immune response.
- **4:** B-cell receptor and B-cell machinery, complement, innate immunity and inflammation, coagulation and platelets, wounding or wound healing, erythropoiesis and haemopoiesis, iron homeostasis.
- **3:** T cells, cytokine and NF-kB/TLR signalling, phagocytosis and chemotaxis, oxidative stress, hypoxia (from anaemia), epidermis and ECM repair, energy and glucose homeostasis, starvation, pain.
- **2:** weak links such as angiogenesis, endocrine signalling, learning (for behaviour), bone (for beak deformity) and antiviral pathways.
- **1:** everything else.

A transcriptomic study of urban versus non-urban finches turned up in the search (Knutie et al., bioRxiv 2023.03.06.531275). Under the study rules, none of its gene-expression results were used.

# Literature review: host responses to *Philornis downsi*

## What the larvae do
- First-instar larvae live in the nares and ear canals of nestlings. Second and third instars live in the nest base and come out at night to feed on blood and tissue.
- The effects on nestlings are blood loss, low hemoglobin (anemia), slower growth (mass, tarsus, primary feathers), high in-nest mortality and younger age at death.
- Larvae also cause naris enlargement and tissue loss. This leaves lasting beak deformation that changes adult song and mating signals, and may affect hybridization.
- Protein depletion has been proposed as a cost.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Adaptive / humoral resistance.** Adult finches, especially brooding females, produce *P. downsi*-specific IgY antibodies. Levels are higher at parasitized nests and during nesting, and higher antibody levels have been linked to fewer parasites. B-cell and humoral immunity is therefore the most directly documented mechanism. Inflammation and innate immunity at feeding sites are strong predictions from it.
- **Tolerance.** Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate the parasite: fledging success holds up despite parasitism. Tolerance depends on rainfall, food and parental provisioning, and parasitized mockingbird nestlings beg more. The predicted physiology is compensating for blood loss (erythropoiesis, iron homeostasis), wound healing and tissue repair, hemostasis or coagulation, and nutrient and energy allocation.
- **Behavioral defenses.** Adults preen the nares and the nest base. Nestlings stand on top of one another. Females brood less and stand in the nest more. Weakly begging nestlings get less food. Adults remove dead nestlings. Finches rub *Psidium galapagense* leaves on their feathers as a repellent (self-medication). For genetics, these behaviors map only weakly onto GO terms such as learning, cognition and sensory processes.
- **Stress physiology.** Parasitism did not change corticosterone, body condition or hematocrit in attending female medium ground finches, so the evidence for stress-hormone involvement is weak.
- **Proposed physiology.** Responses to oxidative stress and hypoxia (anemia), pain or nociception, and skin and keratinocyte repair are all plausible.

## Scoring logic
- **Score 5:** immune response and adaptive immune response, the documented antibody-based resistance.
- **Score 4:** B-cell processes, inflammation, innate immunity and defense response, wound healing and response to wounding, blood coagulation, and erythropoiesis and hemopoiesis.
- **Score 3:** T-cell processes, cytokine signaling, NF-kB signaling, complement, platelets, angiogenesis, skin and extracellular-matrix repair, iron homeostasis, oxidative stress, hypoxia and pain.
- **Score 2:** more remote links, such as antibacterial and pattern-recognition signaling, JAK-STAT, cell migration, nutrient and energy metabolism, hormone signaling, learning and hearing, and bone and beak development.
- **Score 1:** everything else, including housekeeping, neural development and similar processes.

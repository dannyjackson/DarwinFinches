# Literature review: host responses to Philornis downsi (run_021)

## What the larvae do
- Eggs/first instars are laid in or enter nestling nares and ear canals; later instars live in nest base and feed at night on nestling blood and tissue (hematophagy + tissue damage). Larvae now also feed on incubating/brooding females.
- Blood loss estimated at 18-55% of nestling blood volume; reduced haemoglobin, reduced growth, high nestling mortality (often >50%), enlarged/deformed nares and beaks in survivors, which degrades song quality.
- Possible secondary infection of wounds and protein/nutrient depletion (proposed).

## Documented / proposed host defenses
- **Resistance (acquired immunity):** Medium ground finches produce P. downsi-binding antibodies (IgY); levels higher in nesting adults and in females (brooding exposure), experimentally induced by parasites (Huber et al. 2010; Koop et al. 2013). Fitness benefit of the response unclear; mockingbirds and finches showed no strong antibody effect in Knutie et al. 2016.
- **Inflammation/innate immunity, wound repair, haemostasis:** a priori predictions from tissue-feeding, blood-feeding larvae (skin/epidermal barrier, coagulation, wound healing, cytokine signaling).
- **Tolerance:** compensation for blood loss (erythropoiesis, iron homeostasis, hypoxia responses, haemodynamic compensation); in mockingbirds, increased nestling begging and parental provisioning offset damage, and tolerance declines in dry years (food-limited) (Knutie et al. 2016; McNew et al. 2019).
- **Behavioral:** nestlings moving to avoid larvae; self-anointing with Psidium galapageium leaves; incorporation of permethrin-treated cotton / tobacco material (self-fumigation; Knutie et al. 2014; Pérez-Beauchamp et al. 2024); female nest attendance.
- Plasma glucose measured; no significant response in finches.
- Transcriptomic/genomic studies of Galápagos birds (incl. Knutie et al. bioRxiv 2023.03.06.531275 and related urban-finch expression work) were deliberately not used.

## Scoring logic
5 = antibody/adaptive immune response; 4 = inflammation, innate/defense response, B/T-cell activation, haematopoiesis/erythropoiesis, coagulation, wound healing; 3 = cytokine/NF-kB/TLR/JAK-STAT signaling, complement, chemotaxis, oxidative stress, hypoxia, iron homeostasis, skin/keratinocyte and ECM repair, blood pressure, nutrient-stress responses, nociception; 2 = speculative links (antibacterial defense of wounds, angiogenesis, xenobiotic response, circadian/night feeding, learning, bone/beak); 1 = everything else.

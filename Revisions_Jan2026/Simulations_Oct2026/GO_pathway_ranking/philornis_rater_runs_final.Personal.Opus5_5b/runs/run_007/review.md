# Run 007 review: host responses to *Philornis downsi*

## What the larvae do
- Larvae are nest-dwelling and feed at night on the blood and tissue of nestlings (and sometimes brooding females). First and second instars live inside the nares and ear canals. Later instars live in the nest base and feed from outside. This causes lesions, swelling, internal bleeding and enlarged nares or beak deformation that can last into adulthood, which in turn alters song.
- Nestlings have lower hemoglobin (anemia) and lower body mass, grow shorter feathers, and suffer high mortality (about 55% on average in parasitized nests).
- Nest-base temperature affects how fast larvae develop.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
1. **Immune resistance.** Adult female medium ground finches produce *P. downsi*-specific antibodies (IgY), and antibody titres were associated with lower parasite load. Experiments show the response is induced by the parasite. In other studies the antibody response was weak, both in finches and in mockingbirds.
2. **Tolerance through blood replacement and energy compensation.** Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate the parasite: parasitized nestlings beg more, parents feed them more, and plasma glucose rises, while fledging success is not reduced. Because hemoglobin is depleted, replacing lost blood through erythropoiesis is a strong a priori tolerance prediction.
3. **Inflammation, wound response and haemostasis.** These are expected from feeding wounds that bleed.
4. **Behavioral defenses.** Nestlings stand on siblings, nestlings and females preen and eat larvae, females probe the nest base, and finches self-fumigate their nests with permethrin-treated cotton.
5. **Stress physiology.** Corticosterone in females showed no clear change.

## Scoring rationale
- **5:** inflammation, adaptive immunity and B-cell activation, immune response, wound healing and response to wounding, blood coagulation.
- **4:** hemopoiesis and erythrocyte differentiation, innate immunity and defense, T-cell processes, complement, cytokines including TNF, IL-6, IL-8 and IL-17, platelets, iron homeostasis, hypoxia.
- **3:** NF-kB and TLR signalling, chemotaxis, phagocytosis, skin and keratinocyte development, angiogenesis, extracellular matrix and collagen, oxidative stress, glucose and energy homeostasis, starvation, pain, blood pressure.
- **2:** general stress, metabolism, growth and insulin/TOR signalling, bone (beak), learning and memory, antiviral pathways, cell migration and similar.
- **1:** everything else.

# Philornis downsi and Galápagos land birds: mechanisms used for scoring

## What the larvae do
- First-instar larvae live in the nares and ear canals of nestlings. Second and third instars hide in the nest base and come out mostly at night to feed on nestling blood and tissue. They also feed on brooding females.
- Effects on hosts: blood loss, with about 28% lower haemoglobin and roughly 0.8 g/dL less per larva; reduced mass and feather growth; enlarged or deformed nares that persist into adulthood and affect song; and high nestling mortality (about 50%, ranging from 3% to 100%).
- The wounds cause tissue damage and open lesions. Pathology from other Philornis species points to local inflammation and secondary infection.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Resistance (immune):** Adult female medium ground finches produce P. downsi-specific antibodies (IgY), at higher levels in parasitized nests. These antibodies are linked to fewer parasites in some studies. Nestling antibody responses may come too late to help. Proposed but untested: inflammatory, cellular and innate responses at bite sites.
- **Tolerance:** Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate parasitism. Their fledging success is unaffected in wet years even though haemoglobin and growth fall. Tolerance depends on food: higher parental provisioning and food supply buffer the costs. Proposed physiological routes are compensating for blood loss (erythropoiesis and iron use), wound repair and nutrient or energy homeostasis.
- **Behavioral:** Nestlings beg more and parents provision more. Finches rub leaves of *Psidium galapageium* (self-medication, with repellent effect) and incorporate fumigated cotton when it is supplied. Nestling behaviour is associated with naris deformation.
- **Stress physiology:** Female corticosterone and hematocrit did not differ with parasitism, which is weak evidence against an HPA-mediated effect.

## Scoring logic
- **5:** Adaptive and antibody immune response; defense response.
- **4:** B and T cell activation and differentiation, inflammation, wound healing and response to wounding, coagulation and platelets, erythropoiesis, hemopoiesis, iron homeostasis, general immune regulation, complement and cytokine production.
- **3:** Cytokine and NF-kB/JAK-STAT signaling, TLR and innate pathways, phagocytosis, oxidative stress and ROS, chemotaxis, hypoxia response (anaemia), skin, epidermis and keratinocytes, angiogenesis, ECM and collagen (repair), nutrient-level and starvation responses, energy homeostasis, pain perception.
- **2:** Antiviral pathways, MAPK cascades, hormone and lipid/glucose metabolism, circadian rhythm (nocturnal feeding), bone and beak development (naris deformation), learning and memory (behavior), cell migration, proliferation and adhesion, generic stress and apoptosis.
- **1:** Housekeeping processes, such as RNA processing, DNA replication, the cell cycle, cilia, synapses and most developmental patterning.

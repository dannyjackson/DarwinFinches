# P. downsi host-response review (run_006)

## What the larvae do
- Larvae live in nest material and feed at night on the blood and tissue of nestlings (and on brooding females). Early instars feed inside the nares, and some larvae burrow into the skin. This causes lesions, swelling, internal bleeding and enlarged or deformed nares and beaks that persist into adulthood. The beak damage changes song and may affect mate choice and hybridisation.
- Costs to hosts: lower hemoglobin from blood loss (anemia; a 25% blood loss is often fatal), lower body mass, shorter feathers, and heavy nestling mortality (about 55% on average, up to 100%).

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Resistance, immune:** adult females carry P. downsi-binding antibodies (IgY), which rise during nesting. Huber et al. 2010 report that the parasites trigger acquired immunity in medium ground finches. In nestlings the responses were weak or not significant. This is an adaptive, B-cell and humoral response. Inflammation and innate immunity at bite and skin sites are strong a priori predictions.
- **Tolerance:** mockingbirds tolerate the flies through increased nestling begging and parental provisioning that offset the damage. Urban finches show higher fledging and hemoglobin with fewer flies, and their diet appears more protein-rich. Predicted physiology: replacing lost blood (erythropoiesis, iron homeostasis), hemostasis and wound repair, nutrient and energy homeostasis, and responses to hypoxia and oxidative stress.
- **Behavioural:** nestlings stand on top of siblings, and females and nestlings preen and eat larvae. Parasitized females brood less. Self-fumigation with permethrin-treated cotton was used experimentally. No corticosterone stress response was found.

## How the scores map to mechanisms
- 5: adaptive immune response, B-cell activation, erythrocyte differentiation.
- 4: immune and defence response, inflammation, cytokines, NF-kB, T-cell activation, complement, wound healing, coagulation and platelets, hematopoiesis, iron homeostasis, hypoxia.
- 3: skin, ECM and fibroblast repair, angiogenesis, oxidative stress, nutrient, starvation and energy homeostasis, gluconeogenesis, pain perception, phagocytosis, chemotaxis, antibacterial defence (secondary infection of wounds).
- 2: bone and beak remodelling, learning and hearing (song), muscle, apoptosis, MAPK, ER stress, antiviral responses.
- 1: everything else.

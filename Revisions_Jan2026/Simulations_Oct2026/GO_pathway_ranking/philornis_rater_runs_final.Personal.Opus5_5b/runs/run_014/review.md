# Philornis downsi: host effects and defenses (rater run_014)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- Larvae live in the nest. They are semi-hematophagous and feed on nestlings' blood and tissue. They make wounds, including naris (nasal opening) enlargement and deformation, that persist into adulthood.
- Effects documented in nestlings include blood loss, lower hemoglobin and anemia, slower growth, and high mortality (16-100% across host species). Populations of small tree finches and warbler finches have declined.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Resistance (adaptive immunity):** Adult medium ground finches produce P. downsi-specific IgY antibodies. In nests where parasites were experimentally present, antibody levels rose, and higher titres were linked to fewer parasites. Nestlings show little or no antibody response. Mockingbirds show no significant antibody response either. Proposed resistance mechanisms include humoral immunity, local inflammation at bite sites, and possibly blood clotting and complement.
- **Tolerance:** Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate parasite loads similar to the finches' with little fitness cost. Compensation comes through nestling begging and parental provisioning, and also through replacing lost blood (red cell production, iron homeostasis) and repairing wounds. Tolerance drops in drought years, so food and energy balance matter. The case for metabolic, stress and glucose homeostasis is weaker; plasma glucose and corticosterone were examined but showed little or no effect.
- **Behavioral:** Nestling begging, parental provisioning and nest-material choice. Finches will take up insecticide-treated cotton, but that is a human management intervention, not a host defense. The neural and behavioral GO terms were scored low because they are too generic.

## Scoring rationale
- **5:** Antibody, adaptive and immune response; wound healing and response to wounding.
- **4:** Coagulation and platelets; inflammation; red cell production and iron homeostasis; B-cell processes; innate immunity and complement.
- **3:** Cytokine, T-cell and NF-kB signalling; leukocyte recruitment; oxidative stress and hypoxia (anemia); skin and epidermis repair; extracellular matrix and collagen; angiogenesis; energy and glucose homeostasis; starvation responses; pain.
- **2:** Broader tissue-repair growth-factor signalling, stress and metabolism, hormone, growth and behavior-related terms.
- **1:** All other terms, for example generic cell biology, development, neural and RNA processing.

I did not use any transcriptomic or genomic studies of Galápagos birds.

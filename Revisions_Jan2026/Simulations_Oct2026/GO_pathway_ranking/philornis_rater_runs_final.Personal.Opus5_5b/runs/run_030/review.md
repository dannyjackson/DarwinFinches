# Philornis downsi and Galápagos land birds: mechanisms used for scoring

## What the larvae do
- Adult flies lay eggs in nests; 1st-instar larvae enter nestling nares/ear canals, later instars live in nest material and feed nocturnally on nestling blood and tissue (hematophagous, semi-subcutaneous feeding).
- Effects: blood loss, lower hemoglobin and anemia, reduced growth, high nestling mortality (often >50%), tissue damage and open wounds, and permanent naris/beak deformation in survivors. Deformed beaks change song, which affects mating signals and possibly hybridization.

## Host responses documented or proposed
- **Resistance (adaptive/humoral):** Darwin's finches make P. downsi-specific antibodies (IgY). Adult and nestling titres are linked to parasite load and to experimental fly exposure. Higher antibody levels have been associated with fewer larvae.
- **Innate/inflammatory:** an inflammatory and cellular response at feeding sites (wound healing, leukocyte recruitment, cytokines, complement). Oxidative stress follows from blood feeding.
- **Tolerance:** keeping fitness despite parasitism. Mockingbirds tolerate the fly better than finches. Proposed mechanisms are compensatory erythropoiesis/hemoglobin replacement, iron homeostasis, tissue repair, and energy/resource allocation (provisioning and food availability affect tolerance).
- **Hemostasis:** coagulation and platelet function at bite sites.
- **Behavioral/endocrine:** begging intensity, parental provisioning, nest sanitation and preening. Corticosterone (stress-hormone) responses in breeding finches have been tested experimentally.
- **Developmental:** beak, bone and cartilage growth and remodelling (naris deformation), epidermis/keratinocyte repair.

## Scoring logic
- 5: erythropoiesis, wound healing and response to wounding, adaptive/humoral immunity, inflammation, coagulation.
- 4: core immune, leukocyte, cytokine and chemotaxis processes, platelets, iron homeostasis, hematopoiesis, epidermis/skin repair.
- 3: oxidative stress, angiogenesis, ECM and collagen, skeletal and beak tissue, growth-factor signalling, NF-kB/JAK-STAT, hormone and stress responses, nutrient/energy status.
- 2: antiviral immunity, metabolism, apoptosis, general signalling, sensory and learning processes (begging, song).
- 1: housekeeping processes (RNA processing, DNA replication, synapse and neural development, cilia, and so on).

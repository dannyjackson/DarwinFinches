# Philornis downsi and Galápagos land birds: mechanisms used for scoring

## What the larvae do
- Larvae of *P. downsi* are semi-hematophagous. They live in the nest material and feed on the blood and tissue of nestlings and of brooding females. First-instar larvae can enter the nares, and this produces deformed or enlarged nasal openings. In adults this has been linked to altered beak function and song.
- The documented effects are blood loss, lower hemoglobin and anemia, open wounds, slower nestling growth and high nestling mortality (16 to 100%). Populations of Small Tree-finches and Green Warbler-finches have declined.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Resistance (adaptive immunity):** Adult female medium ground finches produce *P. downsi*-specific IgY antibodies, and parasite exposure induces this response experimentally (Koop et al.). Nestlings have little effective resistance.
- **Resistance (innate and inflammatory):** Local inflammatory responses at feeding wounds are an a priori prediction for any blood-feeding ectoparasite.
- **Tolerance:** Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate parasitism. Tolerance varies with rainfall and food or provisioning, and it means compensating for blood loss and maintaining growth. The a priori tolerance mechanisms (inferred here from hemoglobin loss) are red blood cell production (erythropoiesis), recovery of iron and hemoglobin, and nutritional and energy compensation.
- **Stress physiology:** Female corticosterone did not differ with parasitism (Knutie et al. 2013). Nestling stress is proposed but untested.
- **Behavior:** Nestlings beg more, and parents adjust provisioning. Preening and nestling movement have not been shown to be effective. Nest-lining behavior is exploited by self-fumigation (permethrin-treated cotton).

## Scoring logic
- **5:** adaptive immune response (the IgY response).
- **4:** erythrocyte production (tolerance of blood loss), core immune and inflammatory processes, B cells, complement, hemostasis, coagulation and platelets, wound healing, hemopoiesis, and iron homeostasis.
- **3:** cytokine signaling, NF-kB, T cells, chemotaxis, hypoxia and oxidative stress, angiogenesis, skin and ECM repair, nutrient and energy homeostasis, and pain.
- **2:** generic microbial or pattern-recognition pathways, stress and hormone responses, metabolism, bone remodeling (beak deformation), learning and memory (behavior), and xenobiotic response (insecticide).
- **1:** everything else.

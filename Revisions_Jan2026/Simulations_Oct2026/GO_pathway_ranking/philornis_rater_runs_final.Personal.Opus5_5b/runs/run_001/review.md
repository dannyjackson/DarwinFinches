# Philornis downsi and host responses: review summary (run_001)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- First-instar larvae live in the nares and ear canals of nestlings. Second and third instars live in the nest base and feed on blood and tissue at night. This causes heavy blood loss (estimated 18-55%), lower haemoglobin, lower body mass, slower growth, shorter primary feathers and high nestling mortality (often above 50%, up to about 90%). Brooding females may also be bitten on the brood patch.
- Damage to the nasal cavity causes enlarged or deformed nares that persist into adulthood, with knock-on effects on beak function and song.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
1. **Adaptive (humoral) immunity, resistance.** Adults (especially breeding females) produce P. downsi-binding IgY. In experiments, antibody levels rose in parasitized nests and were negatively associated with parasite load (Huber et al. 2010; Koop et al. 2013). Maternal antibody transfer to eggs has been proposed. Nestlings show little detectable antibody response.
2. **Innate and inflammatory responses at the feeding site.** Proposed as a resistance mechanism (cytokine signalling, leukocyte recruitment, complement, coagulation, wound healing and re-epithelialisation of skin and nasal tissue).
3. **Tolerance through blood replacement and compensation.** Parasites lower haemoglobin in all hosts studied. Mockingbirds tolerate the fly: parasitized nestlings beg more and parents feed them more, which offsets the costs. Finches do not do this. Tolerance falls in dry years when food is short (McNew et al. 2019). Urban finch nestlings, which eat more protein, survive parasitism better (Knutie et al., urban-rescue study). I used only the published survival and diet results from that study, not its gene-expression results. These findings point to erythropoiesis, iron handling, nutrient and energy metabolism, and glucose homeostasis as candidate tolerance mechanisms.
4. **Behaviour.** Parents bring fumigated (permethrin-treated) cotton into nests, preen nestlings' nares and change how much they brood and visit. Nestlings stack on each other and sometimes eat larvae. Begging intensity does not honestly signal need in parasitized finch nests. Parents of small tree finches did not compensate by provisioning more.
5. **Stress physiology.** No clear corticosterone effect was found in medium ground finches.

## How the scores were assigned
- **5:** Terms that directly name the documented mechanisms: antibody, B-cell or adaptive immune response, and defense or immune response in general.
- **4:** Core components of those mechanisms or strong predictions from them: inflammatory and cytokine regulation, NF-kB, TLR, T-cell regulation, complement, coagulation and platelets, wound healing, haemopoiesis and erythrocyte differentiation, iron homeostasis, keratinocyte differentiation.
- **3:** Downstream or supporting processes: oxidative stress, hypoxia, angiogenesis, ECM and collagen, skin and epidermis development, nutrient and starvation responses, energy and glucose homeostasis, chemotaxis, phagocytosis, JAK-STAT, bacterial and LPS responses (secondary wound infection).
- **2:** Weak or indirect links: general growth signalling, metabolism, development, antiviral responses, behaviour and learning, apoptosis regulation.
- **1:** No specific link: housekeeping, RNA, DNA, cell cycle, synaptic and developmental-patterning terms.

# Philornis downsi and host responses: review for GO scoring

## What the larvae do
- First-instar larvae live in nestlings' nares and ear canals. Later instars live in the nest base and come out at night to pierce the skin and feed on blood and tissue fluids. They sometimes also feed on brooding or incubating females.
- Effects on hosts: high nestling mortality, lower hemoglobin (blood loss and anemia), slower growth (worse in dry years), and enlarged or deformed nares that persist into adulthood and degrade song.

## Documented or proposed host defenses
- **Adaptive immunity (resistance):** medium ground finch females produce P. downsi-specific IgY antibodies, which are linked to lower parasite intensity. These antibodies did not improve fledging success, and a later study found no significant antibody response.
- **Tolerance:** Galápagos mockingbird nestlings beg more and their parents provision more, which offsets the costs of the parasite. Finch parents did not raise begging, provisioning or plasma glucose. Tolerance falls in dry years because of food or energy limits. Parasitized nestlings lose hemoglobin, so replacing red blood cells (erythropoiesis) is a plausible tolerance mechanism.
- **Diet:** a protein-rich (urban) diet is associated with better survival of parasitized nestlings. I used only that study's field and diet observations. I did not use its gene-expression results, as the rules require.
- **Behavioral:** females stand upright while brooding in infested nests. Finches rub their feathers with leaves of *Psidium galbanum*, a natural repellent, and adopt permethrin-treated cotton as nest material.
- **General biology:** the following were treated as plausible supporting processes:
  - inflammation, wound healing and skin or epidermal repair at the bite sites;
  - hemostasis and coagulation (larval feeding wounds);
  - energy and nutrient homeostasis (tolerance through compensation);
  - stress responses to hypoxia from anemia.

## Scoring logic
| Score | Terms |
|---|---|
| 5 | Adaptive and humoral immune responses (antibody response documented) |
| 4 | Core components: B-cell receptor signaling, defense or inflammatory response, wound healing, hemopoiesis and erythrocyte differentiation, iron homeostasis, coagulation and platelets, innate immunity |
| 3 | Downstream or supporting: cytokines and TNF/IL signaling, T-cell processes, keratinocyte and epidermis development, extracellular matrix and collagen, chemotaxis, complement, glucose and energy homeostasis, hypoxia and starvation responses, pain perception |
| 2 | Weak or indirect: general stress and oxidative stress, angiogenesis, growth factor and hormone signaling, bone and beak ossification, insulin, circadian rhythm |
| 1 | Housekeeping, neural, developmental and meiotic processes with no specific link |

## Independence note
When I fetched the urban-finch abstract (USFQ link below), I saw that it includes gene-expression results from Galápagos nestlings: red-blood-cell and pro-inflammatory pathways in urban birds, and immunoglobulin pathways in non-urban birds. The rules exclude this kind of study, and I did not use those results. Three of my scores overlap with what that abstract reports: hemopoiesis and erythrocyte differentiation at 4, inflammation at 4, and adaptive immunity at 5. Those scores rest on other evidence:
- documented hemoglobin loss (McNew et al. 2019; Flinders hemoglobin study);
- skin-piercing feeding wounds;
- P. downsi-specific antibodies (Koop et al. 2013).

Even so, the exposure may have influenced me, and the person evaluating independence should know about it.

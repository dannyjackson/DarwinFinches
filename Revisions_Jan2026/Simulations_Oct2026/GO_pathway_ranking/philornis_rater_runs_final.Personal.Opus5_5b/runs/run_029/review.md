# Literature review: Philornis downsi and host responses (run_029)

## What the larvae do
- First-instar larvae live in the nares and ear canals of nestlings and eat the keratin and tissue there. This deforms the beak and nares, which changes song in adults. Late second- and third-instar larvae live in the nest base and feed at night on nestling blood and fluids by piercing the skin.
- Effects on hosts: blood loss (lower hemoglobin), lower growth and mass, and high nestling mortality (often above 50%, up to 100%). Larvae also occur in nests during incubation and sometimes feed on brooding females or adults.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Adaptive or humoral resistance:** brooding female finches and nestlings produce *P. downsi*-binding antibodies (IgY). Higher titres go with lower parasite intensity, but not with higher fledging success (Koop et al. 2013). In a related system, swallow IgY levels predicted lower loads of a different nest fly.
- **Tolerance:** Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate the fly while finches do not. Mockingbird parents compensate by provisioning more, and their nestlings' glucose is higher (Knutie et al. 2016). Replacing lost blood (erythropoiesis) and better nutrition, such as more protein in urban areas, are proposed tolerance routes. Urban nestlings survive parasitism much better (McNew et al.).
- **Innate or inflammatory resistance:** inflammation and repair of the skin wound at the feeding site are proposed as resistance and tolerance mechanisms.
- **Behavioural:** finches line nests with permethrin-treated cotton ("self-fumigation"). Brooding females that stand more or change their posture may limit larval access. Begging and provisioning also respond to parasitism.
- **Stress or endocrine:** adult female corticosterone did not change with parasitism (Knutie et al. 2013). Glucose and energy balance during growth are plausible supporting traits.

## How the scoring was applied
- **5:** adaptive immunity, B cell activation, and erythrocyte differentiation (blood replacement).
- **4:** wound healing, coagulation, platelet activation and aggregation, inflammation, innate immunity, complement, iron homeostasis, hemopoiesis, B cell proliferation and differentiation.
- **3:** cytokine signalling, T cells, phagocytosis, chemotaxis, NF-kB, hypoxia, oxidative stress, glucose and energy metabolism, angiogenesis, ECM and collagen, TGF-beta, epidermis and keratinocytes (naris and skin damage), pain.

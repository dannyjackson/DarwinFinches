# Philornis downsi and Galapagos land birds: mechanisms review (run_002)

## What the larvae do
- Adult flies lay eggs in nests; larvae live in nest material and feed externally (and in early instars inside the nares) on nestling blood and tissue at night. Effects: blood loss with reduced haemoglobin, open wounds, enlarged nares and beak deformation, reduced growth, and very high nestling mortality (often >50%).
- Females (brooding) are also bitten.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Resistance (immune):** nestlings and adult females produce P. downsi-specific antibodies (IgY); higher antibody levels associated with fewer parasites (Huber et al. 2010; Koop et al. 2013). Inflammatory/innate responses to bites and wounds proposed.
- **Tolerance:** Galapagos mockingbirds tolerate high loads with little fitness cost; food supplementation increases tolerance in finches, implying resource-based compensation (erythropoiesis to replace blood loss, tissue repair, growth/energy allocation) (Knutie et al. 2016, 2017).
- **Behavioural:** nestling begging, parental provisioning shifts, preening/defensive behaviour, nest sanitation (and self-fumigation with treated cotton) (O'Connor et al.; Cimadom et al.; Heyer et al.). Corticosterone/stress-hormone responses tested.
- Implied physiology: hypoxia from anaemia, haemostasis around feeding wounds, skin/epidermis repair, angiogenesis, nutritional and energy homeostasis.

## Scoring logic
5 = immune/antibody responses; 4 = wound healing, erythropoiesis, haemostasis, inflammation, innate immunity, haematopoiesis, iron homeostasis, B-cell signaling; 3 = cytokine/T-cell signalling, hypoxia, skin/keratinocyte, angiogenesis, ECM repair, starvation/nutrient and stress responses; 2 = broader/indirect (generic microbe responses, metabolism, hormones, learning, bone/beak development); 1 = housekeeping, neural/developmental, cell-cycle etc.

# P. downsi host-response review (run_011)

## What the larvae do
- First-instar larvae live in nestlings' nares/nasal cavity; later instars live in nest material and feed on nestlings' blood and tissue at night. This causes open wounds, blood loss (lower haemoglobin), deformed or enlarged nares and beaks, slower growth, and high nestling mortality (often >50%, up to 100%) in Darwin's finches. Adult females (which brood) can also be bitten.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Resistance (immune):** Finches produce P. downsi-specific IgY (antibody) responses. Nestling and adult antibody levels are negatively related to parasite load (Huber et al. 2010; Koop et al. 2013). Inflammatory and innate responses at wound and feeding sites have been proposed.
- **Tolerance:** Hosts may tolerate blood loss through haemoglobin recovery and erythropoiesis, iron homeostasis, wound repair and tissue repair. Resources matter: food supplementation and urban diets improve nestling survival (Knutie et al. 2016; Knutie et al. 2019 urban finches). Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate the fly because they are larger and receive more parental provisioning (Knutie et al. 2016).
- **Behavioral:** Adults change brooding and nest attendance, and nestlings may change begging or preening. Self-fumigation with cotton treated with permethrin has been tested experimentally. Female attendance predicts parasite numbers.
- **Supporting physiology:** Stress and oxidative responses, energy homeostasis and growth.

## Scoring logic
- **5:** immune response, adaptive immunity, B cell activation, wound response and healing, blood coagulation, erythrocyte differentiation.
- **4:** innate and defense response, B cell and antibody regulation, inflammation, platelet and complement activity, haematopoiesis, iron homeostasis.
- **3:** T cell and cytokine signalling, NF-kB and JAK-STAT signalling, phagocytosis, oxidative stress and hypoxia, epidermis and keratinocyte development, ECM and collagen, pain.
- **2:** angiogenesis, metabolism and nutrient responses, hormone and stress responses, behavior and learning, circadian processes.
- **1:** everything else.

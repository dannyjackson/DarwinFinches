# Philornis downsi and Galápagos land birds: mechanisms used for scoring

## What the larvae do
- Adult flies lay eggs in nests. First-instar larvae live in the nestlings' nares, where they enlarge or deform the nares and beak, which can affect later song. Second- and third-instar larvae hide in the nest material and come out at night to feed externally on nestlings' blood and tissue. Larvae sometimes feed on brooding females.
- The effects on nestlings are blood loss, lower hemoglobin (anemia), smaller body mass and slower growth, and mortality that rises with parasite intensity and can reach total loss of the brood. Larger hosts (mockingbirds, vegetarian finches) are much less affected.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
1. **Acquired (antibody) immunity:** Female medium ground finches have higher levels of antibodies that bind *Philornis* during nesting than before it, and females have higher levels than males. Antibody levels were inversely related to parasite numbers but did not improve nestling survival. No clear nestling antibody response has been found.
2. **Responses to blood loss and wounds:** Lower hemoglobin points to erythropoiesis, iron homeostasis, coagulation and hemostasis, wound healing and re-epithelialization, and inflammation at feeding sites. These are strong a priori predictions.
3. **Tolerance through energy and provisioning:** Parasitized mockingbird nestlings beg more and their parents feed them more, which offsets the cost of parasitism. Their plasma glucose was also higher. Tolerance depends on condition and food supply, which varies with rainfall from year to year.
4. **Behavioral defenses:** Finches rub leaves of *Psidium galapageium* on their feathers, and the leaf extracts repel adult flies and slow larval growth. Other proposed defenses are adults picking at nestlings' nares, choice of nest height and position, and sibling climbing. Brooding behavior affects nest temperature. Females' stress hormones (corticosterone) and hematocrit did not change with parasitism.

## How the scale was applied
- **5:** Adaptive and antibody (B-cell) immunity, immune and defense response, and wound healing or response to wounding.
- **4:** Inflammation, innate immunity, T-cell activation, complement, coagulation and platelets, erythrocyte differentiation, hemopoiesis, and iron homeostasis.
- **3:** Cytokine and NF-kB signalling, response to hypoxia, oxidative stress, keratinocyte and epidermal processes, ECM and collagen, angiogenesis, chemotaxis, phagocytosis, glucose homeostasis, and energy homeostasis.
- **2:** Growth and metabolic signalling, skeletal and beak development, nutrient and starvation responses, hormonal and stress signalling, learning and memory, sound perception (song), and antiviral defense.
- **1:** Generic housekeeping, neural, reproductive and developmental processes with no specific link.

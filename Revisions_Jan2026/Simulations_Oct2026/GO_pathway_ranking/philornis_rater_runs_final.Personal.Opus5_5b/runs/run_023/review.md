# P. downsi host-response review (run_023)

## What the larvae do
- First-instar larvae enter the nares (and ear canals) of nestlings. Later instars live in the nest base and feed at night on blood and tissue, which causes blood loss (lower haemoglobin), wounds, enlarged or deformed nares and beaks, slower growth and high nestling mortality. Larvae also feed on brooding females.
- Survivors with damaged nares or beaks may sing poorer song, with possible effects on mate choice and hybridisation.

## Documented or proposed host defenses
1. **Adaptive (antibody/IgY) resistance:** Adult female medium ground finches raise P. downsi-binding antibodies during nesting. In Philornis hosts on the mainland, nestling IgY levels correlate negatively with parasite load (tree swallows vs bluebirds). Parasitized non-urban finch nestlings showed antibody responses.
2. **Pro-inflammatory/innate resistance:** Urban finch nestlings that survived parasitism were linked to a pro-inflammatory response.
3. **Tolerance through blood recovery:** Nestlings make more red blood cells (erythropoiesis) to make up for blood loss. Haemoglobin is the main measure of cost. A protein-rich diet is proposed to support this.
4. **Behavioural and parental tolerance:** In mockingbirds, nestlings beg more and parents feed them more, which offsets the cost. This tolerance depends on food and rainfall. Female time on the nest and nestling activity are linked to parasite load and naris damage. Finches use permethrin-treated cotton when it is provided. Hybridisation of tree finches may reduce infestation.
5. **Stress and metabolism:** Glucose and energy-balance responses were measured but are not consistent across studies.

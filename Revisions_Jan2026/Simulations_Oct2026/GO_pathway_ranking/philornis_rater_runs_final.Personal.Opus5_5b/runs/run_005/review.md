# P. downsi host-response review (run_005)

## What the larvae do
- Adult flies lay eggs in nests. First-instar larvae live in the nestlings' nares, consume tissue and keratin, and can enlarge or deform the naris and beak. Second- and third-instar larvae live in nest material and feed on blood and fluids at night by piercing the skin. This causes blood loss and lower haemoglobin, and in-nest mortality is often very high. Larvae also feed on brooding females.
- Long-term effects on survivors include deformed nares and beaks, which alter song (vocal performance) and may affect mate choice and hybridisation.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Resistance (humoral immunity):** Brooding females and nestlings produce P. downsi-specific antibodies (IgY/IgG ELISA). Higher antibody titres are linked to fewer parasites, but responses in nestlings are weak or absent.
- **Tolerance:** Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate the parasite. Parasitised nestlings beg more and parents increase provisioning, which offsets energy and blood loss. Finches lack this compensation. Proposed physiological routes to tolerance are replacing lost blood (erythropoiesis and haemoglobin) and resource or energy compensation (glucose and nutrient status). Environmental (food) conditions modulate tolerance.
- **Tissue repair:** Proposed routes are wound healing, epidermal and skin repair, coagulation, and inflammation at feeding sites.
- **Behavioural:** Females stand upright while brooding, and mockingbirds stand on the nest rim. Allopreening is proposed. Finches rub Psidium galapageium leaves on their feathers as a repellent (self-medication).

## Scoring rationale
- **5:** Humoral and adaptive immunity (B cells), erythropoiesis and haemopoiesis, wound healing.
- **4:** Innate immunity and inflammation, cytokines, T cells, coagulation and platelets, iron homeostasis.
- **3:** NF-kB, TLR and JAK-STAT signalling, angiogenesis, ECM and skin, oxidative stress, hypoxia, glucose and energy homeostasis, starvation, pain.
- **2:** Antiviral and antibacterial responses, insulin and TOR signalling, hormones, circadian rhythm, learning, bone and beak development, lipid storage.
- **1:** Everything else.

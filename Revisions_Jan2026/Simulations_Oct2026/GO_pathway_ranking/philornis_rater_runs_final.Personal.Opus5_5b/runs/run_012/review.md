# Rater run_012: Philornis downsi host-response review

## What the larvae do to hosts
- Free-living larvae hide in nest material and come out to feed on nestlings, mostly at night. They feed on blood and fluids. First-instar larvae enter the nares and nasal cavity, causing tissue damage and deformed nares and beaks, which later alters song. Later instars feed externally.
- The documented effects on nestlings are blood loss with lower hemoglobin, slower growth, smaller size, and high mortality, sometimes reaching 100%. Mortality is higher in small hosts and in dry years.
- Adult females at parasitized nests showed no change in hematocrit, corticosterone or body condition.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Adaptive (humoral) immunity:** adult female medium ground finches make P. downsi-specific IgY antibodies, and antibody titre is inversely related to parasite intensity. In nestlings an antibody response is weak or absent. In a related Protocalliphora system, nestling IgY was negatively related to parasite load.
- **Tolerance to blood loss:** Galápagos mockingbird nestlings beg more and parents feed them more, so they compensate. From blood loss, I predict a priori that red-cell replacement (erythropoiesis, iron handling) and hemostasis act as tolerance mechanisms.
- **Wound and inflammatory responses:** feeding wounds and myiasis tissue damage imply local inflammation, coagulation, re-epithelialization and skin repair.
- **Behavioral and nutritional defenses:** these include female brooding and nest attendance, nestlings crawling over nestmates, possible self-medication with Psidium galapageium leaves, use of insecticidal or tobacco materials in nests (anthropogenic), and protein-rich diets. Behavioral and metabolic GO terms got only weak support.

## Scoring rationale
- **5:** immune response and adaptive immune response, the antibody-based resistance that has been documented.
- **4:** core parts of that mechanism or strong predictions from it: B-cell activation and signaling, innate defense and inflammation, wound healing and hemostasis, hemopoiesis and erythrocyte differentiation.
- **3:** cytokine, NF-kB, TLR, complement and T-cell regulation, platelets, iron homeostasis and hypoxia (anemia), and epidermis development.
- **2:** antiviral and antibacterial responses (secondary infection or vectoring), oxidative stress, angiogenesis and ECM repair, energy and nutrient homeostasis, thermogenesis, and pain and learning (song).
- **1:** all other housekeeping, developmental and neural terms.

I did not use the results of Galápagos transcriptomic or genomic studies (e.g. Knutie et al. bioRxiv 2023.03.06.531275 and its published urban-finch version).

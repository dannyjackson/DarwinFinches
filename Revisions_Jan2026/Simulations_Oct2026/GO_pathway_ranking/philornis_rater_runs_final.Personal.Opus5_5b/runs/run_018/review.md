# Philornis downsi and host responses: summary of the literature review (run_018)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- Free-living larvae live in the nest material and feed at night on the blood and tissue of nestlings, and also on brooding females. Early-instar larvae enter the nares and ear canals. This causes tissue wounds, permanent enlargement or deformation of the naris (beak damage), large blood loss (estimated at 18-55% of blood volume), lower haemoglobin, reduced growth and frequently above 50% nestling mortality.
- Adult females at parasitized nests showed no change in corticosterone, hematocrit or body condition (Knutie et al. 2013).

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Resistance (adaptive immunity):** Medium ground finches produce parasite-specific antibodies (IgY) against *P. downsi*, and higher titres are associated with fewer parasites. Nestlings mount a weak response that comes too late. In Knutie et al. 2016, neither finches nor mockingbirds showed a significant antibody response.
- **Resistance (innate/inflammatory):** Proposed as a local response at feeding wounds: inflammation, complement, and leukocyte recruitment and chemotaxis. Supported by general avian ectoparasite and myiasis biology.
- **Tolerance:** Compensating for blood loss through erythropoiesis, haemostasis/coagulation and wound repair (re-epithelialization, ECM/collagen). Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate the parasite: parasitized nestlings beg more and parents provision more. Finches did not show this, and their plasma glucose did not change. Better nutrition, such as higher protein intake in urban nests, appears to improve outcomes.
- **Behavioral:** Self-medication by rubbing feathers with *Psidium galapageium* leaves, which contain insect-repellent compounds. Nestlings jostle within the nest to avoid larvae. Birds use permethrin-treated cotton when offered. Hybrid tree finches on Floreana carry fewer parasites.

## How the scores were assigned
- **5:** Immune/antibody (B cell) response, erythropoiesis and wound responses.
- **4:** Innate, inflammatory and complement responses, haemostasis/coagulation, hemopoiesis and iron homeostasis.
- **3:** Downstream support processes: T cell help, cytokines, phagocytosis, hypoxia, angiogenesis, epidermis/ECM repair, pain, blood pressure.
- **2:** Speculative links: antibacterial and antiviral PRR signalling, oxidative stress, endocrine and nutrient-state responses (begging and provisioning), learning, ossification (beak).
- **1:** Everything else, including generic housekeeping, neural development and reproductive terms.

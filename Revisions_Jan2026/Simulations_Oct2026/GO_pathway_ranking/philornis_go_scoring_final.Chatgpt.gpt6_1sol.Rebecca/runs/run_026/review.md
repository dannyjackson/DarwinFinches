# A priori physiological relevance review: Philornis downsi

This independent rating used only the supplied GO list, a targeted review of published ecological, physiological and behavioral literature, and general biological knowledge. No genomic, transcriptomic or gene-expression findings from Galapagos birds were used. Scores express potential mechanistic relevance, not proof of adaptation or evidence that any particular GO pathway changes in these birds.

## Mechanisms and evidential distinctions

**Tissue injury, blood loss and erythrocyte replacement.** Early larvae inhabit nares and other tissues; later larvae reside in nest material and feed externally, especially at night. Consequences include wounds, anemia, impaired growth, mortality and persistent naris/beak deformation. Fessl et al. directly describe larval stages and substantial blood loss. Dudaniec et al. found lower hemoglobin and more immature erythrocytes with greater parasite intensity. Erythrocyte differentiation and hemopoiesis therefore have especially direct support. Iron homeostasis, hypoxia responses, circulatory compensation and hemostasis are strong physiological predictions. Repair of epithelia, extracellular matrix, skin and vessels is strongly predicted by injury, although individual repair signaling pathways were not directly established in these studies.

**Immune resistance, with important limitations.** An experimental study found higher P. downsi-binding antibody levels in mothers at parasitized nests; mothers with stronger responses tended to have fewer parasites. This did not prevent reproductive failure, and equivalent induced responses were not established for males or nestlings. Another comparison detected no significant antibody response in either finches or mockingbirds. Adaptive immunity and B-cell mechanisms thus have strong relevance without implying universal effectiveness. Innate defense, complement, inflammation, immune-cell adhesion and recruitment are reasonable predictions; individual cytokines and signaling intermediates receive lower ratings where their role is only supportive. Bacterial-defense terms have a plausible secondary link through wounds, rather than evidence that the fly is a demonstrated vector. Antiviral-specific processes receive little support.

**Tolerance and resource compensation.** Experimentally parasitized mockingbird nestlings increased begging, with increased parental food provisioning apparently compensating for damage; medium ground finches did not show the same compensation. Mockingbird tolerance varied among years and weakened under dry conditions. Nutrient and energy homeostasis, glucose/lipid/amino-acid metabolism and growth control can support tolerance, although most individual metabolic pathways remain indirect. Bigger body size and thermal/energy buffering are proposed contributors.

**Behavioral defense and costs.** Preening, avoidance and altered parental attendance have been observed or considered, with uncertain or limited protection. Reduced brooding could worsen nestling thermoregulation. Handling responsiveness correlated with greater naris damage at high parasite intensity, rather than establishing that a particular behavioral phenotype protects hosts. Finches also rub Psidium galapageium leaves on feathers; extracts repel adult flies and inhibit larval growth in experiments. This supports a proposed self-medication mechanism, not demonstrated field protection. Sensory, motor, learning, circadian and neuroendocrine processes may support these behaviors, but generic neural development and synapse assembly do not become direct defenses solely because behavior matters. Mechanical damage to the song-producing beak similarly does not establish neural song-learning pathways as a response.

## Scoring conventions

Each term was judged individually. Five denotes a process directly encompassing documented host responses; four denotes a core component or strong prediction; three denotes plausible support; two denotes a weak/indirect link; one denotes no specific connection. Generic housekeeping is not promoted merely because it is necessary for life. Positive and negative regulation can both contribute to defense or tolerance. Platelet terms received a supporting score reflecting avian thrombocyte/hemostatic analogies, rather than assuming birds have mammalian platelets.

The input supplies GO:0160240 without a descriptive term name. Searches and attempted official ontology lookups did not resolve its identity. It is retained and scored 1 conservatively because a specific mechanistic link could not be established from the supplied annotation; this is an annotation limitation, not a biological finding.

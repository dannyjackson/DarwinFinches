# A priori physiological relevance to Philornis downsi

## Literature basis

The ratings use host physiology and natural-history/experimental evidence, without consulting selection results or Galápagos-bird genomic, transcriptomic, gene-expression, or epigenomic studies. One epigenomic paper appeared incidentally among broad search results; its findings were excluded. Each GO term was judged separately. A broad housekeeping role alone received little weight; narrower immune, blood-loss, wound, or nutritional functions received more weight. Directional regulation can be relevant in either direction because excessive responses can cause damage and allocation trade-offs.

**Direct injury and anemia.** Adult flies are free living; larvae parasitize birds. Early larvae occupy nares and tissues; later stages shelter in nest material and feed on nestlings' blood. Tissue consumption, lesions, bleeding, reduced hemoglobin, poor growth and mortality are central effects. Blood-cell replacement, iron balance, oxygen-deficit responses and hemostasis are strong physiological predictions; the evidence does not establish every molecular pathway as a measured defense. Fessl, Sinclair & Kleindorfer (2006), *The life-cycle of Philornis downsi ... and its impacts on nestling survival*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16899139/ . Lasting naris damage can also impair song and pairing, supporting attention to tissue integrity without treating every developmental or neural term as a specific fly response: Kleindorfer et al. (2019), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6571467/ .

**Immune resistance.** Koop et al. (2013) experimentally found increased fly-binding antibodies in breeding female medium ground finches. Higher antibody levels were associated with fewer parasites, but the direction of causation and protective efficacy are uncertain; parasitized nests nevertheless failed. Neither males nor nestlings showed the corresponding induced response. Their discussion proposes antibody-facilitated skin inflammation, effects on blood-feeding access, and defensive host molecules. Adaptive immunity and its B-cell components therefore have clear a priori relevance; innate sensing, cytokines, leukocyte activity, inflammatory regulation and barrier repair are strong or supporting predictions rather than universally demonstrated finch defenses. https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3930052/ . Virus-specific pathways lack an established specific connection to this extracellular fly.

**Tolerance and energy compensation.** Knutie et al. (2016) found that parasitized Galápagos mockingbird nestlings increased begging, associated with increased parental food provisioning, whereas finches lacked comparable compensation in that experiment. Antibody responses were not significant in either host in that comparison. This supports energy homeostasis, nutrient responses and metabolic compensation, with more general biosynthesis and organellar functions less specific: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27220210/ . McNew et al. (2019) found that mockingbird tolerance varied among years and declined with low rainfall, consistent with resource-dependent parental compensation: https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2019.0049 ; author-provided abstract https://datadryad.org/dataset/doi%3A10.5061/dryad.n11h95s .

**Behavioral resistance and avoidance.** McNew & Clayton's 2018 review covers rare larval removal/preening, parental attention to nestling nares, avoidance movements, and potential effects of nesting time and location. These make sensory, motor and learning functions possible supporting routes, but do not make all synaptic or embryonic neural development processes specific defenses. https://www.annualreviews.org/doi/pdf/10.1146/annurev-ento-020117-043103 . Knutie et al. (2014) demonstrated that finches incorporated experimentally supplied insecticide-treated cotton, substantially reducing parasites: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24801182/ . This documents a usable nest-material behavior, not an intrinsic host pesticide-detoxification mechanism. Pike et al. (2021) observed fly visits concentrated when parents were away, consistent with parent-mediated danger to flies: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8813692/ .

**Stress and repair limits.** An experimental study of breeding female finches did not find parasite-treatment differences in baseline or induced corticosterone, condition or hematocrit. Thus generic hormonal and stress pathways remain plausible supporting processes, not demonstrated adult protective responses: Koop et al. (2013), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23892015/ . Oxidative stress, apoptosis, autophagy, growth signaling and vascular repair were evaluated as downstream physiological possibilities, not as results established by excluded molecular studies. Reproductive gamete processes, radiation-specific responses and specialized embryonic structures have no comparably specific connection.

## Application of the scale

5 denotes a process directly matching a documented response/effect mechanism, such as immune response, energy compensation or response to wounding. 4 denotes core immune machinery or strong blood-loss, barrier, repair or hypoxia predictions. 3 denotes supporting metabolism, cellular repair, signaling, behavior or inflammatory components. 2 denotes weak or indirect physiological connections, including much ubiquitous cellular maintenance. 1 denotes no specific connection. No score distribution was targeted. The unnamed GO:0160240 was retained exactly as supplied; search results identify it as RNA polymerase II transcription initiation surveillance, a nonspecific transcriptional process, rated 1.

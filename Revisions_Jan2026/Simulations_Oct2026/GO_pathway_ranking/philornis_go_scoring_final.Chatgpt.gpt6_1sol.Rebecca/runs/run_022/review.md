# Independent literature review and scoring rationale

The assessment uses published natural-history, ecological, behavioral and physiological evidence, together with general biology. No genomic, transcriptomic or gene-expression findings from Galápagos birds were used. Searches covered larval feeding and injury, immune resistance, tolerance and behavioral defenses. Indexed abstracts/article text supplied evidence where direct NCBI/publisher access was blocked. The 1,008 supplied terms were assessed without selection results or a target score distribution.

## Mechanisms

**Blood loss and tissue damage.** First and early second instars occupy nostrils and other tissues; older larvae live in nest material and feed externally, principally at night. Myiasis causes wounds, nasal deformation, anemia, reduced growth and mortality. Increased immature erythrocytes with parasite intensity provide direct evidence of compensatory blood-cell production. Erythropoiesis therefore has direct support. Iron homeostasis, coagulation/hemostasis, hypoxia compensation, vascular integrity and epithelial repair are strong predictions; the literature does not establish every associated molecular pathway as a demonstrated defense.

**Immune resistance.** Experimental parasite manipulation showed that nesting female medium ground finches produce parasite-binding antibodies. Higher antibody levels were associated with fewer parasites, but protective causation remains uncertain and the response did not rescue offspring survival in that experiment. Males and nestlings lacked the corresponding induced response. Adaptive immunity is directly relevant; B-cell functions are core supporting mechanisms. Innate recognition, inflammation, complement, phagocytosis and control of immunopathology are plausible predictions rather than universally demonstrated effective defenses. Bacterial defenses may support protection at damaged barriers; viral-specific defenses have no established specific connection here.

**Tolerance.** Experiments on Galápagos mockingbirds link increased nestling begging and parental provisioning with compensation for parasite damage. Multi-year evidence shows environmentally dependent tolerance and provisioning. Nutrient availability, energy balance, maintenance of growth and replacement of blood/tissue are consequently relevant. Generic metabolic processes provide support, but metabolic necessity alone does not make a pathway directly parasite-specific.

**Behavioral resistance and avoidance.** Occasional larval removal, preening/scratching, parental nest sanitation and fly predation have been observed, with limited evidence of effective protection in finches. Galápagos flycatchers have been observed removing larvae and catching adult flies. Finches anoint feathers with Psidium galapageium leaves; extracts repel flies, while protection of wild nests remains unproven. Nest attendance, nesting location and breeding timing may alter exposure. Learning, sensory, motor and circadian mechanisms are supporting or indirect links rather than documented parasite-specific neural mechanisms.

## Applying the scale

Scores distinguish directly documented host mechanisms (5), core components or strong physiological predictions (4), plausible supporting processes (3), weak indirect links (2), and processes with no specific link (1). Broad cellular machinery was not elevated simply because immune cells also use it. Tissue growth and repair were distinguished from unrelated embryonic patterning and gametogenesis. Negative regulation can support tolerance by limiting damage, but is not automatically beneficial. Parasite developmental biology was not counted as host defense.

GO:0160240 has a repeated-identifier label in the input. Its public definition is RNA polymerase II transcription initiation surveillance. Its identifier was retained and its actual process assessed without renaming any input term.

# Independent physiological relevance review

## Mechanisms and scoring basis

Adult *Philornis downsi* are free-living; larvae feed on nestling blood and tissues. Early instars invade the nares and sometimes ear canals, while later instars commonly shelter in nest material and feed externally, especially at night. Documented consequences include tissue destruction, anemia, reduced growth and survival, and persistent naris/beak deformation. The hemoglobin study in small ground finches also found increased immature erythrocytes associated with infestation. Compensatory erythropoiesis is consequently directly relevant; iron regulation and hypoxia responses are strong predictions. Hemostasis, vascular function, epithelial repair, keratinocyte differentiation and extracellular matrix remodeling are plausible responses to injury. Their molecular involvement is not assumed to have been individually demonstrated. Platelet-specific terms receive supporting rather than maximal scores because avian hemostasis involves nucleated thrombocytes.

An experimental study detected parasite-binding antibody responses in adult female medium ground finches, but not adult males or nestlings. It did not establish effective nestling protection. A separate finch/mockingbird comparison found no significant antibody response in either species. Adaptive immunity and B-cell processes therefore remain relevant without assuming universal or effective resistance. Innate recognition, inflammation, cytokines, complement, leukocyte recruitment and phagocytosis are strong or supporting predictions from injury and immune defense, not automatically documented fly-specific pathways. Antibacterial defense may support protection against secondary contamination of feeding wounds; virus-specific mechanisms have no comparably specific link.

Mockingbirds can tolerate infestation through behavioral energy compensation: parasitized chicks begged more and received more food, unlike finches in the same comparison. Tolerance varied among years and declined in drought, consistent with resource-limited compensation. Energy homeostasis, nutrient sensing, fuel mobilization, growth and thermal balance are therefore relevant. Most individual metabolic pathways remain supporting processes rather than directly demonstrated defenses.

Observed or proposed behavioral responses include preening or removal of larvae, avoidance movements, changes in brooding and attendance, and adult predation on visiting flies. These do not consistently prevent nestling mortality. Finches can incorporate experimentally supplied insecticide-treated cotton and reduce infestation; this is assisted nest self-fumigation, not evidence of endogenous insecticide production. Persistent naris damage can affect later foraging and song, providing downstream consequences rather than proof of adaptation. General neural development, specific synaptic machinery, gametogenesis and embryonic organ patterning should not be rated highly merely because behavior and growth matter.

Each term was assessed individually: 5 directly represents a documented host mechanism; 4 is a core component or strong prediction; 3 is plausible support or a downstream response; 2 is a weak or generic connection; 1 lacks a specific link. Broad housekeeping was not automatically promoted because it is necessary in all cells. Regulation in either direction can matter. No target score distribution was imposed. No genomic, transcriptomic or gene-expression findings were used. GO:0160240 was supplied without a descriptive name; an ontology lookup identified RNA polymerase II transcription initiation surveillance, judged a weak generic connection, while its supplied identifier was retained.

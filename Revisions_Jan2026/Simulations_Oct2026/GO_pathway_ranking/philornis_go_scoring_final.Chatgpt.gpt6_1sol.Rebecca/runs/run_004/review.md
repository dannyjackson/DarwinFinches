# A priori physiological relevance review

Ratings were based on the published parasitological, behavioral, immunological and ecological literature below, together with general biological knowledge. No Galapagos-bird genomic, transcriptomic or gene-expression results were used. Only the specified input file was read locally.

## Mechanisms and interpretation

**Blood and tissue injury.** Early larvae occupy nestling nares and other tissues; older larvae hide in nest material and feed externally, principally at night. Blood loss, anemia, lesions, impaired growth and mortality are major consequences. Tissue consumption can permanently enlarge nares and alter beak function and song. These observations make hemostasis, erythrocyte replacement, iron balance, oxygen-deficit responses, epithelial repair, extracellular matrix remodeling and vascular repair strong predictions; their molecular involvement is inferred, rather than demonstrated by these field studies.

**Resistance.** Experimental parasite removal showed higher parasite-binding antibody levels in mothers at infested medium-ground-finch nests. This supports adaptive/humoral immune responses, although responses differ by age, sex and study, and do not establish effective nestling protection. Innate inflammation, leukocyte recruitment, complement and tissue-defense signaling are strong or supporting predictions, not demonstrated fly-specific molecular mechanisms. Antibacterial defenses have a plausible supporting role around wounds but are less specific than responses to the fly itself; antiviral processes were not treated as direct defenses against this insect.

**Behavior and tolerance.** Observed defenses include occasional larval removal/preening, scratching, repositioning and parental removal or predation of flies. They do not establish broadly effective finch resistance. Mockingbird nestlings increased begging and received greater provisioning, apparently compensating for parasite damage; this tolerance weakened in dry years. Consequently energy balance and nutritional compensation have substantial relevance, while generic neural, sensory, motor and endocrine pathways have weaker, supporting relevance. Nest location, attendance and breeding timing are proposed avoidance routes. Insecticide-treated nest material demonstrates a behavioral route to assisted parasite control, not evolved endogenous detoxification.

Scores distinguish directly observed broad mechanisms (5), core components or strong physiological predictions (4), supporting processes (3), weak/indirect links (2), and processes lacking a specific connection (1). Generic housekeeping functions were not upgraded merely because all responses require them. Embryonic organ patterning, meiosis and gamete-specific processes generally lack a specific link. Regulatory direction alone does not establish whether a process would be beneficial. GO:0160240 had no name in the supplied list; its ontology definition was checked online and it was rated as RNA polymerase II transcription initiation surveillance without changing the input ID.

# Independent a priori physiological relevance review

Philornis downsi adults are free living; larvae are the damaging parasitic stage. Early larvae occupy nestling nares and other tissues, while older larvae in nest material feed externally on blood, particularly at night. Published natural-history and parasite-removal work identifies blood loss, anemia, low hemoglobin, wounds, nasal deformation, impaired growth and high nestling mortality. These observations make hemostasis, blood-cell replacement, iron supply, oxygen-deficit responses, epithelial integrity and wound repair strong physiological predictions. They do not establish that every predicted compensatory process has been demonstrated experimentally.

Resistance includes parasite-specific immunity and behavioral removal or avoidance. Experiments found increased fly-binding antibodies in parasitized adult female medium ground finches; male and nestling responses were not detected in that experiment, and the female response did not rescue offspring survival. Inflammation, complement, leukocyte activity and immune signaling are strong supporting predictions, rather than evidence that every particular cytokine or pathway is an established defense. Skin inflammation and the ingestion of host defensive factors are proposed mechanisms for impairing ectoparasite feeding. Secondary wound-associated microbial defense is plausible but less specific than defense against the fly itself.

Tolerance can maintain fitness without lowering parasite abundance. In a finch–mockingbird comparison, parasitized mockingbird nestlings increased begging, with increased parental provisioning apparently compensating for parasite damage; finches did not show the same compensation. Consequently, energy balance, nutrient availability and metabolism plausibly support tolerance, with general metabolic machinery receiving lower scores than the compensatory mechanism itself. Blood replacement and repair are strong predictions from blood loss and tissue damage, not claims of demonstrated gene-level responses.

Behavioral defenses considered in the literature include preening, picking larvae from nares, consuming larvae, nest sanitation, posture changes, nest-site selection and breeding timing. Evidence for effective natural behavioral resistance in finches is limited, and measured changes are inconsistent across studies. Nest-material collection can be exploited experimentally: finches incorporate insecticide-treated cotton, reducing parasites. This is an assisted control method, not proof of evolved insecticide preference or an intrinsic detoxification defense. General sensation, motor control and learning therefore have only supporting or indirect relevance; pain perception has a stronger direct injury-related rationale. An experimental adult-female study found no parasite-treatment effect on corticosterone, so endocrine and stress responses are not treated as universally established adaptive defenses.

## Scoring interpretation

Each term was judged individually from this non-genomic review and general biology. A score of 5 was reserved for terms directly capturing documented host-response mechanisms (immune/adaptive response and wound healing). A score of 4 identifies core immune components or strong predictions from blood loss, wounds and compensatory nutrition; 3 identifies plausible supporting physiology; 2 identifies weak or generic links; and 1 identifies processes with no specific defensible link. Negative results and unproven efficacy do not erase physiological relevance, but are not recast as demonstrated protection. Generic housekeeping and specialized developmental processes were not promoted merely because all host responses require functioning cells. Avian coagulation terms referring to platelets are interpreted cautiously as conserved hemostatic processes relevant to nucleated avian thrombocytes, not as evidence of mammalian platelets in birds.

No genomic, transcriptomic or gene-expression results from Galápagos birds were used. A search result containing such results was excluded. The input supplied GO:0160240 with its ID repeated instead of a name; the authoritative GO report identifies RNA polymerase II transcription initiation surveillance, which was treated as generic transcriptional machinery without renaming the input or output identifier (https://release.geneontology.org/2026-06-19/reports/go-cam-stats/go-cam-unique-go-bp-terms.html).

# Independent physiological relevance review

Scores were assigned a priori from the non-genomic Philornis literature and general biology, without using selection, transcriptomic, expression, or genomic results from Galapagos birds. Each input term was retained, including the input whose name repeats its GO identifier. Scores indicate potential mechanistic relevance, not evidence that selection occurred.

## Host injury and predicted compensation

Early P. downsi larvae invade nestling nostrils and other tissues; later larvae shelter in nest material and feed externally, often at night. Blood and tissue removal cause anemia, impaired growth, injury, deformation, and mortality. Blood-cell replacement, iron balance, oxygen delivery, hemostasis, epithelial repair, and extracellular-matrix reconstruction therefore have strong physiological connections. Cellular metabolism, growth, vascular remodeling, antioxidant defenses, and stress responses are supporting predictions; their individual molecular pathways have not generally been demonstrated as fly-specific defenses.

- Fessl, Sinclair and Kleindorfer (2006), *The life-cycle of Philornis downsi ... and its impacts on nestling survival*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16899139/
- Kleindorfer et al. (2019), *Introduced parasite changes host phenotype, mating signal and hybridization risk*: nasal damage is linked to altered song characteristics, supporting possible sensory, motor, and developmental consequences without demonstrating general neuronal mechanisms: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6571467/

## Resistance

An experiment demonstrated P. downsi-binding antibody responses in adult female medium ground finches. Males and young nestlings did not show a comparable detectable response. Stronger female responses were only marginally associated with lower parasite abundance, and did not rescue nestling survival in that experiment. Thus adaptive immunity is directly relevant; B-cell development and activation are core components. Innate recognition, inflammatory regulation, cytokines, complement, and phagocytosis are plausible defenses or repair-associated responses, but should not be mistaken for individually established Philornis mechanisms.

- Koop et al. (2013), *Experimental demonstration of a parasite-induced immune response in wild birds*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3930052/

## Tolerance and behavior

Parasitized mockingbird chicks increased begging, associated with increased parental feeding and compensation for damage. Comparable increases were absent in medium ground finches in that experiment. Mockingbird tolerance varied among years and deteriorated under drought, consistent with resource-dependent compensation. This supports energy homeostasis, nutrient sensing, mobilization of reserves, and growth maintenance.

Preening, removal of larvae, nest sanitation, repositioning and altered brooding have been observed or proposed, with effectiveness and expression varying by age and species. Reduced brooding may itself impose thermoregulatory costs. Finch incorporation of supplied insecticide-treated cotton demonstrates a feasible behavioral route to reducing exposure; natural leaf-rubbing has also been proposed as a repellent defense. These observations give broad cognition, motor, sensory, and circadian processes limited supporting relevance rather than establishing every neuronal pathway.

- Knutie et al. (2016), *Galapagos mockingbirds tolerate introduced parasites that affect Darwin's finches*: https://doi.org/10.1890/15-0119.1
- McNew et al. (2019), *Annual environmental variation influences host tolerance to parasites*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6408884/
- Knutie et al. (2014), *Darwin's finches combat introduced nest parasites with fumigated cotton*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24801182/
- McNew and Clayton (2018), *Alien Invasion: Biology of Philornis Flies Highlighting Philornis downsi*: synthesis of injury, immunity, behavioral resistance, and tolerance: https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-ento-020117-043103

## Limits guiding scores

An adult-female experiment did not detect parasite-treatment effects on baseline or induced corticosterone, body condition, or hematocrit. Endocrine stress pathways remain plausible, rather than documented universal defenses: Knutie et al. (2013), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4383185/.

The highest scores distinguish directly demonstrated immune response from strong mechanistic predictions. General repair, energetic, cellular-stress, and developmental support receive intermediate scores. Housekeeping machinery and unrelated organogenesis, reproduction, viral-specific responses, and DNA/RNA maintenance receive low scores unless a more particular physiological connection exists. Negative regulation can be relevant through limiting damage or changing defense intensity; it was not assumed to be beneficial.

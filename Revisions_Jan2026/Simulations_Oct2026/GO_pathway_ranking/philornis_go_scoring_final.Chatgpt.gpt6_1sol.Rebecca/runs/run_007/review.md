# Independent physiological relevance review

## Mechanisms used for scoring

Early *Philornis downsi* larvae invade nestling nares and other soft tissues; later larvae occupy nest material and take blood meals from nestlings. Brooding females can also be exposed. Tissue injury and blood loss cause anemia, impaired development and mortality. Persistent naris/beak damage can affect subsequent feeding and song. These observations make erythrocyte replacement, hematopoiesis, oxygen-stress responses, hemostasis, epithelial repair and tissue maintenance strong a priori candidates. Hypoxia, clotting and repair are mechanistic predictions from the documented damage unless specifically measured, rather than assumed demonstrations of resistance.

Parasite-binding antibodies are experimentally documented in adult female medium ground finches. Their association with fewer parasites was suggestive, not proof that antibodies cause resistance; they did not rescue reproduction in that experiment. Males and young nestlings lacked detectable induced antibody responses in that study. Antibody production and its core cellular machinery therefore have strong relevance, while individual cytokine pathways, innate recognition, complement, phagocytosis and inflammatory regulation remain predictions or supporting mechanisms. Inflammation and itching were proposed as ways to impede blood feeding or trigger removal. Bacterial defense is plausible after skin disruption, but secondary infections and pathogen transmission are not established explanations; antiviral specificity receives little support.

Tolerance can occur through energetic compensation. Parasitized Galapagos mockingbird nestlings increased begging, associated with greater parental provisioning, whereas comparable compensation was absent in the studied medium ground finches. Consequently energy balance and nutrient use are relevant; general metabolic and mitochondrial processes are supporting rather than fly-specific mechanisms. Developmental and survival costs make oxidative protection, stress responses and cell survival plausible downstream processes. An experimental maternal corticosterone study found no parasite-treatment effect, so generalized endocrine stress is not treated as a demonstrated protective mechanism.

Preening/larval removal, allopreening and nest sanitation have been observed or proposed, but are inconsistent and often uncommon. Brooding females also change posture and reduce brooding in parasitized nests. Nest-material collection can be exploited through experimental self-fumigation with treated cotton; this is assisted control, not evidence of an evolved ability to detoxify insecticides. Learning, sensory and neuromotor functions have possible supporting roles but do not make every neural pathway specifically associated. Embryonic patterning, reproduction-specific cell processes, DNA/RNA machinery and other remote housekeeping functions receive low scores where the term offers no particular mechanistic connection.

## Rating practice

Each input term was assessed for its own physiological meaning, with no target score distribution. A documented host response can be relevant despite being insufficient to prevent mortality. Core predicted repair and immune processes score below mechanisms directly demonstrated in this system; generic downstream support scores below core components. Mammalian labels such as platelet processes were interpreted cautiously as analogous avian thrombocyte/hemostatic functions. GO:0160240, whose supplied name repeats its ID, was identified from the GO public report as RNA polymerase II transcription initiation surveillance and rated for that meaning: https://current.geneontology.org/reports/go-cam-stats/go-cam-unique-go-bp-terms.html . No genomic, transcriptomic or gene-expression results from Galapagos birds were used.

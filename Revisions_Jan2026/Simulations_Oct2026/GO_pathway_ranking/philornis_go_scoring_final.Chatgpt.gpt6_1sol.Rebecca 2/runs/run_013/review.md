# A priori physiological relevance to Philornis downsi

## Literature basis and mechanisms

The review used non-genomic work on parasite biology, host physiology, immunology, and behavior. No host genomic, transcriptomic, gene-expression, or selection-study results were used to inform scores. Scores express potential relevance, not evidence that a particular pathway has evolved or that a defense is effective.

* **Blood loss and tissue injury.** Early larvae inhabit the nostrils and other host tissues; later larvae live in nest material and feed externally on nestlings. Tissue destruction and substantial blood loss produce anemia and mortality. These observations strongly motivate hemostasis, erythrocyte production, iron handling, oxygen homeostasis, epithelial repair, extracellular-matrix remodeling, and vascular repair. Those specific physiological compensations are predictions unless directly measured, rather than all being demonstrated defenses. Source: Fessl, Sinclair and Kleindorfer (2006), *The life-cycle of Philornis downsi ... and its impacts on nestling survival*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16899139/ .

* **Immune resistance, with limited demonstrated protection.** An experimental medium-ground-finch study found elevated fly-binding antibodies in mothers at parasitized nests, with a tendency for higher maternal antibodies to accompany fewer parasites. Nestlings and fathers lacked comparable induced responses, and maternal immunity did not rescue reproductive success. The authors discuss inflammation, defensive molecules, and itching-linked preening as potential mechanisms. Adaptive immunity and B-cell processes therefore have a direct mechanistic connection; cytokines, leukocyte activity, innate recognition, and inflammatory regulation are strong predictions or supporting processes, without assuming that any particular cytokine pathway was demonstrated. Source: Koop et al. (2013), *Experimental demonstration of a parasite-induced immune response in wild birds*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3930052/ ; https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.651 .

* **Tolerance through energy compensation.** Experimental comparison found that mockingbirds, unlike medium ground finches, could tolerate similar parasite densities: parasitized mockingbird nestlings begged more and received more food. Neither species showed a significant antibody response in that comparison. Thus response depends on species, age, and context. Energy homeostasis and nutrient acquisition are central; glucose, lipid, amino-acid metabolism, growth regulation, and stress physiology can support compensation without each being directly documented. Source: Knutie et al. (2016), *Galapagos mockingbirds tolerate introduced parasites that affect Darwin's finches*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28792593/ . Tolerance decreased in dry years, consistent with limits on compensatory provisioning: McNew et al. (2019), *Annual environmental variation influences host tolerance to parasites*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6408884/ .

* **Behavioral defenses and proposed repellency.** Preening or removing larvae and avoiding contact can potentially reduce feeding; ineffective or absent behavioral responses also matter and are not evidence of universal protection. Finches have been observed rubbing Psidium galapageium leaves on feathers; experiments showed repellency against adult flies and suppression of larval growth. An antiparasitic function in wild birds remains a hypothesis. Learning, sensory perception, movement, and behavioral timing are supporting links, not grounds to treat every synaptic or neural-development term as strongly relevant. Source: Cimadom et al. (2016), *Darwin's finches treat their feathers with a natural repellent*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5056383/ . Human-provided insecticide-treated cotton can also be incorporated into nests, demonstrating a possible behavioral route for parasite reduction rather than an endogenous detoxification mechanism: Knutie et al. (2014), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24801182/ .

## Scoring interpretation

I assessed terms individually, without targeting a score distribution. Score 5 denotes processes directly matching documented immune response, injury response, or energy compensation; score 4 denotes their core components or strong physiological predictions. Score 3 covers plausible supporting metabolism, repair, or signaling. Score 2 denotes weak or nonspecific links, and score 1 denotes no specific association, including most reproductive-cell processes, embryonic patterning, and housekeeping nucleic-acid operations. Broad signaling or protein processing was not upgraded merely because it can occur in immune cells. Antiviral processes were not treated as direct fly responses; antibacterial pathways have only a plausible secondary wound-defense relationship. Mammalian-named platelet processes were interpreted cautiously as avian hemostatic analogues, not literal evidence of avian platelets. Opposite regulatory directions can both be relevant to balanced responses and received mechanism-based judgments.

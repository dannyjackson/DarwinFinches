# Physiological basis for the ratings

These are independent, a priori judgments of potential host response, based on the non-genomic literature below and general biology. A response need not be effective or beneficial to be relevant. Conversely, a process is not directly demonstrated merely because it could contribute to survival. No transcriptomic, gene-expression, epigenomic or genomic findings from Galápagos birds were used; excluded studies encountered in searches were disregarded.

## Mechanisms and evidence

**Blood loss and tissue injury.** Early larvae occupy and damage nestling nares and soft beak tissue; later larvae largely reside in nest material and emerge to feed externally on blood and tissue. Larvae also attack brooding females. Consequences include anemia, depressed hemoglobin, impaired growth, persistent naris deformation and substantial nestling mortality. Increased immature erythrocytes associated with infestation support compensatory erythropoiesis. Thus erythrocyte production and hematopoiesis are directly relevant; iron homeostasis, oxygen delivery, hypoxia responses, hemostasis, epithelial integrity and repair are strong physiological predictions. Fibroblasts, collagen, extracellular matrix, angiogenesis and growth-factor pathways can support repair, but their specific participation has generally not been measured in these studies.

**Resistance and immunity.** An experimental study found elevated fly-binding antibodies in parasitized medium ground finch mothers and a tendency toward fewer parasites in mothers with higher antibody levels. This did not rescue offspring survival; comparable induced responses were absent in fathers and nestlings. Another experiment found no significant antibody response in either finch or mockingbird hosts. Antibody-based defense is therefore documented but context-dependent and insufficient, rather than universally protective. B-cell functions, adaptive immunity and immune regulation are strongly relevant. Innate inflammation, phagocytosis and cytokine signaling are reasonable predictions from blood feeding and tissue injury; specific cytokines, inflammasomes and pathogen-specific responses receive less direct evidential weight.

**Tolerance and resource compensation.** Mockingbird nestlings increased begging and parental provisioning in a comparison in which mockingbirds tolerated parasites better than medium ground finches. A four-season experiment found tolerance declined in drier years and tracked provisioning, supporting dependence on resources. Energy and nutrient balance, growth, fuel mobilization and physiological maintenance are relevant. Oxidative homeostasis and cellular stress responses are plausible downstream processes, rather than documented molecular mechanisms. A metabolic process does not receive a high rating merely because all living cells require it.

**Behavioral defenses.** Video observations describe increased self-preening, eating larvae, standing on nestmates, and parental sanitation or allopreening. These can remove or avoid larvae, but effectiveness is limited and variable. Altered brooding can also have thermoregulatory costs. Finches incorporate provided insecticide-treated cotton into nests, an experimentally demonstrated assisted defense. Leaf anointing with Psidium galapageium has been observed and leaf extracts repel parasitic insects experimentally; its field protective benefit against this fly remains a proposed explanation. Sensation, motor function, learning, circadian behavior and neural signaling can support such behavior, but most specific neural-developmental terms have only indirect links.

## Scoring interpretation

Scores follow the supplied 1–5 definitions without imposing a target distribution. Broad housekeeping processes and unrelated reproductive or embryonic programs receive low scores even though they are indispensable to life. Generic signaling, transport and metabolism are distinguished from mechanisms that specifically repair damage, replace blood or support immune defense. Regulatory terms can be relevant in either direction because excessive responses also have costs; this does not establish the adaptive direction of any future selected variant.

# Independent literature review and scoring rationale

The ratings concern potential physiological responses to *Philornis downsi*, not evidence that any particular gene or pathway has evolved. Each supplied term was judged by its own meaning; a regulatory term can be relevant regardless of whether its direction would ultimately benefit the host. No genomic, transcriptomic, expression, or epigenomic results from Galápagos birds were used. An omics paper appeared incidentally in search results and was excluded from the evidence and rationale.

## Mechanisms and evidence

**Blood loss and physical injury.** Early larvae occupy nestling nares and other tissue; later larvae reside in nest material and feed externally, predominantly at night. Nasal tissue destruction and blood feeding cause anemia, impaired condition, and mortality. Blood loss makes erythrocyte replacement, iron management, oxygen limitation responses, hemostasis, and cardiovascular compensation strong physiological predictions. Epithelial barriers, repair, extracellular matrix remodeling, and vascular repair are also closely connected, although a predicted repair mechanism is not automatically a demonstrated effective defense. Fessl, Sinclair and Kleindorfer (2006), *The life-cycle of Philornis downsi ... and its impacts on nestling survival*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16899139/

**Growth and energy allocation.** Experimental parasite reduction improved nestling performance, and the literature identifies reduced growth and anemia as important costs. Cellular metabolism can support compensatory growth and repair, but generic metabolic or biosynthetic terms do not by themselves identify an antiparasite adaptation. Koop et al. (2011), *Experimental Demonstration of the Fitness Consequences of an Introduced Parasite of Darwin's Finches*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3092749/

**Immune resistance and its limitations.** An experiment in medium ground finches detected parasite-binding antibody responses in mothers, with a tendency toward fewer parasites when responses were stronger. Nestlings and males did not show equivalent induced responses, and maternal antibodies did not rescue reproductive success. The paper proposes that antibody-associated inflammation, itching, and defensive molecules could impair ectoparasite feeding. This supports adaptive immunity directly and innate immunity, leukocyte function, cytokines, inflammatory regulation, and B-cell processes as mechanistic components or predictions. It does not establish that every immune response is beneficial or that these birds have demonstrated protection against unrelated viruses. Koop et al. (2013): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3930052/

**Tolerance through food and parental care.** Galápagos mockingbirds can compensate through increased nestling begging and parental provisioning, whereas finches in the comparison lacked this response. A later multiyear experiment showed that tolerance varies with environmental conditions and deteriorates in dry years. These studies support energy balance, nutrient availability, growth compensation, and behavioral regulation; individual neural-development and synaptic pathways remain much more indirect. Knutie et al. (2016), *Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate introduced parasites that affect Darwin's finches*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27220210/ ; McNew et al. (2019), *Annual environmental variation influences host tolerance to parasites*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6408884/

**Behavioral resistance and avoidance.** Limited observations include removal of larvae by preening and changes in nest posture/brooding; the latter can compromise thermal care. Guava-leaf rubbing has been proposed as self-medication, without establishing effective natural protection against this fly. Human-provided insecticide-treated cotton can be incorporated into nests, demonstrating an assisted behavioral control opportunity rather than an endogenous detoxification defense. McNew and Clayton (2018), review: https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-ento-020117-043103 ; Knutie et al. (2014), self-fumigation experiment: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24801182/

## Application of the scale

Direct host immune/defense terms receive the highest ratings. Core immune components, blood replacement, hypoxia responses, wound repair, skin maintenance, and energy homeostasis receive strong ratings. Supporting metabolic, signaling, cell migration, redox, and stress processes receive intermediate ratings when a concrete physiological route exists. General housekeeping processes generally have weak specific relevance. Reproduction-specific, embryonic patterning, narrowly specified neural development, and unrelated pathogen-specific processes receive low ratings rather than being elevated simply because parasitism can impair fitness.

The supplied name for GO:0160240 repeats its identifier. A web lookup did not resolve its meaning; it is retained without renaming and assigned 1 because no specific physiological connection could be established from the supplied information. This is an identification limitation, not evidence that its actual function is unrelated.

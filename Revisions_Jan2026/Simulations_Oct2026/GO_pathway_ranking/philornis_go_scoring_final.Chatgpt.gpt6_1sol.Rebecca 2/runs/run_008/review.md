# Independent a priori mechanism review

Scores reflect host physiology, not genomic findings. I reviewed the parasite's biology, experimental host-defense studies, and general biological mechanisms. I did not use results from genomic, transcriptomic, or gene-expression studies of Galapagos birds. An excluded epigenetic study appeared in search results but was not used. Only the designated input was read locally.

## Mechanisms and evidence

- **Blood loss and tissue injury.** Early larvae occupy nares and other tissues; later stages feed externally on blood while sheltering in nest material. Anemia, reduced hemoglobin, impaired growth, and mortality provide the central physiological challenges. Increased immature erythrocytes are a documented compensatory response. Thus erythropoiesis/hemopoiesis have direct support; iron handling, clotting, oxygen-stress responses, vascular repair, and skin/epithelial repair are strong predictions or supporting processes rather than individually established adaptations. Sources: Fessl et al. (2006), *The life-cycle of Philornis downsi...*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16899139/ ; Dudaniec, Kleindorfer and Fessl (2006), *Effects... on haemoglobin level and nestling survival*: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1442-9993.2006.01553.x .

- **Immune resistance, with important limits.** Experimental parasite removal showed parasite-binding antibody responses in adult female medium ground finches, but not males or nestlings. The association between female antibody levels and parasite abundance was marginal and does not prove effective protection. The proposed inflammatory mechanism includes inhibiting feeding and exposing larvae to host defensive molecules. Adaptive immunity, B-cell processes, and inflammation therefore merit high a priori relevance; particular cytokine pathways have less direct support. Innate defense and protection against opportunistic wound infection are strong or plausible predictions. Viral-specific pathways do not acquire direct relevance merely because they share immune components. Source: Koop et al. (2013), *Experimental demonstration of a parasite-induced immune response...*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3930052/ .

- **Tolerance through food and energy compensation.** Mockingbird nestlings increased begging, with associated parental provisioning that appeared to compensate for parasite effects. Comparable compensation was absent in medium ground finches in that experiment. Mockingbird tolerance varies among years and declines with drought/resource limitation. These findings support nutritional supply, energy balance, growth maintenance, and downstream carbohydrate/lipid/amino-acid metabolism, without implying that every metabolic pathway is specifically adaptive. Sources: Knutie et al. (2016), *Galapagos mockingbirds tolerate introduced parasites that affect Darwin's finches*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27220210/ ; McNew et al. (2019), *Annual environmental variation influences host tolerance to parasites*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6408884/ .

- **Behavioral resistance, avoidance and trade-offs.** Larval removal/preening and positioning atop nestmates have been observed, but are infrequent and lack evidence of reliable protection in finches. Female standing and reduced brooding may avoid bites while impairing nestling thermal support. Nest-site choice and timing are potential avoidance mechanisms, with much evidence correlational or from other Philornis hosts. Sensory processing, learning, circadian behavior, muscle function, and thermoregulation are plausible supporting links, not grounds for assigning all neuronal development terms high scores. Review: McNew and Clayton (2018), *Alien Invasion: Biology of Philornis Flies...*: https://www.annualreviews.org/doi/pdf/10.1146/annurev-ento-020117-043103 .

- **Self-medication and parental care vary by species.** Birds incorporate experimentally supplied insecticide-treated cotton, demonstrating a usable nest-material behavior rather than endogenous detoxification adaptation: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24801182/ . Finches also anoint feathers with guava leaves containing fly-repellent compounds, but seasonal patterns do not establish that this behavior evolved against P. downsi: https://doi.org/10.1111/eth.13153 . Green Warbler-Finch females reduced provisioning at high infestation, emphasizing that compensation cannot be generalized: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10188583/ .

## Scoring interpretation

Each supplied term was considered independently. Directly documented erythrocyte replacement earns 5; immune effector mechanisms and key responses to blood loss/injury earn 4 when core or strongly predicted. General metabolism, repair support, and behavioral support often earn 3; broad housekeeping or signaling with only indirect connections earns 2. Specialized embryonic patterning, reproduction, RNA machinery, or unrelated agent-specific responses generally earn 1 where there is no specific mechanistic link. Presence or absence of an observed response does not establish adaptive value, and proposed mechanisms were not treated as proven effective defenses. No target score distribution was used.

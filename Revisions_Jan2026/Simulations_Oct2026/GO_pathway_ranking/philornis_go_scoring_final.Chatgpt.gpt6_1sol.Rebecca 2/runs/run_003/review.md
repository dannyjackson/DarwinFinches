# Independent physiological relevance review

Scope: This rating uses the supplied GO terms, published ecological, behavioral and physiological evidence, and general biology. It does not use genomic, transcriptomic, gene-expression, or selection results from Galápagos birds. Only the specified input file was read locally. Each term was assessed for a host response, rather than for a role in fly physiology. Broad cellular involvement alone was not treated as strong evidence. No target score distribution was used.

## Mechanisms and evidence

**Blood and tissue loss.** Early Philornis downsi larvae occupy nares and other tissues; later larvae live in nest material and feed on nestlings' blood. Tissue destruction, enlarged/deformed nares, anemia, impaired growth and high mortality are established effects. These give strong a priori links to wound responses, hemostasis, epithelial repair, erythrocyte production and iron homeostasis. Hypoxia responses and vascular repair are strong predictions, not demonstrations of the corresponding molecular pathway. Craniofacial and cartilage processes have weaker relevance to lasting nasal injury; embryonic organ patterning does not automatically imply repair.

- Fessl, Sinclair & Kleindorfer (2006), *The life-cycle of Philornis downsi ... and its impacts on nestling survival*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16899139/
- Fessl and colleagues (2006), experimental parasite reduction demonstrating growth and fledging costs: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0006320705002983
- Common and colleagues (2021), age/timing dependence and damage by early larval stages: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7882474/

**Immune resistance.** Experimental fly reduction showed increased fly-binding antibodies in parasitized medium ground finch mothers, but not fathers or young nestlings. An association between maternal antibodies and fewer larvae was marginal, and immunity did not rescue reproductive success in that experiment. Antibody responses were absent in another finch/mockingbird comparison. Thus adaptive immunity is directly relevant, while B-cell activation and differentiation, helper T-cell functions and immune regulation are strong mechanistic predictions. Local inflammatory resistance, complement, cytokines, leukocyte recruitment and innate recognition are plausible components; specific cytokines or receptors are not established as effective Philornis defenses. Antibacterial defenses could support damaged skin but are less directly tied to this fly; virus-specific processes have weak relevance.

- Koop and colleagues (2013), *Experimental demonstration of a parasite-induced immune response in wild birds*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3930052/
- McNew & Clayton (2018), *Alien Invasion: Biology of Philornis Flies Highlighting Philornis downsi*: https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-ento-020117-043103

**Tolerance and nutritional compensation.** Galápagos mockingbird nestlings increased begging, correlated with increased parental food delivery, while comparable finches did not. Mockingbirds maintained fitness despite infestation. Across years this tolerance depended on environmental resources. Nutrient acquisition, glucose and energy balance, growth, protein turnover and metabolic allocation therefore have plausible supporting roles. Individual housekeeping pathways remain weak links unless their function supplies a more specific physiological prediction.

- Knutie and colleagues (2016), *Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate introduced parasites that affect Darwin's finches*: https://doi.org/10.1890/15-0119.1
- Knutie and colleagues (2019), *Annual environmental variation influences host tolerance to parasites*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6408884/

**Behavioral resistance and avoidance.** Observed or proposed responses include preening, removal/consumption of larvae, nest sanitation, agitation and postural changes. Young nestlings have limited motor capacity and experimental support for increased preening is weak. Sensation, pain and motor function can support these defenses, but generic neuronal development, synapse organization or ion transport is insufficiently specific for a high score. Nest placement and breeding timing may alter exposure. Leaf rubbing with naturally repellent Guayabillo and incorporation of experimentally insecticide-treated nest material illustrate behavioral options; supplied insecticides are management interventions, not evidence for an evolved host detoxification defense.

- O'Connor observations and behavioral limitations summarized by McNew & Clayton (2018), above.
- Cimadom and colleagues (2016), *Darwin's finches treat their feathers with a natural repellent*: https://www.nature.com/articles/srep34559
- Knutie and colleagues (2014), *Darwin's finches combat introduced nest parasites with fumigated cotton*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24801182/

**Stress and damage limitation.** Stress signaling, redox balance, autophagy and cell survival may support compensation and repair. They are plausible predictions rather than documented molecular mechanisms. A maternal corticosterone experiment did not support parasite-induced maternal stress as the explanation for nestling loss; steroid-related terms were therefore not uniformly promoted.

- Knutie and colleagues (2013), experimental corticosterone study: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0016648013003134

## Scoring conventions

Scores distinguish documented immune/injury-response classes (5), core defense components and strong physiological predictions (4), downstream/supporting mechanisms (3), nonspecific or weakly connected mechanisms (2), and processes without a specific physiological connection (1). Regulation in either direction can be relevant; a negative-regulation term was assessed as regulation of the named mechanism, not automatically assumed protective. Reproductive development, embryonic patterning and generic RNA/chromosome machinery were not elevated simply because parasitism affects fitness. All GO IDs and input order were preserved.

The input supplies no name for GO:0160240 beyond its ID. An official GO report identifies it as RNA polymerase II transcription initiation surveillance; it was rated as generic transcriptional machinery (1), without altering the supplied term or ID: https://current.geneontology.org/reports/go-cam-stats/go-cam-unique-go-bp-terms.html

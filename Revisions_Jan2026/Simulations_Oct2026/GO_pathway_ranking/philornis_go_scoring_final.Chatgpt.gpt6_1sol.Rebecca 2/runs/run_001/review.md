# A priori literature basis for GO ratings

The review used published ecological, experimental, behavioral, and physiological work on Philornis downsi and Galápagos land birds. No bird genomic, transcriptomic, or gene-expression results were used. Scores describe potential mechanistic relevance, not evidence that a particular GO term is under selection, nor a prediction that the process must increase or decrease.

## Mechanisms and evidence

**Blood loss and local injury.** Early larvae occupy nestling nares and other tissues; later larvae live in nesting material and repeatedly feed on nestling blood. Tissue consumption and blood removal produce wounds, anemia, impaired growth, mortality, and persistent nasal damage. These observations make hemostasis, erythrocyte replacement and iron supply, oxygen limitation, epithelial repair, extracellular matrix remodeling, vascular support, and maintenance of growth strong physiological candidates. The detailed cellular repair pathways are generally predictions from injury, rather than mechanisms experimentally demonstrated to confer resistance. Sources: Fessl, Sinclair & Kleindorfer (2006), “The life-cycle of Philornis downsi ... and its impacts on nestling survival,” https://doi.org/10.1017/S0031182006001089 (abstract: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16899139/); Koop et al. (2011), “Experimental Demonstration of the Fitness Consequences of an Introduced Parasite of Darwin’s Finches,” https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3092749/ . The latter experimentally reduced parasites and discusses anemia and possible energetic and sleep-related effects; those proposed mechanisms should not be confused with directly measured pathways.

**Immune resistance.** Koop et al. experimentally found higher fly-binding antibody levels in mothers at parasitized medium-ground-finch nests. The tendency toward fewer parasites with higher maternal antibodies supported a possible defense, but the association was marginal and immunity did not rescue reproductive success. Males and nestlings did not show the corresponding induced antibody or behavioral defense. Thus adaptive immunity and B-cell processes have a direct mechanistic basis; T-cell support, innate sensing, leukocyte recruitment, inflammatory regulation, cytokines, complement, and phagocytic clearance have strong or supporting biological rationales with less direct Philornis-specific evidence. Specific antiviral responses do not receive a high score simply because they are immune processes. Source: Koop et al. (2013), “Experimental demonstration of a parasite-induced immune response in wild birds: Darwin’s finches and introduced nest flies,” https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.651 ; full text https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3930052/ .

**Energetic and nutritional tolerance.** In a parasite-manipulation comparison, parasitized Galápagos mockingbirds increased begging, associated with more parental food provisioning; their reproductive performance was buffered despite parasite loads. Medium ground finches did not show equivalent compensatory begging, provisioning, or plasma-glucose changes, and suffered reduced fitness. Energy balance, nutrient sensing, glucose utilization and maintenance of growth therefore have a credible tolerance rationale. Individual metabolic pathways remain supporting mechanisms, not demonstrated specific adaptations. Tolerance is conditional: a later multi-year experiment found lower mockingbird tolerance in drier conditions. Sources: Knutie et al. (2016), “Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate introduced parasites that affect Darwin’s finches,” https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27220210/ ; McNew et al. (2019), “Annual environmental variation influences host tolerance to parasites,” https://doi.org/10.1098/rspb.2019.0049 .

**Behavioral resistance and avoidance.** Nest videos document grooming and avoidance, with larvae active especially at night. Other observations include larval removal/consumption, and changes in position in the nest. Sensory detection, learning, pain, motor function and circadian timing can support these defenses, but a generic synaptic or neural-development term does not itself establish a specific antiparasite mechanism. Sources: O’Connor, Robertson & Kleindorfer (2010), “Video analysis of host–parasite interactions in nests of Darwin’s finches,” https://doi.org/10.1017/S0030605310000086 ; Pike et al. (2021), “Behavior of the Avian Parasite Philornis downsi ... in and Near Host Nests,” https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8813692/ . Finch incorporation of experimentally provided insecticide-treated cotton also demonstrates a behavioral route to protection, but is assisted control, not evidence of evolved physiological insecticide detoxification: Knutie et al. (2014), https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2014.03.058 .

**Persistent damage.** Nasal deformation can affect adult song and pairing. This supports relevance of repair and local structural integrity, but does not make every embryonic patterning, skeletal, auditory, or neural-development process a direct fly defense. Source: Kleindorfer et al. (2019), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6571467/ .

## Rating interpretation

Each supplied term was judged individually. Five denotes direct capture of an observed host response, four a core component or strong injury/defense prediction, three a plausible supporting process, two a weak or nonspecific connection, and one no specific connection. General housekeeping, reproduction, organ-patterning and RNA-processing terms were not upgraded merely because all responses require viable cells. Both positive and negative regulation can matter to defense or tolerance; direction alone did not determine score. Canonical platelet terms were interpreted through avian thrombocyte hemostasis, without assuming birds have mammalian platelets. GO:0160240 lacked a supplied name; its identity was checked as RNA polymerase II transcription initiation surveillance (https://current.geneontology.org/reports/go-cam-stats/go-cam-unique-go-bp-terms.html) and rated as a general transcriptional process. Input GO identifiers and order are preserved.

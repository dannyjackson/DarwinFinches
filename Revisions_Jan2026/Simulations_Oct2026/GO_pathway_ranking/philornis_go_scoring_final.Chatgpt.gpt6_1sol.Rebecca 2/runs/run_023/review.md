# Independent a priori physiological relevance review

## Scope

Scores use published ecological, parasitological, behavioral and conventional immunological observations plus general biological knowledge. No genomic, transcriptomic, gene-expression, methylation or selection results from Galápagos birds were used. Search results containing such work were excluded from the evidentiary basis. Each supplied GO ID is retained, in input order; scores represent relevance, not evidence that a particular gene or pathway has evolved in these birds.

## Mechanisms and evidence

**Blood feeding and tissue injury.** Early Philornis downsi larvae inhabit nestling nares and other tissues; later larvae shelter in nest material and feed on blood, especially at night. Nasal damage can persist, and substantial blood loss, anemia, impaired growth and mortality are documented. These observations strongly motivate erythrocyte replacement and hematopoiesis, iron management, oxygen-stress responses, hemostasis, epithelial repair and extracellular-matrix remodeling. Erythrocyte differentiation has especially direct support from reported increases in immature erythrocytes with infestation; most specific wound-repair signaling pathways remain predictions rather than demonstrated P. downsi mechanisms.

- Fessl, Sinclair & Kleindorfer (2006), *The life-cycle of Philornis downsi ... and its impacts on nestling survival*, Parasitology. **Immune resistance.** Experimental parasite removal showed higher parasite-binding antibody levels in mothers at parasitized medium-ground-finch nests. This did not establish effective protection; nestling and male responses were not equivalent. Therefore adaptive immunity is directly relevant, while B-cell development/activation, immune signaling, leukocyte recruitment, innate recognition, inflammatory regulation, phagocytosis and complement are core or strongly plausible defenses. Particular cytokine or receptor pathways are predictions. Antiviral pathways receive less weight because the fly is not a virus; antibacterial defenses have a supporting wound-contamination rationale, not proof of bacterial disease.

- Koop et al. (2013), *Experimental demonstration of a parasite-induced immune response in wild birds*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3930052/

**Tolerance and energetic compensation.** Experimental comparisons found mockingbird nestlings increased begging and their parents increased provisioning, apparently compensating for damage; equivalent compensation was absent in finches in that experiment. Neither species showed a significant parasite-binding antibody response there. Nutrition, energy homeostasis and nutrient metabolism are consequently relevant, but generic molecular housekeeping is only weakly informative.

- Knutie et al. (2016), *Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate introduced parasites that affect Darwin’s finches*, Ecology: https://esajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1890/15-0119.1

**Behavioral defense.** Nest sanitation, maternal and nestling preening and larval removal have been observed, with age and study differences and limited evidence of reliable efficacy. Begging, provisioning and altered brooding connect sensory, motor, learning and thermoregulatory processes to defense, but broadly specified synapse development or embryonic neural patterning has a much weaker connection. Birds can incorporate provided insecticide-treated cotton, demonstrating a management-assisted behavioral route rather than endogenous insecticide production.

- Koop et al. (2013), above; behavioral observations and limitations.
- Pike et al., *Behavior of the Avian Parasite Philornis downsi ... in and Near Host Nests*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8813692/
- Knutie et al. (2014), *Darwin’s finches combat introduced nest parasites with fumigated cotton*: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0960982214003509

## Scoring interpretation

A 5 identifies a process that directly captures observed immunity, compensatory blood-cell production or energetic compensation. A 4 identifies a core component or strong prediction from those observations. A 3 identifies plausible supporting physiology such as repair, oxidative protection, metabolism or regulatory signaling. A 2 identifies a weak connection, including ubiquitous cellular machinery or broadly specified neural function. A 1 identifies no specific connection, particularly reproductive, embryonic-patterning and unrelated damage processes. Polarity of regulation does not automatically make a process irrelevant: restraint can limit immunopathology. Platelet terminology is interpreted cautiously as the conserved hemostatic function performed by avian thrombocytes. GO:0160240 was supplied without an interpretable name and could not be independently resolved; it receives 1 because no specific link could be established, without renaming it.

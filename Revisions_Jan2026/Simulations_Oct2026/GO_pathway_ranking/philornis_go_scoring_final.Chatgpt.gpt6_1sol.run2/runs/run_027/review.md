# A priori review: Philornis downsi host responses

This assessment uses ecological, parasitological, behavioral and antibody studies, plus general physiology. It does not use genomic, transcriptomic or gene-expression findings. Search results referring to those approaches were excluded from the evidence used for scoring.

## Damage and physiological predictions

Early larvae inhabit the nares and other host tissues; later larvae shelter in nest material and feed externally on nestling blood. Nasal tissue destruction, substantial blood loss and nestling mortality therefore provide the principal physiological anchors. Fessl, Sinclair & Kleindorfer (2006), *The life-cycle of Philornis downsi ... and its impacts on nestling survival*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16899139/ ; https://doi.org/10.1017/S0031182006001089 .

Blood replacement, iron homeostasis, hemostasis and responses to reduced oxygen delivery are strong a priori predictions from blood feeding, rather than claims that every such molecular mechanism has been measured. Tissue repair, epithelial integrity, extracellular matrix remodeling and vascular repair are similarly strong or supporting predictions from feeding wounds. Generic development, transcription and cell division do not automatically become specific defenses.

Nasal damage can persist and affect vocal performance and pairing. This supports possible sensory, behavioral and tissue-integrity consequences, but does not make all neural or craniofacial developmental processes directly implicated. Kleindorfer and colleagues (2019), *Introduced parasite changes host phenotype, mating signal and hybridization risk*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6571467/ .

## Resistance

Experimental reduction of flies demonstrated parasite-associated antibody responses in adult female medium ground finches. A relationship with parasite abundance did not establish improved reproductive success. Nestling and male responses were not demonstrated in that experiment. Thus adaptive immunity is directly documented, its cellular machinery is strongly plausible, and particular inflammatory signaling pathways are generally predictions rather than directly demonstrated defenses. Koop et al. (2013), *Experimental demonstration of a parasite-induced immune response in wild birds*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3930052/ ; https://doi.org/10.1002/ece3.651 .

Preening, removing or consuming larvae, and repositioning within nests have been observed, though responses are limited and age dependent. Nest placement and breeding timing are proposed avoidance routes; some evidence in the broader Philornis literature comes from other hosts or species of fly. McNew & Clayton (2018), *Alien Invasion: Biology of Philornis Flies Highlighting Philornis downsi*: https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-ento-020117-043103 . Pike et al., *Behavior of the Avian Parasite Philornis downsi ... in and Near Host Nests*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8813692/ .

Finches also apply Psidium galapageium leaves to plumage; experiments assessed antiparasitic effects, supporting a proposed self-medication route without establishing universal field protection. *Darwin's finches treat their feathers with a natural repellent* (2016): https://www.nature.com/articles/srep34559 .

## Tolerance

Mockingbird nestlings can increase begging, associated with greater parental provisioning, and survive parasite burdens that harm finches. The comparison did not establish a significant antibody response in either species and did not show equivalent food compensation in finches. Knutie et al. (2016), *Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate introduced parasites that affect Darwin's finches*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27220210/ ; https://doi.org/10.1890/15-0119.1 .

Tolerance varies with environmental conditions and is reduced during resource limitation, making nutrient sensing, energy balance and supporting metabolism plausible mechanisms. This does not establish any particular metabolic pathway as a measured defense. *Annual environmental variation influences host tolerance to parasites* (2019): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6408884/ .

## Scoring interpretation

Each supplied term was judged individually. Scores distinguish documented host responses (5), core machinery or strong physiological predictions (4), supporting processes (3), weak generic connections (2), and processes without a specific link (1). Resistance and tolerance include regulatory restraint as well as activation; a negative regulator can therefore remain relevant. Antiviral specialization, reproductive machinery and unrelated embryonic organ patterning were not promoted simply because parasitism affects fitness. No target score distribution was imposed. GO:0160240 was supplied without an informative name and no identity was verified in retrieval; it is retained exactly and conservatively scored 1 rather than assigned an invented mechanism.

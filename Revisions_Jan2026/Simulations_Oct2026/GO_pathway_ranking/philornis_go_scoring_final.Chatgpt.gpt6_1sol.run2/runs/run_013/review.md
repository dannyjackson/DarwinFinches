# Independent physiological relevance review

## Evidence and scoring basis

Adult Philornis downsi are free-living; their larvae parasitize nestlings and sometimes brooding females. Early instars feed inside nares and other tissues; later larvae shelter in nest material and take blood externally, particularly at night. Documented consequences include tissue destruction, wounds, substantial blood loss, anemia, reduced hemoglobin, impaired growth, and nestling mortality. Surviving birds can retain enlarged nares and altered beak function. These observations support strong a priori links to erythropoiesis, iron handling, oxygen limitation, hemostasis, epithelial protection and repair, extracellular matrix remodeling, and vascular repair. These specific compensatory processes are predictions from observed injury unless directly studied; they are not all demonstrated defenses.

Immune resistance is possible but varies with host and age. Koop et al. experimentally found elevated fly-binding antibodies in parasitized medium ground finch mothers, with a suggestive inverse association with parasite numbers; males and nestlings lacked a comparable induced response. Maternal antibodies did not prevent brood failure. Knutie et al. subsequently found no significant induced antibody response in either host species in their comparison. Accordingly, adaptive immunity and B-cell machinery are relevant, but effective generalized immunity cannot be presumed. Innate inflammation, leukocyte recruitment, phagocytosis, complement, cytokine signaling, and defense against wound-associated bacteria are mechanistic predictions or supporting processes, rather than established fly-specific pathways. Virus-specific mechanisms have only weak indirect relevance.

Behavioral responses include preening, occasional larval removal, and nestling avoidance movements, with uncertain efficacy. Mockingbird tolerance is associated with increased nestling begging and parental provisioning, whereas medium ground finches did not show equivalent compensation in the comparative experiment. Multi-year work linked reduced tolerance during drought to reduced provisioning. This supports energy balance, nutrient sensing, growth maintenance, and metabolic compensation. Neural, sensory, learning, and muscle processes can support these behaviors, but most narrowly defined synaptic or neurodevelopmental terms have only indirect relevance.

Finches also anoint feathers with Psidium galapageium leaves; laboratory extracts repel adult flies and inhibit larval growth, supporting proposed behavioral self-medication without establishing universal protection. A corticosterone manipulation study found no parasite-treatment effect on adult female baseline or stress-induced corticosterone, so endocrine stress is not treated as a proven mechanism.

Scores use the requested scale, assessed individually without a target distribution. Directly encompassing documented responses receive 5; core machinery or strong injury-based predictions receive 4; plausible supporting processes receive 3; weak links receive 2; processes lacking a specific connection receive 1. Generic housekeeping is not upgraded solely because it is required for life. Developmental terms are distinguished by tissue and timing. Regulation in either direction may be relevant without implying that activation or suppression is beneficial. No genomic, transcriptomic, gene-expression, epigenetic, or selection-study results were used; incidental search hits in those categories were excluded.

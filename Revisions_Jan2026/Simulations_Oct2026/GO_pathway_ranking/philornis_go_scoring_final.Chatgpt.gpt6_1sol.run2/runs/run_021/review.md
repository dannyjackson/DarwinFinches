# A priori physiological review: Philornis downsi

This assessment uses published natural-history, experimental parasitology, physiological and behavioral evidence, plus general biology. It does not use genomic, transcriptomic or gene-expression results from Galápagos birds. Each GO term was judged individually; scores do not represent evidence of selection or a desired score distribution.

## Parasite injury and host compensation

Adult flies are free-living. Early larvae inhabit nestling nostrils and other tissues; later larvae shelter in nest material and feed externally, mainly at night. Blood feeding and tissue consumption cause anemia, wounds, impaired growth, substantial mortality and persistent nasal deformity. These observations establish a strong prior for blood-cell replacement, iron availability, oxygen delivery, hemostasis, epithelial barriers and tissue repair. The distinction matters: documented injury supports repair as a strong prediction, but does not establish that every repair pathway has been experimentally demonstrated to respond.

Dudaniec and colleagues found lower hemoglobin and increased immature erythrocytes with greater infestation, providing particularly direct support for erythropoiesis and hematopoietic compensation. Hypoxia responses, circulatory adjustment and energetic support are strong or supporting physiological predictions; specific intracellular housekeeping processes receive lower scores when no particular connection follows.

## Resistance, tolerance and behavior

An experiment found increased fly-binding antibodies in parasitized medium ground finch mothers, with an association suggesting possible resistance. The response did not rescue reproductive success; fathers and nestlings lacked corresponding induced responses. Another comparison found no significant antibody response in finches or mockingbirds. Thus adaptive/humoral immunity has direct support, while B-cell function and immune regulation are strong mechanistic priors. Innate recognition, inflammation, leukocyte recruitment, phagocytosis and complement are biologically credible predictions for ectoparasitic injury, not claims of demonstrated effective Philornis resistance. Virus-specific defenses and unrelated pathogen-specific processes have much weaker links.

Mockingbird nestlings increased begging in parasitized nests, associated with greater parental provisioning and maintained reproductive performance. This supports nutritional and energetic compensation as tolerance. Comparable compensation was not established in medium ground finches. Nutrient sensing, glucose handling, protein and lipid use, growth support and recovery are relevant with specificity-dependent scores.

Video observations document larval removal or consumption, preening, scratching, agitation and repositioning, with behavior limited by nestling age. Brooding disruption may also impose thermoregulatory costs. Sensory, motor, learning and circadian processes can support these responses, but generic neural development is indirect. Incorporation of experimentally supplied insecticide-treated cotton shows that nest-building behavior can deliver protection; it does not demonstrate evolved autonomous recognition of an insecticide or justify high scores for general xenobiotic pathways. Nasal damage can alter later song, supporting limited downstream sensory/communication links rather than universal relevance of neural pathways.

## Scoring interpretation

5 denotes a process directly encompassing a documented host response; 4 a core component or strong prediction; 3 supporting or downstream plausibility; 2 a weak or nonspecific connection; 1 no specific connection. Oppositely directed regulatory terms were assessed for possible modulation, rather than assuming increased activity is always beneficial. Embryonic patterning, gametogenesis, generic nucleic-acid processing and unrelated specialized processes were not elevated merely because all living cells require them. Platelet terms were treated cautiously because birds have nucleated thrombocytes. GO:0160240 was identified as RNA polymerase II transcription initiation surveillance using the GO report below; its supplied identifier was preserved.

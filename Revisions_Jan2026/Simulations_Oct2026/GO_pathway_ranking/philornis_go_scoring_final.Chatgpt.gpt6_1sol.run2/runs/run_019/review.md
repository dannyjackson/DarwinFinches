# Independent a priori literature review

## Host injury and compensation

Philornis downsi adults are free living; larvae are parasitic. Early larvae occupy nestling nostrils and sometimes other tissues, while older larvae shelter in nest material and feed externally on nestling blood. Tissue feeding and blood loss cause wounds, reduced haemoglobin, impaired development and substantial nestling mortality. Naris damage can persist and affect song. These observations strongly motivate host processes for erythrocyte replacement, iron handling, oxygen-stress compensation, haemostasis, epithelial repair and tissue maintenance. Except for the documented haematological response, these cellular mechanisms are predictions from injury, rather than experimentally demonstrated molecular defenses.

Dudaniec and colleagues found that immature red blood cells increased with parasite intensity and were negatively associated with haemoglobin. Erythrocyte differentiation therefore has particularly direct support. Bleeding and oxygen limitation support coagulation and hypoxia responses, but do not establish that every associated signaling pathway is specifically deployed against the fly.

## Resistance, tolerance and behavior

Koop and colleagues experimentally demonstrated increased parasite-binding antibodies in parasitized adult female medium ground finches. Males and nestlings did not show the same detectable induction. The association between female antibody level and reduced parasite abundance was marginal, and immune activation did not establish effective nestling protection. Adaptive immunity receives direct support; B-cell functions are core components. Innate recognition, inflammatory control, cytokines, leukocyte recruitment and phagocytosis are biologically reasonable predictions for an ectoparasite that breaches tissue, but specific cytokine pathways have less direct support. Antiviral mechanisms are not automatically antiparasite mechanisms; bacterial defenses are plausible secondary support for damaged barriers, without assuming demonstrated secondary infection.

Knutie and colleagues found that Galápagos mockingbirds could tolerate infestation through increased nestling begging associated with increased parental provisioning. Finches did not show comparable compensation in that experiment. McNew and colleagues showed that mockingbird tolerance varied with rainfall and food conditions. Thus energy balance, nutrient availability and growth maintenance are relevant, while particular metabolic enzymes or general cellular machinery warrant lower scores than the compensatory mechanism itself.

Observed or proposed behavioral defenses include preening, larva removal or consumption, agitation and positioning above nestmates. Their effectiveness is limited or unproven. Adult finches also alter brooding posture. Avoidance through nest-site choice or breeding timing is plausible, although evidence varies among Philornis hosts. Insecticide-treated material collected by finches demonstrates a potential management-assisted nest defense, not an intrinsic physiological insecticide response. General neural development, synaptic functions, sensory functions and circadian processes consequently receive restrained scores unless a specific connection is evident.

## Scoring interpretation

Each supplied term was judged separately without imposing score proportions. Directly observed adaptive immune and erythrocyte responses received 5; core components or strong predictions, including wound repair and haemostasis, received 4. Supporting metabolism, growth, stress responses and less specific signaling generally received 3. Remote links received 2; processes without a specific connection, including much reproductive, embryonic and basal information-processing machinery, received 1. Regulation in either direction can be relevant: scores represent potential association, not an assertion that increasing the process is beneficial. Avian thrombocytes provide the functional connection for platelet-related haemostasis terms. The input labels GO:0160240 only with its identifier; its identity could not be established from the review search, so it received a conservative 1 without renaming it.

No genomic, transcriptomic, gene-expression or epigenomic results from Galápagos birds were used. A genomic/epigenetic result surfaced incidentally in search and was excluded. Only the designated input file was read locally.

# A priori physiological relevance review

The scoring uses published natural-history, experimental parasitology, immunology, and behavioral evidence, together with general biological knowledge. No genomic, transcriptomic, gene-expression, or selection results from Galapagos birds were used. Every input term was considered independently; scores express physiological relevance rather than evidence that a particular gene is selected or that the response is effective.

## Mechanisms and evidence

- **Blood loss and compensatory blood production.** Early larvae occupy the nares and other tissues; later larvae usually shelter in nest material and feed on nestlings, especially at night. Blood feeding and tissue injury can cause severe anemia, impaired growth, and death. Increased immature erythrocyte counts with parasite intensity support a compensatory erythropoietic response. Thus erythrocyte differentiation and hematopoiesis have direct relevance, while iron homeostasis, oxygen-stress responses, cardiovascular compensation, and energy metabolism are strong predictions or supporting mechanisms. Sources: Fessl, Sinclair & Kleindorfer (2006), *The life-cycle of Philornis downsi...*, https://doi.org/10.1017/S0031182006001089 ; Dudaniec, Kleindorfer & Fessl (2006), *Effects ... on haemoglobin level and nestling survival ...*, https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1442-9993.2006.01553.x .

- **Tissue damage and resistance/tolerance at feeding sites.** Feeding damages skin and nasal tissues and can produce persistent naris/beak deformities. Wound repair, epithelial integrity, extracellular matrix remodeling, hemostasis, and repair-associated migration/proliferation are therefore relevant predictions. These repair pathways are inferred from documented injury rather than individually demonstrated molecular responses. Inflammation and host defensive molecules have also been proposed to impair ectoparasite feeding. Source: McNew & Clayton (2018), *Alien Invasion: Biology of Philornis Flies Highlighting Philornis downsi, an Introduced Parasite of Galapagos Birds*, https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-ento-020117-043103 .

- **Immune resistance with important limits.** Experimentally parasitized medium ground finch mothers developed greater P. downsi-binding antibody levels; stronger responses tended to accompany fewer parasites. Nestlings and males did not show the same induced response, and maternal antibodies did not prevent complete reproductive failure in that experiment. This directly supports adaptive immunity and makes B-cell mechanisms core predictions; innate immune activation and inflammatory regulation are plausible cooperating mechanisms. It does not establish universal protection or every immune pathway as equally relevant. Source: Koop et al. (2013), *Experimental demonstration of a parasite-induced immune response in wild birds*, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3930052/ .

- **Behavioral resistance and avoidance.** Preening, occasional larval removal/consumption, nest sanitation, nestling agitation or repositioning, and altered maternal brooding have been observed, but responses are inconsistent and constrained by nestling motor development. Reduced brooding could additionally impair nestling thermal balance. Feather treatment with Psidium leaves is a proposed antiparasite behavior supported by repellency experiments, without proof of effective parasite control by wild birds. Insecticide-treated nesting material provides experimental evidence that nest-material choice can reduce parasite abundance. Sources: McNew & Clayton (2018), above; Cimadom et al. (2016), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5056383/ ; Knutie et al. (2014), https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2014.03.058 .

- **Energetic tolerance and parental compensation.** Galapagos mockingbirds tolerated parasitism better than medium ground finches in a manipulation experiment. Greater nestling begging was associated with increased parental feeding, suggesting compensation for damage; finches lacked a comparable response in that study. Nutrient use, energy homeostasis, growth, sensory/behavioral integration, and resource allocation can consequently support tolerance. Source: Knutie et al. (2016), *Galapagos mockingbirds tolerate introduced parasites that affect Darwin's finches*, https://doi.org/10.1890/15-0119.1 .

## Scoring interpretation

Scores of 5 identify directly captured documented host responses; 4 denotes core components or strong predictions; 3 denotes plausible supporting/downstream processes; 2 denotes weak, indirect, or speculative connections; 1 denotes no specific connection. Generic cellular machinery was not promoted solely because immunity or repair requires living cells. Development of unrelated embryonic organs, meiosis, and many housekeeping nuclear processes lack a specific link. Antiviral terms do not imply that the fly is a demonstrated virus vector; antibacterial terms generally reflect possible consequences of damaged barriers. Platelet terms receive only supporting relevance because birds use nucleated thrombocytes.

The input supplied GO:0160240 without a term name. Its official GO definition was checked as RNA polymerase II transcription initiation surveillance (https://amigo.geneontology.org/amigo/term/GO%3A0160240), assigned low relevance, and its original GO ID was preserved.

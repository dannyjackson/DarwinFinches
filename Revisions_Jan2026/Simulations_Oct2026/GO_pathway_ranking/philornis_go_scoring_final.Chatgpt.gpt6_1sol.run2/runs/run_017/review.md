# Independent a priori mechanism review

Scores reflect physiological relevance, not evidence that any particular GO term or gene has evolved in response to the fly. No genomic, transcriptomic, or gene-expression results from Galápagos birds were used. The only local source read was this run's input_terms.txt. Terms were retained exactly by GO ID; the unexpanded GO:0160240 entry was identified through the GO public resource as RNA polymerase II transcription initiation surveillance and treated as nonspecific housekeeping.

## Host injury and candidate physiological responses

First and early second instars inhabit nostrils and other host tissues; older larvae live in nest material and feed externally, often at night. Tissue feeding and blood extraction cause open lesions, swelling, bleeding, enlarged/deformed nares, reduced hemoglobin and impaired growth and survival. These observations make wound responses, skin/epithelial integrity and repair, hemostasis, erythrocyte replacement, iron handling, oxygen limitation and energy compensation relevant a priori. Detailed inflammatory cascades, vascular repair and oxidative-stress protection are predictions from these injuries rather than mechanisms individually demonstrated against this parasite. Birds have nucleated thrombocytes rather than mammalian platelets: platelet-named GO terms receive relevance for their conserved hemostatic function, without assuming avian platelet biology.

- Fessl, Sinclair & Kleindorfer (2006), *The life-cycle of Philornis downsi ... and its impacts on nestling survival*: primary evidence for the developmental shift in larval feeding sites and severe blood loss. https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/parasitology/article/abs/lifecycle-of-philornis-downsi-diptera-muscidae-parasitizing-darwins-finches-and-its-impacts-on-nestling-survival/597D469905598A7105BBE59990009B32
- Kleindorfer & Dudaniec (2016), *Host-parasite ecology, behavior and genetics*: the injury, natural-history and behavioral sections synthesize lesions, anemia, growth deficits and persistent naris damage. Genetic findings were excluded from scoring. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s40850-016-0003-9

## Resistance and limits of immune protection

Koop and colleagues experimentally found higher fly-binding antibody levels in mothers at parasitized nests. An inverse antibody–parasite association suggested possible defense, but was marginal, and did not rescue reproductive success. Males and nestlings did not show the same induced response. Thus adaptive/antibody immunity is directly relevant, while B-cell activation/differentiation, T-cell help and regulation are core predicted components. Innate recognition, complement, leukocyte recruitment, cytokines and inflammation are biologically credible predictions; specific antiviral programs or intracellular symbiont entry are much less directly connected to this extracellular larval parasite.

- Koop et al. (2013), *Experimental demonstration of a parasite-induced immune response in wild birds*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24567824/

## Tolerance, nutrition and behavior

Mockingbirds can tolerate parasite burdens through enhanced begging associated with increased parental food provisioning. Comparable compensation was absent in medium ground finches in the same comparison. This makes energy homeostasis directly relevant and nutrient sensing, mobilization, assimilation and growth plausible supporting processes. Tolerance is environmentally contingent: a four-season manipulation found lower tolerance during limited rainfall, consistent with resource constraints. These findings do not establish that every metabolic process or insulin pathway is a fly-specific mechanism.

- Knutie et al. (2016), *Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate introduced parasites that affect Darwin's finches*: https://esajournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1890/15-0119.1
- McNew et al. (2019), *Annual environmental variation influences host tolerance to parasites*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30963843/

Preening, eating larvae and altered nestling positions are documented behavioral responses. Subsequent observations show larval dislodging by flapping and preening, but removal success is uncertain. Sensory perception, pain, learning, motor activity and circadian coordination therefore have possible supporting roles; generic synaptic machinery or embryonic neural development has a weaker connection. Nest attendance and avoidance, nest sanitation and nest-material choices are potential behavioral defenses, not grounds to elevate all developmental or sensory terms.

- O'Connor et al. (2010), *Video analysis of host–parasite interactions in nests of Darwin's finches*: https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/oryx/article/video-analysis-of-hostparasite-interactions-in-nests-of-darwins-finches/BF956C9751763DAFAEE36345498A4A01
- Pike et al. (2021), *Behavior of the Avian Parasite Philornis downsi ... in and Near Host Nests*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8813692/

A test in breeding female medium ground finches found no detectable parasite-treatment effect on baseline or stress-induced corticosterone, condition or hematocrit. Hormonal stress is consequently a plausible, context-dependent supporting hypothesis, not an established universal defense.

- Koop et al. (2013), *Experimental test of the effect of introduced hematophagous flies on corticosterone levels of breeding Darwin's finches*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23892015/

## Scoring application

I evaluated terms for documented mechanisms (5), core components or strong predictions (4), supporting physiology (3), weak indirect links (2), or no specific link (1). Broad signaling, cellular trafficking and neuronal processes generally have weak links; constitutive transcription/RNA processing and particular reproductive or embryonic patterning processes generally have no specific link. Directional regulation was assessed for possible tuning of defense and damage limitation, not assumed beneficial merely because it increases a response. Ratings do not predict selection direction or require a particular score distribution.

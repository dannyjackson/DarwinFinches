# Independent a priori mechanism review

## Scope and interpretation

Scores concern biological relevance to a potential avian host response, not evidence of selection, expression changes, or demonstrated protection. Only the supplied term list was read locally. No genomic, transcriptomic, or gene-expression results from Galapagos birds were used. Each listed term was considered individually; broadly necessary cellular machinery was not promoted merely because immunity requires living cells. No target score distribution was imposed.

## Mechanisms supported by the literature

**Blood feeding, anemia, and tissue injury.** Early Philornis downsi larvae inhabit nestling nares and other tissues; later larvae shelter in nest material and feed externally on blood. Tissue destruction and anemia together can kill nestlings. Persistent naris/beak deformation and impaired growth are additional consequences. These observations make erythrocyte replacement, hematopoiesis, iron balance, oxygen-shortage responses, hemostasis, epithelial repair, and connective-tissue repair strong candidates. Regenerative immature-red-cell responses have actually been observed. Specific coagulation cascades, angiogenic pathways, and wound-repair signaling remain physiological predictions rather than demonstrated fly-specific molecular defenses.

- Fessl, Sinclair & Kleindorfer (2006), *The life-cycle of Philornis downsi ... and its impacts on nestling survival*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16899139/ ; https://doi.org/10.1017/S0031182006001089
- Dudaniec, Kleindorfer & Fessl (2006), *Effects ... on haemoglobin level and nestling survival*: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1442-9993.2006.01553.x ; https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/effects-of-the-introduced-ectoparasite-philornis-downsi-on-haemog/
- *Timing of infestation influences virulence and parasite success ...* (2021): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7882474/

**Immunological resistance is possible but variable.** Experimental parasite removal demonstrated higher fly-binding antibodies in parasitized medium-ground-finch mothers. Higher maternal antibodies were associated with fewer parasites, but this did not rescue reproductive success; males and nestlings lacked equivalent induced responses. A later mockingbird/finch comparison did not detect significant antibody responses in either species. Consequently, adaptive immunity and its B-cell machinery have a clear a priori connection, without assuming universal protective immunity. Skin inflammation, pruritus, and ingested immune effectors are proposed routes of resistance in the literature. Innate immune regulation, cytokines, leukocyte recruitment, and complement are plausible, with the exact molecular pathway generally less directly established. Intracellular infection and virus-specific pathways have no equivalent fly-specific support. Bacterial defense is a possible consequence of damaged barriers, not evidence that P. downsi transmits a particular pathogen.

- Koop et al. (2013), *Experimental demonstration of a parasite-induced immune response in wild birds*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3930052/
- Knutie et al. (2016), *Galapagos mockingbirds tolerate introduced parasites that affect Darwin's finches*: https://doi.org/10.1890/15-0119.1 ; https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27220210/

**Tolerance and energetic compensation.** Mockingbird nestlings increased begging and parents increased provisioning in response to parasitism; corresponding compensation was not evident in medium ground finches. Tolerance varied across years and declined under drought/resource limitation. Nutrient availability, energy homeostasis, metabolic substrate use, body growth, and cardiovascular support therefore have plausible roles. This does not establish every metabolic or developmental pathway as a direct antiparasite mechanism.

- Knutie et al. (2016), above.
- *Annual environmental variation influences host tolerance to parasites* (2019): https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30963843/

**Behavioral defenses.** Preening, attempts to remove larvae, and positioning away from larvae have been reported or proposed, often without sufficient efficacy. Finch anointing with Psidium galapageium leaves is documented, and extracts repel adult flies experimentally; a protective effect of natural anointing against nestling infestation remains unproven. Incorporating experimentally supplied insecticide-treated cotton can suppress parasites, demonstrating a usable nesting behavior rather than innate pesticide production. Sensation, learning, and motor activity are supporting possibilities; generic synaptic or neural developmental terms have substantially weaker specificity.

- Cimadom et al. (2016), *Darwin's finches treat their feathers with a natural repellent*: https://www.nature.com/articles/srep34559
- Knutie et al. (2014), *Darwin's finches combat introduced nest parasites with fumigated cotton*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24801182/

**Stress is not automatically a demonstrated defense.** An experiment found no treatment-associated difference in baseline or acute stress-induced corticosterone in breeding females; nestling and resource-dependent effects remain possible. General stress, oxidative balance, and endocrine/metabolic regulation were therefore treated as supporting or indirect connections rather than proven fly-specific mechanisms.

- *Experimental test of the effect of introduced hematophagous flies on corticosterone levels of breeding Darwin's finches* (2013): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4383185/

## Applying the scale

Score 5 was reserved for processes directly encompassing observed immune or blood-cell responses and responses to injury. Score 4 marks strong physiological predictions or core machinery of those mechanisms. Score 3 covers meaningful supporting routes, including energetics, repair signaling, and inflammatory mediators whose particular involvement is not established. Score 2 marks weak/general links, including most housekeeping, trafficking, and nonspecific signaling. Score 1 marks processes with no specific supported connection, especially reproduction, unrelated embryonic organogenesis, specialized neural development, and narrowly specified nucleic-acid machinery. Birds have thrombocytes rather than mammalian platelets; platelet terms were interpreted cautiously as functional hemostatic analogues, not literal proof of avian platelet biology. The unnamed input entry GO:0160240 was retained exactly and received a conservative score; no term was renamed or omitted.

# Independent a priori review: Philornis downsi host responses

## Evidence basis

Scores reflect a review of ecological, behavioral, parasitological and immunological literature plus general biological knowledge. No genomic, transcriptomic or gene-expression results were used. A broad search incidentally returned an excluded gene-expression article; none of its findings contributed to these judgments. Only the designated input file was examined on disk. GO:0160240 was supplied without a descriptive name; the Gene Ontology report identifies it as RNA polymerase II transcription initiation surveillance (https://release.geneontology.org/2026-06-19/reports/go-cam-stats/go-cam-unique-go-bp-terms.html). Its original ID was retained.

## Host damage and candidate responses

**Blood loss and injury.** First and early second instar larvae occupy nares and other tissues; older larvae shelter in nest material and feed externally, especially at night. Tissue consumption and repeated feeding cause wounds, nasal damage, anemia, reduced hemoglobin, impaired growth and nestling death. This supplies strong a priori reasons for blood coagulation, erythrocyte replacement, hematopoiesis, iron homeostasis, oxygen-stress responses and repair of epithelial barriers. Those compensatory pathways are physiological predictions; they should not be mistaken for direct experimental demonstrations of each molecular mechanism. Fessl, Sinclair and Kleindorfer (2006), *The life-cycle of Philornis downsi ... and its impacts on nestling survival*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16899139/ . Experimental parasite reduction also supports effects on mass gain and fledging: Fessl, Kleindorfer and Tebbich (2006), https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0006320705002983 .

**Immune resistance, with important limits.** An experimental manipulation detected higher fly-binding antibodies in mothers at parasitized nests, and a tendency toward fewer parasites where maternal antibodies were higher. Nestlings and males did not show that response; the mothers’ response did not rescue current reproduction. Adaptive immunity is therefore documented, but effective protection is neither universal nor established for every host class. B-cell processes are core components; innate recognition, inflammatory regulation, leukocyte recruitment and complement are plausible defenses against injury or feeding, rather than established fly-specific molecular responses. Koop et al., *Experimental demonstration of a parasite-induced immune response in wild birds: Darwin’s finches and introduced nest flies*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24567824/ . Wound-associated infection makes antibacterial defense relevant, but the fly’s identity does not itself justify high antiviral scores.

**Behavioral resistance and avoidance.** Nest cameras recorded self-preening, larval consumption, nestlings standing on one another, and females probing nares and nest material. These observations connect sensory detection, pain, movement, behavioral flexibility and parental care to potential defense. They do not justify assigning high scores indiscriminately to synaptic or embryonic neural-development terms. Brooding changes can also reduce nestling thermoregulatory protection. O’Connor, Robertson and Kleindorfer (2010), *Video analysis of host–parasite interactions in nests of Darwin’s finches*: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0030605310000086 . Behavioral work in Galápagos flycatchers adds another passerine context: Pike et al. (2021), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8813692/ .

**Tolerance through energy compensation.** Experimental comparisons found that mockingbirds could maintain reproductive output despite parasite densities comparable to those affecting finches. Parasitized mockingbird chicks begged more, associated with greater parental provisioning; finches did not show equivalent compensation in that experiment. Neither host showed a significant antibody response in that comparison. Thus feeding, energy balance, nutrient use and growth can contribute to tolerance without reducing parasite abundance. Knutie et al. (2016), *Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate introduced parasites that affect Darwin’s finches*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27220210/ . Mockingbird tolerance varied across years and weakened under limited rainfall, consistent with a resource-dependent mechanism: Knutie et al. (2019), *Annual environmental variation influences host tolerance to parasites*, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30963843/ .

**Other proposed defenses and persistent effects.** Finches incorporate experimentally supplied insecticide-treated cotton into nests, demonstrating a usable behavioral route for parasite reduction; this does not establish endogenous insecticide metabolism as a natural defense. Knutie et al. (2014), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24801182/ . Leaf rubbing as possible self-medication is discussed in McNew and Clayton’s review, *Alien Invasion: Biology of Philornis Flies Highlighting Philornis downsi, an Introduced Parasite of Galápagos Birds*, https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-ento-020117-043103 . Nasal deformation can have lasting effects on song and foraging, supporting repair and craniofacial integrity more directly than unrelated organ patterning: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6571467/ and https://doi.org/10.1111/1365-2435.14061 .

**Negative evidence for a simple stress explanation.** An experiment found no treatment difference in maternal baseline/acute corticosterone response, condition or hematocrit. Endocrine stress remains biologically plausible but cannot be treated as a demonstrated universal response: Knutie et al. (2013), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23892015/ .

## Scoring interpretation

Each term was assessed for its own physiological content and specificity. The highest category was reserved for terms directly representing documented immune defense. Core immune components and strong predictions from feeding injury, anemia and tissue repair receive 4. Supporting metabolic, redox, cell-survival, repair-signaling and energetic processes generally receive 3. Ubiquitous cellular machinery, broad signaling and remote behavioral substrates usually receive 2, since a functioning cell alone is insufficient evidence of a fly-specific association. Processes with no identifiable specific connection, including reproduction-specific cell biology and many narrowly embryonic organ-patterning events, receive 1. Scores express relevance, not evidence that a pathway is beneficial, upregulated, selected, or causally protective. Both positive and negative regulation can be relevant, depending on physiological context. No target score distribution was imposed.

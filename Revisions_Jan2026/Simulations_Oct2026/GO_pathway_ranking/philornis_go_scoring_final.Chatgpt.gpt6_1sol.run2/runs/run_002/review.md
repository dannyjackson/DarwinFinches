# Independent a priori physiological relevance review

## Scope and interpretation

I reviewed non-genomic literature on Philornis downsi effects and host defenses, then judged all 1,008 supplied biological processes individually. I did not use genomic, transcriptomic, gene-expression, or selection results from Galapagos birds. Search results outside this scope were not used. Scores describe potential physiological relevance, not evidence that a particular gene is selected or that a response is beneficial. There was no target score distribution.

## Mechanisms and supporting literature

**Blood loss, anemia, and compensatory blood production.** Early larvae occupy nostrils and other tissues; later larvae shelter in nest material and feed on nestling blood, predominantly at night. Tissue destruction and substantial blood loss cause nestling morbidity and mortality. Reduced hemoglobin and increased immature erythrocytes associated with parasite intensity provide direct evidence for anemia and compensatory erythropoiesis. These observations support strong prior relevance for erythrocyte differentiation, hematopoiesis, iron homeostasis, and hypoxia responses. Oxygen delivery and circulatory compensation are strongly predicted; generic mitochondrial and energy processes are supporting mechanisms rather than direct parasite-specific responses.

Sources:
- Fessl, Sinclair & Kleindorfer (2006), *The life-cycle of Philornis downsi ... and its impacts on nestling survival*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16899139/
- Dudaniec, Kleindorfer & Fessl (2006), *Effects ... on haemoglobin level and nestling survival ...*: https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1442-9993.2006.01553.x

**Tissue injury, barrier maintenance, and repair.** Myiasis damages nares and skin and leaves feeding lesions. Wounding responses therefore have direct relevance. Hemostasis, epithelial renewal and migration, extracellular matrix/collagen organization, fibroblast proliferation, and angiogenesis are strong physiological predictions from those injuries. This does not establish that each molecular repair pathway has been measured in these birds. Secondary bacterial defense is plausible after barrier disruption, but is less specifically supported than injury itself. Avian thrombocytes perform hemostatic functions; platelet-specific labels received less weight than general coagulation because birds do not have mammalian platelets.

Source:
- Koop et al. (2011), *Experimental Demonstration of the Fitness Consequences of an Introduced Parasite of Darwin's Finches*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3092749/

**Immune resistance, with important limits.** An experimental study found increased fly-binding antibodies in parasitized female medium ground finches, but not males or nestlings. Higher maternal antibody levels tended to accompany fewer parasites, without rescuing reproductive success. A later host comparison did not detect a significant antibody response in either species. Consequently, adaptive immunity and B-cell machinery have strong relevance, while cytokine signaling, complement, leukocyte recruitment, and inflammatory control are mechanistic predictions rather than demonstrated fly-specific pathways. Virus-specific processes were not elevated merely because they are immune related.

Sources:
- Koop et al. (2013), *Experimental demonstration of a parasite-induced immune response in wild birds*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3930052/
- Knutie et al. (2016), *Galapagos mockingbirds tolerate introduced parasites that affect Darwin's finches*: https://doi.org/10.1890/15-0119.1

**Nutritional and behavioral tolerance.** Experimentally parasitized mockingbirds increased nestling begging and parental provisioning, apparently compensating for damage; medium ground finches did not show those compensatory changes. Tolerance varied among years and declined under low rainfall. These findings support nutrient sensing, energy homeostasis, growth maintenance, starvation responses, and metabolic reserve use. They do not make every biosynthetic process a direct defense.

Source:
- *Annual environmental variation influences host tolerance to parasites* (2019): https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30963843/
- Knutie et al. (2016), above.

**Behavioral resistance and avoidance.** Nest cameras documented grooming, larval consumption, probing of nestlings' nares and nest material, and positioning away from larvae. Flycatchers also removed larvae and captured adult flies. These observations support sensory detection, pain processing, coordinated movement, and potentially learning as supporting processes; highly specific synaptic assembly and embryonic nervous-system pathways have much weaker links.

Sources:
- O'Connor, Robertson & Kleindorfer (2010), *Video analysis of host-parasite interactions in nests of Darwin's finches*: https://doi.org/10.1017/S0030605310000086
- *Behavior of the Avian Parasite Philornis downsi ... in and Near Host Nests* (2021): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8813692/

**Proposed chemical protection and stress mechanisms.** Leaf-anointing occurs naturally, and Psidium extracts repelled adult flies and inhibited larval growth experimentally; a protective benefit of anointing against P. downsi in wild birds remains unproven. Treated-cotton self-fumigation demonstrates use of supplied nest materials, not evolved endogenous insecticide production. An experimental maternal corticosterone study found no parasite-induced increase, limiting a specific endocrine-stress explanation. Redox balance, autophagy, and proteostasis remain plausible supporting responses rather than directly demonstrated defenses.

Sources:
- Cimadom et al. (2016), *Darwin's finches treat their feathers with a natural repellent*: https://doi.org/10.1038/srep34559
- Knutie et al. (2014), *Darwin's finches combat introduced nest parasites with fumigated cotton*: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24801182/
- *Experimental test ... on corticosterone levels of breeding Darwin's finches* (2013): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4383185/

## Scoring boundaries

Directly matching documented response categories received 5; core machinery or strong predictions received 4; supporting physiology received 3; weak, generic, or speculative connections received 2; unrelated or highly specialized housekeeping/developmental functions received 1. Positive and negative regulation can both be relevant through response activation, restraint, or repair; direction alone did not determine the rating. Embryonic patterning, gametogenesis, and virus-specific activity were not equated with post-hatching injury tolerance.

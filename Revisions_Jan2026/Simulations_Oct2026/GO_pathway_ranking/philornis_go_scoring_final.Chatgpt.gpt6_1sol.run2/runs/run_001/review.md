# A priori physiological relationship to Philornis downsi

## Evidence and mechanisms

The free-living adult fly is not a blood-feeding parasite. Early larval instars feed within nestling nares and sometimes other cavities; later instars reside in nest material and feed, predominantly at night, on nestling blood and tissue. Brooding females are also exposed. Consequences include blood loss, reduced haemoglobin, impaired growth, wounds, persistent naris/beak deformation, and nestling mortality. These observations make blood replacement, haemostasis, oxygen delivery, tissue repair, and maintenance of growth relevant candidate responses. They do not establish that every developmental or metabolic pathway specifically responds to the fly. Fessl et al. (2006) describe the life cycle and impacts; Dudaniec et al. (2006) associate infestation with haemoglobin and nestling survival; experimental parasite reduction improves finch performance in Koop et al. (2011).

Resistance means reducing parasite burden. Koop et al. (2013) experimentally demonstrated increased P. downsi-binding antibodies in female medium ground finches at parasitized nests. Higher maternal antibody levels tended to accompany fewer larvae, but the response did not rescue reproductive success. Neither males nor nestlings showed equivalent induced responses in that experiment. The paper proposes antibody-assisted inflammation and interference with ectoparasite feeding. I therefore distinguish directly documented immune responses from predicted B-cell, cytokine, innate recognition, complement, leukocyte, and inflammatory components. Infection control at damaged skin is plausible but does not make antiviral or intracellular-pathogen pathways direct fly defenses.

Tolerance means limiting damage at a given burden. Knutie et al. (2016) found Galapagos mockingbirds more tolerant than medium ground finches; enhanced nestling begging correlated with parental provisioning. McNew et al. (2019) showed that mockingbird tolerance weakened in dry years, consistent with resource constraints on compensation. Nutrition, substrate mobilization, energy balance, oxygen supply, growth maintenance, and repair can therefore support tolerance. I do not equate generic metabolism or generic protein/RNA processing with demonstrated tolerance mechanisms.

Behavioral responses include preening, agitation or repositioning, avoiding the nest base, parental inspection/probing or removal of larvae, and altered brooding. Evidence varies by age and species; effective protection is often limited. The 2013 experiment did not establish enhanced protective behavior, particularly in very young nestlings. Reduced brooding may expose young to thermal stress. Cimadom et al. (2016) observed four finch species applying Psidium galapageium leaves to feathers; extracts repelled adult flies and inhibited larval growth, supporting a proposed self-medication defense rather than proving protection of naturally anointing birds. Sensory, motor, learning, and circadian processes are supporting possibilities; generic neuronal development or synapse maintenance has a much weaker specific connection.

Physiological stress and oxidative homeostasis are plausible downstream responses, not assumed demonstrated adaptations. Koop et al. (2013, corticosterone study) found no parasite-treatment difference in baseline or stress-induced corticosterone, adult female condition, or haematocrit. This negative evidence prevents treating broad endocrine-stress pathways as established mechanisms. Wound remodelling, angiogenesis, red-cell replacement, and iron homeostasis are strong biological predictions from the injuries and anaemia; specific cytokines and signalling cascades were not established as fly-specific pathways in these studies.

## Scoring decisions and independence

Scores measure a priori physiological association, not evidence that selection occurred, the direction of selection, or pathway effectiveness. Score 5 is reserved for terms directly capturing a documented host mechanism; 4 denotes core components or strong biological predictions; 3 denotes plausible downstream/supporting processes; 2 denotes weak or generic links; 1 denotes no specific relationship. An inhibition term can be relevant to regulation or damage limitation without being an automatically beneficial response. Avian haemostatic relevance of platelet-labelled terms was evaluated through thrombocyte physiology rather than assuming birds possess mammalian platelets. Embryonic patterning, meiosis, gamete function, and generic RNA processing were not promoted merely because a living host requires them.

Only this run's input_terms.txt was read on the machine. No other rater's files, project documents, memory sources, or genomic/transcriptomic results were used. An excluded gene-expression result surfaced incidentally during searching and was not used.

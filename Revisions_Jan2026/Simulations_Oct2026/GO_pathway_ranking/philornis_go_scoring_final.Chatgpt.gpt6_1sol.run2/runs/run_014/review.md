# Independent a priori review

Scoring uses non-omic literature on Philornis downsi and general physiological knowledge. No genomic, transcriptomic, or gene-expression findings were used. A prohibited expression study appeared among search results and was excluded in its entirety. No selection results were consulted. Scores represent potential association, not evidence that a process improves fitness or has evolved in these birds.

## Mechanisms and evidence

- **Blood feeding and tissue injury:** Early larvae inhabit nestling nares and other tissues; later larvae occupy nest material and feed externally. Blood loss, anemia, tissue destruction and nestling mortality give a direct physiological basis for blood-cell replacement and a strong prediction for hemostasis, wound repair, epithelial barriers, iron handling and hypoxia responses. Persistent naris/beak malformations demonstrate developmental damage, but do not make all embryonic or skeletal development specifically relevant. Sources: Fessl et al. (2006), *The life-cycle of Philornis downsi ... and its impacts on nestling survival*, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16899139/ ; Galligan and Kleindorfer (2009), *Naris and beak malformation ...*, https://academic.oup.com/biolinnean/article-abstract/98/3/577/2532299 .
- **Blood regeneration and growth costs:** Non-omic studies report lower hemoglobin, increased immature red-cell counts in anemic nestlings, and impaired mass gain/fledging. These support erythropoiesis as compensation, with nutrient utilization and energy metabolism as supporting mechanisms. Sources: Dudaniec et al. (2006), *Effects ... on haemoglobin level and nestling survival ...*, https://www.researchgate.net/publication/227817204_Effects_of_the_introduced_ectoparasitePhilornis_downsion_haemoglobin_level_and_nestling_survival_in_Darwin%27s_Small_Ground_Finch_Geospiza_fuliginosa ; Fessl et al. (2006), *An experimental study on the effects of an introduced parasite in Darwin's finches*, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0006320705002983 .
- **Immune resistance:** An experimental study found higher fly-binding antibodies in mothers at infested nests, with a tendency toward fewer parasites at higher antibody levels. Males and nestlings did not show comparable induced defenses, and maternal antibodies did not rescue fledging. Antibody/adaptive immunity is documented; inflammatory, complement, leukocyte and cytokine processes are mechanistic predictions rather than demonstrated effective resistance in every host. Source: Koop et al. (2013), https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3930052/ .
- **Behavioral and nutritional tolerance:** Parasitized mockingbird chicks increased begging, associated with increased parental provisioning and tolerance. Finches did not show equivalent compensation in that experiment. Thus energy balance and feeding-related physiology are relevant, while generic neural processes have weaker specificity. Source: Knutie et al. (2016), *Galapagos mockingbirds tolerate introduced parasites that affect Darwin's finches*, https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27220210/ .
- **Behavioral resistance and avoidance:** Preening/removal of larvae is reported but uncommon; nest positioning and breeding timing can influence exposure. Finches also rub Psidium leaves on feathers; extracts repel adult flies and inhibit larvae, although protection in naturally treated birds remains proposed. Source: Cimadom et al. (2016), https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27721475/ ; broader synthesis: McNew and Clayton (2018), https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-ento-020117-043103 .

## Scoring interpretation

Direct immune responses, blood regeneration and energy compensation receive the strongest scores. Hemostasis, repair, hypoxia adaptation and immune components are strong predictions; generic metabolism, growth and stress signaling usually support them less specifically. Housekeeping, unrelated embryonic structures and reproductive processes lack specific links. Antiviral pathways are not treated as direct defenses against a fly; antibacterial processes have a possible secondary wound-related role. Positive and negative regulation can both matter; direction alone does not establish benefit. Mammalian platelet terminology is interpreted cautiously in relation to avian thrombocyte/hemostatic functions.

# Literature basis and independent scoring rationale

Only the supplied run_011 input file was read locally. Scores use the natural-history, experimental parasitology and behavioral literature below, together with general vertebrate physiology. No host genomic, transcriptomic or gene-expression results were used. Each term was considered as a host process, not as a process in the fly. Scores describe potential mechanistic relevance rather than evidence that selection has occurred or that a defense is effective.

## Mechanisms

Early Philornis downsi larvae occupy nares and other tissues; older larvae live in nesting material and feed externally. Blood and tissue consumption causes anemia, lesions, nasal deformation, impaired growth and substantial nestling mortality. Thus erythrocyte replacement, iron management, oxygen limitation, hemostasis, epithelial repair and tissue regeneration are strong predictions. Actual increases in immature red cells support compensatory erythropoiesis. Tissue repair, angiogenesis, redox regulation and energetic metabolism are principally inferred supporting mechanisms, not demonstrated fly-specific molecular pathways.

A manipulated-parasite experiment found increased fly-binding antibodies in medium ground finch mothers, with a negative association between maternal antibody levels and parasite abundance. This did not rescue offspring survival; neither males nor nestlings showed equivalent induced responses. Humoral immunity and its B-cell components therefore have a strong basis; cytokines, innate recognition, inflammatory regulation, phagocytosis and complement are reasonable supporting predictions. Immune responses should not automatically be interpreted as protective. A later finch–mockingbird comparison found no significant antibody response in either nestling host species.

Mockingbirds can tolerate parasites through increased nestling begging and associated parental food provisioning, whereas the comparable finches did not compensate in that experiment. Tolerance also varies with environmental conditions and rainfall. Energy homeostasis, nutritional substrate use and growth support consequently have direct or plausible links; generic neural machinery is much less specific than feeding, begging or parental behavior itself.

Preening, scratching, larval removal/consumption, probing nests, altered brooding and fly predation have been observed, often infrequently and with uncertain population-level benefit. Galapagos flycatchers capture adult flies and remove larvae, while nestling movements sometimes dislodge larvae. Sensorimotor and sensory processes are at most supporting links unless they specifically represent the documented behavior. Finches also collect experimentally supplied permethrin-treated cotton, reducing nest parasites; this is assisted nest protection, not evidence for evolved insecticide detoxification. Plant anointing has been observed, with repellent effects suggested experimentally, but its protective role against P. downsi in nature remains uncertain.

## Scale application

5: term directly captures a documented mechanism or response (e.g. immune response, damage response, adaptive immunity or energy compensation). 4: core immune components or strong predictions from blood loss and wounds. 3: plausible supporting energy, repair, stress, vascular, immune or behavioral processes. 2: weakly connected, broadly used cellular machinery without a specific demonstrated link. 1: no specific connection, including most embryonic patterning, gametogenesis, specialized unrelated morphogenesis, and detailed nucleic-acid maintenance processes. Neither a generic signaling label nor an unrelated pathogen defense was promoted simply because it might share genes with antiparasite immunity. Mammalian platelet terms were interpreted cautiously as potential avian thrombocyte/hemostatic analogues, not literal proof of platelet biology in birds.

# Independent literature basis and scoring rationale

This review used parasite natural-history, host physiology, experimental parasite-removal, and behavioral literature. No transcriptomic, expression, or genomic findings from Galápagos birds informed the ratings. Only this run's supplied terms were read from the machine. Each GO process was judged for host relevance, rather than fly biology or a desired score distribution. Ratings describe potential association, not evidence that a process is adaptive or under selection.

## Host injury and candidate physiological responses

Early larvae invade nestling nostrils and other tissues; later larvae shelter in nest material and feed externally, principally at night. Blood loss, reduced hemoglobin, skin wounds, nasal/beak deformation, poor growth, and mortality are established consequences. These support a priori links to wound responses and repair; hemostasis; erythrocyte replacement, hematopoiesis and iron supply; oxygen limitation; and nutritional compensation. Recovery-specific molecular pathways are predictions unless directly studied. Skeletal development is less specific than skin repair: damaged beak anatomy does not make every embryonic skeletal pathway a demonstrated antiparasite mechanism.

- Dudaniec, Kleindorfer and Fessl (2006), *Effects ... on haemoglobin level and nestling survival*: https://researchers.mq.edu.au/en/publications/effects-of-the-introduced-ectoparasite-philornis-downsi-on-haemog/

## Resistance and its limits

Adult female medium ground finches can mount parasite-binding antibody responses. An experimental study supported parasite induction, with a marginal association between antibodies and lower parasite abundance, but did not demonstrate improved offspring survival. Nestling immunity can be developmentally limited; responses vary with host and study. Thus adaptive immunity directly matches the literature; B-cell processes, immune regulation and innate/inflammatory defenses are core or strong predictions rather than proof of effective protection. Inflammation, phagocytosis and antimicrobial defenses are also plausible against local damage and secondary wound contamination; the fly is neither a virus nor an intracellular pathogen, so those specialized terms receive weaker ratings.

- Koop et al. (2013), *Experimental demonstration of a parasite-induced immune response in wild birds*: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3930052/
- McNew and Clayton (2018), *Alien Invasion: Biology of Philornis Flies Highlighting Philornis downsi*: https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-ento-020117-043103

## Tolerance and behavior

Parasitized Galápagos mockingbirds increased begging and parental provisioning in an experimental comparison, consistent with compensation despite parasite exposure; finches did not exhibit the same compensation. Mockingbird tolerance varied among years and declined under dry conditions. Energy homeostasis, nutrient responses, fuel metabolism and growth maintenance are consequently relevant, while individual generic metabolic reactions remain supporting processes. Body size may contribute to tolerance, without establishing developmental pathways as direct defenses.

Preening, consuming larvae, parental probing of nostrils/nest material, scratching and avoidance/repositioning have been observed, with uncertain or limited protective effectiveness. Sensory processing, learning and motor function can support these behaviors but generic neuronal development has little specificity. Leaf anointing is a proposed form of prophylactic self-medication: plant extracts repel adult flies and affect larvae in laboratory assays; fitness benefits to wild hosts are unproven.

## Applying the scale

Directly matching broad immune responses and wound responses received 5. Core repair, inflammatory regulation, hematopoietic and innate-defense mechanisms generally received 4. Supporting physiology received 3; broad signaling, trafficking and weakly connected processes received 2; unrelated specialized development, reproduction, and many generic information-processing processes received 1. A negative regulator can be relevant because limiting damage or balancing a response matters; relevance does not imply that increasing its activity is beneficial. Platelet terms were discounted because avian hemostasis uses nucleated thrombocytes. No numeric target distribution was used.

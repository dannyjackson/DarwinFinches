# Independent literature-based rating rationale

Only the supplied input term file was read locally. Ratings use the ecological, parasitological, immunological and behavioral literature below and general biological knowledge; no genomic, transcriptomic or gene-expression findings from Galápagos birds were used. Each term was considered for its own physiological meaning, with no desired score distribution.

## Mechanisms and evidence

**Blood loss and tissue injury.** Early fly larvae inhabit nestling nostrils and other tissues; later larvae occupy nest material and feed externally on blood. Nasal damage, anemia, impaired growth and mortality are central consequences. Fessl, Sinclair and Kleindorfer (2006), *The life-cycle of Philornis downsi ...*, documents this stage-dependent feeding:
https://doi.org/10.1017/S0031182006001089
Dudaniec et al. (2006), *Effects of the introduced ectoparasite Philornis downsi on haemoglobin level and nestling survival ...*, found lower hemoglobin with parasitism and increased immature erythrocytes with parasite intensity, supporting compensatory blood-cell production:
https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1442-9993.2006.01553.x

**Immune resistance, with important limits.** Koop et al. (2013), *Experimental demonstration of a parasite-induced immune response in wild birds*, experimentally demonstrated fly-binding antibody responses in adult female medium ground finches. Higher maternal antibody levels were associated with fewer parasites, but did not rescue reproductive success. Comparable induced responses were not detected in fathers or young nestlings. Antibody-associated inflammation and interference with blood feeding are proposed mechanisms, not demonstrated molecular pathways in this system:
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3930052/
Thus adaptive immunity is directly relevant; B-cell activation, immune regulation and inflammatory effectors are strong predictions. Specific cytokines, innate-recognition pathways, complement and cellular effectors receive mechanistic rather than direct-evidence credit.

**Behavioral resistance.** O'Connor, Robertson and Kleindorfer (2010), *Video analysis of host-parasite interactions in nests of Darwin's finches*, documented nest sanitation, preening and nestling attempts to avoid or consume larvae. Such actions are age-dependent and need not be sufficient to prevent mortality:
https://doi.org/10.1017/S0030605310000086
Sensory detection and coordinated movement are relevant supporting physiology; generic synaptic or neural-development terms are less specific.

**Nutritional tolerance.** Knutie et al. (2016), *Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate introduced parasites that affect Darwin's finches*, found contrasting outcomes at similar parasite densities. Mockingbird begging and parental provisioning responses support compensation for parasitism; finches did not show comparable increases in begging, provisioning or glucose. Nestling antibody responses were not universally detectable:
https://doi.org/10.1890/15-0119.1
McNew et al. (2019), *Annual environmental variation influences host tolerance to parasites*, showed that mockingbird tolerance varied with environmental conditions and declined during limited rainfall, implicating resource-dependent provisioning:
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6408884/

**Stress is plausible, not universal.** Knutie et al. (2013), *Experimental test of the effect of introduced hematophagous flies on corticosterone levels of breeding Darwin's finches*, did not find the predicted parasite-induced maternal corticosterone response. This limits strong attribution of generic endocrine-stress pathways:
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4383185/

## Application to GO terms

Erythrocyte production and acquired immunity receive the strongest direct-mechanism ratings. Hemostasis, wound repair, skin barriers, iron management and hypoxia responses are strong physiological predictions from blood and tissue damage, rather than claims that each pathway has been measured. Tissue remodeling, metabolic compensation, oxidative defense and secondary antibacterial defense are plausible supporting processes. Ubiquitous signaling, trafficking and neuronal processes generally have weaker specificity. Unrelated reproductive, embryonic-patterning, antiviral and specialized housekeeping processes receive low ratings; their general importance to life does not establish a fly-specific association.

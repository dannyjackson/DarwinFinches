# Philornis downsi and host responses: literature summary for GO-term scoring (run_011)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- First-instar larvae live in the nares and ear canals of nestlings. Later instars stay in the nest base and feed on blood and tissue at night. They are semi-haematophagous and cause open wounds and tissue damage, including deformed nares and beaks.
- In finches and mockingbirds this reduces nestling haemoglobin by about 14%, causing anaemia and blood loss. Nestlings grow more slowly and lose mass, and their glucose drops. Nestling mortality is high (16-100%), and fledging success falls. Some host species, such as the small tree finch and warbler finch, have declined.
- Adult female medium ground finches showed no change in corticosterone or haematocrit.

## Host defences documented or proposed
1. **Resistance by adaptive immunity.** Adult medium ground finches produce *P. downsi*-specific antibodies, measured by ELISA. Levels are higher during nesting, and higher antibody levels are associated with fewer parasites (Huber et al. 2010; Koop et al. 2013). Nestlings showed little or no detectable antibody response (Knutie et al. 2016). Antibodies could also cause hypersensitivity or allergy-like effects.
2. **Tolerance.** Mockingbirds tolerate the fly. Their nestlings beg more and their parents provision more, which offsets lost blood and glucose. Tolerance depends on conditions: it breaks down in drought years. Finches did not compensate (Knutie et al. 2016; McNew et al. 2019; small tree finch provisioning study). The proposed physiological bases are replacing blood (erythropoiesis, iron), repairing tissue (wound healing, re-epithelialisation), and managing energy and nutrition.
3. **Inflammation and innate responses to wounds and myiasis** (proposed). Expected components are local inflammation, cytokine signalling, leukocyte recruitment, haemostasis and coagulation, and possibly secondary bacterial infection.
4. **Behaviour.** Finches self-fumigate by lining nests with permethrin-treated cotton (Knutie et al. 2014). Other proposed defences are nest-lining choice, brooding, begging and provisioning. These have a weak link to specific GO processes, beyond general neural, learning and behaviour terms.

## Scoring logic
- **5:** core antibody and adaptive immunity (immune response, B cell activation, adaptive immune response).
- **4:** broader immunity and inflammation, haematopoiesis and erythropoiesis, iron homeostasis, wound healing, coagulation and platelets, complement, and T/B cell processes.
- **3:** cytokine and NF-kB/JAK-STAT signalling, chemotaxis and phagocytosis, skin and keratinocyte biology, ECM and collagen, angiogenesis, hypoxia, energy and nutrient homeostasis, and pain perception.
- **2:** general stress, oxidative stress, metabolism and growth signalling, bone and skeletal (beak) development, learning and memory, and antiviral or antibacterial defence.
- **1:** everything else, including housekeeping, cell-cycle, RNA processing, neural development and similar terms.

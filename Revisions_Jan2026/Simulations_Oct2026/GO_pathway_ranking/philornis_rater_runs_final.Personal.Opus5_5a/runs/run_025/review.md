# Host responses to Philornis downsi: literature summary for GO scoring

## What the larvae do
- First-instar larvae live in the nestling's nares and body cavities; later instars feed externally at night on nestling blood and tissue. This causes blood loss (lower hemoglobin, anemia), skin lesions and wounds, reduced growth, high nestling mortality, and enlarged or deformed nares and beaks in survivors, which go on to sing altered songs. Brooding females are also bitten.
- Parasitized nests are linked to changes in parental and nestling behavior (more begging, changes in provisioning) and in stress physiology (corticosterone studies).

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Resistance (adaptive humoral immunity):** P. downsi-binding IgY antibodies in adult female medium ground finches. Antibody levels track parasite load and are lower when nests are experimentally fumigated (Huber et al. 2010; Koop et al. 2013). The response is weak or absent in nestlings, which have immature immune systems.
- **Inflammation, wound healing and tissue repair:** proposed responses to the feeding lesions and naris damage.
- **Tolerance:** replacing lost blood (erythropoiesis, iron balance, hemostasis) and managing energy. Mockingbirds, which have larger nestlings, appear to tolerate the parasite. Urban finches with access to more food or human food survive better, which suggests nutrition-mediated tolerance.
- **Behavioral defenses:** self-fumigation with permethrin-treated cotton (only a human-provided tool), nest sanitation and parental care/provisioning. Neural processes are weakly linked to these behaviors.
- **Stress and endocrine responses:** corticosterone and the HPA axis. Evidence is mixed.

## Scoring rationale
- **5:** adaptive and general immune response, which is documented in this system as the antibody response.
- **4:** core parts of that response or direct predictions from it: B/T cells, inflammation, complement, cytokines, wound healing, red-cell production, coagulation and platelets, and iron homeostasis.
- **3:** downstream signaling or support: NF-kB, TNF/IL, TLR and JAK-STAT signaling, chemotaxis, phagocytosis, skin and keratinocyte biology, angiogenesis, hypoxia, energy and nutrient status, and hormone/stress response.
- **2:** weak links: antibacterial and antiviral defense, oxidative stress, ECM and TGF-beta signaling, bone and beak development, learning and memory, and metabolism.
- **1:** everything else.

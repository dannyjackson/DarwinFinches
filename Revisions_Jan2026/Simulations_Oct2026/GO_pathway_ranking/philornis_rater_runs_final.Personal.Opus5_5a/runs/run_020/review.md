# P. downsi host-response review (run_020)

## What the larvae do
- Adult flies lay eggs in nests; first-instar larvae enter nestlings' nares/body and later instars feed externally (at night) on nestling blood and tissue. This causes blood loss (lower haemoglobin), tissue damage, and wounds. In young nestlings it causes naris/beak deformation. Nestling growth falls, and mortality often exceeds 50%. Adult female blood can also be taken.
- These effects also occur in other Galápagos passerines, such as mockingbirds and warblers. Mockingbirds appear to *tolerate* the larvae better than finches.

## Documented or proposed host defenses
- **Resistance (adaptive humoral):** Parasite-specific IgY antibodies rise in breeding females of nests with flies. Higher antibody titres correlate with fewer larvae (Koop et al. 2013).
- **Tolerance:** Tolerance is linked to replacing lost blood (erythropoiesis, haemoglobin recovery) and to wound repair. Food or rainfall conditions modify it, and parental compensation through more provisioning also plays a part. Mockingbirds keep up growth despite high loads.
- **Inflammatory / innate defenses:** These are proposed as local responses at feeding sites. Haemostasis and coagulation work against larval feeding.
- **Physiological stress and energetics:** Corticosterone, nutrient/energy balance and oxidative stress follow from blood loss and fast growth.
- **Behavioural:** Nestling begging, movement and position in the nest, and parental brooding and nest-sanitation behaviour have been proposed. Self-fumigation with permethrin cotton is human-mediated.

## Scoring logic
- 5: immune response, adaptive immunity, B-cell activation, hemopoiesis/erythrocyte differentiation, wound healing/response to wounding
- 4: core immune, coagulation, platelet, iron homeostasis, inflammation and hypoxia processes
- 3: cytokine/NF-kB/TLR signalling, skin/ECM/angiogenesis repair, stress/oxidative stress, energy balance
- 2: indirect links (antiviral/antibacterial immunity, metabolism under starvation, learning/behaviour, hormones)
- 1: no specific link

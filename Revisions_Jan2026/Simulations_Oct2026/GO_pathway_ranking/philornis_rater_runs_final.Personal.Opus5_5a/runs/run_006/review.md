# Literature review: host responses to *Philornis downsi* (run_006)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- First-instar larvae live in nestlings' nares. Later instars hide in the nest base and come out at night to feed on nestlings' blood and tissue, and sometimes on brooding females.
- This causes blood loss and reduced haemoglobin (about 28% lower; roughly 0.8 g/dL lower per larva). It also causes open wounds and tissue damage, enlarged and deformed nares and upper beaks (which can last into adulthood and affect song), lower body mass, shorter feathers, slower growth, and nestling mortality that can reach 100%.

## Documented or proposed host defences
- **Resistance through antibodies.** Adult female medium ground finches produce *P. downsi*-specific IgY, and levels rise during nesting. In tree swallows, a related host of a *Protocalliphora* nest fly, nestling IgY is negatively related to parasite load. Nestling immune systems are naive, and their antibody responses are weak.
- **Resistance through inflammation and innate immunity** at the feeding or wound site. This is proposed for *Philornis* hosts generally.
- **Tolerance.** Proposed mechanisms are blood recovery (erythropoiesis), coagulation and haemostasis at bite sites, tissue repair and wound healing, and parental compensation through provisioning. Mockingbirds tolerate the fly in wet years; tolerance is lower in dry years and is linked to provisioning rate. Finches do not compensate, and female warbler-finches provision less in heavily infested nests. Haemoglobin is reduced even in tolerant hosts.
- **Behaviour.** Females stand in the nest, probe the nest base, preen nestlings and eat larvae. Nestlings stand on their siblings, preen and eat larvae. Finches also "self-fumigate" by taking up treated cotton (a conservation intervention). No corticosterone stress response was detected in females.
- **Physiology.** Parasitized nestlings can have higher blood glucose, attributed to stress or an immune response. A protein-rich diet may support host defences.

## How this informed the scoring
- **5:** erythropoiesis and haemopoiesis, coagulation, B cell and antibody (adaptive) immunity, inflammatory response, and wound healing.
- **4:** core supporting immune processes (cytokines, NF-kB, T cells, innate immunity, complement, leukocyte adhesion), platelets, iron homeostasis and hypoxia response.
- **3:** downstream processes such as tissue repair (ECM, angiogenesis, epidermis, TGF-beta), defence against secondary bacterial infection, starvation and nutrient responses, oxidative stress and pain.
- **2:** weak links such as bone and cartilage (beak deformation), metabolism and growth, viral defence and hormones.
- **1:** all other housekeeping and developmental processes.

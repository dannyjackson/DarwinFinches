# Philornis downsi and host responses: literature review (run_007)

## What the larvae do
- First-instar larvae live in the nares and feather quills of nestlings. Later instars hide in the nest base by day and feed at night on blood and tissue. They break the skin and cause lesions, swelling and internal bleeding.
- The result is blood loss and anaemia (each larva lowers haemoglobin; about 25% blood loss can kill), lower body mass and growth, shorter feathers, and high nestling mortality (about 55% on average).
- Naris and beak deformation persists into adulthood and changes song and mating signals.

## Host defences documented or proposed
- **Resistance (adaptive immunity):** adult female *G. fortis* produce P. downsi-binding antibodies (IgY), which rise during nesting, probably through exposure at the brood patch. The antibody response is inversely related to parasite number. No response has been detected in nestlings. It may be costly and might pass to offspring through maternal antibodies.
- **Resistance (innate/inflammatory):** proposed through skin wound responses, inflammation and lesion healing.
- **Tolerance:** Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate the parasite. Parasitized nestlings beg more, parents provision more, and fledging is not reduced despite lower haemoglobin and mass. Proposed tolerance mechanisms are blood recovery (erythropoiesis, haematopoiesis, iron), haemostasis and coagulation, compensatory growth and energy metabolism, and nutrition (protein intake). Finches may hit an energetic ceiling. Plasma glucose was higher in parasitized mockingbirds.
- **Behaviour:** nestlings stand on siblings, and adults and nestlings preen off and eat larvae. Females brood less, which may lower nest temperature (thermoregulation). Finches incorporate permethrin-treated cotton into nests (self-fumigation). There was no corticosterone response in breeding females.

## Scoring rationale
- 5: adaptive immune response and immune response, which match the documented antibody response.
- 4: core blood-loss tolerance processes (haematopoiesis, erythrocyte differentiation, coagulation, platelet activation, iron homeostasis, hypoxia response), wound healing and inflammation, B and T cell activation and signalling, innate immunity and defence response.
- 3: cytokine signalling, chemotaxis, skin and epidermis development, keratinocytes, phagocytosis, energy and glucose homeostasis, thermogenesis, nutrient and starvation responses, angiogenesis, extracellular matrix and collagen, pain perception.
- 2: generic stress, oxidative, metabolic, growth, hormone and learning terms, and antiviral and antibacterial terms.
- 1: everything else (housekeeping, development, neural, reproductive processes).

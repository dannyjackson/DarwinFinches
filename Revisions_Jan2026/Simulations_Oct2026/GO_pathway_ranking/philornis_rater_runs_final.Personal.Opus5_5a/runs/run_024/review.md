# Philornis downsi and host responses: review summary for GO-term scoring

## What the larvae do
- P. downsi adults lay eggs in nests. First-instar larvae enter the nestling's nares or
  other body openings and feed internally; later instars live in nest material and feed
  externally at night, taking blood and fluids from nestlings (and brooding females).
- Effects: blood loss (lower haemoglobin, anaemia), open wounds and tissue damage,
  enlarged nares and beak deformation (which later alters song), slower growth, and high
  nestling mortality (often more than 50%, up to 100% in some years and species).
- Small-bodied hosts suffer most. Larger hosts such as mockingbirds, and nestlings that
  are better fed, lose fewer young.

## Documented or proposed host defenses
- **Resistance (immune):** nestlings and adults produce P. downsi-specific antibodies
  (IgY). Higher antibody titres in females are linked to fewer parasites in the nest.
  Inflammatory or cellular responses at feeding sites, and complement, are a priori
  candidates.
- **Tolerance:** keeping up growth and survival despite blood loss. The proposed routes
  are erythropoiesis and haemoglobin recovery, coagulation and wound repair, controlled
  inflammation (limiting immunopathology), and energy and nutrient metabolism supported by
  parental provisioning. Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate the parasite: their fitness is
  little affected because parents provision more. In finches, tolerance varies with food
  availability from year to year and with urban living (human food).
- **Behavioural:** finches add permethrin-treated cotton to their nests
  ("self-fumigation"). Other candidate behaviours are parental provisioning, brooding and
  nest sanitation, begging, and adult preening. These have weak GO correspondence.
- **Sensory and neurological effects:** nostril and beak damage affect song, so beak or
  bone repair is a downstream consideration.

## Scoring logic
- **5:** haemostasis/coagulation and platelets; wound healing; inflammatory, immune and
  defense response; the B-cell and antibody (adaptive) response; erythropoiesis and
  haematopoiesis.
- **4:** complement; cytokine and NF-kB signalling; T-cell activation and regulation;
  phagocytosis and chemotaxis; iron homeostasis; epidermis and keratinocyte
  differentiation; angiogenesis; hypoxia response; pain; bone and collagen
  (beak/naris damage).
- **3:** JAK-STAT, oxidative stress and redox, extracellular matrix, cell migration and
  adhesion, apoptosis, energy and lipid/glucose metabolism, starvation, hormone response,
  growth factors (TGF-β, VEGF, EGF), and antiviral and other general defense.
- **2:** generic signalling, ion transport, ubiquitin/proteostasis, autophagy and
  cytoskeleton.
- **1:** housekeeping and unrelated developmental processes (RNA processing, DNA
  replication, meiosis, cilia, synapses, embryonic patterning).

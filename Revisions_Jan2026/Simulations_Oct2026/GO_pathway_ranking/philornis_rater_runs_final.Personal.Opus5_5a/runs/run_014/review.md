# Philornis downsi and host responses: summary for GO term scoring

## What the larvae do
- Larvae of the invasive muscid fly *P. downsi* live in nests. They feed on the blood and tissues of nestlings, and to a lesser extent of brooding females. Early instars feed inside the nares, and later instars feed externally, mainly at night.
- Effects on nestlings: blood loss (lower haemoglobin), slower growth, and high mortality (about 50% on average, up to 100%). Larval feeding enlarges or deforms the nares, which persists into adulthood and changes song (vocal performance) and possibly mate choice and hybridisation.
- Brooding females carry little measurable cost: hematocrit, body condition and corticosterone did not change with parasite removal.

## Host defences documented or proposed
- **Resistance (immunological):** Medium ground finches produce *P. downsi*-binding IgY antibodies. Antibody titres rise with parasite exposure and are negatively related to larval load and feeding. This acquired, B-cell/antibody-mediated immunity is the best-documented mechanism. A related system (bluebirds and *Protocalliphora*) links nutrition and the gut microbiota to antibody levels; haptoglobin was not linked to antibody levels or parasite load.
- **Tolerance:** Mockingbirds tolerate the parasite: fledging success is maintained despite parasitism. Tolerance is lower in dry years and tracks parental provisioning, which suggests compensation through nutrition and replacing lost blood (erythropoiesis, iron homeostasis, coagulation, wound repair, energy and metabolic homeostasis).
- **Behavioural:** Parasitised nestlings beg more and parents feed them more. Nestlings preen off larvae and stand on nest mates, but this does not improve fledging. How much time the female spends in the nest relates to parasite load. Some finches use permethrin-treated cotton (self-fumigation) when it is provided.
- **Stress physiology:** Corticosterone was tested in females and did not differ between parasitised and parasite-free nests. Effects in nestlings remain unknown.

## Scoring rationale
- **5:** blood loss and its replacement (hemopoiesis, erythrocyte differentiation, coagulation), wound healing and response to wounding, inflammation, and antibody/B-cell/adaptive immunity.
- **4:** platelets, iron homeostasis, B and T cell activation and signalling, innate immunity, cytokines, complement, hypoxia response, leukocyte chemotaxis.
- **3:** tissue repair (skin and epidermis, ECM, angiogenesis), oxidative stress, nutrient and energy homeostasis and growth (tolerance through compensation), hormone and stress signalling, NF-kB and TNF signalling.
- **2:** generic metabolic, signalling, behavioural and cognitive, or skeletal (beak) processes with indirect links.
- **1:** housekeeping processes (DNA, RNA, cell cycle, cilia, synapse structure, development) with no specific link.

# Philornis downsi and Galápagos land birds: mechanisms review (run_017)

## What the parasite does
- Adult flies lay eggs in nests. First-instar larvae live in nestlings' nares. Older (2nd/3rd instar) larvae hide in the nest base and come out at night to feed on the blood and tissue of nestlings, and sometimes on brooding females.
- The documented effects are open wounds and lesions, enlarged nares, and beak/naris deformation in survivors (which alters song). Blood loss causes anemia: hemoglobin is about 14% lower in parasitized finch nestlings. Growth is reduced and nestling mortality is high, often 50% or more.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Resistance (adaptive/humoral):** Females and nesting adults carry P. downsi-binding IgY antibodies (Huber et al. 2010). Nestling antibody responses were weak or undetectable in later work. Evidence from P. sialia in bluebirds also supports antibody-mediated resistance in nestlings.
- **Resistance (inflammatory/innate):** Proposed local inflammatory and innate responses at feeding sites, including cytokines, leukocyte recruitment and complement. These are plausible but less directly documented.
- **Tolerance:** Compensating for blood loss (erythropoiesis, iron homeostasis, hemostasis/coagulation, wound repair). Mockingbirds tolerate the parasite through increased nestling begging and parental provisioning, and this tolerance is resource-dependent (drops in dry years). Diet and protein intake may influence defense.
- **Stress physiology:** Stress-induced corticosterone, body condition and hematocrit of breeding females did not differ with parasitism (Knutie et al. 2013). This gives only weak support for HPA or stress-axis terms.
- **Behavioral:** Finches rub leaves of the endemic Psidium galapageium on their feathers. The leaf extracts repel adult P. downsi and slow larval growth. This behavior has no clear GO process counterpart.

## Scoring logic
- **5:** Wound healing/response to wounding, blood coagulation, erythrocyte differentiation, adaptive immunity/B cells, inflammatory and immune response.
- **4:** Platelets, hemopoiesis, iron homeostasis, innate immunity/defense, complement, T cells, cytokine signaling, inflammation regulation.
- **3:** Leukocyte signaling (TLR, NF-kB, JAK-STAT, TNF/IL), chemotaxis, phagocytosis, re-epithelialization/keratinocyte/ECM/collagen, angiogenesis, hypoxia, oxidative stress, nociception.
- **2:** Antiviral pathways, MAPK cascades, growth factor and endothelial signaling, nutrition/starvation and energy homeostasis, learning, bone (beak) development, generic stress.
- **1:** Everything else.

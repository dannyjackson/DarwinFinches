# P. downsi host-response review (run_013)

## What the larvae do
- Larvae live in the nest. They feed on nestlings' blood and tissue, mostly at night. Early instars feed inside the nares, and later instars feed externally, burrowing into the skin.
- Effects on nestlings:
  - lesions, swelling and internal bleeding
  - anaemia: hemoglobin falls about 0.8 g/dL per larva, and nestlings that lose about 25% of their blood die
  - enlarged and deformed nares or beaks that persist into adulthood and can affect song and foraging
  - reduced mass and feather growth
  - high mortality, about 55% on average and up to 100%
- Brooding females are also bitten.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Resistance (adaptive humoral):** Adult female finches produce *P. downsi*-specific IgY antibodies. Experimentally parasitized nests induce this response, and higher titers are linked to fewer parasites (Huber et al. 2010).
- **Resistance (nutrition):** Food supplementation raises parasite-binding antibody levels and lowers parasite load. This was shown in a bluebird–blowfly analogue study and proposed for finches. Gut microbiota is implicated.
- **Tolerance:** Mockingbirds tolerate the parasite, but tolerance depends on rainfall and on parents' provisioning rate. Parasitized birds still lose hemoglobin and grow more slowly (McNew et al. 2019). Possible underlying mechanisms are blood replacement (erythropoiesis, iron), wound repair and growth compensation.
- **Behaviour:**
  - Nestlings stand on their siblings to avoid larvae.
  - Nestlings and females preen and eat larvae.
  - Females at parasitized nests change how they brood.
  - Finches self-fumigate by bringing permethrin-treated cotton into the nest (Knutie et al. 2014).
  - Cigarette butts appear in urban nests, but at the amounts found they are probably ineffective.
- **Stress axis:** Corticosterone and hematocrit did not differ between parasitized and unparasitized breeding females.
- **Microbiome:** Parasitized nestlings have lower gut bacterial richness.

## Scoring rationale
- **5:** immune and adaptive (antibody) responses, and B cell activation.
- **4:**
  - innate and general defense
  - inflammation and its regulation
  - wound healing
  - coagulation and platelets
  - erythropoiesis, hemopoiesis and iron homeostasis
  - complement
  - B cell differentiation and BCR signalling
- **3:**
  - T cell activation and differentiation
  - cytokine, NF-kB and JAK-STAT signalling
  - phagocytosis and chemotaxis
  - oxidative stress and hypoxia (from blood loss)
  - angiogenesis, ECM, skin and keratinocyte repair
  - pain perception
  - responses to nutrient levels and energy homeostasis
  - IGF signalling (growth compensation)
- **2:** antiviral and antibacterial (bacterial infection of wounds), stress hormone, metabolic, bone and beak development, learning and behaviour, circadian, and other indirect links.
- **1:** all other terms.

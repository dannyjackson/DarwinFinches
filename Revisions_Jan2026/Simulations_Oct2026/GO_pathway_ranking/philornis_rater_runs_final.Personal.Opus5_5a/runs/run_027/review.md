# P. downsi host-response review (run_027)

## What the larvae do
- First-instar larvae enter the nares and ear canals of nestlings. They enlarge and deform the naris and beak, which may later affect song.
- Second- and third-instar larvae hide in the nest base and come out at night to feed on nestling blood and tissue. They also feed on brooding females.
- Effects on nestlings include blood loss, lower hemoglobin, smaller size and slower growth, and high mortality (up to 100%). Heavy parasitism may also deplete protein.
- Corticosterone, hematocrit and body condition in breeding females were not affected.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Immune resistance:** Female medium ground finches produce P. downsi-specific IgY antibodies, and antibody titre was inversely related to parasite intensity. No response has been detected in nestlings. In related systems, a nestling IgY response gave resistance in swallows, and food supplementation increased antibody resistance in bluebirds.
- **Tolerance:** Parasitized mockingbird nestlings beg more and parents bring more food, so the nestlings compensate. Finches do not show this, and plasma glucose was not raised. The predicted mechanisms are recovery from blood loss (erythropoiesis, hemoglobin and iron), wound repair and limits on inflammation. Diet and protein may also matter.
- **Behavioral defenses:** Nestlings climb over nestmates in heavily parasitized nests. Grooming and larva removal are rare. Adults rub guava leaves (P. guajava) on their feathers, which may repel flies, and heavily parasitized mothers change how they brood. Breeding timing, nest height and nest microclimate affect exposure.

## Basis of the scores
- **5:** immune and adaptive immune responses, and B-cell activation (antibody).
- **4:** innate immunity, defense response, coagulation and platelets, wound healing, erythropoiesis, hemopoiesis, iron homeostasis, complement, B-cell signaling and T-cell activation.
- **3:** inflammation and cytokine regulation, NF-kB, TLR and JAK-STAT signaling, phagocytosis and leukocyte adhesion, epidermis, keratinocyte and ECM repair, angiogenesis, hypoxia, nutrient and glucose homeostasis, and pain perception.
- **2:** indirect links, such as oxidative stress, generic bacterial and LPS responses, TGF-beta, angiogenesis subprocesses, starvation and metabolism, bone and beak development, and circadian rhythm or learning.
- **1:** everything else.

I did not use any transcriptomic, gene-expression or genomic results from Galápagos birds.

# Philornis downsi: host effects and defenses (basis for GO scoring)

## What the larvae do
- First-instar larvae enter nestling nares and feed on blood and tissue. This enlarges or deforms the nares and in severe cases destroys the beak. Second and third instars live in the nest base and feed on exposed skin at night. The resulting damage includes open lesions, swelling and bleeding under the skin.
- Blood loss lowers hemoglobin (about 0.8 g/dL less per larva) and slows growth in body mass and feathers. Mortality is high, up to 100% of a brood. Adult brooding females are also bitten.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Resistance (immune):** Adult female medium ground finches make P. downsi-specific IgY antibodies. Levels rise during nesting and in parasitized nests, and higher antibody levels have been associated with lower parasite loads. Maternal transfer of antibodies to chicks has been proposed. These findings point to adaptive and humoral immunity (B cells), inflammation and innate immunity.
- **Tolerance:** Hosts compensate for blood loss and keep growing. This involves erythropoiesis, iron homeostasis, hemostasis (coagulation and platelets), wound healing (skin and keratinocytes) and energy and nutrient allocation. Tolerance depends on food: mockingbirds tolerate the parasite in wet years, and a protein-rich diet improves survival.
- **Behavior:** Females brood less, probe the nest base, preen nestlings and eat larvae. Nestlings stand on siblings, preen themselves and beg. Finches incorporate permethrin-treated cotton into nests ("self-fumigation"). Nestling reactivity predicts naris deformation. These behaviors suggest a role for learning and possibly cognition.
- **Stress:** Experiments found no effect of the parasite on corticosterone in adult females.

## Scoring logic
- **5:** Immune response, adaptive immune response and B cell activation (the antibody response is documented).
- **4:** Immune and defense processes, B cell receptor signaling, hematopoiesis and erythropoiesis, inflammation, complement, coagulation and platelets, and wound healing.
- **3:** Cytokine, NF-kB, JAK-STAT and T cell signaling; oxidative stress; iron; skin and epidermis; ECM and collagen; angiogenesis; hypoxia; phagocytosis; energy and starvation; learning.
- **2:** Weakly linked growth-factor, metabolic, hormonal, bone and beak, and vascular signaling processes.
- **1:** Generic cellular housekeeping, developmental and neural processes.

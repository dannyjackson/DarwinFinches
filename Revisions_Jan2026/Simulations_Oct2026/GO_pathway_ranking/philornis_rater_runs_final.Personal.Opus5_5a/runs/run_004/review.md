# P. downsi and Galápagos land birds: mechanism review (run_004)

## What the larvae do to their hosts
- First-instar larvae live in the nostrils and ear canals of nestlings, or in feather quills. Second and third instars hide in the nest base and come out at night to feed on the blood and tissue of nestlings, and of brooding females.
- Larvae attach externally; they do not live under the skin.
- The feeding causes:
  - blood loss, with lower haemoglobin and anaemia (about 28% lower Hb, and about 2% lower Hb per larva);
  - lower body mass and shorter primary feathers;
  - deformed nares and beaks, which later change song and mating signals;
  - very high nestling mortality, sometimes 100%.
- Heavily parasitized females brood less.
- Female corticosterone and hematocrit did not differ between parasitized and non-parasitized nests in medium ground finches.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
1. **Adaptive/humoral immunity (resistance).** Breeding medium ground finch females produce P. downsi-binding antibodies (IgY), at levels inversely related to parasite intensity. This did not improve nestling survival. Nestling antibody responses were weak or absent.
2. **Tolerance through blood replacement.** Mockingbirds tolerate the flies. Parasitized nestlings have lower Hb, and the authors propose rapid red blood cell replacement (erythropoiesis) as a tolerance mechanism.
3. **Tolerance through energy compensation.** Parasitized mockingbird nestlings beg more and their parents feed them more. Their glucose is also higher. Finches do not compensate this way.
4. **Proposed innate and inflammatory responses.** These include inflammation at feeding wounds, wound healing and repair of skin and epidermis, haemostasis and coagulation, and responses to secondary bacterial infection.
5. **Behavioral defenses.** Preening is rare. Finches may rub guava leaves on feathers, and they incorporate permethrin-treated cotton when it is offered. Changes in nest site and breeding timing are another option. These map poorly onto GO cellular processes.
6. **Condition and diet effects.** Supporting processes include nutrition, energy and glucose homeostasis, and oxidative stress from blood loss and inflammation.

## Scoring logic
- **5:** erythrocyte differentiation and its positive regulation, adaptive immune response, and immune response.
- **4:** B-cell and immunoglobulin processes, wound healing and response to wounding, inflammation, innate and defense responses, coagulation, haematopoiesis, complement, and iron homeostasis.
- **3:** T-cell activation, cytokine and chemokine signalling, NF-kB and JAK-STAT signalling, keratinocyte and epidermis development, ECM and collagen, angiogenesis, hypoxia and oxidative stress, glucose and energy homeostasis, platelets, and pain perception.
- **2:** more peripheral immune, metabolic, vascular and skeletal (beak) processes, and broad stress responses.
- **1:** all other terms, including housekeeping, neural, developmental and reproductive processes.

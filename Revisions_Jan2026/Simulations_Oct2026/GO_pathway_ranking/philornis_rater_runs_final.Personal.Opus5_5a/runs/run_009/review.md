# Literature review: host responses to Philornis downsi (run_009)

## What the larvae do
- Free-living adult flies lay eggs in nests; first-instar larvae enter nestlings' nares (causing naris enlargement, beak deformation) and later instars feed externally and at night on nestling blood and tissue, creating open wounds.
- Effects: blood loss (lower haemoglobin), reduced growth, high nestling mortality (often >50%, up to 100% in some nests), lower fledging success; some effects on brooding females.

## Documented / proposed host defenses
- **Resistance (immune):** P. downsi-specific antibody (IgY) responses in nestlings and adults, higher in adults; antibody levels associated with fewer larvae / fitness benefits (Huber et al. 2010; Koop et al. 2013). Innate responses (inflammation at feeding sites, wound repair, coagulation) are predicted.
- **Tolerance:** compensating for blood loss (erythropoiesis, haemoglobin recovery, iron homeostasis), maintaining growth despite parasitism; tolerance can be enhanced by food supplementation/urban resources (Knutie et al. 2016 Ecology; urban-living rescue study).
- **Behavioral:** parental provisioning increases (begging, feeding rate), nestling begging/body movement, female brooding/preening and nest sanitation; possible self-medication with Psidium galapagense leaves in nests (Cimadom et al. 2016); host stress hormone (corticosterone) assessed with mixed results.

## Scoring approach
- 5: adaptive/humoral immune response, defense response, erythrocyte production (blood-loss tolerance).
- 4: innate immunity, inflammation, cytokine regulation, coagulation/platelets, wound healing, B/T cell activity, iron homeostasis.
- 3: inflammatory signalling cascades (NF-kB, JAK-STAT, TNF, IL-1/IL-17), phagocytosis, hypoxia/oxidative stress, skin/keratinocyte and ECM repair, angiogenesis, nociception.
- 2: generic metabolism/energy/nutrient-stress, hormone signalling, antiviral/antibacterial pathways, vascular development, behavior/learning, circadian.
- 1: housekeeping, development unrelated to the above, neural/synaptic detail, DNA/RNA processing, reproduction.
- Genomic/transcriptomic studies of Galápagos birds (e.g., bioRxiv 2023.03.06.531275) were excluded.

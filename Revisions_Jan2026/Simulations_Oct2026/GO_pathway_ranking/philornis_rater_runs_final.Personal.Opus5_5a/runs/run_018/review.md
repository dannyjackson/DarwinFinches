# P. downsi host-response review (run_018)

## What the larvae do
- First-instar larvae live in the nares and ear canals of nestlings and cause nares and beak deformation. Older instars hide in the nest base by day and feed externally on nestling blood and tissue at night. They also feed on brooding females.
- The results are blood loss (lower hemoglobin), reduced mass and growth, and high nestling mortality (up to 100%). The larvae open wounds, which carry a possible risk of secondary infection or pathogen transmission.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Adaptive/humoral resistance:** Female medium ground finches produce P. downsi-specific antibodies (IgY), and titers rise with exposure (Koop et al. 2013). Nestlings show little antibody response.
- **Innate/inflammatory resistance:** Proposed from general ectoparasite biology: inflammation at feeding sites, cytokines, leukocyte recruitment and complement.
- **Tolerance of blood loss:** Hemoglobin loss suggests that erythropoiesis, hemostasis and coagulation, wound healing and skin repair, and nutritional or energetic compensation would help hosts cope. Higher protein intake is associated with better outcomes.
- **Behavioral tolerance:** Galápagos mockingbird nestlings beg more and parents provision more (Knutie et al. 2016). Finches do not show this.
- **Behavioral resistance:** Brooding and standing behavior, preening or picking off larvae, and possible guava-leaf self-anointing.
- **Comparison with other hosts:** Bluebirds and other mainland hosts differ in tolerance versus resistance to Philornis (Knutie lab).

## Scoring logic
- **5:** Immune response and adaptive immune response.
- **4:** B cell and antibody processes, inflammation, innate defense, erythropoiesis and hemopoiesis, coagulation and platelets, wound healing, and iron homeostasis.
- **3:** Cytokine, NF-kB, JAK-STAT, T-cell and chemotaxis signaling; oxidative stress; hypoxia; skin and ECM repair; TGF-beta.
- **2:** Antiviral and antibacterial responses (secondary infection), angiogenesis, nutrition and energy homeostasis, nociception, and bone and beak development.
- **1:** Generic housekeeping, neural or developmental processes, and anything else.

No genomic or transcriptomic results from Galápagos birds were used.

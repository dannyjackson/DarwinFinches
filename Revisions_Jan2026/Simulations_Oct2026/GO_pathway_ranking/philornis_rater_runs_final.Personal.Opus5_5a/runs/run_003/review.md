# Literature review: Philornis downsi and host responses (run_003)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- First-instar larvae enter the nares (and ear canals) of nestlings, causing naris enlargement and beak deformation. Later instars live in the nest base and feed at night on blood and tissue fluids through skin wounds. They also feed on brooding/incubating females.
- Effects reported: blood loss of 18-55%, lower hemoglobin and hematocrit (anemia), slower growth (especially feathers), multiple body wounds with possible secondary infection, very high nestling mortality (often >50%, up to 100%), and altered song in adults with deformed beaks.
- Effects on adult females were weak: no change in corticosterone, condition or hematocrit (Knutie et al. 2015).

## Host defences documented or proposed
- **Resistance (adaptive immunity):** finches mount *P. downsi*-specific antibody (IgY) responses, and higher antibody levels in females are linked to fewer larvae (Huber et al. 2010; Koop et al. 2013). Related nest-fly systems show nestling IgY responses that scale negatively with parasite load (Grab et al. 2019).
- **Tolerance:** mockingbirds tolerate the parasite better than finches. This tolerance depends on parental food provisioning and rainfall, and it involves keeping growth and survival despite blood loss (Knutie et al. 2016, 2019). Proposed physiological pathways: blood replacement (erythropoiesis, iron handling, haemostasis), wound repair and nutrient/energy metabolism.
- **Behavioural:** finches line nests with permethrin-treated cotton (self-fumigation; Knutie et al. 2014). Other proposed behaviours are female nest attendance, nestlings avoiding larvae, begging and parental compensation, and nest sanitation.
- **Supporting processes (inferred):** inflammatory and innate responses at the feeding wounds, defence against secondary bacterial infection, coagulation and platelet function (larval anticoagulant feeding), keratinocyte/epidermal repair, angiogenesis, oxidative stress from blood feeding and haem, and the stress or HPA axis.

## How these were used in scoring
- **5:** adaptive and antibody-mediated responses (B cell activation).
- **4:** coagulation, erythropoiesis, iron homeostasis, hypoxia, wound healing, inflammation, complement, innate immunity, lymphocyte activation.
- **3:** cytokine and NF-kB signalling, antibacterial defence, phagocytosis, oxidative stress, angiogenesis, epidermal repair, glucose and energy homeostasis.
- **2:** weak links, such as general growth factor, nutrient, circadian, learning and bone/beak development.
- **1:** all other terms.

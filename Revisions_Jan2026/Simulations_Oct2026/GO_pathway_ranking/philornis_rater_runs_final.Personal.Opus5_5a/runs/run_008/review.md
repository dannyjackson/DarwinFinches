# Literature review: P. downsi host-response mechanisms (run_008)

## What the larvae do
- Semi-hematophagous. First instars live in the nares and ear canals and eat keratin and tissue. Later instars live at the nest base and feed on nestlings at night: they pierce the skin, take blood and fluids, and cause swelling, lesions and internal bleeding.
- Hosts suffer blood loss and anemia. Hemoglobin falls by about 0.8 g/dL per larva, and nestlings that lose about 25% of their blood die within hours. Parasitized nestlings show reduced mass and feather growth.
- The nares become enlarged and the beak deformed, which can last into adulthood. This changes song (vocal performance and mate signalling).
- In-nest mortality ranges from 16% to 100%. Since about 2006, larvae also feed on incubating or brooding females.

## Documented or proposed host defenses
- **Resistance (immune):** brooding females make P. downsi-specific antibodies (IgY), and these are associated with lower parasite intensity. Experimental parasitism induces this antibody response. Inflammation and cell-mediated responses at feeding wounds are proposed.
- **Tolerance:** replacing lost blood (erythropoiesis, hemostasis/coagulation, iron homeostasis) and repairing wounds. Mockingbirds tolerate the fly through more nestling begging and parental provisioning, which offsets the energy and nutrient cost. Finches showed no rise in plasma glucose or provisioning. Diet and protein intake affect outcome.
- **Behavioral:** nestlings stand on siblings, preen and eat larvae. Females brood erect and probe the nest base. Use of fumigated material in nests is a managed intervention, not a natural defense.
- **Stress axis:** adult corticosterone and hematocrit did not change, so evidence for an HPA response is weak.

## Scoring rationale
- **5:** immune response, adaptive immunity, B cell activation (antibodies), response to wounding, wound healing.
- **4:** coagulation, erythropoiesis, inflammation, defense response, platelet processes, iron homeostasis, complement.
- **3:** cytokine and NF-kB signaling, T cells, skin and keratinocytes, oxidative stress, hypoxia, angiogenesis, ECM and collagen, TGF-beta, energy and glucose homeostasis, nutrient response, pain.
- **2:** pathogen-specific (bacterial or viral) immune terms, broad metabolic terms, hormone, circadian, learning and hearing terms.
- **1:** generic cellular machinery and development.

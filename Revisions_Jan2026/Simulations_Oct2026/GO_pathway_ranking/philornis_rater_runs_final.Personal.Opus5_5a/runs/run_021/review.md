# Philornis downsi and Galápagos land birds: mechanisms used for GO scoring

## What the parasite does
- Larvae of *P. downsi* feed on the blood and tissue of nestlings. First instars live in the nares, and later instars hide in the nest base and feed at night. This causes blood loss (lower hemoglobin), reduced growth, enlarged or deformed nares and beaks (with downstream effects on song), and high nestling mortality. Brooding females are occasionally bitten.
- Female finches at parasitized nests showed no change in corticosterone or hematocrit (Knutie et al. 2013), so evidence for an HPA stress-axis response in adults is weak. Nestlings were not tested.

## Documented or proposed host defenses
1. **Adaptive/humoral immunity (resistance).** Adult medium ground finches produce *P. downsi*-specific IgY. Levels are higher in nesting birds and in females, and experimental exposure induces the response (Huber et al. 2010; Koop et al. 2013). Antibody levels in nestlings are low (Knutie et al. 2016, 2017). Inflammatory and type I interferon signaling have been proposed for resistant urban nestlings (McNew et al., urban rescue study).
2. **Tolerance.** Mockingbirds tolerate the parasite despite large drops in hemoglobin. Proposed mechanisms are red blood cell replacement (erythropoiesis), more begging, and increased parental provisioning. Plasma glucose and energy status also shift. Larger body size and a higher-protein diet (urban δ15N) are associated with better outcomes.
3. **Behavioral.** Possible behaviors include nest sanitation and preening, and finches incorporating permethrin-treated cotton or human materials into nests (self-fumigation). Begging intensity and parental feeding are also involved.
4. **Wound repair and hemostasis.** These follow from the tissue damage and bleeding that larvae cause (inference from the larval feeding mode).

## How scores were assigned
- **5:** adaptive and immune response, B cell activation, erythrocyte differentiation, wound healing and response to wounding, inflammatory response.
- **4:** innate immunity, hemopoiesis, coagulation and platelet processes, complement, T/B cell regulation, iron homeostasis, cytokine production.
- **3:** cytokine signaling (NF-κB, JAK-STAT, TNF, IL), phagocytosis, chemotaxis, skin and epidermis development and ECM repair, angiogenesis, hypoxia, oxidative stress, glucose and energy homeostasis, pain perception.
- **2:** antiviral and antibacterial responses, metabolic and insulin pathways, bone and beak growth, learning, memory and hearing (song), circadian rhythm and hormone signaling, general stress responses.
- **1:** everything else.

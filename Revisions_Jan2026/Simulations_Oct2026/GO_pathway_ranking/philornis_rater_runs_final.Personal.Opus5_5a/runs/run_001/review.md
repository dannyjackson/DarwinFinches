# Philornis downsi and Galápagos land birds: mechanism review (run_001)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- First-instar larvae live in nestlings' nares and ear canals. Second- and third-instar larvae hide in the nest base and come out at night to chew through skin and feed on blood and tissue fluids. They also feed on incubating and brooding females.
- Effects on hosts: blood loss, with lower hemoglobin and hematocrit (anemia); open skin wounds; reduced growth; disturbed sleep and lost energy from nocturnal feeding; and very high nestling mortality, often more than 80% in heavily infested nests.
- Larvae feeding in the nares enlarge them and deform the beak. This persists into adulthood and changes song and mating signals.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
1. **Adaptive (humoral) immunity, resistance.** Medium ground finches and nestlings produce antibodies (IgY) specific to P. downsi, measured by ELISA. Antibody levels rise during nesting, and higher levels go with fewer parasites. A food-supplementation study in a related nest-fly system linked IgY levels to lower parasite load.
2. **Inflammation and innate immunity at the bite site.** Proposed as a resistance mechanism: inflammation, acute-phase responses and cytokine signalling, with skin and wound repair.
3. **Tolerance through blood replacement and clotting.** Proposed to offset blood loss: red blood cell production (erythropoiesis) and iron homeostasis, plus clotting (hemostasis) and wound healing to limit bleeding.
4. **Nutrition and energy.** Protein-rich diets and food supplementation may increase resistance and tolerance. This implicates nutrient sensing and energy balance, but as weak or indirect links.
5. **Behavioral tolerance.** Mockingbirds tolerate the fly: parasitized nestlings beg more and parents feed them more. Finches showed little or no parental compensation. These are behavioral and neural traits, so the link is weak at the GO level.
6. **Behavioral resistance.** Adult finches rub leaves of *Psidium galapageium* onto their feathers. Leaf extracts repel adult flies and slow larval growth. Nestlings also seem to move away from larvae.
7. **Stress physiology.** Corticosterone in breeding females was not affected by the fly, and plasma glucose did not respond. This gives little support for a stress or metabolic route.

## Scoring logic
- **5:** immune response, adaptive immunity, defense response, inflammation, and the wounding and wound-healing terms.
- **4:** B cells and antibody production, erythropoiesis and blood formation, iron homeostasis, coagulation and platelets, innate immunity, complement, and regulation of immune and inflammatory responses.
- **3:** T cells, cytokines, NF-kB and JAK-STAT signalling, chemotaxis and phagocytosis, epidermis and keratinocytes, angiogenesis and extracellular matrix/TGF-beta tissue repair, hypoxia (from anemia), and oxidative stress.
- **2:** antibacterial and LPS terms (secondary infection of wounds), nutrient and energy balance, learning and memory, the roof-of-mouth term (beak and nares), and general stress.
- **1:** everything else.

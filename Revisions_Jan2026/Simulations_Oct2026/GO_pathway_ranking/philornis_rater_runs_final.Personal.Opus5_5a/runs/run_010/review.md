# Philornis downsi and host responses: review summary (run_010)

## What the larvae do to hosts
- *P. downsi* is an introduced nest fly. Its larvae live in nest material and feed on the blood and tissue of nestlings (and brooding females) at night. Second and third instar larvae feed externally. First instars enter the nares and damage the beak and nasal cavity, which deforms the nares.
- Effects on nestlings: blood loss with lower haemoglobin/hematocrit (anaemia), slower growth, and lower fledging success, often close to 100% mortality in finches.
- Long-term effects: malformed nares and beaks alter vocal performance (song) and may affect mate choice and hybridization.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
1. **Adaptive/humoral immunity (resistance):** Medium ground finches make *P. downsi*-binding IgY antibodies. Levels are higher during nesting and in females, which brood. Antibody level was negatively associated with larval feeding success and parasite load (Koop et al. 2013; WSU ecoimmunity work). The shared story is that larval feeding induces the response, but it is weak or slow, and on its own it is not an effective resistance.
2. **Tolerance:** Galápagos mockingbirds tolerate the parasite. Parasitized nestlings have lower haemoglobin but still fledge in wet years. Tolerance rises with parental provisioning and food availability, and parasitized nestlings beg more. Compensation for blood loss (erythropoiesis, iron homeostasis, haemoglobin recovery), growth and energy/nutrient allocation are the main proposed tolerance mechanisms.
3. **Wound and tissue repair:** Larval feeding wounds and naris lesions point to haemostasis/coagulation, inflammation, wound healing and skin/keratinocyte repair as a priori defense or tolerance processes.
4. **Stress physiology:** Fly removal did not change female corticosterone (Knutie et al. 2016 GCE). Endocrine stress pathways are therefore at most weakly supported.
5. **Behavioral defenses:** Nestlings preen and stand on nestmates, and females brood. Finches rub *Psidium galapageium* leaves (terpenoids) into their feathers (self-medication), and they use permethrin-treated cotton when it is provided. These behaviors are hard to map onto GO processes. Learning, cognition and chemosensation get at most weak scores.

## Scoring rationale
- 5: adaptive immune response, immune response.
- 4: lymphocyte (T/B) activation and signalling, immune regulation, innate immune response, inflammation, cytokine production, complement, coagulation, platelet activation, wound healing, erythrocyte differentiation, haematopoiesis, iron homeostasis.
- 3: TNF/IL-1/NF-kB/TLR/JAK-STAT signalling, chemotaxis and phagocytosis, oxidative stress, angiogenesis, ECM/collagen/skin and keratinocyte repair, hypoxia, nutrient/starvation responses, pain.
- 2: general metabolic, growth, endocrine, bone (beak), and broad stress or antiviral/antibacterial terms.
- 1: housekeeping, neural development and other unrelated processes.
- Following the instructions, no host transcriptomic or genomic results were used. The Knutie et al. bioRxiv 2023 preprint appeared in search results, but its content was not consulted.

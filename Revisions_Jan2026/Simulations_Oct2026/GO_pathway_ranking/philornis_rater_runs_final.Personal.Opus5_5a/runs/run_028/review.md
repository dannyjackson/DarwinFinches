# Philornis downsi and Galápagos land birds: mechanisms used for scoring

## What the larvae do
- First-instar larvae live in the nares and nasal cavity of nestlings. Later instars move to the nest material and feed at night on blood and tissue from nestlings and brooding females. The bites cause open wounds, blood loss, lower haemoglobin (anaemia), slower growth and high nestling mortality, often more than 50%.
- Damage to the nares and beak in nestlings leads to enlarged nares and beak deformation in adults. This changes song, a mating signal, and may affect hybridisation.
- In adults, parasitism has been linked to stress-hormone (corticosterone) effects.

## Host defences, documented or proposed
- **Resistance (immune):** Finches produce P. downsi-specific IgY antibodies (an adaptive or humoral B-cell response). Antibody titres were linked to fewer parasites or better outcomes. Inflammation and wound repair at the feeding sites are expected.
- **Tolerance:** Hosts limit the damage caused by blood loss through erythropoiesis, haemoglobin and iron recovery, haemostasis and coagulation, and wound healing. Parasitized mockingbird and finch nestlings beg more and get more parental provisioning, which means more energy and nutrient intake. Tolerance drops in drought years, when food is scarce.
- **Behavioural:** Nestlings preen off larvae or stand on nest mates. Adults self-fumigate nests with Psidium leaves (proposed). These behaviours are only weakly effective.

## How this was applied to the scores
- **Score 5:** blood loss and haemostasis, erythropoiesis, wound healing, and adaptive or B-cell immunity.
- **Score 4:** core immune, inflammatory and haematopoietic processes, and iron homeostasis.
- **Score 3:** cytokine signalling, skin and ECM repair, angiogenesis, hypoxia, oxidative stress, nutrient and starvation responses, and steroid-hormone (corticosterone) signalling.
- **Score 2:** antiviral and antibacterial immunity, metabolism, bone or beak growth, and stress and hormone signalling.
- **Score 1:** everything else.

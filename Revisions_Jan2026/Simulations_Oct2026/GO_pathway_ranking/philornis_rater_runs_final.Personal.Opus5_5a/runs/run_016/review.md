# Philornis downsi and Galápagos land birds: host-response mechanisms (run_016)

## What the larvae do
- Adult flies lay eggs in nests. First-instar larvae get into the nestlings' nares and feed internally. Second- and third-instar larvae live in nest material and feed on blood and tissue at night. The results are blood loss (lower hemoglobin, anemia), skin and tissue wounds, naris and beak deformation in survivors, reduced growth, and high nestling mortality in Darwin's finches. Brooding females are also bitten.
- Parasitized mockingbird nestlings have consistently lower hemoglobin and grow more slowly. The growth cost is greater in dry years.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
1. **Resistance (adaptive/humoral immunity):** Medium ground finches produce P. downsi-specific IgY antibodies. In females, antibody titres rise with parasite load and fall when nests are fumigated. Nestlings show little antibody response. Higher titres are linked to fewer larvae and better fledging success in some contexts, including urban finches with access to human food.
2. **Tolerance:** Mockingbirds tolerate P. downsi better than finches. Tolerance depends on resources: it rises with rainfall and parental provisioning. Proposed physiological routes are compensating for blood loss (erythropoiesis, iron), energy and nutrient allocation, and growth maintenance.
3. **Wound and blood responses:** Proposed mechanisms include haemostasis and coagulation at feeding sites, inflammation, and wound healing and tissue repair (skin and keratin).
4. **Stress physiology:** Corticosterone responses to the flies have been tested in breeding finches.
5. **Behavior:** Video studies of host-parasite interactions in nests and nestling behavior (position, activity) predict naris deformation. Parents may use defensive or grooming behaviors. Finches self-fumigate by taking permethrin-treated cotton into nests, which is human-provided and not evolved.

## How the scores map to these mechanisms
- **5:** immune response and adaptive immune response (the documented IgY resistance).
- **4:** B-cell and humoral processes, regulation of immune response, complement, defense response, innate immunity, inflammation, wound healing, coagulation and platelets, and erythropoiesis/hemopoiesis (compensating for anemia).
- **3:** T-cell and cytokine signaling, NF-kB and TLR signaling, phagocytosis and chemotaxis, epidermal and keratinocyte repair, collagen and ECM, angiogenesis, iron homeostasis, hypoxia, oxidative stress, nutrient and starvation responses, energy homeostasis, and pain perception.
- **2:** general stress, cell migration and proliferation, bone and skeletal development (beak deformation), hormone and glucose/lipid metabolism, learning and circadian behavior, and similar.
- **1:** all remaining terms.

# P. downsi host-response review (run_002)

## What the larvae do
- First-instar larvae enter the nares and ear canals of nestlings. Second and third instars live in the nest base and feed at night on blood and serous fluids. The result is blood loss with lower haemoglobin (anaemia), slower growth, enlarged or deformed nares and beak (which affects later song), and high nestling mortality (often above 50%, up to 100%). Mortality is worse in dry, food-limited years.
- No adult effects have been shown. Nesting females showed no change in corticosterone or body condition.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
1. **Adaptive immune (resistance):** Nesting female medium ground finches produce P. downsi-binding antibodies (IgY, measured by ELISA). Antibody levels were inversely related to parasite load (Koop et al. 2013). No immune response has been shown in nestlings. In a related nest-fly system, food supplementation raised IgY and lowered parasite load, showing resource-dependent resistance (Knutie 2020, bluebirds).
2. **Tolerance of blood loss:** Haemoglobin loss implies compensation through red-cell production and iron metabolism (a strong a priori prediction). Mockingbirds tolerate the fly through extra begging and extra parental provisioning, which depends on food supply. Diet and protein intake are linked to parasite outcomes.
3. **Wound and tissue repair:** Feeding damages skin and nasal tissue, so wound healing, inflammation, epidermis and keratinocyte processes, coagulation and platelet function, and extracellular-matrix and collagen remodelling are all relevant.
4. **Behavioural:** Rare preening of larvae. Nestlings climbing over nestmates. Changes in female brooding. Self-anointing with Psidium galapagense leaves, which repel flies (Cimadom et al. 2016). Timing and placement of nests. Possible selection for smaller clutches.
5. **Innate and inflammatory:** Proposed general responses to ectoparasite feeding include cytokines, NF-kB, JAK-STAT, TLRs, phagocytes and complement.

## Scoring logic
- **5:** Immune response, adaptive immune response, and erythrocyte differentiation (including its positive regulation).
- **4:** Core immune, inflammatory and B-cell processes, complement, haemopoiesis, iron homeostasis, wound healing and coagulation.
- **3:** Cytokine, innate-signalling and T-cell processes, hypoxia and oxidative stress, nutrient response, skin, ECM and angiogenesis-adjacent processes.
- **2:** Antimicrobial and antiviral processes, metabolic and growth signalling, bone and muscle growth, behaviour and learning, hormone response.
- **1:** All other terms.

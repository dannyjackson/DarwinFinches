# Philornis downsi: host effects and defenses (a priori review for GO scoring)

## What the larvae do
- Adult flies lay eggs in nests. First-instar larvae enter the nares and ear canals of nestlings, and later instars live in the nest base and feed at night on nestling blood and tissue. They also feed on brooding females.
- Documented costs include blood loss with reduced hemoglobin (anemia), open wounds and tissue damage, reduced growth, and very high nestling mortality. Larvae in the nares cause deformation and enlargement of the nares and beak, which changes song (vocal performance) later in life.
- Larvae may also deplete nestling protein and energy reserves (glucose changes have been reported) and cause physiological stress.

## Host defenses documented or proposed
- **Resistance (immune):** Nestlings and adult females mount a parasite-specific IgY antibody response, and antibody levels are negatively related to parasite load. Antibodies may cause tissue swelling that blocks larval feeding. Inflammatory and innate immune responses at the feeding site are also proposed. Overall resistance in Galápagos birds is described as weak, because the parasite is novel.
- **Tolerance:** Hosts can compensate for blood loss (erythropoiesis, iron and hemoglobin recovery), repair wounds through clotting and skin repair, and draw on energy and nutritional reserves. Tolerance has been shown in mockingbirds, where it falls in dry years. Better diet (higher protein) is linked to fewer parasites and higher survival.
- **Behavioral:** Female in-nest attendance predicts parasite numbers. Parents may compensate by provisioning more, and nestling begging changes. Finches rub *Psidium galapageium* leaves on their feathers, which repels adult flies and reduces larval growth (self-medication).
- **Other proposed factors:** stress hormones (glucocorticoids) and hypoxia from anemia, and gut microbiota that affect immunity.

## How the scores were assigned
- **5:** adaptive and B cell immune response, inflammatory response.
- **4:** innate and general immune or defense processes; coagulation and platelets; wound healing; erythropoiesis and iron homeostasis; T cell activation; cytokine signaling.
- **3:** specific cytokine and NF-kB or TLR pathways, phagocytosis, chemotaxis, hypoxia, epidermis and collagen repair, oxidative stress, glucose and nutrient homeostasis, pain.
- **2:** angiogenesis, extracellular matrix, growth-factor signaling, antiviral and interferon regulation, metabolism and stress hormones, learning and behavior, circadian rhythm.
- **1:** all other terms.

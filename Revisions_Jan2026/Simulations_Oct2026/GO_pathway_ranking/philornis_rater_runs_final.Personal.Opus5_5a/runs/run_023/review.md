# Philornis downsi host-response review (run_023)

## What the larvae do
- Adult flies lay eggs in nests; 1st-instar larvae enter nestling nares/ear canals; later instars live in nest base and feed nocturnally on nestling blood and tissue (hematophagy, skin lesions).
- Effects: blood loss and reduced haemoglobin, naris deformation/tissue damage, reduced growth, high nestling mortality in Darwin's finches (often >50%).

## Documented / proposed host defenses
- **Resistance (immune):** Nestlings and adult females produce P. downsi-specific IgY antibodies; higher antibody titres associated with fewer parasites (Huber et al. 2010; Koop et al. 2013). Implies adaptive humoral immunity, B-cell activation, inflammation at feeding sites.
- **Tolerance:** Galápagos mockingbirds and some finches tolerate parasitism (maintain growth/survival despite loads), linked to food availability and parental compensation (Knutie et al. 2016; annual environmental variation in tolerance). Supporting physiology: wound healing, haemostasis/coagulation, erythropoiesis to replace blood loss, energy/nutrient allocation.
- **Behavioural:** Parental provisioning and begging changes in parasitized nests; self-fumigation/nest-lining with Psidium galapagoium leaves (repellent to the fly); nest sanitation proposed.
- Downstream: reduced future reproduction of parents, oxidative stress, secondary infection risk.

## Scoring logic
5 = defense/immune response/adaptive immunity; 4 = humoral/B-cell, innate immunity, inflammation, wound healing, coagulation/platelets, erythropoiesis, cytokine signaling; 3 = T-cell, phagocytosis, chemotaxis, TLR/NF-kB/TNF/JAK-STAT, oxidative stress, iron homeostasis, skin/epidermis, angiogenesis, ECM repair, nutrient stress, pain; 2 = antiviral, generic stress/apoptosis, metabolic/energy, hormonal, growth, adhesion/migration; 1 = everything else.
